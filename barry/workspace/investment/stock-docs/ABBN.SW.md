# ABBN.SW — ABB Ltd

## Business Overview

ABB is a Swiss-listed global electrification and automation company. The post-Robotics portfolio is increasingly focused on three areas: Electrification, Motion, and Automation. It sells electrical distribution and protection gear, drives, motors, industrial automation systems, process-control/DCS software and services into utilities, data centers, buildings, transport, marine, ports and process industries.

The business is not a hyper-growth disruptor; it is a mature quality industrial exposed to structural capex themes: grid investment, electrification, energy efficiency, industrial productivity, automation, decarbonization and local-for-local supply chains. ABB reported 2025 revenue of $33.2bn, operational EBITA of $6.3bn and operational EBITA margin of 19.0%. Q1 2026 was strong: orders +32% reported / +24% comparable, revenues +18% / +11% comparable, operational EBITA margin 23.5%, free cash flow $1.25bn, and ROCE 27.2%.

ABB agreed in October 2025 to sell its Robotics division to SoftBank for $5.375bn enterprise value, expected to close mid-to-late 2026. Robotics had 2024 revenue of $2.3bn, about 7% of ABB Group revenue, and 12.1% operational EBITA margin, so the sale simplifies ABB toward higher-margin electrification and automation while adding cash for capital allocation.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.78 / 0.78

ABB has demonstrated profitable, cash-backed earnings over the available recent Yahoo window. Net income was positive in each of 2022–2025 and rose from $2.5bn to $4.7bn; diluted EPS rose from $1.30 to $2.59. Free cash flow tracked net income well after the weak 2022 working-capital/cash year: FCF was $3.5bn, $3.9bn and $4.5bn in 2023–2025, with cumulative FCF/net income of about 0.83 over the four-year window. Receivables stayed around 21–22% of revenue, so there is no obvious receivables blowout in the data.

The caveat is cyclicality and source depth. Four years is not the full 7–10 year cycle the trait wants, and industrial order cycles can move earnings. Confidence would firm up with the FY2025 Integrated Report’s longer historical segment tables and accounting-quality notes, especially restructuring/one-off charges and working-capital commentary.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.82 / 0.80

Returns are strong and improving. The returns-on-capital script shows ROIC rising from 13.4% in 2022 to 19.3% in 2025, ROCE rising to 22.2%, and an estimated incremental ROIC around 50% over the window. ABB’s Q1 2026 release reported ROCE of 27.2%, and the 2025 Capital Markets Day raised the through-cycle ROCE target to >20%.

The main caution is goodwill: goodwill was still about 39.5% of capital in 2025, so reported ROIC includes a meaningful acquisition footprint. Confidence would firm up by recomputing tangible operating returns and incremental returns by business area from the FY2025 report.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.76 / 0.72

ABB’s moat is a mix of installed base, engineering capability, product breadth, global/local manufacturing, channel reach and high cost of failure for industrial electrical/automation systems. Gross margin improved materially, from 33.0% in 2022 to 41.1% in 2025, while operating margin rose from 11.6% to 17.2%. The Capital Markets Day also lifted the operational EBITA margin target to 18–22%, with business-area ranges of 22–26% for Electrification, 18–22% for Motion and 14–18% for Automation.

This is good evidence of pricing/productivity power, but not pure pricing power: mix, portfolio pruning, productivity and cycle also matter. Confidence would firm up with business-area price/volume bridge data and order-margin commentary from the annual report and quarterly financial booklet.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.78 / 0.72

ABB has a credible multi-year reinvestment runway in electrification and automation. Management confirmed a through-cycle comparable revenue growth target of 5–7% plus 1–2% acquired growth, and linked it to electricity expansion, grid upgrades, data centers, energy efficiency, decarbonization, industry automation and local-for-local investment. Q1 2026 supports that runway: book-to-bill was 1.29 and orders were +24% comparable, with strength in data centers, grids, transport infrastructure, marine, ports, HVAC and buildings.

The score is not higher because ABB is already large and mature; the runway is steady compounding, not exponential. Confidence would improve with segment TAM/order-backlog detail and business-area growth-vs-margin bridges after the Robotics divestment closes.

### Capital Allocation — 0.76 / 0.75

Capital allocation is mostly rational. ABB funds organic investment, R&D, dividends, bolt-on M&A and buybacks while keeping leverage low. In 2025 it generated $4.47bn FCF, paid $1.91bn dividends, bought back $1.50bn of stock and spent $0.75bn on acquisitions. Over 2022–2025 diluted shares fell about 4.1%. The dividend was covered 2.34x by FCF in 2025 and the Q1 2026 update confirmed a CHF0.94 dividend plus a new buyback of up to $2.0bn.

The Robotics sale looks value-conscious: $5.375bn EV for a business with 2024 revenue of $2.3bn and lower 12.1% operational EBITA margin, while simplifying ABB around higher-margin core areas. The caveat is buyback valuation; at ~29x current earnings and ~40x FCF, buybacks are less obviously accretive. Confidence would firm up with buyback execution prices and the full proceeds-deployment plan after the Robotics sale closes.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.88 / 0.82

The balance sheet is strong for an industrial. Net debt/EBITDA was about 0.5x in 2025 and peaked at only 0.8x over the four-year Yahoo window. ABB had net interest income in 2024–2025, so interest coverage is not a constraint. Near-term debt cover was 15.2x in 2025 using cash plus a year of FCF against current debt. The company also states a commitment to maintaining a strong investment-grade rating.

Confidence would firm up with the full debt maturity ladder, fixed/floating split and pension/off-balance-sheet notes from the annual report, but the available numbers clearly support a high score.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.84 / 0.68

ABB is a 140-year-old institution with roughly 110,000 employees, decentralised ABB Way operating processes and diversified business areas. The thesis does not hinge on any one founder or irreplaceable individual. CEO Morten Wierod matters, but the business advantage sits more in installed base, engineering, channels and operating model than in one person.

Confidence is moderate because I did not inspect the full succession/bench-depth disclosures. The FY2025 governance report and executive leadership succession commentary would firm this up.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.66 / 0.55

The available evidence points broadly positive but incomplete. Yahoo governance flags are mostly low risk overall (overall risk 2, board risk 1, compensation risk 1, shareholder-rights risk 1), and ABB’s Capital Markets Day links strategy to ROCE, free cash flow conversion, margins and EPS growth — metrics that at least resemble owner outcomes.

This is the lowest-confidence quality trait because I did not inspect the remuneration report in detail. The score would firm up with the FY2025 compensation report: STI/LTI metrics, vesting periods, ownership requirements, clawbacks, executive shareholding and any adjustments that could over-reward size or adjusted EBITA.

### Why the other strategies lost

Cash Cow is credible but secondary: ABB generates real free cash and returns capital, yet management still has a meaningful reinvestment runway and explicitly targets 5–7% comparable revenue growth plus acquired growth, so it is better framed as a wonderful-and-fair compounder than a pure harvest vehicle. Rule Breakers loses because ABB is mature and already profitable; it benefits from megatrends but is not an emerging winner with explosive optionality. Freeroll loses mechanically: there is no hard valuation floor near the price, no obvious mispricing, and no time-bound catalyst that forces a re-rating.

## Overall

ABB is a high-quality industrial compounder with strong secular positioning, rising margins, high returns on capital, cash-backed earnings and a conservative balance sheet. The main weakness is price. At CHF84.92 the stock is near its 52-week high, around 30x forward/current earnings and roughly 40x FCF depending on the cash-flow anchor. That is not absurd for a strong compounder, but it leaves little margin for disappointment.

My view: attractive business, only fair-to-expensive stock. Worth watching and owning only if Nick is comfortable underwriting mid/high-single-digit business growth plus margin durability, or if a pullback raises the expected return.

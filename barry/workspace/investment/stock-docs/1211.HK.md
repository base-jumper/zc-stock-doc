# 1211.HK — BYD Company Limited

## Business Overview

BYD (Battery/Your Dreams) is China's and the world's largest maker of new-energy vehicles (NEVs), and a vertically-integrated battery/electronics group. The core revenue engine is the **automobiles and related products** segment — 80.7% of FY2025 group revenue (CN¥648.7b). The balance is largely **mobile-handset components and assembly / electronics** (CN¥155b, ~19%), reflecting BYD's origins as a battery-and-electronics manufacturer (a major handset-parts assembler) alongside the battery (Blade) business. BYD is uniquely vertically integrated: it makes its own batteries (incl. the 2nd-gen Blade cell), motors, chips, and much of its EV supply chain in-house, giving cost and margin control.

BYD is the world's #1 NEV maker, the #1 battery-EV brand (2.26m BEVs in 2025 vs Tesla's ~1.8m), #1 in China's NEV market (~23.5% share, 1.21m units YTD-Jul 2026), and—narrowly ahead of Tesla in European registrations in 2026. It is a global top-10 auto group and #1 in China's total passenger-vehicle retail (11.9% share, ahead of Geely). Revenue is now split ~55/45 domestic/overseas, with exports a fast-rising growth engine (1.05m units in 2025, raised 2026 target 1.5m).

## Trait Assessment

*Winning strategy: rule-breakers (normalized score 0.646, confidence 0.678) — BYD is the top dog in a structurally-expanding global EV wave, with a widening battery/tech moat and a long runway, even though the recent earnings cycle is painful.*

### Right Place and Time — score 0.78, confidence 0.72

BYD sits in one of the largest industrial transitions of the century: the shift to electric mobility, with EV adoption still underpenetrated in most of the world. China has already passed ~50% NEV penetration; global adoption is earlier. New demand drivers (5-min flash charging, Gen-2 Blade battery, autonomous driving, lower battery cost) are extending the runway. The industry is structurally low-margin and brutally competitive (China price war), which caps the score below the top band — the strength is the *size and duration* of the wave, not its margin economics.

### Top Dog — score 0.85, confidence 0.80

BYD is the undisputed #1 EV maker globally and at home: ~2.26m BEVs sold in 2025 vs Tesla's ~1.8m; #1 in China NEV retail (23.5% share, Jan-Jul 1.21m units); #1 in China total passenger retail; and #1 in Europe v. Tesla in 2026 registrations (narrowly over Tesla in Feb 2026: 17,954 vs 17,664). The position is strengthening: a widening lead over #2 (Geely at 11-12%), premium sub-brands scaling, and exports compounding. It defines the category in China and increasingly abroad.

### Sustainable Advantage — score 0.70, confidence 0.70

The moat is real and widening: vertical integration (in-house batteries, motors, chips, most of the EV supply chain), cost leadership from scale, and a technology lead (2nd-gen Blade battery, 5-minute flash-charging network now spanning 5,500 stations across 311 Chinese cities, with a 20k-station 2026 target, God's Eye ADAS) that smaller, loss-making rivals cannot fund. The flywheel (battery + EV + charging + electronics) is structural. The moat is partly cost/scale-based, which is defensible but not impossible to encroach on (Xiaomi, Leapmotor, CATL), so not a full 1.0.

### Evidence of Durable, Accelerating Growth — score 0.45, confidence 0.62

This is the weak leg. The core engine grew spectacularly through 2022-24 (NEV sales 0.6m→4.6m units), but recent numbers are decelerating: FY2025 revenue +3.5%, net profit −19%, and Q1-2026 net profit −55%, NEV sales −30% (domestic). The growth is now **cyclical, not compounding**: a one-time purchase-tax pull-forward + price war temporarily halted it. FCF does not track profit (negative total FCF). So it scores mid-when it does not meet the accelerate-forever bar, but the structural growth story (EV adoption, exports) keeps it above a fail.

### Organic Growth over Acquisitive Growth — score 0.70, confidence 0.70

Growth has been overwhelmingly organic — BYD built its scale through product, brand, and vertical integration, not serial M&A. It's the empire it built, not bought. Minor acquisitions exist but are immaterial.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — score 0.70, confidence 0.68

Founder–chairman Wang Chuanfu has built BYD from battery maker to global EV leader, a long-tested record of navigating challenges (from phone assembly to the EV pivot). The company has strong institutional backing. Frank communication in earnings (management attributed the 2025 profit drop to the domestic price war). Slightly moderate confidence: recent earnings misses and the aggressive global Capex bet.

### Incentive Alignment — score 0.50, confidence 0.55

Directors/insiders hold a small stake (insider ~0.12%, institutions 41.6%). It is founder-led, so there is substantial alignment through the founder's deep ownership, but the modern comp structure and multiple share classes (A/H/ADR) make the pay-for-long-term-per-share value unclear. **Firm-up:** actual share-based-comp quantum (annual report).

### Customer Devotion — score 0.50, confidence 0.60

BYD has genuine loyalty at home (repeated best-seller), and an install-base quality; but it is largely a price/volume-led, commodity-feeling NEV purchase (price-war driven). Great Tang 100k pre-orders shows strong product pull at the premium end. Mixed: devotion exists but price-sensitized, so 0.5.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Freeroll** (0.52/0.59): the setup is real (a trough, cheap forward), but there's no hard asset/income floor near 3× book, and the catalyst is more growth-recovery than a re-rating to a defined fair value — BYD is a lasting compounder, not a cheap-catalyst hold.
- **Cash-cow** (0.46/0.62): free-cash-flow-generation (0.25) vetoes — negative FCF, heavy capex, not a cash return.
- **Wonderful-and-fair** (0.53/0.61): real quality and returns, but pricing power is compromised by the price war and the negative-FCF reinvestment runway is a heavy capex, not a moat-compounder.

## Overall

BYD is an exceptional, founder-run global franchise: the world's #1 NEV maker, structurally expanding, dominant at home, surging abroad, with a widening battery/tech moat. The detractors are real: growth is no longer compounding (it's cyclical), the price war has squeezed margins, and the balance sheet carries record short-term borrowings after a massive global investment pace. It is genuinely the rule-breaker the leaderboard read — a top-dog in a great wave — but at this stage it's a **maturing** rule-breaker: the compounding engine that made it a great compounder has decelerated, and the return is now carried more by scale economics and international expansion than by accelerating earnings. The agent score (0.5) reflects that: wonderful business, durable moat, fair-to-slightly-rich price, moderate near-term return.

# NVDA — NVIDIA Corporation

## Business Overview

NVIDIA sells accelerated-computing platforms: GPUs and networking, systems, and the CUDA software ecosystem. Data Center produced $75.2 billion, or 92% of Q1 FY2027 revenue; Edge Computing contributed $6.4 billion. The profit engine is a fabless model combining premium silicon with networking and a deeply embedded software stack. Q1 revenue was $81.6 billion, up 85% year on year, with a 74.9% GAAP gross margin. The central driver is capital spending on AI training and inference; the principal constraints are customer concentration, export controls, supply-chain dependence and the risk that hyperscalers' custom accelerators reduce NVIDIA's share. In August 2026 NVIDIA broadened its role from silicon supplier to infrastructure credit provider: it signed residual value guarantees with SB Energy covering ~4.25 GW of IT load at the Ohio PORTS-Pike campus (option for ~3.8 GW more, cumulative payment cap $105 billion), invested $1.5 billion in SB Energy, and will supply the DSX full stack as OpenAI's tenant of the campus from 2028.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.95 / 0.90

AI compute is in a structural infrastructure buildout rather than an ordinary semiconductor replacement cycle. Q1 Data Center revenue grew 92% year on year and Q2 guidance implies another 11% sequential company-wide increase, despite assuming no Data Center compute revenue from China. Demand duration is uncertain, but expanding inference, agentic AI and sovereign/enterprise deployment broaden the workload base beyond frontier-model training.

### Top Dog — 0.98 / 0.95

NVIDIA is the clear leader in accelerated AI compute. The evidence is economic as well as narrative: $75.2 billion of quarterly Data Center revenue, 74.9% gross margin and continued sequential growth at enormous scale. Its lead spans compute, NVLink networking and systems software, making the relevant product a platform rather than a standalone GPU.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.94 / 0.90

CUDA switching costs, developer familiarity, optimized libraries, a rapid architecture cadence and integrated networking form mutually reinforcing advantages. Gross margin recovered to 74.9% in Q1 FY2027 and revenue growth remained 85%, strong evidence that competitors have not forced commodity economics. The live threat is not a single merchant-GPU rival but hyperscaler custom silicon paired with improving software portability.

### Durable Growth — 0.93 / 0.95

Revenue rose from $27.0 billion in FY2023 to $215.9 billion in FY2026, while annual FCF rose from $3.8 billion to $96.7 billion. Q1 FY2027 revenue then rose 85% year on year to $81.6 billion, and management guided Q2 to $91.0 billion ±2%. Growth will inevitably decelerate from this base, but the engine remains exceptionally strong and cash-generative.

### Organic Growth — 0.88 / 0.85

The multi-year revenue increase was overwhelmingly generated by demand for internally developed platforms, not acquisitions. FY2026 acquisition cash spend rose to $14.5 billion, but remained small beside $215.9 billion of revenue and $96.7 billion of FCF; diluted share count fell about 2.2% over FY2023–FY2026. The latest 10-K acquisition note would firm up the economics and goodwill created by the FY2026 deals.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.92 / 0.85

Founder-CEO Jensen Huang has repeatedly placed NVIDIA ahead of major computing transitions and built CUDA into an industry platform. The company continues to invest aggressively in architecture, systems and software while retaining very high margins. This score is deliberately paired with a much lower key-person-resilience score: Huang's record is an asset, but his unusual strategic importance is also fragility.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.72 / 0.85

Founder ownership and long-duration equity incentives provide alignment, and repurchases have more than offset employee issuance recently (diluted share count down ~2.2% over FY2023–FY2026). The FY2027 proxy (filed May 12, 2026) closes most of the prior evidence gap: FY2026 Stretch Plan goals were exceeded, driving maximum Variable Cash, SY PSU and MY PSU payouts; the CEO must hold shares worth 10× base salary (NEOs 3×) and all currently meet the guideline; all directors except the CEO are independent, with an independent lead director, declassified board, majority voting, proxy access and annual engagement (34% of shares contacted in Fall 2025). At the June 24, 2026 annual meeting, say-on-pay passed with ~94% support and all ten director nominees were elected. Remaining weaker points: significant equity compensation and a high market valuation that can make buybacks value-destructive.

### Customer Devotion — 0.88 / 0.80

Platform adoption is visible in demand, ecosystem breadth and customers' willingness to commit large infrastructure budgets; CUDA's installed developer base and optimized libraries create loyalty through utility and switching costs. The August 2026 PORTS-Pike agreement with SB Energy — with OpenAI as the tenant, an initial commitment to 4.25 IT-GW of NVIDIA DSX full-stack deployment, an option to extend to ~8 IT-GW, and OpenAI indemnifying NVIDIA for guaranteed amounts actually paid — is direct evidence of a top-tier customer locking itself into NVIDIA's platform for 20-year leases beginning in 2028. NVIDIA does not disclose a conventional retention or NPS measure, so part of the score remains inferred; revisit only if NVIDIA publishes developer retention/usage metrics or independent large-sample developer surveys.

### Why the other strategies lost

Wonderful-and-Fair also fits the business quality, but Rule Breakers better captures the still-exceptional industry wave and growth engine. Cash Cow loses because NVIDIA is reinvesting into a large runway rather than being defined by distributions. Freeroll is ruled out by the absence of a defensible valuation floor and time-bound re-rating catalyst.

## Overall

NVIDIA is an exceptional business: dominant position, a widening platform moat, extraordinary growth, high cash conversion, 70%+ recent ROIC and a net-cash balance sheet. The detractors are valuation, reliance on continued hyperscaler AI spending, export restrictions, custom accelerators, supply concentration, and key-person dependence. No universal disqualifier was found in the latest 10-Q/10-K review.

## Valuation

The canonical Rule Breakers valuation uses only the exit-multiple component because NVIDIA is already a mature category leader with substantial current earnings; a survivor-style TAM role model would be less reliable than its own earnings base. The $214.72 quote (2026-08-22) is paired with an estimated current normalized EPS of $9.79 — triangulated between FY2027 consensus of $9.01, the +1y consensus of $13.00 and the Q1 FY2027 quarterly run rate — implying a 21.93× entry P/E. The model uses five-year aggregate earnings growth of 30%, 25%, 20%, 15% and 12% (an ~19.9% CAGR, still below near-term consensus, which projects ~44% growth next year), 1.0% annual net buybacks (diluted share count fell ~2.2% over FY2023–FY2026), a 22× exit P/E and a 0.47% dividend yield on the new $1.00 annual dividend. These are demanding but not heroic assumptions, and they do not require multiple expansion. The script estimates a 22.13% annualized ROI (2.72× over five years): +20.22% from business growth, +1.01% from buybacks, +0.06% from multiple movement and +0.62% from income. `agent_score: 0.78` reflects superb quality offset by a still-narrow margin for execution error and the new, untested off-balance-sheet guarantee exposure.

## Thesis Connections

Direct exposure to the AI infrastructure thesis: accelerated compute, high-speed networking and software tooling are foundational picks-and-shovels for training, inference, agentic AI and physical AI. The PORTS-Pike transaction extends this into the financing layer of AI infrastructure: NVIDIA is now securing land, power and shell capacity and guaranteeing residual values so OpenAI can take a 20-year lease on a campus that exclusively runs NVIDIA's DSX platform — demand assurance, platform lock-in and a new form of capital commitment in one.

## Watch

- Compare Q2 FY2027 results (expected late August 2026; Q2 guide was $91.0 billion ±2%) with actual Data Center growth, gross margin and China treatment.
- Track the SB Energy PORTS-Pike build: ready-for-service conditions beginning 2028, phased capacity ramp, and whether the ~3.8 GW option is exercised (would take the deal toward 8 IT-GW and the $105 billion cap is the initial commitment's ceiling).
- Track OpenAI's credit quality and lease payments; the residual guarantees only bite on an OpenAI trigger event, and OpenAI indemnifies amounts actually paid.
- Track hyperscaler custom-accelerator deployment and evidence of CUDA workload portability.
- Track China/export-control changes; Q2 guidance assumes zero China Data Center compute revenue.
- Review FY2026 acquisition economics and the increase in goodwill (10-K note).
- Monitor Jensen Huang succession planning and second-line leadership visibility.

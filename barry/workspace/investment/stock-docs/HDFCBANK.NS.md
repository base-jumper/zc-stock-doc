# HDFCBANK.NS — HDFC Bank Limited

## Business Overview

HDFC Bank is India's largest private-sector bank by assets. It earns primarily from the spread between interest on loans and investments and its funding cost, supplemented by fees, payments, foreign exchange, distribution and other financial-services income. The July 2023 amalgamation with HDFC Ltd added a large mortgage book and controlling interests in life insurance, general insurance, asset management and other subsidiaries. At June 2026 the bank had 9,694 branches, 20,958 ATMs, more than 100 million customer relationships, ₹31.7tn of deposits and ₹31.3tn of advances under management.

The core economic drivers are deposit growth and mix, prudent loan growth, net interest margin (NIM), fee income, operating efficiency and credit losses. Q1 FY27 average deposits grew 13.3% year on year and advances under management 10.8%; reported return on assets was 1.85%, return on equity 13.8%, gross NPA 1.17% and CET1 17.4%. The main current tension is that the enlarged post-merger balance sheet is growing while CASA has fallen to 32.3% and NIM to 3.26%, keeping returns on equity below their pre-merger level.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.91 (confidence 0.96)

The FY26 annual report's ten-year table shows profit after tax rising in every year from ₹145.5bn in FY17 to ₹746.7bn in FY26, including through COVID and the HDFC Ltd merger. Adjusted Q1 FY27 PAT grew about 9.8% year on year after removing the prior-year HDB Financial divestment gain, special provisions and tax credit; reported PAT grew 5.0%. Credit costs were 40bp, gross NPA 1.17% and net NPA 0.41%, supporting the view that profits have not been bought with weak underwriting.

For a bank, conventional free cash flow is not an owner-earnings measure because deposits, advances and regulatory capital are operating inputs. Cash-backing is therefore read through asset quality, provisioning and capital rather than operating cash flow. FY26 received an unmodified audit opinion, no audit qualification, and an opinion that financial-reporting controls were effective. The former chairman's March 2026 resignation created a governance question, but the independent-director committee's external legal review reported that the implications of his statement were not substantiated by the record and interviews. Overall, the long record is unusually consistent and auditable.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.62 (confidence 0.96)

ROE and return on tangible equity are the appropriate metrics because equity capital is the product. Reported ROE has eased from 17.39% in FY23 to 16.09% in FY24, 14.56% in FY25, 14.29% in FY26 and 13.8% in Q1 FY27; that is below the roughly 15% rubric anchor for a strong financial institution. The post-merger equity and lower funding spread explain much of the decline, while average ROA has remained close to 1.9% and Q1 FY27 ROA was 1.85%.

The bank still creates value above a reasonable cost of equity, but the trend is eroding rather than high and rising. Incremental returns now depend on converting younger branches into low-cost deposits, reducing inherited borrowings and restoring asset mix and NIM. This is the largest quality gap in the compounder case.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.74 (confidence 0.91)

A bank's closest analogues to pricing power are the cost and stability of deposits, the ability to remain selective on loan spreads, fee-bearing customer relationships and scale efficiency. HDFC Bank has a strong brand, regulatory licence, nationwide distribution, more than 100 million customer relationships and the largest private-bank balance sheet. Average deposits grew 13.3% in Q1 FY27, management said the bank continued to gain deposit share, and branch deposits per branch had risen to about ₹3.3bn from ₹2.66bn in FY23. Overall customer NPS was 70 from roughly 2.4 million FY26 responses.

The moat is real but not fully translating into current pricing. CASA has fallen from 38% around the merger to 32.3%, corporate spreads are described as intense, and NIM declined to 3.26% of assets. Management expects maturing borrowings, more retail assets and customer acquisition to help, but this is not yet visible in the margin. The moat is best described as wide but temporarily narrowed, with fintech competition, service quality, cyber risk and aggressive deposit pricing as the main threats.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.79 (confidence 0.90)

India's banking market still offers a long organic runway through nominal GDP growth, formalisation, housing, MSME credit and deeper financial-product penetration. The annual report cites FY26 system credit growth of 14.1%. HDFC Bank's FY26 advances grew 12.1% and deposits 14.4%; in Q1 FY27, small and mid-market loans grew 18.7%, corporate and wholesale 18.6%, and retail 7.2%. The bank ranks among the top three MSME lenders in 25 states and has a leadership position in 15.

The physical network also has embedded runway: about 40% of branches are less than five years old, yet aggregate deposits per branch continue to rise. CET1 of 17.4% leaves ample regulatory capital to support growth. The constraint is not opportunity but the return earned on it: a large bank cannot grow far above the system indefinitely, and reinvestment only compounds well if ROE recovers toward the mid-teens.

### Capital Allocation — 0.67 (confidence 0.88)

The bank retains most earnings to fund loan and deposit growth while maintaining a large capital cushion. FY26's ₹15.50 total dividend represented a 31.9% payout, including a ₹2.50 special interim dividend; the regular final dividend was ₹13. The HDB Financial IPO and partial divestment satisfied regulatory requirements while leaving HDFC Bank with a 74.1% stake. No buyback programme is masking equity compensation, and the balance sheet remains conservatively capitalised.

The large HDFC Ltd amalgamation was strategically coherent—adding mortgages and valuable financial-services subsidiaries—and more than 95% of new-to-bank home-loan customers opened savings accounts by disbursement. But value creation is not yet proved: post-merger ROE, CASA and NIM remain below prior levels, and management is still harvesting integration and funding synergies. Book value per share has nevertheless risen from an adjusted ₹87.28 in FY17 to ₹365.68 in FY26. Allocation is therefore sensible but not yet exemplary.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.90 (confidence 0.98)

Conventional net-debt/EBITDA is inapplicable to a bank. Regulatory capital, liquidity and credit quality show high resilience. At June 2026 CET1 was 17.4%, Tier 1 17.8% and total capital 19.6%, versus a total regulatory requirement of 11.9%. Average LCR was 115% and NSFR 119%, both above 100%. Gross NPA was 1.17%, net NPA 0.41%, and total provisions were ₹724bn; management also expects existing buffers to cover the April 2027 transition to expected-credit-loss provisioning without a material ongoing increase in credit cost.

Funding is diversified: the FY26 annual report showed the top 20 depositors at only 4.81% of deposits. The principal weakness is mix rather than solvency—CASA is low and wholesale deposits remain expensive. There is no evidence of a financing constraint or hidden liability capable of forcing equity issuance.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.78 (confidence 0.90)

The institution is professionally managed, has no identified promoter and has more than 200,000 employees, established risk systems and a deep executive bench. CEO Sashidhar Jagdishan joined in 1996; Deputy MD Kaizad Bharucha joined in 1995 and built key credit and risk frameworks; other executive directors carry finance, governance, mortgage, risk and technology experience. The franchise, customer relationships and underwriting systems are institutional rather than personal.

The score is below exceptional because governance succession is active. The former part-time chairman resigned in March 2026, Rajiv Kumar was appointed chairman in June/July, and the board said the CEO's reappointment process was still under consideration at the Q1 call. A departure would be disruptive, but the bank's scale and bench mean it should not break the thesis.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.78 (confidence 0.94)

The compensation design is substantially aligned with prudent long-term banking. At least 50% of material risk-taker pay is variable; 50–67% of variable pay is delivered in non-cash instruments depending on quantum; at least 60% is deferred for at least three years. ESOPs vest in four annual tranches, and malus/clawback can be applied for deteriorating risk or profitability, misconduct, fraud, reputational harm or material restatement. RSU metrics include total shareholder return, asset quality, ROA, profitability, ROE and relative peer performance—not revenue growth alone.

Alignment is tempered by limited personally purchased insider ownership and ongoing equity issuance. Yahoo reports insiders collectively hold about 0.15%; FY26 option/RSU exercises added about 0.42% to the year-end share count before considering the economically neutral bonus issue. CEO fixed-pay remuneration was about 156 times median employee remuneration, but pay is regulated, risk-adjusted and subject to independent committee, board and RBI approval. The annual report states there were no materially significant related-party transactions that could conflict with the bank's interests.

### Why the Other Strategies Lost

**Rule Breakers** lost because banking is established and highly competitive, while normalized earnings growth is around 10% rather than durable high or accelerating growth; HDFC Bank is a top dog, but not an emerging hyper-growth disruptor. **Cash Cow** lost because bank cash flow is not freely distributable FCF and HDFC Bank still retains capital for a substantial reinvestment runway; the 27–32% payout does not define the case. **Freeroll** lost because 1.9× book, roughly 2.1× tangible book and a 1.8% dividend yield do not provide a hard floor, and margin normalization is a gradual operating path rather than a forcing catalyst.

## Overall

HDFC Bank remains a high-quality Indian financial compounder with an exceptional profit record, leading scale, strong asset quality, ample capital and a long domestic runway. The 2023 merger broadened the franchise and created cross-sell opportunities that should be difficult to replicate.

The detractors are material: ROE has slipped below the strategy's strong threshold, CASA and NIM remain depressed, merger synergies are not fully proven, and near-term governance succession deserves monitoring. The bank fits **Wonderful and Fair**, but the quality rating is held back until funding economics and returns on equity visibly recover.

## Valuation

The August 14, 2026 NSE close was ₹727. The earnings anchor is ₹52 per share: above FY26 EPS of ₹48.62 and the Q1 FY27 annualised run-rate of about ₹49.6, but below FY27 consensus EPS of ₹56.4. It therefore estimates current earning power rather than using a stale trailing or pure forward figure. The implied entry P/E is about 14.0×.

The five-year exit-multiple model assumes aggregate earnings growth of 11%, 10%, 10%, 9% and 9%; 0.5% annual share-count dilution; a 27% payout, close to the regular FY26 dividend rather than assuming the special dividend repeats; and a 14× exit P/E, essentially no re-rating. The assumptions reflect FY26 EPS growth of 10.1%, adjusted Q1 FY27 PAT growth of about 9.8%, and consensus growth of 14.5% for FY27 and 11.0% for FY28, faded rather than extrapolated.

The model produces an annualized ROI of about **11.4%**. Its annual attribution is approximately 9.8 percentage points from business growth, -0.5 points from dilution, near zero from multiple change and 2.1 points from dividends. That is a respectable growth-carried return, but not a large margin of safety. `overall.agent_score` is **0.62**: quality is above average and price is fair, while sub-15% ROE and the modest expected return prevent a stronger rating.

## Thesis Connections

- **Financialisation and formalisation of India:** HDFC Bank offers broad exposure to deposit growth, housing, MSME formalisation, payments and household financial products.
- **Housing and urbanisation:** the merged mortgage franchise supplies a long-duration customer-acquisition and cross-sell channel.
- **Digital operating leverage:** AI, process redesign and maturing branches could improve service and cost efficiency, but this is an execution thesis rather than a separately valued technology business.

## Watch

- Quarterly **ROE/ROA**: require evidence that ROE reverses its decline from 14.3% in FY26 and 13.8% in Q1 FY27 without weaker underwriting.
- **NIM, CASA and funding mix**: watch NIM versus 3.26%, CASA versus 32.3%, borrowing share versus 11%, and management's stated path back toward roughly 38% CASA.
- **Deposit and branch productivity**: confirm that deposit growth continues to beat or match system growth and younger branches keep lifting deposits per branch.
- **Credit quality and ECL transition**: monitor gross NPA, net NPA, credit cost, provision coverage and the April 2027 expected-credit-loss implementation.
- **Governance succession**: confirm RBI/shareholder approvals for chairman Rajiv Kumar and the board/RBI outcome on CEO Sashidhar Jagdishan's reappointment.
- **Merger economics**: track mortgage cross-sell, reduction of inherited borrowings, retail asset mix and return on incremental equity.
- **Customer and technology execution**: monitor overall NPS, service complaints, outages, cyber incidents and digital fraud; FY26 disclosed one limited lead-generation data breach.
- **Valuation refresh**: rerun if price moves materially from ₹727 or current EPS power diverges from ₹52.

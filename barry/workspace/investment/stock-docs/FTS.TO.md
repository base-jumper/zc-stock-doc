# FTS.TO — Fortis Inc.

## Business Overview

Fortis is a North American regulated electric and gas utility holding company. It owns nine regulated utilities across Canada, the United States and the Caribbean, serving about 3.5 million electricity and natural-gas customers with roughly 9,900 employees. The business is effectively a portfolio of regulated local monopolies: earnings are driven by allowed returns on rate base, capital investment, customer/load growth, regulatory outcomes, financing costs, and weather/timing noise.

The current plan is straightforward: Fortis is executing a CAD 28.8 billion 2026-2030 capital plan that management says should grow midyear rate base from CAD 42.4 billion in 2025 to CAD 57.9 billion by 2030, a 7% CAGR. Management guides to 4-6% annual dividend growth through 2030. Q1 2026 showed execution broadly on track: EPS of CAD 0.99, CAD 1.4 billion of capital expenditures, approval of UNS Gas formulaic rates, continuing progress on TEP, ITC data-centre/load-growth projects, and Tilbury LNG.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent Profits — 0.75 score, 0.80 confidence

Fortis has a clean regulated-utility earnings profile. Yahoo statement data show no loss years in the available 2022-2025 annual set, diluted EPS rising from CAD 2.78 in 2022 to CAD 3.40 in 2025, and company-adjusted EPS of CAD 3.53 in 2025 versus CAD 3.28 in 2024. Net income growth is steady enough for underwriting; the helper script measured 2.8% net-income growth dispersion over the available annual window.

The weakness is cash backing if free cash flow is treated literally: FCF is negative because capex materially exceeds operating cash flow during the buildout phase. For a regulated utility this is not automatically low-quality accounting, because growth capex enters rate base and is funded with regulated utility debt/equity, but it still means the earnings stream relies on continuing capital-market and regulatory access. Confidence would improve with a longer 7-10 year filing-level EPS/allowed-ROE bridge.

### Returns on Capital — 0.48 score, 0.75 confidence

Returns are durable but not high in the classic compounder sense. The helper script shows ROIC around 5.1-5.7% from 2022-2025, ROE around 6.6-7.6%, and incremental ROIC of about 9.0% over the window. That is normal for regulated infrastructure but below the level usually wanted from a high-return wonderful business.

Interpretation: Fortis compounds by earning regulated returns on a growing capital base, not by earning software-like returns on intangible capital. That makes the stock lower-quality than the best compounders, but more predictable than many cyclical asset-heavy businesses.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.70 score, 0.70 confidence

The moat is regulatory rather than brand-led. Local monopoly utility franchises, rate-base recovery, and formula/adjustor mechanisms provide a route to recover prudent costs and earn allowed returns. Q1 2026 included UNS Gas approval at a 9.61% allowed ROE with 56% common equity and formulaic rates; TEP's rate case remained in progress.

This is good protection but not unlimited pricing power: affordability, political/regulatory scrutiny, and lag between spending and rate recovery can compress earnings. Fortis itself noted UNS Energy headwinds from costs associated with rate-base growth not yet reflected in customer rates.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.78 score, 0.85 confidence

This is the core positive. The CAD 28.8 billion 2026-2030 plan is large relative to the equity base and supports 7% guided rate-base CAGR. Growth opportunities include ITC transmission investment, MISO long-range transmission projects, Arizona load growth/data-centre demand, grid reliability/resiliency spend, and FortisBC LNG/renewable-gas infrastructure.

The runway is not speculative hypergrowth; it is visible regulated infrastructure spend. The main caveat is that rate-base growth only creates value if allowed returns exceed funding costs and regulators permit timely recovery.

### Capital Allocation — 0.72 score, 0.75 confidence

Capital allocation is disciplined for a regulated utility: reinvest heavily into rate base, fund primarily with operating cash flow and regulated utility debt, use DRIP/common equity flexibility as needed, and maintain a long dividend-growth record. Fortis has raised the dividend for 52 consecutive years and targets 4-6% annual growth through 2030.

The tradeoff is shareholder dilution and negative reported FCF while capex runs above operating cash flow. Shares outstanding rose about 5.2% over the helper-script window, largely tied to DRIP/equity funding. That is acceptable if the new capital earns regulated returns, but it caps per-share compounding.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.55 score, 0.75 confidence

Leverage is high but typical for the sector. Yahoo-derived metrics show latest annual net debt/EBITDA around 5.8x, interest coverage around 2.7x EBIT, and negative FCF. The offset is the regulated asset base, stable operating cash flow, and Morningstar DBRS confirming Fortis' A (low) issuer/senior unsecured ratings with stable outlook in May 2026.

This is not a fortress balance sheet. It is financeable regulated leverage. The key watch item is refinancing cost and regulatory lag: higher interest costs that are not recovered promptly would pressure EPS and dividend coverage.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.85 score, 0.70 confidence

Fortis is a decentralized regulated utility holding company, not a founder-led execution story. Gary Smith's May 2026 retirement is notable but his responsibilities are being assumed by other senior executives, and the utility operating companies have established local governance. The business model should not break on one executive departure.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.65 score, 0.55 confidence

Governance appears clean from the available surface data: Yahoo governance risk scores are low, and the strategy is explicitly framed around regulated growth, balance-sheet strength, and dividend growth. Insider ownership is low, so this is not owner-operator alignment. Confidence is only moderate because this run did not deeply review the 2026 circular's compensation scorecard.

### Why the other strategies lost

Cash Cow loses on free cash flow: Fortis pays a reliable dividend, but reported FCF is persistently negative because capex exceeds operating cash flow during the rate-base buildout. Freeroll loses because there is no obvious cheap valuation plus time-bound re-rating catalyst; at ~24x trailing EPS and ~21x forward EPS, the stock is not neglected. Rule Breakers loses because Fortis is mature regulated infrastructure, not an emerging category winner with explosive customer devotion or product-led upside.

## Overall

Fortis is a high-predictability regulated utility compounder with a very visible investment runway, a strong dividend record, and reasonable regulatory diversification. The best-fit strategy is **Wonderful and Fair**, but with an important asterisk: the business is wonderful for stability, not for high returns on capital.

The stock looks fully priced after a strong run. At CAD 81.25 it trades near its 52-week high, about 24.0x trailing EPS, 21.0x forward EPS, 1.8x book, and above Yahoo's mean analyst target of CAD 79.43. The setup is therefore not broken, but the expected return is mostly dividend plus modest EPS growth while valuation mean-reverts.

## Thesis Connections

Fortis has a mild connection to power-backed compute and electrification themes. ITC's Big Cedar project includes data-centre load growth, including support for 300 MW at the first data centre and additional transmission work for another 1,600 MW of new data-centre load expected by 2028. This is thesis-relevant but not a pure compute-infrastructure play; Fortis captures regulated infrastructure returns, not hyperscaler economics.

## Watch

- Annual review of payout ratio, dividend-growth guidance, and whether EPS growth keeps pace with rate-base growth despite dilution.

# TRN.MI — Terna S.p.A.

## Business Overview

Terna is Italy's national electricity transmission grid operator. It owns and operates the high-voltage transmission network, dispatches power flows, and earns mainly regulated returns on its regulated asset base (RAB). The core revenue driver is therefore not merchant power prices but allowed tariff revenue tied to RAB growth, depreciation/returns on new assets, service quality and regulatory parameters.

The current plan is unusually investment-heavy. Terna's 2024-2028 Industrial Plan Update targets **€17.7b** of total capex, including **€16.6b** for regulated Italian grid investment, and expects RAB to reach about **€32b in 2028**, a **9% CAGR**. Key projects include the Tyrrhenian Link, Adriatic Link, Sa.Co.I.3, Elmed, Sicily grid upgrades, security equipment and digitalisation. Management says roughly **90%** of planned investments were authorised and **80%** covered by contracts at the plan update.

The latest reported trading update remains Q1 2026: revenue was **€988.7m** (+9.6%), EBITDA **€697.6m** (+7.0%), group net profit **€276.5m** (+0.4%), capex **€511.4m**, and net debt **€12.17b** after an €850m hybrid green bond. 2026 guidance is revenue **€4.41b**, EBITDA **€2.93b**, group net profit **€1.12b**, and capex around **€4.2b**. First-half results are scheduled for **29 July 2026**, making guidance delivery, capex execution and leverage the next material checks.

No universal disqualifier found. This is a regulated, levered infrastructure compounder with political/regulatory risk, not a fraud/solvency/business-model kill-shot case.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.82 / confidence 0.75

Terna has demonstrated consistent earning power. Yahoo annual data shows net income rising from **€789m in 2021** to **€1.062b in 2024**, with no loss years in the four-year window and low net-income growth dispersion. Q1 2026 remained profitable at **€276.5m** despite higher financing costs and taxes.

The caveat is cash conversion. Reported earnings do not convert to free cash flow during this investment phase because capex is far above depreciation and operating cash flow: 2024 FCF was about **-€1.11b**, and TTM FCF was negative. For a regulated grid, this is not an accounting-quality red flag in the same way it would be for a capital-light business; it reflects regulated growth capex being added to the asset base. Still, it means the earnings-quality score is not perfect.

Confidence would improve with a longer 7-10 year filing review through the full interest-rate and regulatory cycle.

### Returns on Capital — 0.58 / confidence 0.75

Returns are stable but not conventionally high. Yahoo-derived ROIC was about **5.1%-5.8%** from 2021-2024, ROCE roughly **8.2%-8.8%**, and ROE about **14%** recently. The right reading is utility-specific: Terna earns regulated returns on a large asset base, so a mid-single-digit ROIC can still create value if the allowed return remains above funding cost and the RAB compounds.

The positives are stability and incremental investment visibility; the negatives are leverage-flattered ROE and modest absolute ROIC. This supports a quality infrastructure thesis, but it is not a classic 20%+ ROIC compounder.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.78 / confidence 0.75

Terna's moat is structural rather than brand-based: it is the national transmission grid operator in a regulated monopoly-like role. Revenue growth in Q1 2026 was explicitly driven by regulated activities, higher tariff revenue linked to RAB expansion, and remuneration of depreciation/impairments on new assets entering operation.

This gives durable pricing/tariff protection, but not unconstrained pricing power. Regulation is both the moat and the cap: returns are protected when the regime is fair, but political/regulatory reset risk limits upside.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.82 / confidence 0.80

This is Terna's strongest wonderful-and-fair trait. Italy's grid needs reinforcement for renewable integration, congestion relief, interconnection, security and digitalisation. The plan's **€17.7b** capex and targeted **€32b 2028 RAB** imply a long, visible reinvestment runway, with high authorisation/procurement coverage reducing execution uncertainty.

The runway is regulated rather than entrepreneurial: Terna can deploy very large capital sums, but the return on that capital is bounded by allowed regulatory economics.

### Capital Allocation — 0.68 / confidence 0.70

Capital allocation looks mostly rational: heavy capex is going into the regulated asset base and grid-security projects that should earn regulated returns; M&A is not the core growth driver; dividends are meaningful and policy-backed. The 2024-2028 plan sets minimum DPS as the higher of 2024 DPS of **€0.3962** or 2023 DPS growing **4% p.a.**

The drawback is funding strain. Dividends are covered by accounting earnings but not by free cash flow during the capex surge; leverage and hybrid issuance fund the gap. That is acceptable for regulated infrastructure if allowed returns and financing access hold, but it lowers the score versus a self-funding compounder.

### Conservative Debt — 0.62 / confidence 0.75

Leverage is material but broadly appropriate for regulated infrastructure. Yahoo/script data show 2024 net debt/EBITDA around **4.35x**, down from **5.8x** in 2021, with EBIT interest cover still strong at roughly **12x**. Q1 2026 reported net debt of **€12.17b**, down from **€13.00b** at 2025 year-end after the hybrid green bond.

For a regulated utility, ~4-5x net debt/EBITDA is not automatically aggressive, but the capex plan, negative FCF and refinancing-rate exposure mean debt is a real constraint. The score is moderate-good, not high.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.86 / confidence 0.75

The business is institutional and regulated, not founder/key-person dependent. The 2026 succession is now resolved: the new board appointed **Pasqualino Monti** CEO and general manager in May, replacing the earlier interim arrangement. That orderly handover supports the low key-person-risk assessment, although delivery under the new leadership still needs observing.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 / confidence 0.45

I have not done a deep remuneration-report pass, so confidence is deliberately low. Yahoo governance fields are mixed: compensation risk is low, but board/shareholder-rights risk fields are elevated. The business is partly state/strategic-infrastructure influenced, which can align with long-term grid investment but may not always maximise minority-shareholder outcomes.

**Why other strategies lost:** Cash Cow fails because free cash flow is currently deeply negative under the capex programme, even though dividends are attractive. Rule Breakers has some grid-modernisation tailwind and a monopoly position, but Terna is mature regulated infrastructure, not an emerging high-upside disruptor. Freeroll fails because there is no hard downside floor near today's price and no clear ≤4-year re-rating catalyst; at ~19x trailing earnings, the stock is not obviously mispriced value.

## Overall

Terna is a solid regulated infrastructure compounder. The best part of the thesis is the unusually visible RAB growth runway: Italy needs grid investment, Terna is the vehicle, and the planned capex base is large enough to move earnings over time. The moat is also robust because the national transmission grid is not realistically replicable.

The main detractors are valuation and funding. At **€10.35/share** on 23 July, close to the **€10.41** 52-week high, the stock trades around **18.8x** 2026 consensus EPS and **11.9x EV/EBITDA**. That is not outrageous for a high-visibility utility, but it leaves limited room for multiple expansion; the analyst mean target of about **€9.80** also sits below the market price. Free cash flow remains negative while capex runs above operating cash flow, so shareholder returns depend on regulation, debt markets and the business growing into the investment programme.

My view: **good watchlist infrastructure name, fair-to-slightly-full price**. I would rather own it for steady compounding/income than for upside asymmetry.

Fits an electrification/grid-modernisation thesis: renewables, interconnections, energy security, congestion relief and data-centre/large-load growth all require transmission investment. Terna is a direct regulated pick-and-shovel for Italy's grid buildout.

# KGH.L — Knights Group Holdings plc

## Business Overview

Knights is a UK-listed legal and professional services group focused on regional markets outside London. It operates a corporatised, one-team model rather than the traditional partnership model: central business services, a single technology platform, regional client teams and selective recruitment/acquisitions of legal professionals. Its services span real estate, dispute resolution, private wealth, corporate/commercial, residential property, employment, remortgage services, clinical negligence and IFA advice.

FY2026 was a step-up year. Underlying revenue rose 28.2% to £207.7m, with 7.1% organic growth and the rest mainly from acquisitions; underlying PBT rose 18.6% to £33.2m; underlying FCF was £40.3m; and net debt excluding leases was £65.4m after acquisition spend and capex. The company says it has about 5% share of a c.£4bn regional UK legal services market, 29 locations, 1,220 average fee earners and 29 acquisitions completed over 14 years.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.58 / confidence 0.76

Knights has real earning power, but the cleanest read is on underlying numbers rather than reported IFRS profit. Reported net income was negative in FY2022, then £7.9m, £9.8m and £7.6m in FY2023-25; FY2026 reported PAT was £4.7m, while underlying PAT was £24.7m and underlying EPS was 28.14p. Yahoo shows cumulative FCF/net income of 2.44x across FY2022-25, and the FY2026 presentation shows 163% underlying cash conversion, so cash backing is strong. The caveat is recurring acquisition, contingent-consideration and amortisation adjustments: non-underlying costs are material enough that reported and underlying earnings tell different stories. Confidence would firm up with a full 7-10 year reported-vs-underlying bridge and per-acquisition exceptional-cost outcomes.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.68 / confidence 0.80

Cash generation is good for a professional services roll-up, though not elite on a clean reported basis. Yahoo shows TTM FCF of about £26.2m and a 12.6% FCF margin; FY2025 reported FCF was £13.4m, but the company’s FY2026 underlying FCF was £40.3m after a working-capital inflow and reduced lock-up. Cash discipline is a genuine strength: lock-up was 84 days in FY2026, comfortably inside the 90-day target and below the cited PwC top-26-to-50 law-firm average of 138 days. Capex intensity rose in FY2025/FY2026 because of technology and office investment, so I am not treating the peak FY2026 FCF as fully normalised. Confidence would improve with maintenance-vs-growth capex and a longer lock-up/cash-conversion history.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.72 / confidence 0.78

The business is not a melting ice cube. Revenue has grown from £125.6m in FY2022 to £207.7m in FY2026, FY2026 organic growth was 7.1% and second-half organic growth was 11.8%. Demand for legal and professional services is economically sensitive in places, especially real estate/corporate work, but diversified service lines and private-client/disputes/clinical negligence exposure soften that. The bigger durability question is talent retention: legal services revenue walks out the door if senior professionals leave. Knights reported qualified-fee-earner churn of 10% and eNPS of +46 in FY2026, both supportive, but this remains the trait to watch.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.62 / confidence 0.70

Knights has a narrow-to-moderate moat, not a fortress. The moat sources are a premium regional brand, national breadth, centralised support, technology and the ability to offer senior lawyers a corporate alternative to traditional partnerships. FY2026 fees per fee earner rose to £170k, up c.18% in two years, and management cited pricing discipline as a driver of organic growth; gross profit margin was high at 49.4%. But legal services remains relationship-heavy and competitive, and individual lawyers can take clients elsewhere. Confidence would firm up with client retention, price-vs-volume and win/loss data.

### Capital Allocation — 0.58 / confidence 0.72

Allocation is coherent but acquisition-heavy. Knights reinvests in senior hires, offices and technology, pays a modest progressive dividend targeting about 20% of underlying PAT, and uses acquisitions as a core growth pillar. The FY2026 dividend was 5.63p, covered by underlying cash flow; Yahoo shows dividends well covered by reported FCF in FY2022-25. The caution is that cumulative acquisition spend was £47.1m across FY2022-25 and FY2026 added c.£30.3m of acquisition consideration, with goodwill/intangibles rising to £112.2m. Management has a clear integration playbook and shows lock-up improvements in acquisitions, but the model depends on continued deal discipline. Confidence would improve with purchase multiples, earn-out outcomes and post-deal ROIC by cohort.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.43 / confidence 0.76

This is the main limiter. Yahoo-derived ROIC was 2.3%, 8.3%, 9.3% and 6.6% across FY2022-25, with ROCE 7.8% in FY2025. FY2026 underlying PBT margin of 16.0% is healthy, and the scorer script estimated incremental ROIC around 15.6%, but consolidated returns including goodwill are not high enough for a wonderful-business score. The business may earn attractive returns on organic hiring and integration, but acquisition goodwill absorbs much of that value at group level. Confidence would improve with return-on-invested-capital data excluding leases and by acquisition vintage.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.60 / confidence 0.78

Debt is acceptable but not conservative enough to ignore. Company covenant leverage was 1.50x at 30 April 2026, net debt excluding leases was £65.4m and the RCF was extended to £159m until July 2029, giving flexibility. Yahoo’s lease-inclusive debt view is tougher: FY2025 net debt/EBITDA was 2.06x and TTM snapshot net debt/EBITDA was 3.27x because it includes leases and updated total debt. Interest and lease costs are manageable while cash conversion stays high, but the balance sheet would constrain acquisitions if organic growth slowed. Confidence would firm up with the exact bank covenant definition, maturity ladder and fixed/floating mix.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.55 / confidence 0.62

The model is institutionalising, but people still matter. CEO David Beech is closely associated with the strategy and culture, and legal services businesses depend on partner/senior-associate relationships. Knights has an expanded management team, 16 Client Services Directors after adding five in FY2026, and a central platform that should reduce dependence on one person. Still, the thesis depends on retaining many senior fee earners and a distinctive culture. Confidence would improve with a deeper succession and partner-retention review.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.62 / confidence 0.55

Alignment is probably decent but under-researched. Yahoo shows insiders holding about 33% and institutions about 42%, which gives meaningful owner exposure. Governance flags are mixed: Yahoo’s overall governance risk is 7, with board risk 8 and compensation risk 7, while shareholder-rights risk is low at 1. I have not yet read the latest remuneration report in enough detail to judge pay metrics, hurdle rigor or dilution. Confidence would firm up by reading the FY2026 annual report/remuneration section when published.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair loses on low consolidated ROIC and acquisition-heavy growth. Rule-breakers loses because this is a mature professional-services consolidator, not a dominant player in a new, fast-expanding category, despite useful technology/AI optionality. Freeroll loses because there is no hard valuation floor and no concrete ≤4-year catalyst; the case is cheap cash generation plus execution, not an event-driven re-rate.

## Overall

Knights is a decent cash-generative small-cap compounder/cash-cow hybrid: cheap on normalised FCF and forward EPS, with strong cash discipline and a credible regional consolidation playbook. The weaknesses are equally clear: reported earnings need adjustment, acquisitions are central to the growth model, debt is not trivial once leases are included, and consolidated ROIC is only modest. The stock looks investable only if management keeps organic growth positive, avoids overpaying for firms, and converts the FY2026 technology/platform investment into operating leverage.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a regional-services consolidation thesis: fragmented UK regional legal services, growing technology/compliance burdens for small firms, and a central platform that can improve acquired practices’ lock-up, cross-selling and recruitment. The AI/technology angle is practical rather than moonshot: better document management, onboarding, compliance and productivity inside a professional-services platform.

## Watch

- FY2027 organic growth: whether the FY2026 H2 acceleration continues without needing more acquisitions.
- Lock-up days and underlying cash conversion; deterioration would directly weaken the cash-cow thesis.
- Acquisition discipline: purchase multiples, contingent consideration, integration outcomes and post-deal lock-up improvement.
- Net debt/covenant leverage after further acquisitions, especially if lease-inclusive leverage stays high.
- Reported vs underlying earnings gap: recurring “non-underlying” costs should narrow as integration normalises.
- Qualified-fee-earner churn and senior-hire productivity; this is the operating heart of the model.
- FY2026 annual report/remuneration details for incentive alignment and governance.

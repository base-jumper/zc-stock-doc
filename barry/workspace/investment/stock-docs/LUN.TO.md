# LUN.TO — Lundin Mining Corporation

## Business Overview

Lundin Mining is a copper-focused miner with three operating complexes: Candelaria and Caserones in Chile and Chapada in Brazil. Copper supplied about 85% of Q1 2026 revenue after the sale of its European and US mines. The operating base funds brownfield expansion and Lundin's 50% share of Vicuña, a staged copper-gold-silver project owned jointly with BHP. Revenue and profit are driven primarily by metal prices, payable volumes, grades, recoveries, by-product credits and site costs.

## Trait Assessment

The scorer classifies Lundin as a **weak cash-cow fit** (0.552 score, 0.821 confidence), with **wonderful-and-fair a genuine near-tie** (0.551, 0.817). That result describes a hybrid: the existing mines generate substantial cash, while Vicuña creates a large reinvestment runway. The low absolute scores matter more than the hairline ranking difference.

### Earnings Quality

**Score 0.40; confidence 0.88.** Reported earnings are real but not predictable. Net income moved from US$427m in 2022 to US$242m in 2023, a US$204m loss in 2024 and US$1.28bn profit in 2025. Cumulative 2022–2025 FCF equalled about 0.91× cumulative net income, but annual conversion varied sharply with working capital, capex, disposals and metal prices. One loss year and high profit-growth dispersion make this a weak anchor for either quality strategy.

### Free Cash Flow Generation

**Score 0.58; confidence 0.85.** H1 2026 reported FCF was US$578.6m and trailing FCF was about US$962m, a strong recent result. The four-year average FCF margin was only 11.4%, however, and capex consumed 51%–96% of operating cash flow in 2022–2025. Capex has exceeded depreciation rather than being deferred, so recent cash is genuine but capital-heavy. This is moderate—not archetypal—cash-cow generation.

### Fundamental Stability

**Score 0.72; confidence 0.85.** Copper demand has a structural electrification and grid-investment tailwind, and Lundin's three operating mines provide diversification. Revenue, operating margin and FCF have recovered strongly since 2023. The cash stream nevertheless remains exposed to copper prices, grades, weather, jurisdiction and mine-plan execution; Q2's severe Chilean winter disruption is a live reminder. The business is cyclical, not a secular melting ice cube.

### Pricing Power

**Score 0.28; confidence 0.90.** Lundin is a commodity price-taker. Its defence is asset quality and cost position, not an ability to set copper prices. Vicuña's study projects first-quartile costs after by-product credits, which could protect margins, but this is cost advantage rather than customer-level pricing power and remains PEA-stage.

### Capital Allocation

**Score 0.65; confidence 0.78.** Management sold Neves-Corvo and Zinkgruvan for US$1.31bn net proceeds, exited 2025 in net cash, and bought 15.1m shares during 2025 at an average C$14.05—well below today's C$37.66. It has since bought another 6.1m shares in 2026 at C$35.70 while also committing heavily to Vicuña and buying Caserones/Los Helados interests. Dividends are amply covered but tiny. The allocation is coherent and counter-cyclical in 2025, though Vicuña's eventual capital bill is the decisive unproven test. The final feasibility study and funding plan would raise confidence.

### Returns on Capital

**Score 0.52; confidence 0.90.** ROIC averaged about 9.6% over 2022–2025, rising from 5.1% in 2023 to 15.1% in 2025; recent incremental ROIC appears strong but is flattered by high metal prices and asset sales. Returns are positive but not durably above a miner's through-cycle cost of capital. Goodwill is only 2% of invested capital, so acquisitions do not materially obscure the result.

### Conservative Debt

**Score 0.88; confidence 0.90.** June 2026 net cash was US$79m and approximately US$75m on 5 August. At 2025 year-end, net debt/EBITDA was only 0.08×, EBIT covered net interest 22.5× and cash plus one year of FCF covered current debt 4.3×. This is appropriately conservative for a cyclical miner, although Vicuña's US$7.1bn Stage 1 budget could materially change the balance-sheet profile.

### Key-Person Risk

**Score 0.62; confidence 0.65.** Lundin-family leadership and relationships matter, but mine operations and technical development are institutional and Vicuña has BHP as an equal partner. A leadership change would disrupt capital allocation more than day-to-day production. The 2026 circular's succession disclosures and evidence through a Vicuña construction cycle would firm this up.

### Incentive Alignment

**Score 0.62; confidence 0.55.** Yahoo reports insiders own about 21%, providing substantial skin in the game, and buybacks have reduced shares since the Filo issuance. Family influence also creates governance-concentration risk, and the detailed compensation metrics, holding requirements and dilution controls were not fully reviewed. The 2026 management information circular is the specific source needed to firm this up.

### Reinvestment Runway

**Score 0.80; confidence 0.85.** This is the wonderful-and-fair differentiator and the reason the two classifications are close. Lundin has brownfield projects at all three mines, Saúva at Chapada, and 50% of Vicuña. The Vicuña PEA outlines a 70-plus-year mine and staged expansion, but its US$7.1bn first stage, 14.8% base-case IRR and long payback make the reinvestment opportunity large rather than proven high-return.

### Why the other strategies lost

Wonderful-and-fair is a genuine dual-fit, but ranks fractionally behind because its large reinvestment runway comes with PEA-level execution and return uncertainty. Rule Breakers scores slightly higher on pure trait quality (0.596) but lower after confidence (0.736): physical production is flat today and copper mining lacks customer devotion and a scalable winner-takes-most moat. Freeroll loses because C$37.66 is 3.35× book and nearly 5× tangible book with only a 0.3% yield—there is no close, binding floor despite a real Vicuña catalyst.

## Overall

Lundin combines an unusually clean balance sheet, competent existing operations and one of the mining industry's largest development options. The weak points are equally important: earnings are metal-price sensitive, sustaining capital is high, the stock has already risen 145% over 52 weeks, and Vicuña remains a capital-heavy PEA with permitting, execution, country and financing risk. The winning 0.552 cash-cow score is weak/mixed rather than an endorsement; this is a hybrid growth-project underwriting, not a classic cash cow.

## Valuation

At C$37.66, Yahoo Finance reports a C$32.1bn market value, 15.2× EV/EBITDA and 3.35× book. The operating business generated US$578.6m of reported FCF in H1 2026 and ended Q2 with US$79m net cash.

The cash-cow exit-multiple model uses **C$1.575 current FCF per share**, derived from approximately US$962m trailing FCF divided by 851.3m shares and translated at C$1.3938/US$. It assumes 2% aggregate growth, 0.5% annual net buybacks, the 0.29% dividend yield, a five-year hold and a 12× exit P/FCF versus 23.9× at entry. The resulting expected annualized ROI is **-10.3%**: business growth contributes 2.0%, buybacks 0.5% and income 0.4%, overwhelmed by roughly -12.9% a year of multiple compression. This deliberately values the existing cash stream and does not credit unbuilt Vicuña cash flow.

Vicuña's PEA gives the 100% project a US$9.5bn after-tax NPV at an 8% discount rate using US$4.60/lb copper, US$3,300/oz gold and US$40/oz silver. Lundin owns 50% but must fund half of US$7.1bn Stage 1 capital. That option is material, but a PEA NPV should not be added dollar-for-dollar before feasibility, funding and sanction. The **agent score is 0.35**: project optionality and balance-sheet resilience partially offset an unattractive current-cash-flow valuation.

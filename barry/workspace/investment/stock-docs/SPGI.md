# SPGI — S&P Global Inc.

## Business Overview

S&P Global is an essential financial-information and benchmarks business. Its continuing divisions are Market Intelligence, Ratings, Energy, and S&P Dow Jones Indices. The common thread is proprietary data, trusted benchmarks, workflow integration, and regulated or mission-critical customer use cases. Customers pay because SPGI's data, ratings, indices, and market intelligence are embedded in capital markets, commodity markets, investment workflows, and enterprise risk processes.

The business is unusually high quality: large recurring subscription revenue, strong incremental margins, benchmark/network effects, and a brand built over decades. S&P Global completed the Mobility spin-off on July 1, 2026; the continuing company now has four divisions: Ratings, Indices, Energy, and Market Intelligence. In Q2, post-spin pro forma revenue rose 11% to $3.678bn, adjusted operating profit rose 15% to $1.998bn, and adjusted diluted EPS rose 23% to $4.83. Pro forma operating margin expanded 410bp to 47.8%, while adjusted operating margin expanded 200bp to 54.3%.

Management's post-spin 2026 outlook calls for 5.9–7.9% revenue growth, 6–8% organic constant-currency growth, adjusted EPS of $17.50–$17.75, and 75–100bp of adjusted operating-margin expansion excluding OSTTRA. The separation sharpens the business around financial and commodity information, but Q3 onward must show that reported margins, corporate costs, leverage, and cash conversion match the pro forma presentation.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.90 / 0.85

This is one of SPGI's clearest strengths. EDGAR history shows revenue growing from $5.661bn in 2016 to $15.336bn in 2025, while the business stayed profitable throughout the period. Yahoo/statement helpers show no loss years in the recent window and cumulative cash conversion of roughly 1.2x net income. Free cash flow margins are exceptional: latest FCF margin about 35.6%, four-year average about 31.5%, with FCF conversion also around 1.2x.

Accounting earnings do include acquisition amortization and deal/divestiture noise, especially after IHS Markit, so adjusted EPS and FCF are better owner-earnings anchors than GAAP EPS alone. Still, the underlying cash economics are very real.

### Returns on Capital — 0.72 / 0.80

Reported ROIC is good rather than visually spectacular because the balance sheet is goodwill-heavy after IHS Markit and other data acquisitions. The helper read showed latest ROIC of 11.4%, average ROIC of 9.0%, and a rising trend, but goodwill represented roughly 59.6% of assets. That accounting base understates the economic return profile of the legacy ratings, indices, and data franchises.

The qualitative return profile is stronger than the raw ROIC: Q2 post-spin pro forma operating margin was 47.8% and adjusted operating margin was 54.3%. The score is therefore high, but not maxed, because acquisition accounting and the newly separated reporting base still make clean capital-return measurement harder. The first full continuing-company annual report would firm up post-spin incremental returns.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.88 / 0.75

SPGI has several moats working together: regulatory/market trust in credit ratings, benchmark and index network effects, proprietary data assets, long-lived customer workflows, and switching costs in data feeds and analytics. The 10-K describes Market Intelligence as a provider of multi-asset-class data and analytics integrated into purpose-built workflow solutions; Ratings benefits from debt issuance and surveillance demand; Indices earns asset-linked, exchange-traded derivative, and data subscription revenue.

The moat is not risk-free. Credit issuance is cyclical, regulators matter, customers can pressure data vendors, and AI may attack parts of the information workflow. But SPGI's brands, datasets, and embedded customer use cases are exactly the assets that should matter more, not less, in noisy markets. Product-level retention, price/mix, and renewal disclosures in the next annual report would firm up the pricing-power score.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.78 / 0.75

SPGI still has a long runway despite its size. Management is leaning into private markets, energy and supply-chain intelligence, enterprise data, AI-enabled tools, and adjacent datasets. Q2 post-spin pro forma revenue grew 11%, with record results in Ratings and Indices, margin expansion in Ratings, Indices, and Market Intelligence, and management reporting rapid adoption of its AI solutions. The maintained 6–8% organic constant-currency revenue-growth outlook is a credible high-single-digit base for a company this mature.

The reason this is not higher is maturity. SPGI remains a roughly $121bn-market-cap company, and part of growth comes from acquisition/tuck-in activity rather than purely organic reinvestment. The separation sharpens the continuing-company runway but makes its historical base less comparable. The Q3 continuing-company 10-Q will firm up how much growth and margin expansion survives outside pro forma reporting.

### Capital Allocation — 0.78 / 0.80

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly and has become more aggressive after the spin. The company combines reinvestment, M&A, dividends, and buybacks; historical helper output showed roughly $4.5bn of dividends and $23.6bn of buybacks over the covered period, with share count down 4.2%. It repurchased $500m in Q2 and $1.5bn year-to-date, while diluted shares fell 3% year over year. Management now expects more than $7bn of total repurchases in 2026, alongside the $0.97 quarterly dividend.

The caution is price and funding discipline. Repurchases around 23x normalized earnings are reasonable for this franchise but not clearly distressed-value purchases, and the historical helper showed shareholder returns above FCF alongside meaningful acquisition spending. The Mobility spin and divestitures simplify the portfolio, but sustained net share shrinkage without re-levering is the next test; Q3 cash flow, debt, and share-count disclosures will firm up that read.

### Conservative Debt — 0.84 / 0.85

Debt remains manageable. June 30 consolidated debt was $15.17bn and cash was $4.13bn, but $2.0bn of notes in that balance became Mobility Global's sole responsibility after the separation; underlying SPGI debt was therefore close to its year-end level. Earlier helper output showed net debt/EBITDA around 1.47x and EBIT interest coverage around 22.7x. That is conservative enough for a recurring, high-margin information business.

Leverage becomes more relevant if the enlarged buyback program persists above free cash flow or acquisition activity accelerates. For now, the spin's debt allocation and cash generation leave ample resilience, but Q3 should confirm the continuing company's net leverage and interest coverage directly.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / 0.65

This is not a founder cult or single-person product story. The moat lives in brands, regulated trust, indices, datasets, customer workflows, and institutional relationships. CEO Martina Cheung matters, especially during the post-spin operating-model and AI/product transition, but the franchise is much bigger than one executive.

Confidence is only moderate because I did not do a deep bench-strength review beyond the 2026 proxy overview and management disclosures.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.70 / 0.70

The 2026 proxy indicates a sensible pay structure: most CEO/NEO target compensation is variable and incentive-based; annual incentives use adjusted revenue and adjusted EBITA margin; long-term PSUs use three-year adjusted EPS targets; and the company has stock ownership guidelines, clawback policies, and anti-hedging/pledging rules. That is reasonably aligned with per-share compounding.

The weakness is that adjusted EPS can be buyback-sensitive and may not fully police returns on invested capital or acquisition discipline. I would prefer explicit ROIC/free-cash-flow-per-share hurdles, but the current structure is not obviously broken.

### Why other strategies lost

Cash-cow is plausible because SPGI throws off enormous FCF and returns a lot of it. It loses because the better thesis is not a no-growth harvest; it is a durable compounder with pricing power, recurring data revenue, and an ongoing reinvestment runway. Rule-breakers loses because SPGI is not an early explosive disruptor. Freeroll is the wrong frame because there is no hard asset/NAV downside floor: valuation support depends on the quality and persistence of earnings.

## Overall

SPGI is a first-class compounder at a fair price. The Mobility separation is complete, Q2 showed strong post-spin growth and margin expansion, and the enlarged buyback program improves near-term per-share compounding. The main detractors are execution against newly recast comparatives, the possibility of stranded costs, and a valuation that still assumes durable high-single-digit growth.

My view: worthy of watching and potentially owning, but not cheap enough to ignore execution. A few quarters of clean continuing-company cash conversion and margin delivery would make the post-spin economics easier to underwrite.

## Valuation

I used the wonderful-and-fair exit-multiple method on normalized earnings because the Mobility spin makes trailing consolidated FCF a stale anchor. The $17.80/share current fundamental triangulates management's $17.50–$17.75 post-spin adjusted EPS guide, the $4.83 Q2 adjusted EPS run-rate, and current-year analyst estimates around $17.83. It is a normalized continuing-company earnings estimate, not raw trailing GAAP EPS.

At $411.93/share, the entry multiple is about 23.1x earnings. I assume 8% aggregate earnings growth for five years, 2.5% annual net share-count reduction, a 22x exit P/E, and a 0.94% dividend yield. The model produces a 10.72% annualized ROI (1.66x over five years), attributed to 8.00% business growth, 2.56% buyback lift, -1.01% multiple compression, and 1.06% income. Agent score is 0.68: exceptional business quality and a credible low-double-digit return, tempered by post-spin execution risk and a still-premium valuation.

## Thesis Connections

Potential FIT connections: capital markets data infrastructure, index/ETF ecosystem, private-market data, commodity and energy intelligence, and AI-enabled enterprise data workflows. SPGI is a picks-and-shovels information business rather than a direct AI compute or software platform bet.

## Watch

- Q3 continuing-company economics: reported margin, stranded costs, cash conversion, and net leverage versus the post-spin pro forma presentation.
- Ratings transaction revenue cyclicality as debt issuance conditions change.
- Organic growth split versus acquisitions and divestitures.
- Whether AI features increase customer value or enable cheaper competition.
- Execution and funding of the more-than-$7bn 2026 buyback plan; track net share shrinkage and debt rather than authorization alone.
- Any regulatory or reputational issues in Ratings, Indices, or data products.

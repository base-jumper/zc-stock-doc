# Trait: Mispriced for an Identifiable Reason

**What we're looking for:**
A clear, defensible reason the stock is cheap **and** a *differentiated view* on why the market is
wrong or the pessimism is overdone. Upside that isn't in the price usually exists because the market
is anchored to something — a low multiple, a scary narrative, recent underperformance — that we have
specific grounds to disagree with. **If we can't articulate why the crowd is wrong, we probably
don't have an edge**, and a low price alone is not a thesis.

This trait scores the **qualitative edge** — *whether* the market is wrong and *why*. It does **not**
score *how much* upside there is: the size of the gap (fair value vs price, and the asymmetry to the
floor) is a separate quantitative judgement made in the Valuation write-up, not here. Keep the two
apart — a rock-solid reason the market is wrong scores high even if the upside happens to be modest.

Common setups:

* **Low multiple we think is temporary** — a depressed forward P/E or EV/EBITDA driven by a cyclical
  earnings trough that should normalise.
* **A popular narrative we disagree with** — the market has extrapolated a story (e.g. "COVID changes
  air travel forever," "this product category is dying") that we judge wrong or overstated.
* **Over-punished short-term underperformance** — a fund manager or operator penalised for a soft
  patch, trading below peers on a multiple that should re-rate once performance normalises.
* **Forced / indiscriminate selling** — index deletion, tax-loss selling, a large holder unwinding;
  price pressure unrelated to fundamentals.
* **Orphaned / misunderstood** — too small, too opaque, too complex (holding co, sum-of-the-parts), 
  or recently spun off and under-followed.

**The discipline:** state the consensus view, then state our differentiated view and the specific
evidence for it — *"The market thinks X; we think Y because Z."* A variant perception backed by
evidence is a mispricing; the same sentence without the evidence is just hope.

**Key questions:**

* Why exactly is the stock cheap? What is the market pricing in?
* What is our differentiated view, and what specific evidence supports it over the consensus?
* Is this a genuine variant perception (we see something others don't) or merely optimism?
* What would make the market change its mind?

**Scoring guidance:**

* **0.70–1.00** A clear, specific reason for the cheapness and a well-evidenced, differentiated view
  on why the market is wrong.
* **0.40–0.69** A plausible thesis, but partly based on hope, or our edge over consensus is thin.
* **0.00–0.39** No real differentiated view — cheap, but we can't say why the market is wrong (or the
  market is probably right).

**Documentation:**

* The consensus view / what the market is pricing in
* Our differentiated view and the specific evidence for it
* The setup type (cyclical multiple, narrative, forced selling, neglect, etc.)
* What would make the market change its mind

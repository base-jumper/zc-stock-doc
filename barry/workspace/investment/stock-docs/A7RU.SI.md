# A7RU.SI — Keppel Infrastructure Trust

## Business Overview

Keppel Infrastructure Trust (KIT) is a Singapore-listed diversified infrastructure business trust sponsored and managed by Keppel. It owns 15 essential businesses/assets across energy transition, environmental services, distribution & storage, and digital infrastructure, with S$9.1b of assets under management at 31 March 2026. The portfolio includes Singapore town gas, power, waste and water assets; Ixom chemicals distribution; Ventura bus operations; Global Marine Group subsea cable services; renewables; and energy-transition infrastructure.

The investment case is mostly an income infrastructure story. FY2025 distributable income (DI) rose 24.4% to S$249.5m, total distribution declared was S$239.8m, and DPU was stable at 3.94 Singapore cents. In 1Q2026, underlying cash flow improved: asset DI rose 13.0% to S$78.0m, while reported DI fell to S$53.7m because 1Q2025 included a divestment gain. At S$0.515/unit, the FY2025 DPU implies a roughly 7.65% yield.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.62 / 0.76

KIT has recurring infrastructure cash flows, but accounting earnings are a noisy anchor. FY2025 DI increased 24.4% to S$249.5m and DPU held at 3.94 cents; Yahoo shows revenue of S$2.28b, operating income of S$241.0m and net income to common of S$93.0m. The gap between DI and accounting profit is normal for a business trust, but it means the cleanest earnings measure is management-defined FFO/DI rather than EPS.

Interpretation: the cash earnings are real and diversified, but headline NPAT includes interest, depreciation, impairments and trust-structure adjustments. Confidence would firm up with a longer per-asset FFO bridge and maintenance-capex history.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.64 / 0.75

This is a high-cash-yield vehicle, not a capital-light compounder. FY2025 DI of S$249.5m covered the S$239.8m total distribution declared, and management states the FY2025 DPU payout ratio was 96%. Yahoo's stricter free cash flow was S$182.1m in FY2025 versus cash dividends paid of S$232.9m, so statutory FCF cover looks weaker than the trust DI measure.

Interpretation: distributions are broadly supported by operating infrastructure cash flows, but growth capex, acquisitions and refinancings make the cash-flow waterfall more complex than a simple industrial cash cow. Confidence would improve with audited maintenance capex versus growth capex by asset.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.78 / 0.80

KIT's assets provide essential services: town gas, power, waste treatment, water/desalination, chemicals distribution, public buses and subsea cable services. The 2026 factsheet says City Energy is the sole producer/retailer of piped town gas in Singapore, KIT's power asset supplies 13% of commercial power, its water assets manage over 21% of Singapore's water supply, and waste assets treat more than 35% of Singapore's incinerable waste.

The main durability risk is not demand collapse; it is asset mix, contract renewal/refinancing, and whether acquired assets keep earning adequate returns. 1Q2026 commentary showed stable KMC contribution, higher AGPC/German Solar/BKR2 contribution, lower Ventura contribution after partial divestment, and new digital-infrastructure DI from GMG.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.70 / 0.70

KIT's moat is infrastructure scarcity and long-term contracts rather than brand-led pricing power. KMC is backed by a long-term capacity tolling agreement through 2040; several Singapore assets are essential utility-like infrastructure; and Ixom and Ventura have defensible local operating positions. These support recurring cash flows.

The limitation is that regulated/contracted assets rarely have unlimited pricing power. Tariffs and contract structures can protect returns, but counterparties and regulators also cap upside. Confidence is moderate because the asset-level contracts are only partly visible from public summaries.

### Capital Allocation — 0.66 / 0.72

Management has been active and mostly disciplined: KIT recycled capital by exiting Philippine Coastal and partially divesting Ventura, unlocked over S$300m in net proceeds in 2025, paid down debt, acquired GMG, and committed up to S$128.1m for an additional 39% KMC stake. The KMC deal is expected to lift pro forma FY2025 DPU from 3.94 cents to 4.18 cents, a 6% uplift, if it had been owned for the full year.

The caveat is that the strategy depends on continual capital recycling, debt and occasional equity/perpetual funding. That can work, but it makes per-unit value creation more fragile than a low-leverage cash-return machine.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.45 / 0.72

Returns are stable but not high. Yahoo-derived ROIC was around 4.0–5.6% over 2023–2025 and ROE was 5.4–9.4%. That is acceptable for infrastructure income assets, but below the threshold for a high-return compounder.

Interpretation: KIT can be a cash-yield holding, but it should not be underwritten as a high-ROIC reinvestment story. The burden is on distributions and capital recycling, not internal compounding.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.44 / 0.80

Leverage is the main flaw. Net gearing rose to 41.9% at 31 March 2026 from 38.7% at FY2025, mainly because cash balances fell. That said, the trust-level interest coverage ratio improved to 8.6x from 7.6x, weighted-average cost of debt was 4.4%, weighted-average debt maturity increased to 3.5 years after refinancing, and about 72% of borrowings were fixed/hedged.

Interpretation: the balance sheet looks manageable rather than conservative. Refinancing execution and interest-rate exposure are watch items, especially with the KMC follow-on acquisition and ongoing acquisition pipeline.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / 0.70

The asset base and Keppel sponsorship make this low key-person-risk. KIT owns operating infrastructure businesses with management teams, contracts and external counterparties; the thesis should not depend on one founder or executive. Confidence is not higher because detailed succession and asset-level management depth are not central in the sources reviewed.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 / 0.50

Alignment is adequate but not proven exceptional. Keppel sponsorship gives sourcing and operating advantages, while the external trustee-manager structure creates potential fee and related-party-transaction concerns. The KMC follow-on acquisition is an interested-person transaction from a Keppel-related fund, though it appears DPU-accretive and strategically sensible.

Confidence would firm up by reviewing the latest fee framework, executive/unit ownership, and independent-director oversight in the annual report.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Freeroll has a decent yield-backed floor and KMC could be a catalyst, but the catalyst is not a clear forced rerating; it is an income improvement. Wonderful-and-fair and rule-breakers lose on low returns on capital and limited organic growth/reinvestment runway. This is best framed as a cash-cow/infrastructure-income stock.

## Overall

KIT is a decent income infrastructure candidate: diversified essential assets, Keppel sponsorship, a high current yield, improving underlying 1Q2026 cash flow, and a near-term DPU-accretive KMC stake increase. The negatives are equally clear: high leverage, modest ROIC, complex trust cash-flow definitions, and reliance on capital recycling/refinancing rather than clean retained-cash compounding.

My valuation-aware score is 0.58: worth watching/ranking as a yield-backed cash cow, but not high enough quality or balance-sheet strength to treat as a core compounder.

## Watch

- 1H2026 results release and whether underlying DI continues to grow after excluding divestment gains.
- Completion/integration of the additional 39% KMC stake and whether actual DPU accretion tracks the 6% pro forma claim.
- Net gearing after KMC funding; keep an eye on the 42% level, refinancing costs and the fixed/hedged debt percentage.
- Maintenance versus growth capex split; this is the key evidence gap for true distribution cover.

# Power Bottleneck

## Thesis

AI data centres, electrification, industrial automation, EVs, and reshoring are increasing electricity demand faster than grids and generation capacity can adapt. Power availability may become one of the key bottlenecks for AI growth and broader economic expansion.

In the near to medium term, the winners are likely to be companies that generate, transmit, store, manage, or enable reliable power at scale. AI may be software, but its limiting input is increasingly physical: electricity, grid connections, cooling, land, transformers, turbines, gas, uranium, and permitting.

## Initial Probability

~80% over a 5–7 year horizon.

This is one of the cleaner and more actionable FITs. The demand signal is already visible through hyperscaler data-centre capex, grid interconnection queues, power purchase agreements, nuclear interest, transformer shortages, and rising concern about data-centre load growth.

## Why This Could Happen

- AI training and inference consume enormous electricity.
- AI demand is growing faster than expected and shifting from training clusters to always-on inference.
- Data centres require firm, reliable, high-quality power.
- Grid upgrades are slow due to permitting, equipment shortages, regulation, and capital intensity.
- Electrification of transport, heating, industry, and manufacturing adds demand outside AI.
- Power availability may become a site-selection constraint for data centres.
- AI labs and cloud providers may increasingly secure power directly through long-term PPAs, co-location, or ownership stakes.

## Key Enablers / Bottlenecks

- Grid transmission capacity.
- Transformer and switchgear availability.
- Gas turbine availability.
- Nuclear life extensions and new nuclear/SMR deployment.
- Renewables plus storage at scale.
- Permitting reform.
- Data-centre cooling efficiency.
- Demand-response software and grid orchestration.
- Ability to sign long-term power purchase agreements.
- Energy-rich jurisdictions willing to host AI infrastructure.

## Broad Winners

- Grid equipment suppliers.
- Transformer, switchgear, and electrical infrastructure manufacturers.
- Utilities with load growth and supportive regulators.
- Independent power producers.
- Nuclear/uranium supply chain.
- Natural gas infrastructure and turbine suppliers.
- Data-centre developers with secured power.
- Cooling and power-management companies.
- Energy storage and battery infrastructure.
- Power-market software and demand-response platforms.

## What To Monitor

- Hyperscaler capex guidance.
- Data-centre electricity demand forecasts.
- Power purchase agreements by Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta, OpenAI partners, xAI, Oracle, etc.
- Data-centre project delays caused by grid constraints.
- Transformer, switchgear, turbine, and cable lead times.
- Grid interconnection queues.
- Power prices in data-centre-heavy regions.
- Nuclear policy and reactor life extension announcements.
- Uranium contracting and spot/term pricing.
- Utilities discussing AI/data-centre load growth in earnings calls.
- Companies acquiring or co-locating with power generation.
- Permitting reform or grid investment packages.

## Risks / Bear Case

- AI compute becomes much more energy efficient, reducing load growth.
- AI demand disappoints or capex slows.
- Overbuilding creates excess data-centre/power capacity.
- Regulators prevent utilities from earning attractive returns on AI-driven grid buildout.
- Commodity cycles lead to poor entry points in uranium/gas/equipment names.
- Political backlash against data-centre power usage.
- Distributed solar/storage reduces need for centralised winners.

## Investment Framing

The cleanest near-term expression is not necessarily owning AI labs. It may be owning the physical bottlenecks that AI labs must pass through.

Most attractive winners should have:

- Direct exposure to grid/data-centre capex.
- Pricing power from equipment shortages or scarce capacity.
- Long-duration contracted demand.
- Strong balance sheets.
- Regulatory frameworks that allow good returns.
- Exposure to firm/reliable power, not only intermittent generation.

This FIT should be monitored actively because it is actionable now.

# NEX.PA — Nexans S.A.

## Business Overview

Nexans is a French electrification pure play that designs, manufactures, and installs cable systems and services across the power value chain: generation and high-voltage transmission, grid distribution, building/usage cables, and industry solutions. The company has deliberately refocused away from lower-value complexity toward electrification markets where grid investment, renewable generation, interconnectors, data-center power demand, and building electrification should keep demand structurally healthy.

In 2025 Nexans reported €6.10bn of standard sales, +10.1% reported and +8.3% organic growth; adjusted EBITDA rose 27.3% to €728m, with an 11.9% adjusted EBITDA margin on standard sales. Free cash flow was €344m and year-end net debt was €266m, or 0.36x adjusted EBITDA. Q1 2026 Electrification organic growth was 4.9% (Transmission +8.8%, Grid +5.7%, Connect +2.5%), while total Group organic growth was 0.1% including Other activities. Nexans confirmed 2026 guidance of €730–810m adjusted EBITDA and €210–310m FCF, with H1 expected softer than H2.

The €680m acquisition of Republic Wire completed on June 1, adding a U.S. low-voltage platform with about €520m of current annual sales. It improves the North American growth runway but materially raises the capital-allocation and post-deal leverage burden. At €134.10/share on July 23, Yahoo showed a market cap of about €5.87bn.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.67 / 0.78

Nexans has been profitable through the four-year Yahoo window: net income was €245m in 2022, €221m in 2023, €279m in 2024, and €352m in 2025. Cash backing is better than the headline earnings line: cumulative FCF/net income over that period is about 1.7x, and the 2025 FCF conversion ratio was 47.3% of adjusted EBITDA in company reporting. That supports the view that earnings are real.

The caveat is predictability. Revenue is lumpy because project timing, metal prices, scope changes, and transmission down-payments move the reported numbers; Yahoo shows revenue falling in 2023 and 2024 before recovering in 2025. Confidence is capped because the available data covers only four years, not a full 7–10-year cycle, and because adjusted EBITDA is a central management metric.

### Returns on Capital — 0.38 / 0.80

This is the main quality weakness. The helper script shows ROIC of 16.7% in 2022, 9.4% in 2023, 5.9% in 2024, and 6.9% in 2025, with ROCE of 8.9% in 2025. Average ROIC across the window is roughly 9.7%, which is not high for an industrial compounder and likely only modestly above cost of capital. ROE looks better at 17.6% in 2025, but the ROE-minus-ROIC gap suggests leverage and capital structure flatter the equity-return view.

Incremental returns are hard to read because the company is actively investing in PWR-Transmission capacity, including the Electra cable-laying vessel and Charleroi extension. To firm this up, use Nexans' segment invested-capital disclosure and post-expansion returns once the new assets ramp.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.55 / 0.60

Nexans has a real but not pristine moat. Positives: century-plus technical expertise, high-voltage project credentials, cable-laying capability, customer-critical infrastructure, and management's stated focus on selectivity and value-driven growth. The 2025 adjusted EBITDA margin reached a recent-history high at 11.9% of standard sales, suggesting improved mix and execution rather than pure commodity pass-through.

Negatives: cable manufacturing still has commodity and project-execution characteristics, gross margin is only about 11–13% in Yahoo data, and competition in electrical equipment is substantial. The moat is narrower than a software or branded-consumer compounder; it is strongest in complex high-voltage transmission projects and weaker in standard usage cables.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.65 / 0.65

The runway is attractive: electrification requires more generation, transmission, distribution, and usage infrastructure, and Nexans is positioned across that chain. Management is already spending to expand transmission capacity, and 2025 growth was led by PWR-Transmission with PWR-Grid and PWR-Connect tracking mid-term guidelines.

The reason this is not higher is the return-on-capital evidence. A runway only compounds if incremental capital earns well; current reported ROIC is mediocre, capex is meaningful, and acquisitions such as La Triveneta Cavi and RCT Cables contribute to growth. The opportunity is real, but the financial proof of high-return reinvestment is not yet strong.

### Capital Allocation — 0.60 / 0.65

Allocation looks sensible but remains in execution mode. Nexans is reinvesting heavily where demand is strongest and paying a covered dividend. The €2.90/share 2025 dividend was up 11.5%; Yahoo shows dividends paid of €116m against €464m of FCF, about 4x FCF cover. Year-end 2025 leverage was low, but the €680m Republic Wire purchase means that historical snapshot no longer represents the current balance sheet.

The caution is acquisition and capex intensity. Cumulative acquisition spend in Yahoo's four-year window was €1.17bn before Republic Wire, while goodwill had already risen to €680m. Republic Wire is strategically coherent and adds about €520m of annual sales, but the €680m enterprise value is material. Deal funding, purchase-price allocation, synergies and post-acquisition returns need to be visible before awarding a high allocator score.

### Conservative Debt — 0.72 / 0.72

The pre-deal balance sheet was a strength: company-reported net debt was €266m at 2025 year-end, 0.36x adjusted EBITDA, down from €681m / 1.19x in 2024. The statement view showed EBIT interest cover of 6.5x and EBITDA interest cover of 10.9x. However, the €680m Republic Wire acquisition completed after year-end, so those leverage figures are stale as a current-risk measure.

The score remains in the conservative band because starting leverage was very low and the acquired business adds earnings, but confidence falls until H1 2026 reports pro-forma net debt/EBITDA, financing terms and the maturity ladder. Debt still appears manageable, but there is less downturn optionality than the year-end snapshot implied.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.78 / 0.55

The thesis is not dependent on a single charismatic founder. Nexans' advantage sits in assets, project references, engineering know-how, customer relationships, and global operations across 41 countries rather than one individual. Confidence is only moderate because I did not review the latest succession and senior-management bench disclosures in detail.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.72 / 0.82

The 2026 CEO plan is reasonably well aligned. Annual variable pay is capped at 150% of fixed compensation and is 65% collective / 35% individual. The collective scorecard includes organic growth (10%), ROCE (20%), EBITDA (30%), FCF (30%) and net income (10%), so returns and cash generation carry meaningful weight alongside a potentially gameable adjusted metric. Long-term awards are capped at 150% of fixed pay and use a three-year 2028 test: 40% relative TSR, 40% EBITDA margin subject to cash-conversion and organic-growth qualifiers, and 20% CSR. The CEO must retain 25% of vested performance shares, subject to a 15,000-share minimum.

That mix points management toward profitable growth and shareholder returns, with modest maximum dilution (the CEO's 2026 award cap is about 0.09% of share capital). It falls short of exceptional because much ownership is granted rather than purchased, annual EBITDA remains prominent, and acquisition integration appears in individual objectives. The remuneration report provides good plan detail; actual hurdle difficulty and Julien Hueber's eventual open-market ownership record remain the main uncertainties.

### Why other strategies lost

Cash-cow scores marginally higher mechanically, but wonderful-and-fair remains the chosen framing because the business is still reinvesting heavily and FCF margins are moderate rather than fat; this is not yet a mature low-capex harvest. Rule-breakers has some fit because electrification is a strong secular wave, but Nexans is a proven industrial rather than an emerging exponential disruptor. Freeroll loses because there is no hard valuation floor near the current price: price/book is about 2.9x, price/tangible book about 5.7x, net cash is negative, and the dividend yield is only about 2.2%.

## Overall

Nexans is a good electrification infrastructure business at what looks like a reasonable, but not screamingly cheap, price. Q1 supports the secular demand case and the Republic Wire deal expands the U.S. runway. The main detractors are still quality and execution: reported ROIC is not high enough yet to call it a first-class compounder, while growth now relies on continued capex plus integration of a material acquisition whose post-deal leverage and returns are not yet reported.

My view: worth watching, especially if the transmission-capacity investments convert into higher sustainable ROIC. At today's price it is investable-looking but not a high-conviction quality bargain.

## Valuation

I used the wonderful-and-fair exit-multiple method on FCF per share. The anchor remains €10.75/share of current owner-earnings/FCF, triangulated from the latest full-year cash-flow data and 43.65m ordinary shares; there is no newer interim cash-flow statement yet. At €134.10/share, the entry multiple is about 12.5x FCF. I assume 8% aggregate FCF growth for five years, no dilution, a 14x exit P/FCF, and a 2.15% dividend yield.

Those assumptions produce a 12.74% annualized five-year ROI: 8.0% from business growth, about 2.3% from re-rating and about 2.2% from income, with no dilution assumed. The result should be read as a reasonable base-case return, not a guaranteed floor. Agent score remains 0.54: acceptable expected return and manageable balance-sheet risk, offset by only-middling ROIC, acquisition integration and uncertainty around post-deal leverage.

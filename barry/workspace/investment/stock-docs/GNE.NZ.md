# GNE.NZ — Genesis Energy Limited

## Business Overview

Genesis Energy is a New Zealand integrated gentailer: it sells electricity, reticulated gas and LPG to households and commercial/industrial customers, generates electricity from hydro, thermal, wind/solar PPAs and solar joint ventures, and owns a 46% interest in the Kupe oil and gas field. FY25 scale was roughly 520k customers, 6,207 GWh generated and 6.6 PJ of Kupe gas.

The business is mature and asset-heavy. Its distinctive asset is Huntly, which Genesis is reframing from legacy baseload thermal generation into a flexible security-of-supply portfolio: Rankine units, fuel reserve, firming options, a 100 MW / 200 MWh battery due Q1 FY27, and work on biomass to displace coal over time. The investment case is therefore mostly yield-plus-modest-growth: monetise flexibility in a volatile NZ electricity market, defend retail netbacks, and fund renewables/flexible capacity without breaking the balance sheet.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.65 / 0.75

Genesis has demonstrated real earning power, but it is not a smooth compounder. Yahoo annual data shows positive net income in each of FY22-FY25: NZ$222m, NZ$196m, NZ$131m and NZ$169m. Cash backing is better than the profit line: cumulative FCF/net income over the same four years was about 1.35x. The caveat is volatility: net income fell in FY23 and FY24 before rebounding in FY25, and earnings are exposed to hydrology, gas availability, wholesale prices and Huntly dispatch. This is dependable enough for a cash-cow frame, but not pristine predictable earnings.

Confidence is below very high because Yahoo only gives four annual years here; a deeper 7-10 year cycle from filings would firm this up.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.55 / 0.70

Free cash is real but moderate. FY22-FY25 FCF was NZ$168m, NZ$335m, NZ$288m and NZ$177m, with an average FCF margin around 8.6% and FY25 only 4.8%. TTM FCF was about NZ$260m on Yahoo, while the FY25 ordinary dividend was 14.3 cps, close to covered but not with a fat margin when using FY25 FCF. The business is also entering a higher investment period: FY26 stay-in-business capex guidance is NZ$130-140m, and growth capex of up to NZ$300m is targeted at renewables.

The cash is distributable, but this is not a capital-light cash machine. Maintenance/growth capex split and asset condition at Huntly would firm the score.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.68 / 0.70

The demand base is durable: electricity security, retail energy supply, flexible capacity and gas/LPG all remain essential in New Zealand. FY25 actually highlighted the need for Genesis' portfolio during low hydrology and declining gas availability. Management's FY26 normalised EBITDAF guidance of NZ$430-460m is below FY25's NZ$470m but still within a stable earnings band, while the Gen35 pathway targets mid-to-upper NZ$500m EBITDAF by FY28.

The main structural concern is transition risk. Huntly's coal/gas role is valuable for security-of-supply, but emissions, gas availability, carbon costs and renewable buildout can shift economics. I read this as a durable but changing franchise, not a melting ice cube yet.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.60 / 0.65

Genesis has partial pricing power rather than a clean monopoly moat. FY25 retail gross margin increased by NZ$65m despite higher fuel and network costs, with management citing disciplined pricing, simplification, product optimisation and pass-through of regulated transmission/distribution costs. The moat is the integrated portfolio: retail demand, hydro, Huntly flexibility, Kupe gas exposure and contracted firming capacity.

The limits are clear: NZ retail energy is competitive, generation is commodity-linked, and regulatory/political scrutiny constrains supernormal returns. Good enough for a utility cash-cow, not a high-moat compounder.

### Capital Allocation — 0.58 / 0.65

Capital allocation is balanced but somewhat stretched. Genesis pays a meaningful dividend: FY25 DPS was 14.3 cps and the current indicated yield is about 5.5%. It is also investing in assets that fit the strategy: Huntly BESS, solar, wind development, digital systems and demand flexibility. The company maintains a BBB+ rating and 2.6x debt leverage.

The drawbacks are dilution and capex pressure. Shares outstanding rose over FY22-FY25, partly helped by dividend reinvestment, and the upcoming growth capex means the dividend-plus-growth mix needs careful funding. There is no evidence of reckless M&A, but the Ecotricity and ChargeNet moves add execution risk.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.42 / 0.75

Returns are modest. Yahoo-derived ROIC was about 6.9%, 6.6%, 4.5% and 5.0% across FY22-FY25; ROCE was roughly 5.5-8.1%. For a regulated/contractual utility this can still create value if funded cheaply, but it is nowhere near a wonderful-business ROC profile. Incremental ROIC from the helper script was weak, likely distorted by investment timing and asset revaluations, but it reinforces the point: Genesis is an infrastructure-style yield asset, not a high-return reinvestment machine.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.62 / 0.75

Debt is acceptable for a utility, not conservative in an absolute sense. FY25 net debt/EBITDA was 2.6x, EBIT interest cover was 4.2x, and S&P reaffirmed BBB+ with stable outlook in Dec 2024. Liquidity headroom is maintained above treasury policy and maturities appear diversified across bank debt, wholesale bonds, retail bonds, USPP and long-dated capital bonds.

The weak spots are rising leverage versus FY23-FY24, current ratio below 1, and near-term debt cover around 0.8x on the script's simple cash-plus-FCF/current-debt measure. This balance sheet supports the dividend in ordinary conditions, but not with huge spare room if hydrology, gas or capex surprises all go the wrong way.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.78 / 0.65

The thesis is embedded in assets, contracts, customers and regulation, not one individual. CEO Malcolm Johns matters for executing Gen35, and the board approved a restricted share rights offer to retain him through FY28/FY29, but Genesis would not break if one person left. The score is high because the core economics sit in the portfolio and market role. Confidence is moderate because succession/bench evidence was not researched deeply.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.62 / 0.70

The remuneration structure is broadly aligned but imperfect. FY25 STI was 40% financial performance, 20% Huntly portfolio, 20% renewables, 10% people/customer/safety and 10% transformation. LTI uses three-year performance rights with absolute TSR and relative TSR hurdles; the FY22 LTI did not vest because TSR and emissions hurdles were not met, which is a good discipline signal. The negatives: FY25 STI paid 117% of target despite the LTI miss, the emissions hurdle was removed from FY25 LTI, and the CEO retention rights are service-based as much as performance-based. Overall, pointed in a shareholder-value direction but not owner-operator perfect.

### Why the other strategies lost

Wonderful-and-fair loses because returns on capital and reinvestment runway are too modest; Genesis may be useful and durable, but it is not a high-return compounder. Freeroll loses because the floor is plausible but not compelling and there is no clean ≤4-year catalyst beyond normal execution of Gen35. Rule-breakers loses mechanically: this is a mature utility, not an emerging dominant growth company.

## Overall

Genesis is a decent income/utility cash-cow candidate: essential demand, a valuable flexible generation portfolio, a covered dividend, and a credible path to modest EBITDAF growth as Huntly flexibility and renewables are monetised. The main detractors are low returns on capital, capital intensity, exposure to hydrology/gas/thermal fuel volatility, and only moderate dividend cover while capex ramps.

I would treat it as a watchable yield-plus-growth utility, not a high-conviction compounder. The stock looks roughly fair at NZ$2.60: enough yield to be interesting, but not much margin of safety if the FY28 uplift slips.

## Valuation

I used the cash-cow exit-multiple method with FCF as the owner-earnings anchor. Inputs: price NZ$2.60; current FCF per share NZ$0.16, roughly FY25 FCF/share rather than the stronger TTM figure; 5-year horizon; 1.5% aggregate growth; 1.5% annual dilution to reflect the recent share-count drift/DRP effect; 14x exit P/FCF, below the 16.25x entry multiple; 5.5% dividend yield.

That setup produces an annualised ROI of about 2.9%. Return is mostly income; business growth and dilution almost cancel out, and multiple normalisation is a drag. This supports a middling agent score: the dividend is useful, but the current price does not underwrite a strong forward return unless Gen35 lifts FCF materially above the conservative NZ$0.16/share anchor.

## Thesis Connections

FIT connection is weak-to-moderate: Genesis is exposed to electrification, grid flexibility, renewables buildout and EV charging through ChargeNet, but the dominant economics remain mature utility cash yield and security-of-supply infrastructure rather than explosive growth.

## Watch

- FY26 normalised EBITDAF versus NZ$430-460m guidance and like-for-like EBITDAF versus NZ$470-510m.
- Whether Gen35 remains on track for mid-to-upper NZ$500m EBITDAF by FY28.
- Huntly Firming Options regulatory review and commercial contribution.
- Huntly BESS completion by Q1 FY27, cost versus NZ$135m forecast, and realised revenue stack.
- Dividend cover after FY26 SIB capex of NZ$130-140m and growth capex up to NZ$300m.
- Net debt/EBITDA, BBB+ rating headroom, and refinancing costs as capex ramps.
- Gas availability, hydrology, carbon costs and thermal fuel reserve usage.
- Evidence that solar/wind/ChargeNet investments earn acceptable incremental returns rather than just increasing asset size.

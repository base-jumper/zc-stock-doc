# Trait: Incentive Alignment

**What we're looking for:**
A pay-and-ownership system that makes executives rich the *same way* shareholders get rich — through
long-term, per-share intrinsic value — and that does not reward them for anything that destroys it.
Munger's framing is the whole trait: *"Show me the incentive and I'll show you the outcome."* Good
people respond to bad incentives, so we judge the **system that points management**, not the people
it points (that is [management quality](management-backing.md)) or the decisions it produced (that is
[capital allocation](capital-allocation.md)). A wonderful business we hold for years is one where the
people running it are paid to keep it wonderful.

**This is the *incentives* axis — orthogonal to quality and to allocation.** A visionary, ethical CEO
([management quality](management-backing.md)) can still sit inside a comp plan that pays for revenue
growth at any cost; a disciplined allocator can be one bad pay design away from empire-building. This
trait asks only: *if the executive does exactly what their pay package rewards, do shareholders win?*

**What to look at:**

| Lever | Aligned | Misaligned |
|---|---|---|
| **Metrics pay is tied to** | Long-run per-share value, return on capital, free-cash-flow per share, multi-year relative TSR | Revenue or absolute size (rewards empire-building), unadjusted EPS (gameable via buybacks), "adjusted EBITDA" that strips real costs, easily-hit annual targets |
| **Skin in the game** | Large holdings **bought** with own money; meaningful multiple-of-salary holding requirements; long post-vesting hold periods | Wealth is all granted options/RSUs with no personal capital at risk; executives net sellers; no ownership guidelines |
| **Time horizon** | Multi-year vesting and performance periods that match a long hold | Annual bonus dominates; short cliffs; rewards this quarter's optics |
| **Hurdles & rigor** | Genuine stretch targets, peer-relative, with clawbacks for restatements or misconduct | Sandbagged or serially reset targets, single-trigger windfalls, options repriced after a fall |
| **Magnitude & dilution** | Pay reasonable versus performance and peers; equity grants modest relative to share count | Egregious quantum regardless of results; mega-grants and heavy stock-based comp that quietly dilute owners |
| **Self-dealing** | Arm's-length dealings; independent, credible comp committee | Related-party transactions, outsized perks, sweetheart loans, golden parachutes, a captured board setting its own pay |

**Why this matters:**
Pay design is a leading indicator of behaviour a clean income statement won't show. Tie a bonus to
revenue and you will get acquisitions; tie it to EPS and you will get buybacks whether or not the
shares are cheap; pay in repriceable options and you reward volatility, not value. Misaligned
incentives are how a genuinely capable management team is steered, year after year, into decisions
that enrich them while eroding per-share value — and because the corrosion is slow and dressed in
"market-competitive" benchmarking, it rarely shows up until the compounding has been lost.

**Key questions:**

* If management maximises exactly what their pay plan measures, do per-share owners come out ahead?
* Are the metrics ones an owner would choose — per-share value and returns on capital — or size and
  gameable accounting figures?
* Did executives buy meaningful stakes with their own money, or merely receive them? Are they holding
  or selling?
* Do vesting and performance periods stretch over years, matching a long-term hold?
* Are targets a genuine stretch, or quietly reset and sandbagged? Are there clawbacks?
* Is total pay sane relative to results, and is stock-based comp diluting owners materially?
* Any self-dealing, related-party transactions, or a board too captured to say no?

**Scoring guidance:**

* **0.70–1.00** Strongly aligned: pay keyed to long-term per-share value and returns on capital,
  multi-year horizons, real skin in the game (capital at risk, holding requirements), rigorous
  hurdles with clawbacks, sane quantum and modest dilution, no self-dealing. Doing what they're paid
  to do makes owners richer.
* **0.40–0.69** Mixed: some sensible long-term metrics alongside gameable ones, ownership that is
  granted rather than bought, short-ish horizons, soft targets, elevated pay or stock-based comp, or
  minor related-party concerns. Broadly pointed the right way but with real soft spots.
* **0.00–0.39** Misaligned: pay rewards size or gameable accounting, dominated by short-term cash
  bonuses or repriceable options, little personal capital at risk, sandbagged targets, egregious
  quantum, heavy dilution, or self-dealing and a captured board. The plan pays for things that erode
  per-share value.

**Documentation:**

* The headline metrics that drive bonus and long-term incentive pay, and whether they reward
  per-share value / returns on capital or size / gameable figures — with source (proxy statement /
  remuneration report)
* Skin in the game: insider ownership, whether stakes were bought or granted, holding requirements,
  and recent buying/selling
* Pay structure: cash-vs-equity mix, vesting and performance horizons, hurdle rigour, clawbacks, any
  option repricing
* Magnitude and dilution: total pay versus performance and peers; stock-based comp as a share of the
  count
* Any self-dealing, related-party transactions, outsized perks/parachutes, or board-independence
  concerns

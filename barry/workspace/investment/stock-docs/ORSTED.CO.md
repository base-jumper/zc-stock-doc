# ORSTED.CO — Ørsted A/S

## Business Overview

Ørsted is a Denmark-listed renewable-power developer, owner and operator, best known for offshore wind. It has 10.2 GW of installed offshore capacity, 8.1 GW under construction, and more than 18 GW total installed renewable capacity across offshore wind, onshore wind, solar, storage, bioenergy and energy trading. Management is explicitly refocusing the group around offshore wind opportunities in Europe and selected APAC markets after a difficult US offshore cycle.

The business model is capital-intensive: Ørsted develops projects, earns contracted/merchant power revenue from operating assets, recycles capital through farm-downs and divestments, and must keep funding a large construction pipeline. That makes this less a classic cash generator and more a turnaround / execution-recovery setup after impairments, dividend suspension, asset sales and a large 2025 rights issue.

## Trait Assessment

### Downside Support / Valuation Floor — 0.42 / 0.65

The floor is real but not hard enough for a clean freeroll. Yahoo shows tangible book value around DKK 105.9 per share and P/TBV about 1.4x at a DKK 147.85 share price. I use DKK 120 as a practical floor for the asymmetric-payoff model: above stated tangible book, near the low end of analyst target ranges, and above the 52-week low of about DKK 102.8. That leaves roughly 19% downside to the floor.

The support is weakened by the fact that offshore wind asset marks have been impaired before and construction assets can consume capital before they defend equity value. The DKK 59.4bn 2025 equity issuance strengthened the balance sheet, but it also shows the floor was not self-funding.

Confidence would improve with a full project-by-project NAV bridge, debt maturity ladder, and current farm-down market evidence.

### Fundamental Stability / No Value Trap — 0.48 / 0.70

The operating franchise is stabilising, but the recent financial record is ugly. Revenue fell from DKK 108.2bn in 2022 to DKK 63.6bn in 2025, EPS swung from DKK 19.2 to a deep 2023 loss, then only DKK 1.9 in 2025, and free cash flow stayed negative every year from 2022-2025. The weakness looks partly cyclical/financing-cost/project-specific rather than terminal: Q1 2026 offshore generation rose 27% year on year, EBITDA excluding new partnerships and cancellation fees rose 11% to DKK 9.5bn, and full-year 2026 EBITDA guidance above DKK 28bn was maintained.

The value-trap risk is still live. US offshore execution, high long-dated rates, turbine/supply-chain cost inflation and ongoing capex can keep eroding equity value if new projects fail to earn acceptable returns.

### Mispriced for an Identifiable Reason — 0.55 / 0.60

The market is punishing Ørsted for a very specific reason: the offshore-wind model broke under higher rates, supply-chain inflation, US project execution risk, impairments, asset-sale dependence and a dilutive rights issue. The variant view is that the recapitalised balance sheet, completed/accelerated divestment programme, Europe/APAC refocus, and delivery of the 8.1 GW construction portfolio can restore a more normal EBITDA base.

That is plausible rather than proven. The stock is not screamingly cheap on book value, and analyst mean fair value near DKK 171 implies only moderate upside from DKK 147.85 unless execution surprise is positive.

### Identifiable, Time-Bound Catalyst — 0.62 / 0.70

The catalyst is concrete: 2026-2028 delivery of the construction portfolio and evidence that the post-rights-issue Ørsted can fund itself without another equity raise. Q1 2026 signposts were constructive: first power from Revolution Wind, first turbine installation at Sunrise Wind, monopile installation at Hornsea 3 and Baltica 2, and Greater Changhua 2b/4 on track toward Q3 2026 commissioning. Management maintained 2026 EBITDA guidance above DKK 28bn and gross investment guidance of DKK 50-55bn.

This is within a 2-3 year window, but not low-risk. The same projects are also the main source of execution risk.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Cash cow:** fails the defining free-cash-flow test. FCF was negative in every recent annual period and TTM FCF was about DKK -19.6bn.
- **Wonderful and fair:** fails on earnings quality and returns on capital. Ørsted had two loss years in the Yahoo four-year window, major impairments, and latest ROIC around 3.3%.
- **Rule breakers:** the renewables/offshore-wind market has a large structural runway and Ørsted is a top-tier player, but this is not an emerging asset-light compounder; growth is capital-intensive, state/tender-driven, and currently diluted by project finance stress.

## Overall

This is a middling freeroll / turnaround candidate, not a quality compounder. The bull case is understandable: Ørsted remains a global offshore-wind leader, the energy-transition demand backdrop is real, the balance sheet has been repaired, and 2026 operational progress is tangible. The bear case is equally real: free cash flow is deeply negative, ROIC is below any reasonable cost of capital, US offshore impairments have shown asset values can disappoint, and shareholder dilution was substantial.

The current view is watchlist-worthy but not high-conviction. It needs proof that EBITDA growth converts to funded equity value rather than another capex/dilution cycle.

## Valuation

For the freeroll lens I used an asymmetric-payoff setup:

- Entry price: DKK 147.85.
- Floor: DKK 120, representing a practical support level above tangible book per share and around the low end of external fair-value ranges, but not a hard liquidation/NAV floor.
- Fair value / sell target: DKK 190, roughly a recovery multiple around 19x forward EPS of about DKK 9.8 or about 7x implied 2026 EBITDA guidance at the enterprise level, assuming execution normalises.
- Probability: 55%, reflecting credible but not decisive early 2026 execution progress.
- Window: 2 years.

The model output is a 3.539% annualised expected ROI, with a 7.2% probability-weighted two-year holding-period return. The payoff is not especially asymmetric: about 28.5% upside to DKK 190 versus 18.8% downside to DKK 120, or 1.51:1 up/down. That is why the agent score is only 0.42 even if the stock can work. The setup has a catalyst, but not enough margin of safety to be a clean bargain.

## Thesis Connections

Potential FIT connection: European energy security, grid decarbonisation and offshore wind build-out. Ørsted is one of the few listed pure-ish ways to own that theme at scale, but the investment lesson here is that theme quality does not automatically create equity quality when project economics are exposed to rates, construction risk and dilution.

## Watch

- 2026 EBITDA versus guidance above DKK 28bn, excluding new partnerships and cancellation fees.
- Gross investments versus the DKK 50-55bn guide; any overspend is a thesis warning.
- Commissioning progress at Greater Changhua 2b/4, Hornsea 3, Baltica 2, Revolution Wind and Sunrise Wind.
- Any new impairments from US rates, supply chain, seabed leases or project execution.
- Net interest-bearing debt, FFO/adjusted net debt and whether free cash flow improves as construction milestones are hit.
- Farm-down/divestment pricing and whether external buyers validate book values.

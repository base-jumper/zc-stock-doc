# TOST — Toast, Inc.

## Business Overview

Toast sells a restaurant-specific operating platform: cloud point-of-sale hardware and software, payments, payroll, lending, marketing, reservations and adjacent workflow tools. Payments drives most reported revenue; subscription and fintech gross profit are the cleaner measures of platform economics. At 30 June 2026 Toast served about 180,000 locations, up 22% year on year, and Q2 gross payment volume rose 22% to $60.7b. Its restaurant focus, integrated product suite and growing installed base create cross-sell and data advantages, while payments exposure also ties results to restaurant volumes.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.82 / 0.88

Restaurant software and payments remain structurally under-digitised, and Toast can expand through US SMB share gains, enterprise, international markets and retail. The company added 30,000 net locations in 2025 and entered Australia, its fourth international market. Attractive recurring software and payment economics coexist with intense competition and restaurant cyclicality. The precise addressable-market boundary is not consistently disclosed, so the score relies more on observed penetration and location growth than a promotional TAM estimate.

### Top Dog — 0.80 / 0.83

Toast's 180,000 locations, $60.7b of Q2 GPV and $2.4b ARR make it a scaled restaurant-platform leader, while 22% location growth indicates continued share capture. The restaurant-specialist suite and distribution are meaningful edges, but the market remains fragmented across legacy POS, cloud platforms, payment providers and point solutions. Confidence would improve with an independently measured US restaurant-POS share series.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.73 / 0.78

The moat is a combination of restaurant-specific workflow depth, integrated payments, switching costs, distribution scale, partner integrations and data from a large installed base. Rising gross margin—18.7% in 2022 to 25.9% in 2025—and sustained location growth indicate improving scale economics. It is a real but contestable moat: well-funded payments and software rivals can subsidise hardware or compete by segment. Confidence would improve with disclosed cohort retention and module-attach trends.

### Durable Growth — 0.86 / 0.93

Toast is a maturing growth platform, so revenue, locations, gross profit and FCF are the appropriate metrics. Revenue rose from $2.73 billion in 2022 to $6.15 billion in 2025 (31% CAGR), though annual growth decelerated from 42% to 28% to 24%. Q2 2026 revenue rose 23% to $1.91b, recurring subscription and fintech gross profit rose 28%, locations rose 22%, and operating income nearly doubled to $152m. Full-year recurring-gross-profit and adjusted-EBITDA guidance increased. Growth is decelerating, but remains strong and increasingly profitable.

### Organic Growth — 0.88 / 0.91

Growth is overwhelmingly built rather than bought. Yahoo statement data show only $55 million of acquisition spending across 2022–2025, unchanged goodwill of roughly $113 million since 2023, and falling goodwill/assets. Against a 31% revenue CAGR, acquisitions are immaterial. The main per-share issue is equity issuance, not purchased revenue: diluted shares rose about 19% from 2022 to 2025.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.70 / 0.78

CEO Aman Narang is a co-founder, supporting long-term product and customer orientation. Management navigated the post-pandemic reset into GAAP profitability and substantial FCF while sustaining more than 20% location growth. The board separates chair and CEO roles and includes experienced software executives. Confidence would improve with a longer public-company capital-allocation record under Narang as CEO.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.60 / 0.90

Founder ownership and multi-year equity awards provide alignment, but historical dilution was material: diluted shares rose from about 512 million in 2022 to 607 million in 2025, while 2025 stock-based compensation was $248 million. The balance improved in 2026: Toast repurchased 19m shares for $486m in the first half, while Q2 diluted weighted-average shares fell to 590m from 605m a year earlier. The dual-class structure still concentrates voting power with pre-IPO holders; the durable test is net share-count control over several years.

### Customer Devotion — 0.70 / 0.65

Rapid location additions, broad product adoption and continued expansion into enterprise accounts support strong product-market fit. However, Toast does not provide a clean net-revenue-retention, logo-churn or independently audited satisfaction series, so preference cannot be cleanly separated from switching costs. Confidence is irreducible for now; it would rise if Toast disclosed retention and cohort expansion or if a deep, representative third-party restaurant-user survey became available.

Rule Breakers wins because growth, market position and organic execution are established while economics are inflecting. Wonderful-and-fair and cash-cow lose on the short profitability record; Freeroll lacks a hard valuation floor and time-bound re-rating catalyst.

## Overall

Toast is a high-quality emerging restaurant platform with visible share gains, widening margins and a decisive transition to GAAP profitability. Q2 reinforced the flywheel through record net location additions, 28% recurring-gross-profit growth and higher full-year guidance. The deductions are straightforward: competitive intensity is high, customer-retention disclosure is thin, first-half FCF fell year over year as inventory and lending consumed cash, and dual-class governance still weakens per-share alignment despite meaningful buybacks.

## Valuation

At $34.48, the stock trades around 31× a normalised current FCF estimate of $1.10 per diluted share. The anchor triangulates $608m FY2025 FCF, $245m first-half 2026 FCF, roughly 580–590m diluted shares and the improving operating-profit run-rate. A five-year exit-multiple model assumes aggregate FCF growth fading from 22% to 12%, flat net shares as buybacks offset SBC, no dividend and a 24× terminal P/FCF. The script produces a 10.49% annualized ROI: 16.55% business growth is partly offset by about 5.20% annual multiple compression. TAM-capture remains omitted because a consistent restaurant-platform market document and mature role model are unavailable; weighted-average therefore carries only exit-multiple. Agent score remains 0.64: execution and guidance improved, but the entry multiple is fuller and recent FCF lagged accounting profit.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a vertical-software/platform thesis: industry-specific workflow ownership plus embedded payments can deepen customer economics and expand ARPU as locations adopt more modules.

## Watch

- Q3 2026 recurring-gross-profit growth against 22-24% guidance and adjusted EBITDA of $210m-$220m.
- Net dilution after buybacks; stock-based compensation as a percentage of gross profit.
- Any disclosed retention, cohort expansion or module-attach metrics.
- Enterprise, international and retail location contribution versus core US restaurants.
- Evidence that AI features improve retention, attach or restaurant economics rather than merely adding cost.

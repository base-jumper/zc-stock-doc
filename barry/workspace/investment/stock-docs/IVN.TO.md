# IVN.TO — Ivanhoe Mines Ltd.

## Business Overview

Ivanhoe develops and operates large, high-grade mines in Southern Africa. Its core economic asset is a 39.6% indirect interest in the Kamoa-Kakula copper complex in the DRC, operated with Zijin Mining; the DRC government owns 20%. Ivanhoe also owns 62% of the producing Kipushi zinc mine in the DRC, 64% of the ramping Platreef polymetallic mine in South Africa, and 100% of the exploration-stage Western Forelands copper licences adjacent to Kamoa-Kakula.

Kamoa-Kakula generated $880 million of Q2 2026 revenue and $385 million of EBITDA on a 100% basis, while Kipushi generated $146 million and $51 million. Ivanhoe reported $179 million of adjusted EBITDA and $46 million of profit. The investment case is therefore a leveraged operational-ramp thesis: recover Kamoa-Kakula from its 2025 seismic and water-ingress disruption, expand toward more than 500,000 tonnes of annual copper from 2028, ramp Platreef, and convert Western Forelands discoveries into a second copper district.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.78 / 0.92

Copper has a durable electrification, grid and data-centre demand tailwind while new supply faces declining grades, multibillion-dollar capital requirements and roughly 17-year discovery-to-production lead times. The stored primary-copper market work projects mine-origin volume from 23.5 Mt in 2026 to 25.6 Mt in 2036 and market value from $300 billion to $410 billion. This is attractive, but not winner-take-most: copper is fungible, highly cyclical and produced by a fragmented group of commercial and state-backed miners. Ivanhoe also has zinc and platinum-group-metal exposure, which diversifies the project pipeline but adds commodity cycles rather than recurring economics.

### Top Dog — 0.66 / 0.88

Ivanhoe is not a top-five global copper producer, and its current attributable share of Kamoa-Kakula production is below 1% of world mine supply. The asset quality is stronger than the company ranking: the March 2026 independent report describes Kamoa-Kakula as a high-grade, multi-decade complex with 466 Mt of reserves at 2.82% copper and a plan for more than 500,000 tonnes a year from 2028. Kipushi is guided to become a top-three zinc mine in 2026, while Platreef is designed as a very large, low-cost polymetallic mine. The gap between world-class assets and Ivanhoe's minority project interests caps the score.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.68 / 0.80

The moat is geological and infrastructural rather than customer-based: unusually large, high-grade orebodies; sunk concentrator, smelter, rail and power infrastructure; and a deep project-development organization. Kamoa-Kakula's on-site smelter cut logistics and treatment costs and produced valuable sulphuric acid, while the reserve supports decades of operation. Those advantages are expensive to copy, but Ivanhoe remains a commodity price taker and is exposed to DRC power, labour, geotechnical and political risks. Confidence would rise after the Q1 2027 updated life-of-mine plan supplies revised geotechnical, hydrological and extraction assumptions; this event is in Watch.

### Durable Growth — 0.62 / 0.92

The long-run operating build has been strong, but recent growth is not durable enough for a high-band score. Kamoa-Kakula produced 388,841 tonnes in 2025 after the May seismic event disrupted Kakula, then 64,328 tonnes in Q2 2026; tightened 2026 guidance is 290,000–310,000 tonnes before a planned recovery to 380,000–420,000 tonnes in 2027 and more than 500,000 tonnes from 2028. Kipushi achieved record Q2 zinc production, and Platreef is targeting commercial production in Q4 2026. These are real growth engines, but the large Kamoa guidance reset and continuing development dependence show that the path is lumpy and execution-sensitive.

### Organic Growth — 0.88 / 0.88

Growth is overwhelmingly built through discovery, mine development and brownfield expansion. Goodwill was only 0.4% of assets at year-end 2025, and the major value drivers—Kamoa phases, the smelter, Kipushi restart, Platreef and Western Forelands drilling—are internally developed rather than a serial-acquisition roll-up. Diluted shares rose about 12.4% from 2022 to 2025, but that funded project construction rather than purchased revenue. The conclusion is high-quality organic growth with meaningful financing dilution.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.80 / 0.90

Founder and Executive Co-Chairman Robert Friedland has built the portfolio over three decades and owns 11.46% of voting shares. CEO Marna Cloete joined in 2006 and has led multiple strategic financings and partnerships. CITIC owns 21.17%, Zijin owns 12.16% and co-operates Kamoa-Kakula, and QIA supplied a $500 million strategic investment in 2025. Delivery of three concentrator phases and the smelter supports execution credibility; the 2025 Kakula failure and subsequent reserve reduction are the material counterevidence.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.72 / 0.92

The 2026 proxy shows substantial founder ownership, executive ownership requirements, and a 2025 long-term incentive mix of 50% performance units, 30% restricted units and 20% options. PSUs vest after three years against relative TSR and strategic goals. Alignment is diluted by one-year relative TSR driving 80% of option and RSU grant sizing, Board discretion, annual vesting of some awards, and the share issuance needed to fund development. Overall, insiders win mainly through share value, but the structure still rewards market performance and project scale more than per-share return on capital.

### Customer Devotion — 0.25 / 0.75

Copper, zinc and concentrate are fungible commodities sold through offtake arrangements. Buyers may value reliable quality and logistics, but there is no evidence of voluntary loyalty, expansion or advocacy of the kind this trait requires; any stickiness comes from contracts and supply qualification, not devotion. This is largely irreducible for now. Renewal and pricing terms in future offtake agreements would add evidence, but would not change the commodity character.

Rule Breakers wins because Ivanhoe combines a strong critical-minerals setup, tier-one assets, organic expansion and founder-backed execution. Freeroll has excellent near-term catalysts but loses on downside support: at C$11.53 the stock trades around 1.96 times book and 2.8 times tangible book, has net debt and pays no dividend, so no hard floor sits close to the quote. Wonderful and Fair loses on low consolidated returns and weak earnings-to-cash conversion during construction; Cash Cow fails outright on negative free cash flow and no capital return.

## Overall

Ivanhoe owns rare assets with a credible path to much higher production, and the current quote reflects substantial damage from Kakula's 2025 disruption. The company nevertheless remains a capital-intensive, partly financed project developer in volatile jurisdictions. The central question is not resource quality; it is whether revised mine design, dewatering, power and development can restore predictable production without another round of schedule slippage or dilution.

## Valuation

The canonical Rule Breakers valuation blends two imperfect lenses, weighted 60% to the better-specified TAM case and 40% to an earnings ramp. The TAM component uses the market document's conservative 0.8% 2036 primary-copper capture, equal to $3.28 billion of attributable revenue. It applies a 35% mature EBITDA margin, below Kamoa-Kakula's 44% Q2 margin and close to FCX's 2025 34.8% margin; a 10x EV/EBITDA exit multiple versus FCX's current 12.4x; 2% annual dilution; and $500 million of terminal net debt after construction-period deleveraging. The C$11.53 listing price is converted through the one-for-one US OTC share at US$8.28 because the market document is in USD.

The exit-multiple component uses normalized current earnings of C$0.24 per share, between 2025's C$0.26-equivalent EPS and the softer 2026 run-rate, then fades aggregate earnings growth from 35% during the Kamoa/Platreef recovery to 5% in year ten. A 15x terminal P/E and 2% annual dilution are deliberately below the current implied 48x P/E. This lens captures zinc and platinum-group metals omitted by the copper TAM, but carries lower confidence because today’s earnings are distorted by equity-accounted Kamoa profits and construction spending.

The TAM component produces a **-2.7% annualized ROI**: terminal enterprise value of $11.48 billion becomes $10.98 billion of equity and US$6.31 per diluted share. The earnings component produces **+1.4%**, as 16.2% annual business growth and a 2.0% dilution drag are mostly consumed by multiple compression. The 60/40 blend is **-1.0% annualized**. This is not a bankruptcy call; it says the current price already capitalizes much of the base-case operating recovery and requires Western Forelands, higher metal prices, stronger margins, or less dilution to deliver an attractive return. Those unmodeled options keep the agent score at 0.40 rather than at the mechanistic value floor.

## Thesis Connections

Ivanhoe is a direct fit with the electrification and constrained-copper-supply thesis. Its high-grade resource and integrated smelter offer operating leverage to sustained copper scarcity, while Western Forelands provides discovery upside. That connection is weakened by Ivanhoe's minority ownership of Kamoa-Kakula and by the capital, power and sovereign risks required to turn resources into attributable cash flow.

## Watch

- September 2026 Western Forelands resource update: scale, grade, classification and any development concept.
- Q3 2026 Kamoa-Kakula solar ramp to 60 MW, Phase 1/2 recoveries toward 90%–95%, and mine development toward 700,000 tonnes per month.
- H2 2026 destocking of roughly 40,000 tonnes of copper and 44,000 tonnes of zinc; confirm conversion to cash rather than a persistent logistics bottleneck.
- Platreef commercial production in Q4 2026 and Phase 2 construction against the Q4 2027 target.
- Q1 2027 Kamoa-Kakula life-of-mine plan: revised geotechnical/hydrological model, extraction rates, capex and the evidence required to firm up sustainable advantage.
- Pro-rata net debt/EBITDA, Kamoa's 2026–27 debt maturities, and any further equity issuance.
- Copper and zinc prices versus Kamoa C1 guidance of $2.60–$3.00/lb and Kipushi guidance of $0.85–$0.95/lb.

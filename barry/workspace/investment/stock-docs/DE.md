# DE — Deere & Company

## Business Overview

Deere & Company is the world's largest agricultural-equipment manufacturer and a leading construction/forestry equipment maker. It sells tractors, combines, sprayers, seeding/crop-care equipment and construction machinery (excavators, dozers, loaders) plus precision-ag technology and a large captive customer-finance arm. FY2025 (ended Nov 2, 2025) net sales and revenue were US$44.7 billion, down 12% from the FY2024 US$50.5 billion peak, with net income of US$5.03 billion (US$18.50 diluted EPS) versus US$7.1 billion a year earlier. The production and precision agriculture segment (large ag) is the biggest profit engine but is in a cyclical downturn; construction & forestry and small ag/turf are growing.

Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended May 3, 2026, reported May 21): net income US$1.77 billion / US$6.55 per share, total revenue US$13.37 billion (+5% YoY). Construction & forestry sales jumped 29% to US$3.79 billion with operating profit +48%; small ag & turf revenue +16%; production & precision ag revenue fell 14% to US$4.5 billion with operating profit -39%. Full-year FY2026 net income guidance was held at US$4.5–5.0 billion, with construction/forestry sales growth raised to ~20%, small ag +15%, and large ag industry volumes guided down 15–20% for the year. USDA aggregate US farm income is ~US$153 billion in 2026, roughly flat with 2025 and far below the US$182 billion 2022 peak.

The stock trades at about US$600 (2026-08-18), ~34× trailing EPS but ~27× forward EPS, near the middle of its 52-week range (US$433–674).

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.45 / confidence 0.85

Deere has been profitable every fiscal year for at least a decade — no losses in the last 10 years through the 2016–17 ag recession. But the earnings path is violently cyclical, not predictable: SEC-EDGAR data shows net income of US$1.5B (FY2016), US$2.2B (2017), US$2.4B (2018), US$3.3B (2019), US$2.8B (2020), US$6.0B (2021), US$7.1B (2022), US$10.2B (2023), US$7.1B (2024), US$5.0B (2025). A shareholder could not have forecast this path with normal compounder precision; the year-over-year dispersion is very high (YoY swings of +43%, -30%, -29%).

Cash conversion is mediocre over the last four years: cumulative FCF of ~US$12.7B versus cumulative net income of ~US$29.6B, or about 0.43×. This is largely because of heavy working capital in receivables (the finance arm) and inventory swings, not earnings fabrication. Accounting is otherwise clean — no serial one-offs or restatements; the company reports both GAAP and non-GAAP, with modest unusual items.

The score sits at 0.45: real, long, unbroken profitability, but cyclicality is the dominant feature and cash conversion lags reported earnings.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.60 / confidence 0.82

The current weakness is a cyclical downturn, not secular decline. Revenue fell from a US$60.2B peak (FY2023) to US$44.7B (FY2025), and EPS fell from US$34.63 to US$18.50, but the drivers are cyclical: farm incomes down from the 2022 peak, high dealer inventory destocking, and high interest rates pressuring equipment financing. Long-run demand drivers — global food production, farm mechanisation, precision agriculture adoption, infrastructure/construction spending — remain intact, and Deere continues to take share in construction & forestry (+29% sales in Q2 FY2026).

The structural threats are real but not imminent: used-equipment oversupply, the shift to precision/autonomous farming could change the equipment sales model, and competitive pressure in precision ag from CNH/AGCO and new entrants. None of these yet looks like a melting-ice-cube trajectory. Verdict: cyclical trough with a durable franchise, so the floor should hold.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.60 / confidence 0.80

Deere has a genuine moat: the largest installed base and dealer network in North American ag equipment, a premium brand (the green-and-yellow dealer franchise is a durable distribution advantage), strong parts/aftermarket and precision-ag integration, and a broad product line. Gross margin has held up well through the downturn (36.5% FY2025 vs 38.6% FY2024 and 37.0% FY2023), and Deere has historically demonstrated pricing power — it raised prices through the 2021–2023 boom, and price realization has remained positive even as volumes fell. The company is the clear #1 in North American large ag equipment and construction.

The moat is real but not impenetrable: CNH Industrial, AGCO and Kubota compete, and the equipment market is capital-goods cyclical where end-customers (farmers, builders) are price-sensitive in downturns. The 0.60 reflects a strong brand/scale/dealer moat with demonstrated pricing power, tempered by end-market cyclicality and real competition.

### Returns on Capital — 0.55 / confidence 0.80

Yahoo-derived ROIC has averaged about 11% over FY2022–FY2025 (14.2% in FY2023, 11.0% in FY2024, 8.4% in FY2025), with ROE around 19–47% (flattered by leverage). This is decent but not elite: the machinery business earns good but cyclical returns, and the finance arm earns modest returns on a huge receivable base, dragging the blended ROIC. ROCE was 12.8–23.3% over the window. The cost of capital is roughly 9–10%, so the spread is positive but modest in the downturn.

The trend is falling from the FY2023 peak, as expected in a down-cycle. Incremental ROIC is negative over the window because capital employed has stayed elevated while earnings fell. This is a genuine cyclical capital-intensive business — good returns at the peak, mediocre at the trough — which caps the score at 0.55.

### Capital Allocation — 0.60 / confidence 0.78

Deere's allocation is disciplined: it returns substantial cash via dividends (US$1.72B in FY2025, payout ~34%) and buybacks (US$1.14B FY2025, US$4.0B FY2024, US$7.2B FY2023), keeps M&A small and bolt-on (US$101M acquisitions in FY2025, cumulative ~US$681M over 4 years — mostly small tuck-ins like Tenna LLC for US$439M in Feb 2026), and has reduced the share count from ~336M (2015) to ~270M (2025) — roughly 20% shrinkage over a decade. It maintains a large dividend and repurchases through cycles.

The main caveat: buybacks have been large even at peak-cycle earnings (FY2023's US$7.2B repurchase near the earnings peak), which is mildly pro-cyclical. The finance arm absorbs a lot of capital. On balance this is a solid, shareholder-friendly allocator with modest pro-cyclical buyback tendencies — 0.60.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.55 / confidence 0.78

Deere's headline leverage looks high: total debt US$64.3B (FY2025) with US$9.7B cash, net debt/EBITDA ~4.8×, EBIT interest coverage ~3.0×, near-term debt cover ~0.6×. But this is almost entirely the captive finance arm — ~US$57B of receivables are funded by the finance arm's debt, which is matched against earning assets, not a solvency risk. The manufacturing operations carry much less debt.

Still, the finance arm is a genuine cyclical credit exposure: if farmer defaults spike in a downturn, Deere's finance book takes losses. The interest coverage of ~3× is thinner than an industrial should carry, and near-term debt cover below 1× means refinancing dependence. No disqualifier — solvency is not threatened, and Deere retains investment-grade access to credit markets (A1/A+ rating area) — but the leverage is not conservative for a cyclical. 0.55.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.85 / confidence 0.75

Deere is an institutional franchise: 180-year-old brand, dealer network, engineering depth, scale. CEO John May (since 2019) is competent but the business would clearly survive his departure; the September 2025 leadership reshuffle (Deanna Kovar to Production & Precision Ag, Justin Rose to Small Ag & Turf, Cory Reed to Lifecycle Solutions) shows an internal bench. No founder control, no super-voting, no single-person dependence. 0.85.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.70 / confidence 0.75

Management has meaningful skin in the game via long-tenure executives and equity grants; insider ownership is low (~0.17%) but executives hold meaningful direct stakes (CEO ~118K shares after a January 2026 sale of 41K shares). Pay is tied to operating performance (ROA, market share, safety) with long-term incentives. The January 2026 CEO sale of ~US$20.8M is routine diversification, not a red flag. Not deeply audited this run — moderate confidence.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.45 / confidence 0.75

Deere is a mature, cyclical machinery company in a slow-growth end market. Reinvestment opportunities exist — precision agriculture, automation/autonomy (the 2026 autonomous tractor rollout), construction share gains — but the core equipment market is largely replacement-driven and cyclical. There is no multi-year high-return runway of the compounder type; this is a business that must return most of its cash. 0.45.

### Right Place and Time — 0.35 / confidence 0.75

Agricultural machinery is a mature, cyclical industry, currently in a down-cycle with farm incomes ~16% below the 2022 peak and large-ag volumes guided down 15–20% in FY2026. Precision-ag and automation are genuine growth pockets, but they are not yet large enough to change the industry's overall cyclical economics. Not a "right place, right time" emerging wave. 0.35.

### Top Dog — 0.85 / confidence 0.85

Deere is the clear #1 in North American large agricultural equipment (roughly 50%+ share in large tractors/combines), the global leader in agricultural machinery by revenue, and a top-3 construction equipment player. Its lead is stable: the dealer network, brand and precision-ag ecosystem are hard to replicate. 0.85.

### Sustainable Advantage — 0.65 / confidence 0.75

The moat sources are scale, brand, dealer network, parts ecosystem and precision-ag data/software integration. These are durable but not unassailable: the equipment industry is capital-intensive, cyclical, and faces price competition from CNH/AGCO/Kubota and technology competition in precision ag. The advantage is stable-to-widening in construction, stable in ag. 0.65.

### Durable Growth — 0.30 / confidence 0.80

Revenue is declining: -11.6% FY2025, -15.6% FY2024 after +16.5% FY2023. The 4-year revenue CAGR is about -4.5%. Growth is currently negative and the industry is in contraction; even the recovery is expected to be modest. This is a cyclical, not a growth company. 0.30.

### Organic Growth — 0.85 / confidence 0.80

Growth is overwhelmingly organic. Deere is not a roll-up; acquisitions are tiny bolt-ons (cumulative ~US$0.7B over 4 years vs ~US$200B revenue), goodwill is only ~4% of assets and flat, and the share count has shrunk ~11% in 4 years. 0.85.

### Management Backing — 0.65 / confidence 0.65

John May has run Deere since 2019 through a boom-bust cycle, communicating clearly through the downturn ("our customers face ongoing challenges... disciplined operations and resilience"). The leadership team is deep and experienced. Not founder-led; a solid professional management team. Confidence moderate because this run did not deep-dive management quality beyond public statements and results. 0.65.

### Customer Devotion — 0.75 / confidence 0.70

Deere enjoys strong customer loyalty: farmers are deeply attached to the green-and-yellow brand, the dealer network, and the precision-ag ecosystem (data, software, integration). Equipment resale values for Deere hold up better than competitors'. But part of this is switching-cost lock-in (dealers, implements, data) rather than pure preference — the trait explicitly separates devotion from captivity. The strong brand and dealer relationships support a 0.75; it is not maxed because some stickiness is ecosystem lock-in.

### Downside Support / Valuation Floor — 0.45 / confidence 0.70

The stock at ~US$600 has real but soft floors: price/book ~5.9× (not an asset floor), net cash negative (-US$147/share net of finance debt), dividend yield only 1.1% with FCF cover ~1.9×. The genuine floor is the cyclical-earnings normalisation: at a trough multiple (~25× trough EPS of ~US$18) the stock has downside to roughly US$450-500, and at mid-cycle earnings (~US$25) with a 20× multiple it's ~US$500. The 52-week low is US$433. So there is maybe 15–25% downside to a defensible trough valuation, but no hard asset or income backstop close to the price. 0.45.

### Mispricing — 0.45 / confidence 0.65

The market is pricing Deere at ~34× trailing EPS and ~27× forward — not obviously cheap. The differentiated view would be that the market is extrapolating the ag downturn (large-ag volumes -15–20%) as permanent, while Deere's diversification (construction +29%, small ag +16%) and the eventual ag recovery support mid-cycle earnings of US$25–30. But this is a modest variant perception; the stock is not deeply cheap and consensus is already constructive (analyst mean target ~US$647, ~8% above price). 0.45.

### Catalyst — 0.55 / confidence 0.65

The identifiable, time-bound catalyst is the US agricultural cycle recovery: FY2027–FY2028 large-ag volumes should normalise from the current 15–20% decline as farm incomes stabilise and dealer inventories clear. Q3 FY2026 earnings (Aug 20, 2026) is the near-term data point. Construction & forestry growth (+20% guided) provides a partial offset in the meantime. The window is 1–3 years, reasonably likely but not certain (farm income could stay weak, tariffs/rates could bite). 0.55.

## Overall

Deere is a high-quality cyclical franchise: the dominant North American ag-equipment maker with a real dealer/brand/scale moat, disciplined capital allocation, strong share-count discipline and a growing construction business that partially offsets the ag downturn. The disqualifier screen is clear: audited accounts are usable, solvency is not threatened, no hidden-liability red flags, no value-leaking control, no business-model kill-shot.

Scored on the quality side, the mechanical leaderboard crowns **cash-cow** (normalized score ~0.56, confidence ~0.80, S×C 0.45) with **wonderful-and-fair** a genuine near-tie (0.56 / 0.79, S×C 0.44). This is a dual-fit: Deere is a durable, cash-returning franchise (cash-cow) that also retains a meaningful — if maturing — reinvestment runway in precision ag, autonomy and construction (wonderful-and-fair). Freeroll and rule-breakers lose decisively.

But the detractors are serious. Earnings are deeply cyclical and currently in a trough; headline leverage is high (mostly captive finance); ROIC is mediocre in the downturn; and — the decisive issue — at ~US$600 the stock is not cheap. The exit-multiple model returns a **negative expected annualized ROI** even on optimistic inputs, because the equity is priced at ~27× forward earnings near the cyclical trough, above mid-cycle fair value. This is a case where business quality is real but the price already embeds a full recovery. Current stance: **watch** — a good company, not a good entry at this price; interest builds toward the ag-cycle bottom and a lower entry multiple.

## Valuation

The cash-cow/wonderful-and-fair exit-multiple lens is the mechanical fit. I use **Earnings** metric because the market prices Deere on P/E and the FCF line is distorted by the captive finance arm's working capital (trailing FCF of ~US$3.8B reflects finance receivables, not owner earnings).

**Anchor:** I use a current normalised EPS of **US$19–20** — between the FY2025 actual (US$18.50), the FY2026 guidance range (~US$17.5 mid-point) and forward consensus (~US$22.5); this is a trough-anchored "today" figure. **Growth** of 5% (through-cycle earnings recovery, cash-cow discipline — extrapolate the franchise, don't underwrite a new runway). **Exit multiple** of 18× (a reasonable through-cycle premium P/E, below the ~30× entry). **Dilution** of -1%/yr (buyback-driven share-count shrinkage). **Dividend yield** 1.06%.

With an US$19 fundamental, the model returns **-3.8% annualized** over five years (0.86× total): +5% business growth and +1.4% income, offset by -10.6%/yr from multiple compression (30→18×) as high trough-priced earnings normalise. With the more optimistic US$20 anchor and 18× exit, ROI is **-2.8%**. Even a generous recovery case (US$19, 6% growth, 20× exit) gives **approximately -0.9%**.

The verdict is unambiguous: **at ~US$600, Deere offers no margin of safety and a negative expected return** through the disciplined exit-multiple lens, because the price already capitalises a strong cyclical recovery. Business quality is 0.56/0.80 dual-fit, but value is absent. I set `overall.agent_score` to **0.32** — a high-quality franchise badly priced at today's level, worth owning only at a materially lower entry multiple (roughly 17–20× mid-cycle earnings, i.e. ~US$430–500).

## Thesis Connections

Deere connects to the global food-security and farm-mechanisation theme: as the world's largest ag-equipment maker, it captures value from rising global food demand, precision agriculture and labour-saving automation. It also links to US infrastructure/construction via its growing construction & forestry business (data-centre, road and residential construction demand). The connection is cyclical: Deere's revenue is tied to farm incomes and construction spending, not to a secular growth wave. It is a quality operator in a mature industry, captured at the right point in the cycle.

- **Valuation:** reconsider if the price approaches the ~US$470–500 floor zone (trough-to-mid-cycle support), or if Q3/Q4 guidance signals a durable ag recovery.
- **Interest rates:** lower rates would reduce farmer financing costs and support equipment demand; higher rates delay the recovery.

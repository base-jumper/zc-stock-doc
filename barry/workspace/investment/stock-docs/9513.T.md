# 9513.T — Electric Power Development Co., Ltd. (J-POWER)

## Business Overview

J-POWER is a Japanese electric-power company with domestic generation, transmission/transformation assets, overseas generation, and power-related businesses including Australian coal interests. FY2025 revenue was ¥1.182tn, down 10.2%; operating profit was ¥101.0bn, down 27.0%; ordinary profit was ¥158.5bn, up 13.2% largely from equity-method gains tied to U.S. thermal-power interest transfers; profit attributable to owners fell 36.7% to ¥58.5bn after impairments and Ohma nuclear project fixed-asset retirement losses.

The business is asset-heavy and transition-heavy. Domestic power generation sold 66.7TWh in FY2025: renewable/hydro output was hurt by lower water supply, thermal volume rose as load factors improved, and the group is explicitly shifting the portfolio under J-POWER BLUE MISSION 2050 and its FY2024–FY2026 medium-term plan. That means the current cash stream is not a simple low-capex utility annuity: old coal assets, renewable buildout, U.S. solar, hydrogen/ammonia/CCS plans, and Ohma nuclear all matter.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — Score 0.55, Confidence 0.70

J-POWER has remained profitable across the four Yahoo-covered fiscal years, but earnings are lumpy. Net income moved from ¥113.7bn in FY2023 to ¥77.8bn, ¥92.5bn, then ¥58.5bn in FY2026; the latest year included impairment losses on renewable assets in Australia and Takasago Thermal Power Plant plus Ohma nuclear fixed-asset retirement losses. Cash conversion looks strong on Yahoo's available fields — cumulative cash conversion of 2.58x net income over FY2023–FY2026 — but this is partly because Yahoo does not map capex cleanly for this issuer.

Interpretation: earning power is real, but not clean enough to call high-quality. Fuel prices, electricity prices, hydro conditions, equity-method items, coal prices, impairments, and project retirement losses all move reported profit. Confidence would improve with a 10-year filing pull and a clearer split between recurring profit and one-offs.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — Score 0.42, Confidence 0.65

Yahoo's trait script reports operating cash flow of ¥224.3bn in FY2026 and a headline “FCF” margin of 19%, but it also flags missing capex data. The primary FY2025 cash-flow statement shows cash from operating activities of ¥224.3bn and cash used in investing activities of ¥193.2bn, mainly higher fixed-asset acquisition spending. That leaves much less genuinely distributable cash after the capital program than the headline Yahoo FCF field suggests.

Interpretation: J-POWER is cash-generative before capex, and the dividend is covered on current cash flow, but this is not a classic capital-light cash cow. The score is held down because the business needs ongoing heavy investment for asset replacement, energy transition, and growth projects. Confidence would improve with maintenance-vs-growth capex disclosure and a longer capex cycle view.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — Score 0.50, Confidence 0.65

Demand for electricity is durable, and J-POWER's mix of generation and transmission assets has strategic value. The latest FY2027 forecast also expects operating revenue to rise 16.7%, operating profit to rise 23.8%, and profit attributable to owners to rise 38.4% to ¥81.0bn.

The offset: the legacy asset base is under transition pressure. Revenue is 35.8% below the FY2023 peak in the Yahoo-covered period; operating margin fell from 10.5% in FY2025 to 8.5% in FY2026; management is phasing out inefficient coal-fired power plants and pursuing lower-carbon replacement technologies. This looks more like a durable but transforming utility than a melting ice cube, but the transformation risk is material.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — Score 0.45, Confidence 0.55

The moat is asset location, scale, grid/generation infrastructure, and the necessity of power supply, not brand-like pricing power. J-POWER can benefit from market prices and capacity mechanisms, but results also swing with fuel costs, coal prices, hydro water supply, wholesale electricity, and regulatory/energy-policy settings. FY2026 revenue declined despite thermal load-factor improvement, while ordinary profit relied meaningfully on equity-method gains.

Interpretation: there is a real infrastructure moat, but not enough demonstrated discretionary pricing power to score this highly. Confidence is moderate-low without contract/regulatory return details by asset.

### Capital Allocation — Score 0.52, Confidence 0.65

Management targets a total payout ratio of about 30%. FY2026 dividends were ¥100/share and FY2027 guidance is ¥105/share; Yahoo/company data show FY2026 dividends of about ¥17.8–18.1bn and buybacks of about ¥20.5bn. Treasury stock also rose sharply to 7.0m shares at FY2026 year-end from 0.17m a year earlier, so the buyback was real rather than only offsetting dilution.

The problem is capital intensity and project risk. Investing cash outflow rose to ¥193.2bn in FY2026, and the company recorded impairments/retirement losses tied to renewable assets, Takasago, and Ohma. The return-of-cash policy is sensible, but per-share value creation is not yet compelling given low ROIC and heavy transition spend.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — Score 0.25, Confidence 0.75

This is the weakest cash-cow trait. ROIC was 4.6%, 3.5%, 3.9%, and 2.9% from FY2023–FY2026, with average ROIC of 3.7%; ROE also fell to roughly 4–5% in FY2026. For a utility some absolute-return tolerance is appropriate, but these figures are still low relative to the capital employed and likely cost of equity.

Interpretation: J-POWER owns important assets, but the accounting return on those assets is not attractive. The stock's cheapness partly reflects that reality.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — Score 0.35, Confidence 0.75

Debt is high even for infrastructure. FY2026 total debt was about ¥1.884tn against cash/short-term investments of ¥397bn, for net debt of roughly ¥1.485tn. Net debt/EBITDA was 5.83x in FY2026, having ranged around 5.05x–6.11x over the four-year period. EBIT interest coverage was acceptable at 6.4x, and current/quick ratios were fine, but the leverage load would constrain flexibility if cash flows weaken.

Interpretation: not an imminent solvency problem on the evidence available, but not conservatively financed. Maturity ladder, fixed/floating split, credit facilities, pension obligations, and project-level debt need primary-source follow-up.

### Key-Person Risk — Score 0.85, Confidence 0.55

This is a large, institutional utility-style group rather than a founder-led business. The thesis rests on assets, regulation, operations, financing, and energy policy rather than one irreplaceable executive. Confidence is only moderate because this pass did not inspect the latest governance/proxy materials in depth.

### Incentive Alignment — Score 0.50, Confidence 0.40

Insiders hold about 9.4% according to Yahoo, which is a useful starting point, but this pass did not verify executive pay metrics, holding requirements, or whether compensation rewards per-share value and return on capital rather than scale. Score is neutral until the remuneration report is reviewed.

### Why the other strategies lost

Rule-breaker is a poor fit: J-POWER is mature, asset-heavy, and not a high-growth disruptor. Wonderful-and-fair is also weak because returns on capital are too low and the reinvestment runway is uncertain rather than clearly high-return. Freeroll has some ingredients — 0.45x book value, 3.05% yield, and a buyback/dividend policy — but the catalyst is not strong or time-bound enough on this pass to make it the primary frame.

## Overall

J-POWER is cheap and strategically important, but it is not an especially attractive cash cow. The stock trades at about 11.1x trailing earnings, 10.0x forward earnings, 0.45x book value, and a 3.05% dividend yield at ¥3,612/share. That looks optically cheap, and the dividend/buyback posture is shareholder-friendlier than many capital-intensive utilities.

The quality problem is that cheapness is matched by low returns on capital, high leverage, and heavy transition capex. This is probably a watchlist income/value idea rather than a high-conviction buy: interesting below book, but the business needs either better visible returns on transition spend or a clearer catalyst to deserve a higher score.

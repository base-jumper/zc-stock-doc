# EQT.ST — EQT AB

## Business Overview

EQT is a global private-markets manager. It earns recurring management fees on fee-generating assets under management (FAUM), plus more volatile carried interest and investment income when fund performance is realised. At 30 June 2026 it managed €291bn of total AUM and €155bn of FAUM across private capital, infrastructure, real estate and newer evergreen strategies. Scale, investment performance, institutional relationships and a global fundraising platform are the economic engine; fund exits recycle capital to clients and support subsequent vintages.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.58 / 0.82 confidence

Adjusted revenue rose from €2.36bn in 2024 to €2.73bn in 2025, with a 60% adjusted EBITDA margin, but IFRS net income slipped to €728m from €776m because carried interest, investment marks, acquisition amortisation and adjustments make reported profit lumpy. H1 2026 adjusted revenue rose 5% and adjusted net income was broadly stable at €691m. Fee-related earnings are recurring, but cash conversion is uneven: 2025 fee-related operating cash flow was €1.07bn while consolidated FCF was €395m after working-capital and investment effects. The earning power is real but less predictable and less clean than a simple subscription business.

### Returns on Capital — 0.62 / 0.68 confidence

Consolidated 2025 EBIT of €966m on €9.96bn invested capital implies only a high-single-digit accounting return, depressed by €4.34bn of goodwill and acquired intangibles. The operating franchise is much more attractive: management's adjusted fee-related EBITDA margin was 52% in 2025 and 50% in H1 2026, reflecting capital-light incremental economics. The score balances strong underlying fee economics against a shareholder-relevant return diluted by acquisition consideration and balance-sheet investments. Confidence would rise with a consistent five-year return on tangible operating capital and incremental return bridge from EQT's annual reports.

### Pricing Power — 0.78 / 0.76 confidence

EQT's moat is its multi-decade investment record, institutional relationships, talent network and scale across more than 50 active funds. BPEA IX closed at its $15.6bn hard cap, almost 40% larger than its predecessor, while Infrastructure VI exceeded target and closed at €21.5bn; these outcomes show fundraising power despite a difficult private-markets cycle. H1 2026 fee-related revenue excluding retroactive fees grew 5%. Fee rates themselves are not disclosed in the release, and large global peers compete aggressively for both capital and talent. Confidence would rise with the 2025 annual report's management-fee-rate and client re-up disclosures.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.86 / 0.88 confidence

FAUM increased from €141bn at year-end 2025 to €155bn at June 2026, and EQT was fundraising for more than 20 funds. It added AI Infrastructure with $9.4bn FAUM, private-wealth evergreens, Scaleup Europe and, subject to closing, Coller Capital's €31bn of secondaries FAUM. Private-market penetration remains below public-market ownership and institutional/private-wealth allocations can expand for years. New strategies reuse EQT's fundraising and operating platform, although Coller makes part of the runway acquisitive rather than purely organic.

### Capital Allocation — 0.66 / 0.76 confidence

EQT returned €757m in 2025 through dividends and buybacks, then €351m in H1 2026. Buybacks largely offset employee-plan dilution rather than opportunistically shrinking the count. Management also invested €1.11bn in 2025 and €770m in H1 2026 to seed strategies and evergreens, while the pending Coller acquisition expands secondaries. Allocation is strategically coherent and leverage remains contained, but BPEA and Coller make acquisition price and integration central to per-share outcomes. Confidence would rise after Coller closes and purchase-accounting, funding and synergy delivery can be assessed.

### Conservative Debt — 0.82 / 0.92 confidence

At June 2026 net debt was €1.60bn, equal to 1.0× trailing adjusted EBITDA and 1.4× fee-related EBITDA. The €1.5bn revolving facility was undrawn and cash was €843m. This is conservative for a fee business with long-dated capital and recurring management fees, and there is no evident liquidity disqualifier.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.70 / 0.72 confidence

Founder Conni Jonsson has handed the chair to BPEA founder Jean Eric Salata, Per Franzén became CEO in 2025, and EQT operates through broad investment committees and nearly 1,900 employees. The platform has survived leadership succession, but private-markets performance still depends unusually heavily on retaining senior dealmakers and fundraisers. Confidence would rise with the 2025 governance report's succession, key-person clauses and decision-rights disclosures.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.82 / 0.78 confidence

Yahoo reports insiders own about 35% of shares, creating substantial economic alignment, and EQT explicitly repurchases shares to offset employee incentive-plan dilution. Long-duration fund carry also ties investment professionals to realised client outcomes. The available releases do not fully disclose executive metric weights, vesting, hurdle resets or clawbacks. Confidence would rise with the 2026 remuneration report and incentive-plan terms.

### Right Place and Time — 0.76 / 0.82 confidence

Private markets continue taking share of institutional portfolios, while infrastructure, secondaries and private-wealth channels widen the addressable pool. The cycle is exposed to rates and exit markets, but more than 20 active fundraises indicate structural runway.

### Top Dog — 0.82 / 0.86 confidence

EQT is one of the few genuinely global private-markets platforms, with €291bn AUM, hard-cap closes and leading sponsor-backed equity-market transaction volume. Blackstone, KKR, Apollo and CVC are formidable, so EQT is a leader rather than a winner-take-all monopoly.

### Sustainable Advantage — 0.80 / 0.78 confidence

Fund performance, long-lived LP relationships, brand, global sourcing and an accumulated operating playbook reinforce each other. Repeated larger successor funds show the advantage is holding. Confidence would rise with independently benchmarked fund returns and LP re-up rates from the annual report and Preqin.

### Durable Growth — 0.67 / 0.86 confidence

Adjusted revenue grew 16% in 2025, but H1 2026 growth slowed to 5%. FAUM rose 10% in six months, while upcoming fund activations create a stronger fee outlook. Growth remains healthy but lumpy and not presently accelerating.

### Organic Growth — 0.52 / 0.82 confidence

Successor funds, AI Infrastructure, Scaleup Europe and evergreens provide organic expansion, but BPEA materially expanded Asia and Coller will add €31bn of secondaries FAUM. Growth is a genuine mix of platform-led fundraising and large acquisitions.

### Management and Backing — 0.82 / 0.76 confidence

EQT combines founder culture, Wallenberg roots and experienced leaders including Per Franzén and Jean Eric Salata. The transition from Conni Jonsson was orderly and the investment bench is broad. Confidence would rise with a longer public record under Franzén and evidence that Coller integration preserves performance and talent.

### Customer Devotion — 0.72 / 0.64 confidence

Hard-cap fundraising, a BPEA IX fund nearly 40% larger than its predecessor and evergreen redemptions of roughly 0.5% of NAV per quarter indicate strong client demand. Direct LP satisfaction, re-up and concentration data are not disclosed. Confidence would rise with the annual report's LP re-up and client-concentration disclosures.

Rule Breakers narrowly wins mechanically on industry position, runway and management, with Wonderful-and-Fair a genuine near-tie because EQT is already profitable and established. Cash Cow loses because EQT is still reinvesting heavily. Freeroll lacks a hard floor and a discrete catalyst.

## Overall

EQT has a scarce combination of global scale, strong fund performance, hard-cap fundraising and multiple credible growth channels. The weak points are lumpy carry, adjusted-versus-IFRS complexity, talent dependence and execution risk from another large acquisition. No universal disqualifier was found. Rule Breakers narrowly leads a dual fit with Wonderful-and-Fair; the current price demands continued low-double-digit earnings growth.

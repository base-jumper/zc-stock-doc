# FORTUM.HE — Fortum Oyj

## Business Overview

Fortum is a Finland-listed Nordic utility built around hydro, nuclear, power optimisation, consumer energy solutions, heating/cooling and selective renewables development. Its core asset base is 4.7 GW of hydro in Finland and Sweden, a fully owned 1.0 GW Loviisa nuclear plant plus 2.2 GW of co-owned nuclear exposure, and a large consumer/small-business energy customer base across the Nordics and Poland. In 2025, 99% of power generation was fossil-free.

The investment case is not a hyper-growth story. It is a low-carbon infrastructure income stock whose earnings depend on Nordic power prices, hydro conditions, nuclear availability, hedging, optimisation premiums and capital discipline. Fortum is also trying to position for Nordic electricity-demand growth from data centres, industrial decarbonisation and electrification, but those growth options need to earn adequate returns in a capital-heavy utility model.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.55 / confidence 0.75

The continuing business is profitable, but the reported history is messy. Yahoo shows 2025 net income of EUR 765m and diluted EPS of EUR 0.85, down from EUR 1.30 in 2024. Reported 2022 and 2023 were loss years because of Uniper/Russia-related discontinued operations, not because the current Nordic generation franchise disappeared. Fortum’s own comparable operating profit was EUR 924m in 2025 and Q1 2026 comparable operating profit improved to EUR 521m from EUR 462m.

Quality is helped by simple, physical assets and low ongoing stock-comp style dilution, but it is still a power-price/hydrology/nuclear-availability business. 2025 was explicitly hurt by lower nuclear and hydro volumes, while Q1 2026 benefited from higher spot prices and generation volumes. That is acceptable utility earnings, not a smooth compounding engine.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.45 / confidence 0.80

Fortum produces cash, but current free cash flow is not strong relative to dividends. Yahoo-derived free cash flow was EUR 341m in 2025 after EUR 840m operating cash flow and EUR 499m capex; that covered only about 27% of the EUR 1.256bn dividends paid. 2024 was better, with EUR 920m FCF, but the 2025 proposed EUR 0.74 dividend still represents a 90% payout of comparable EPS rather than a comfortable FCF payout.

This does not make the dividend impossible — Fortum has a strong balance sheet and long-lived assets — but it does mean the cash-cow label is only moderate quality. If growth capex rises or power prices disappoint, dividend sustainability depends on management flexing the payout ratio rather than on abundant recurring FCF.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.58 / confidence 0.75

The asset base is durable and essential. Electricity demand should not structurally decline, and Fortum’s hydro/nuclear fleet sits in a Nordic system where decarbonisation, data centres and industrial electrification can lift long-term demand. Management continues to see robust underlying demand from industrial sectors and has a 2030 comparable operating profit improvement target of EUR 330m from own actions, excluding price, M&A and capex effects.

The stability caveat is cyclicality. Revenue fell from EUR 7.8bn in 2022 to EUR 5.0bn in 2025, and operating profit is sensitive to power prices, hedges, outages and hydro inflows. A utility can be durable without being predictable year to year; Fortum is exactly that.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.65 / confidence 0.65

Fortum has asset-based pricing power rather than brand pricing power. Hydro flexibility, nuclear baseload, hedging capability and physical optimisation create value in volatile Nordic markets. The 2025 optimisation premium was EUR 9.7/MWh, and management guided 8–10 EUR/MWh for 2026 and 6–8 EUR/MWh from 2027 onward. Those are real advantages versus a plain commodity generator.

Still, wholesale electricity is a market-price business with political and regulatory scrutiny. Fortum can optimise and contract, but it cannot simply raise prices like a software monopoly. The moat is scarcity of reliable low-carbon assets plus operational expertise, not customer lock-in.

### Capital Allocation — 0.55 / confidence 0.70

Current capital allocation is more disciplined than Fortum’s scarred history. Management targets BBB or better credit quality, max financial net debt/comparable EBITDA of 2.5x, project hurdles of WACC +150–400 bps, and a dividend payout of 60–90% of comparable EPS. Fortum expects EUR 2.0bn of committed capex for 2026–2030 excluding acquisitions, including about EUR 750m of growth capex and EUR 250m per year of maintenance capex.

The negatives are hard to ignore. Uniper and Russia left major damage in the historical record, and the 2025 dividend was paid well above FCF. The current strategy looks sensible — focus on Nordic hydro/nuclear, flexibility, customer-led renewables and heat decarbonisation — but investors should demand proof that new data-centre/renewables/flexibility spending clears the stated return hurdles.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.50 / confidence 0.80

Returns are adequate for a utility, not excellent. Yahoo-derived ROIC was about 7.0% in 2025, 10.0% in 2024 and 12.9% in 2023. Fortum’s own long-term target is 14% comparable RONA, while Q1 2026 comparable RONA was 10.9%. The direction needs to improve before this can be called a high-return compounder.

The upside is that Fortum has low goodwill and hard assets that can earn through long cycles. The downside is that capital-heavy utilities rarely compound at high incremental returns unless regulation, contracts or scarcity are strongly in their favour.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.78 / confidence 0.85

This is the cleanest part of the story. Fortum ended 2025 with financial net debt/comparable EBITDA of 1.2x, and Q1 2026 net debt/comparable EBITDA was 1.1x. Yahoo shows 2025 net debt/EBITDA around 1.2x and EBIT interest cover above 30x. Fortum also has BBB+ ratings from S&P and Fitch with stable outlook, above its BBB minimum target.

The balance sheet gives the dividend and capex plan breathing room. It does not eliminate commodity risk, but it reduces the chance that an ordinary down-cycle becomes a forced equity raise.

### Management / Governance / Alignment — 0.62 incentive, 0.50 backing, 0.78 key-person-risk

Governance is stable and institutional. The Finnish State owns 51.26% through the Ownership Steering Department, making Fortum a state-controlled strategic utility. That is a double-edged sword: it supports balance-sheet conservatism and national-energy relevance, but it can also constrain purely shareholder-optimised decisions.

CEO Markus Rauramo has led through the post-Uniper/Russia reset. Remuneration uses short- and long-term incentives tied to measures including relative TSR and environmental/strategic targets, but CEO personal ownership is not a major visible driver in the available evidence. Key-person risk is low because the value is in long-lived assets and institutional processes, not one founder.

### Reinvestment Runway / Right Place and Time — 0.55 runway, 0.68 right-place-and-time

The runway is real but not automatically high-return. Fortum has around 8 GW of wind/solar pipeline in permitting, a target of 1.2 GW ready-to-build pipeline by end-2028, 2.5 GW of new ready-to-deploy flexibility by end-2028, heat decarbonisation investments in Poland, and data-centre-related opportunities in Finland. Nordic electrification and data centres are good places to be if power demand actually materialises.

But management’s own new-nuclear feasibility study concluded that new nuclear is not economically viable on a merchant basis under the current outlook. The growth options therefore need customer contracts, subsidies or very disciplined capital allocation. This is a moderate runway attached to a mature asset base, not a wide-open reinvestment flywheel.

### Downside Support / Mispricing / Catalyst — 0.35 downside, 0.35 mispricing, 0.48 catalyst

The market is not obviously giving Fortum away. At EUR 19.61, Yahoo shows a trailing P/E of about 21.3x, forward P/E about 21.0x, price/book about 2.15x and EV/EBITDA about 14.9x. The dividend yield is useful at roughly 3.8%, but the stock is much closer to its 52-week high of EUR 22.92 than its low of EUR 14.40.

The identifiable catalysts are operational rather than dramatic: improved nuclear availability after 2025 outages, strong 2026 hedges, optimisation premium delivery, Consumer Solutions performance after the Orange Energia acquisition, and proof of demand from data centres/industrial customers. Those can work, but there is no obvious hard asset floor or screaming valuation disconnect.

### Durable / Organic Growth / Customer Devotion — 0.40 durable-growth, 0.42 organic-growth, 0.45 customer-devotion

Analysts expect 2026 revenue growth of about 10% and EPS of EUR 1.04, followed by a softer 2027 EPS estimate around EUR 0.93. That looks more cyclical/normalisation-driven than durable growth. The 2022–2025 Yahoo revenue CAGR is negative because the company was reshaped after Russia/Uniper and because power prices/volumes normalised.

Customer Solutions is a useful stabiliser, with over 2 million customers and management citing a record 2025 comparable operating profit. Fortum’s CSI outcome was 76, matching its 2028 target. That is respectable, but customer devotion is not the main investment edge here.

## Why cash-cow won

Fortum fits best as a cautious cash-cow / infrastructure-income candidate: essential assets, strong balance sheet, recurring dividend policy, and moderate earnings power. It does not qualify as wonderful-and-fair because returns on capital are only adequate and the current valuation is not cheap. It is not a rule-breaker because growth is mature, regulated/commodity-adjacent and capital intensive. It is not a freeroll because downside support is weak at more than 2x book and around 21x earnings.

## Valuation

I used an exit-multiple model because the thesis is dividend-plus-normalised-earnings, not TAM capture.

- Entry price: EUR 19.61.
- Fundamental: EUR 1.04 EPS, roughly the current-year analyst average and close to Q1 2026 run-rate optimism.
- Growth: 2% annual EPS growth, deliberately conservative for a mature utility.
- Exit multiple: 18x earnings, below today’s roughly 18.9x entry multiple on EUR 1.04 EPS and below the trailing P/E.
- Dividend yield: 3.78%, based on the current indicated dividend yield.
- Dilution: 0%.
- Holding period: 5 years.

The model gives a 4.982% annualised expected ROI. Most of the return comes from dividends, not capital appreciation. If the exit multiple compresses to 16x, the ROI falls to about 2.8%; if Fortum sustains 18x with modest growth, the setup is acceptable but not exciting.

## Overall

Fortum is a solid utility, not a bargain. The good: low-carbon hydro/nuclear assets, strong balance sheet, credible Nordic electrification exposure, useful optimisation capability, and a competitive dividend. The bad: mediocre FCF coverage, only moderate ROIC, cyclicality in power prices/hydrology/nuclear availability, and a valuation that already prices in a fair amount of quality.

I would keep it watchlisted rather than prioritise it. Fortum becomes interesting closer to EUR 15–16, or if evidence builds that data-centre/industrial demand can lift earnings without compromising return discipline. At EUR 19.61, expected return is mostly bond-like income with utility risk attached.

# DCBO.TO — Docebo Inc.

## Business Overview

Docebo is a Canadian learning-management / learning-platform SaaS company aimed mainly at enterprise customers. It sells subscription software for employee, partner, customer and external-enterprise training workflows, with implementation/support services around the core subscriptions. Reported financials are primarily in **USD**, while the TSX quote is **CAD**; valuation needs to keep that currency mismatch explicit.

The current business is high-gross-margin SaaS with improving profitability, but not hyper-growth. FY2025 revenue was **US$242.7m**, up about **12%** year over year, after growth of roughly **20%** in FY2024 and **26.5%** in FY2023. Preliminary Q2 2026 revenue was **US$68.3–68.5m**, up **12.5–12.9%**. ARR at June 30, 2026 was **US$255.1m**, up **9.5%**; excluding the largest OEM customer, acquired ARR and FX, ARR grew about **13.9%**.

Profitability has become a real part of the thesis. FY2025 operating margin was about **10%**, net margin about **15.5%**, and free cash flow was **US$27.2m**. Preliminary Q2 adjusted EBITDA was **US$10.9–11.1m**, and management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to **US$274.5–276.5m** while holding adjusted EBITDA guidance at **US$54.5–56.5m**. Gross margin is around **80%**.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.76 / confidence 0.82

The earnings base is now real rather than purely adjusted. Yahoo/filing-derived annuals show four profitable years from 2022–2025, with net income of **US$37.5m** in 2025 and cumulative free-cash-flow conversion of roughly **0.97x** net income across the period. Receivables as a percentage of revenue improved from about **20.4%** to **18.7%**, and stock-based compensation is visible but not thesis-breaking.

The caveat is volatility in the ramp: 2023 net income was only **US$2.8m**, then 2024/2025 stepped up materially. Q1 2026 free cash flow was very strong at **US$27.6m**, but that may include seasonal working-capital benefit, so I do not want to capitalize that quarter blindly. Confidence is decent because the numbers are from filings/Yahoo statement data; a longer cycle record would firm it up further.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.72 / confidence 0.74

The latest return profile is attractive but not long proven. The returns script estimated ROIC of **28.2%** in 2025 and **35.2%** in 2024, up from much lower levels in 2022–2023. That is the fingerprint we want from a capital-light SaaS company: high gross margins, low capex, and software economics showing through as scale improves.

The reason this is not higher is durability. The high ROIC record is short, and incremental ROIC from the simple script looked noisy/negative because the business model and balance sheet moved quickly. Confidence is below 0.8 because a cleaner filing-level invested-capital bridge, including lease liabilities, acquired intangibles and working capital normalization, would improve the estimate.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.66 / confidence 0.62

Docebo has some moat ingredients: enterprise workflow stickiness, integrations, implementation friction, high gross margins, and a subscription revenue base with management emphasizing retention and renewal assumptions. The Q1 2026 press release says customers generally enter annual or multi-year subscription contracts, and ARR gives management visibility into near-term revenue.

But the moat is not obviously dominant. Corporate learning software is crowded, and the AIF explicitly warns about rapid technological change, competitors introducing new technologies earlier, integration demands, customization requirements, and brand/reputation intensity. The AI positioning may help, but it could also compress differentiation if larger HCM or learning-suite vendors bundle similar capabilities. Confidence is modest because I have not yet reviewed independent win/loss data, customer NPS, Gartner/Forrester rankings, or cohort net revenue retention disclosures.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.68 / confidence 0.68

There is a plausible runway in enterprise learning, partner/customer education, credentialing, skills intelligence, and AI-enabled workforce readiness. Q1 2026 customer wins included a Fortune 100 technology leader replacing a legacy system for external education across **100,000 partners and customers**, which supports the idea that Docebo can win complex enterprise deployments rather than only SMB LMS work.

The offset is growth deceleration. Revenue growth slowed from **26.5%** in 2023 to **20.0%** in 2024 and **11.9%** in 2025; preliminary Q2 ARR growth was **9.5%**, or **~13.9%** excluding the largest OEM customer, acquisitions and FX. Management also assumes no new individual customer contracts above **US$1m ARR** in 2026 guidance, which is appropriately conservative but limits near-term upside. Confidence would improve with segment-level TAM, win-rate, net retention and pipeline evidence.

### Capital Allocation — 0.42 / confidence 0.84

Management has become extremely aggressive on buybacks. The capital-allocation script showed cumulative buybacks of about **US$217.5m** across 2022–2025, versus cumulative FCF of roughly **US$71.7m**, and a diluted share-count decline of about **13.7%**. By July 20, 2026 Docebo had bought another **535,784 shares** under its NCIB, then suspended it while launching a fixed-price issuer bid for up to **3,431,372 shares / 13.78%** at **US$20.40** per share.

The bid price was about 10% above the pre-announcement Nasdaq close and below my conservative intrinsic-value range, so the transaction can be per-share accretive. However, about **US$60m** of the **US$70m** bid is intended to be debt-funded despite buybacks already far exceeding internally generated FCF. Intercap intends to participate so that it maintains at least its **~63.9%** ownership. This is a stretched, leveraged allocation decision rather than conservative stewardship, even if the price proves attractive.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.50 / confidence 0.82

The balance sheet is no longer conservative. Preliminary June 30 figures showed **US$45.7m cash** and **US$88.0m borrowings** before the issuer bid. Docebo plans to fund the **US$70m** offer with about **US$10m cash** and **US$60m** drawn from a credit facility recently enlarged from **US$100m to US$150m**. If fully completed as proposed, pro-forma net debt would be roughly **US$112m**, about **2.0x** FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance.

Recurring SaaS cash flows make that leverage serviceable, and the offer documents say liquidity should remain sufficient for investment, but debt will constrain optionality and makes FCF normalization more important. The balance sheet is stretched rather than endangered. Confidence is high on the planned amounts; the remaining gap is the post-close maturity, interest-rate and covenant schedule.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.64 / confidence 0.58

Docebo is no longer a tiny founder-dependent software company, and the enterprise customer base/platform should outlast any one person. CEO Alessio Artuffo and the board are visible in the 2026 circular, and the company has enough scale that operational continuity should be manageable.

That said, the AI/workforce-readiness repositioning is management-led, and the source review here did not deeply test bench strength, product-leadership depth, culture, or succession. Confidence is low; the next useful sources are earnings-call transcripts, executive tenure history, Glassdoor/employee signals, and the detailed proxy succession/governance sections.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.62 / confidence 0.66

Alignment is mixed. Insider ownership is high in Yahoo data at roughly **63.7%**, which usually points management/insiders at long-term per-share outcomes. The company also appears focused on per-share value via buybacks, and management commentary balances growth with profitability rather than growth-at-any-price.

The concern is that incentives and capital allocation could drift toward financial engineering: very large buybacks relative to cumulative FCF, plus valuation-sensitive repurchases, put more weight on management judgement. I have not fully parsed the compensation scorecard from the MIC, so confidence remains below 0.8; the specific firm-up source is the full 2026 management information circular compensation table and STI/LTI metric weighting.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Rule-breakers is plausible but weaker than wonderful-and-fair because Docebo is no longer a hypergrowth disruptor; ARR/revenue growth has decelerated into low-to-mid teens. Freeroll/cash-cow are not the right primary frame because downside support from book value is weak and the thesis is still quality-plus-growth rather than a hard asset, dividend, or liquidation floor.

## Overall

Docebo looks like a solid, profitable vertical SaaS/platform company at a reasonable valuation. The attraction is the combination of ~80% gross margins, real FCF, improving adjusted EBITDA margins, shrinking share count, and a credible enterprise learning/AI workflow runway. Preliminary Q2 results support rather than break the operating thesis: ex-OEM/FX/acquisition ARR growth held near 14% and FY revenue guidance edged up.

The reason this is not a high-conviction buy is that growth quality is unsettled and management is levering the balance sheet for a very large repurchase. ARR is only growing low teens, the largest OEM customer rolloff has distorted comparisons, and the competitive moat is good but not obviously unassailable. The buyback may create substantial per-share value at US$20.40, but it also raises the cost of being wrong.

## Valuation

Market snapshot used for this update: **C$29.12** share price and market cap about **C$739m** at the July 30 close. Yahoo showed TTM revenue of **US$251.0m**, EBITDA of **US$29.5m**, FCF of **US$43.1m**, P/S of **~2.94x**, EV/revenue of **~3.20x**, and EV/EBITDA of **~27.3x**. The enterprise-value figures precede completion of the debt-funded bid, and Q1 working capital likely flatters trailing FCF.

Exit-multiple valuation inputs use CAD for both price and per-share FCF. I retained **C$2.30** of normalized current owner earnings / FCF per share: preliminary Q2 supports the operating run-rate but does not yet provide final cash-flow detail. Other inputs are **5 years**, **8%** annual aggregate FCF growth, **13x** exit P/FCF, no dividend, and a one-time **13.78%** share reduction in year one if the issuer bid completes in full; no further buybacks are assumed.

The model produces an estimated annualized ROI of **11.84%**. The current entry multiple is about **12.7x** normalized FCF versus a **13x** exit multiple, so it does not rely on re-rating; attribution is roughly **8.0%** business growth, **3.0%** buyback lift and **0.5%** multiple re-rating. My agent score falls to **0.63**: the buyback can be accretive, but borrowing heavily for it offsets much of the attraction and makes the outcome more fragile.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a **wonderful-and-fair** style thesis: a high-margin, profitable software company with real cash generation and a fair-looking valuation. It is not a clean rule-breaker unless Docebo can reaccelerate ARR materially through AI/workforce-readiness products and enterprise wins.

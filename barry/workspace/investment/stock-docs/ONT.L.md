# ONT.L — Oxford Nanopore Technologies plc

## Business Overview

Oxford Nanopore Technologies sells nanopore-based molecular sensing and sequencing platforms: small devices such as MinION/GridION, high-throughput PromethION systems, flow cells, kits, software and related services. The core economic model is instrument placement plus recurring consumables usage; in 2025 PromethION was the growth engine, with product-range revenue up 43.1% to £110.6m and larger-device utilisation up 25% year on year.

FY25 revenue was £223.9m, up 22.2% reported and 24.2% constant currency. Growth was broad-based: Research remained largest at £148.6m (+15.1%), while Clinical (£29.8m, +59.9%), Applied Industrial (£27.5m, +27.2%) and BioPharma (£18.1m, +30.4%) grew faster from smaller bases. Gross margin improved to 58.6% and adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to £86.7m. The company ended 2025 with £302.8m of cash, cash equivalents and other liquid investments, and management reaffirmed targets for adjusted EBITDA breakeven in FY27 and cash-flow positive in FY28.

This is a still-lossmaking growth platform, not a proven compounder or cash cow. The investment question is whether nanopore sequencing becomes a major mainstream molecular-analysis platform before cash burn, competition or execution issues erode the upside.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.82 / 0.78

The market setup is attractive. ONT is exposed to long-read/native DNA and RNA sequencing, real-time methylation and deployable sequencing across research, clinical, biopharma QC and industrial workflows. The company defines a $20–25bn serviceable addressable market inside broader >$150bn long-term molecular-analysis opportunities, with management focusing commercial effort on $13–14bn of higher-priority segments. FY25 evidence supports adoption: revenue grew >20% constant currency in every region and applied end-markets grew faster than research. Confidence is under 0.8 because SAM figures are company-defined and need independent market-share triangulation.

### Top Dog — 0.66 / 0.68

ONT appears to be one of the distinctive leaders in nanopore/long-read sequencing, with a differentiated long-read, real-time, portable platform and more than 20,000 peer-reviewed papers to date, including roughly 4,000 in 2025. PromethION growth and clinical partnerships with Cepheid and bioMérieux support real commercial traction. The score is not higher because Illumina remains dominant in broad sequencing spend, PacBio competes in long reads, and ONT’s 2025 revenue is still small versus the serviceable market. Firm-up: independent market-share data by long-read segment and instrument installed base would sharpen this.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.68 / 0.66

The advantage rests on differentiated chemistry/electronics, direct/native molecule sensing, real-time analysis, portable-to-high-throughput device range, accumulated workflows and IP. The 2025 MGI litigation update is supportive: MGI conceded that its Cyclone SEQ WT02 infringes four Australian ONT patents, while remaining defences go to a 2027 trial; ONT also brought UK trade-secret and confidence claims against MGI/BGI entities. The moat is credible but not impregnable: sequencing technology changes quickly, quality/cost leadership can shift, and ONT still needs to prove its platform wins routine regulated workflows at scale. Firm-up: comparative accuracy/cost benchmarks versus Illumina/PacBio in priority clinical and QC applications.

### Durable Growth — 0.77 / 0.82

FY25 revenue growth was 24.2% constant currency after FY24’s lower base, and management guided FY26 constant-currency growth of 21–25%, with FY27 expected broadly similar. Growth came across geographies, products and customer types, with higher-growth applied segments becoming more important. The negatives are real: research still represents 66.4% of revenue; some strategic projects have completed; NIH and China headwinds remain; and growth has not yet converted into profits. Still, the multi-year guide and broad-based FY25 delivery warrant a strong growth score.

### Organic Growth — 0.86 / 0.80

Growth appears overwhelmingly organic: FY25 revenue improvement was attributed to product adoption, higher PromethION flow-cell usage, pricing-model changes, new clinical/biopharma/industrial workflows, and operational execution rather than acquisitions. Cash-flow statements show no material acquisition-led growth in 2025. Confidence is solid because the annual results directly attribute growth drivers and there is no sign of roll-up behaviour.

### Management Backing — 0.62 / 0.65

The new CEO, Francis Van Parys, joined in March 2026 with senior life-science tools experience at Radiometer/Danaher, Cytiva and GE Healthcare, which is directionally positive for a company moving from technology evangelism to commercial scale. The founder, Gordon Sanghera, stepped down after leading since 2005 and remains in an advisory role into early 2027. The board also restructured costs and sharpened strategic focus in 2025. This is a transition, though: execution credibility under the new CEO is not yet proven at ONT. Firm-up: 2026 interim execution versus guidance and evidence that the new operating cadence improves commercial productivity.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.58 / 0.58

Insider ownership is high on Yahoo Finance data at about 43%, which aligns insiders with equity value, but governance/incentive confidence is only moderate. The company has used equity compensation and founder LTIP/pre-IPO awards; diluted shares rose from roughly 824m in 2022 to 966m at FY25 year-end, and a 2024 capital raise added dilution. The annual report says executive remuneration is intended to align with long-term strategy, but I have not fully audited the performance targets. Firm-up: read the FY25 remuneration tables in detail and quantify expected SBC/share-count leakage under the new CEO package.

### Customer Devotion — 0.64 / 0.60

Customer evidence is encouraging but indirect. ONT products are used by scientists in more than 125 countries; peer-reviewed publication volume is large; national/population-scale projects such as UK Biobank and Genomics England work demonstrate platform relevance; and PromethION utilisation rose materially in 2025. Clinical collaborations indicate serious customer pull. The score stays moderate because I do not yet have cohort retention, NPS, renewal, utilisation by vintage, or independent user-satisfaction data. Firm-up: customer retention/utilisation cohorts and third-party workflow benchmarking.

Other strategies lost mechanically. Wonderful-and-fair and cash-cow fail because ONT has no demonstrated durable earnings or positive free cash flow yet. Freeroll is a poor fit because there is no hard downside floor or time-bound catalyst; this is a long-duration adoption/scale bet.

Disqualifier check: no universal disqualifier is triggered on current evidence. Cash burn is significant, but the FY25 liquid-investment balance and management’s FY27/FY28 breakeven path make near-term solvency/dilution risk survivable rather than thesis-ending. Litigation is material to monitor but not currently an equity-threatening hidden liability based on the available disclosures.

## Overall

ONT is a credible Rule Breaker candidate with a real platform, strong revenue growth, improving gross margin, strong cash runway and a large company-defined SAM. The biggest positive is the combination of differentiated technology plus broadening applied-market adoption, especially PromethION utilisation and clinical/biopharma growth.

The hold-back is proof. The business is still deeply loss-making, dilution has been meaningful, research remains the majority of revenue, and the new CEO has not yet established a public track record at ONT. The updated probability-weighted valuation makes that hold-back more visible: the survivor case is attractive, but a majority of plausible outcomes are still below today’s entry price. I rate the qualitative setup as good but not elite until 2026–2027 execution confirms that growth, margin improvement and cost discipline can coexist.

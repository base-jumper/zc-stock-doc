# HIMS — Hims & Hers Health, Inc.

## Business Overview

Hims & Hers is a direct-to-consumer health and wellness platform selling subscription-based access to online clinical consultations, pharmacy fulfillment, and personalized products across sexual health, dermatology, hair loss, mental health, weight management, and adjacent categories. The model is not just telehealth visits; it is consumer acquisition, longitudinal subscriptions, pharmacy/fulfillment, and an expanding product catalogue wrapped in a consumer brand.

The company scaled revenue from $526.9m in 2022 to $2.35b in 2025. Q2 2026 revenue rose 38% to $753.2m and subscribers increased 19% to 2.891m, but the headline now mixes a 16% increase in U.S. revenue with a more than seventeen-fold increase internationally after the June Eucalyptus acquisition. Gross margin fell to 64% from 76%, net income swung to an $86.3m loss, Adjusted EBITDA declined to $60.3m from $82.2m, and free cash flow remained negative at $68.2m. Management raised full-year revenue guidance to $3.1b-$3.3b but narrowed Adjusted EBITDA guidance to $275m-$325m.

The defining controversy remains GLP-1. The FDA's resolution of the semaglutide shortage curtailed the easiest compounded-copy economics, and Hims pivoted toward branded access. That makes the offering more durable legally but helps explain a lower-margin mix. Eucalyptus adds Australia, Canada, Germany, Japan and the UK, while also making acquisition accounting, integration and balance-sheet obligations central to the thesis.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.78 / 0.82

Healthcare access remains expensive and inconvenient, while cash-pay wellness, personalized treatment, pharmacy fulfillment and weight management are becoming more consumer-led. The companion market model estimates the defined U.S. direct-to-consumer telehealth revenue pool can grow from $7.0b in 2026 to $55.0b in 2036. These are structural demand drivers, not a one-quarter cycle.

The score is capped because regulation, pharma-owned channels and intense customer-acquisition competition can redirect the profit pool. Branded GLP-1 access may expand demand while producing materially worse economics than the compounded-product phase.

### Top Dog — 0.72 / 0.78

Hims is one of the most visible consumer digital-health brands in the U.S.; 2.891m subscribers, an owned acquisition funnel and pharmacy/fulfillment infrastructure provide scale smaller telehealth clones lack. Eucalyptus broadens that position internationally, although the acquired footprint is not evidence that the core U.S. lead widened.

Amazon, Ro, Sesame, legacy providers, insurers, pharma direct channels and specialist clinics can all attack pieces of the stack. Confidence would rise with a current category-share series covering Hims, Ro and pharma-direct channels; private-company disclosure makes that partly irreducible until a reputable market-share study appears.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.56 / 0.72

The moat is plausible but not proven. Brand, a scaled acquisition engine, owned pharmacy/fulfillment capability, clinical workflows, personalization data and a subscription base that can be cross-sold are real advantages versus a start-up.

Much of the product surface is not proprietary medicine, and well-funded competitors can copy access and convenience. The Q2 gross-margin decline from 76% to 64% is evidence that product and channel mix can overwhelm scale benefits. Confidence would improve with company-disclosed cohort retention, contribution margin by vintage and cross-category adoption showing that data and personalization deepen the relationship.

### Durable Growth — 0.60 / 0.95

Hims is maturing, so revenue, subscribers, margins and cash conversion all matter. Revenue roughly quadrupled from 2022 to 2025 and grew 38% in Q2 2026, subscribers rose 19%, and monthly revenue per average subscriber rose 21%. Full-year revenue guidance of $3.1b-$3.3b implies 32%-41% growth from 2025.

The quality of that growth deteriorated. Q2 U.S. revenue grew only 16%; much of the headline acceleration came from Eucalyptus. Gross margin fell twelve points to 64%, Adjusted EBITDA declined 27%, net loss was $86.3m and Q2 free cash flow was negative $68.2m. Adjusted EBITDA excludes $47.5m of legal contingencies and $4.6m of restructuring in the quarter. Growth is clearly present, but it is neither cleanly organic nor accompanied by improving unit economics, which keeps this trait in the middle band.

### Organic Growth — 0.40 / 0.95

The 2022-2025 ramp was largely built internally, and Q2 U.S. revenue still grew 16%. The current growth mix is materially different: six-month U.S. revenue grew only 3%, while rest-of-world revenue rose more than thirteen-fold and Q2 management explicitly attributed the international acceleration to the June Eucalyptus close.

Acquisition cash spend was $318m in the first half, goodwill rose from $278m to $1.10b, and goodwill plus acquired intangibles now represent about 42% of assets. Deferred acquisition payables and earn-outs total about $866m. The deal may prove strategically sound, but headline growth and the balance sheet are now meaningfully acquisition-dependent; no related impairment has yet been disclosed.

### Management & Smart Backing — 0.68 / 0.80

Founder-CEO Andrew Dudum's team has demonstrated strong consumer execution through brand building, category launches and pharmacy operations. The pivot from broad compounded GLP-1 access toward branded products was a necessary adaptation, and management raised the 2026 revenue outlook after Q2.

The stewardship read weakened because the Eucalyptus expansion produced large goodwill and deferred consideration while margins and cash flow deteriorated. The company also excludes recurring-looking legal, acquisition and restructuring costs from Adjusted EBITDA. The next proxy's pay metrics and the next 10-Q's Eucalyptus purchase-price allocation would firm up whether management is balancing ambition with per-share discipline.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.42 / 0.75

Yahoo reports insider ownership of about 5.5%. Stock-based compensation was $79.0m in the first half, up 30%, and ordinary shares outstanding increased from 227.2m at December to 233.3m at June. Convertible notes reached $1.37b, adding another potential per-share claim, while Adjusted EBITDA excludes SBC and several deal/legal costs.

This is a real alignment discount, although not a disqualifier. Confidence would rise with the latest proxy's performance-vesting metrics, holding requirements, founder retained ownership and any policy for offsetting dilution.

### Customer Devotion — 0.66 / 0.68

The recurring model and 2.891m subscribers show meaningful preference for convenience, privacy and brand. Q2 subscribers rose 19% and monthly revenue per average subscriber rose 21%, evidence that customers are both joining and spending more.

Subscriber growth is not the same as devotion when marketing spend rose 20% to $262m and the figures now include Eucalyptus. Confidence would improve with Hims' cohort retention, churn, NPS, reorder and cross-category adoption disclosures; until those appear, preference versus paid acquisition remains irreducible.

### Why other strategies lost

Wonderful-and-fair loses because earnings and owner cash flow are unstable. Cash-cow loses because Hims is reinvesting, marketing-heavy and free-cash-flow negative. Freeroll loses because tangible book and the leveraged acquisition balance sheet provide no hard downside floor. Rule Breakers remains the natural lens, but its organic-growth and stewardship traits are now weaker.

## Overall

HIMS still has the brand, subscriber growth and category runway to become a durable consumer-health front door. Q2 resolved the immediate question about revenue reacceleration, but it replaced it with harder questions about acquisition dependence, margins, legal adjustments and cash conversion.

At the August 11 close of **$30.51**, the only applicable valuation is an **11.54%** annualized survivor case. That return is positive but conditional on Hims reaching mature economics far above today's, while current owner earnings no longer support a bottom-up cross-check. The **0.48 agent score** therefore remains watchlist/opportunistic rather than priority-allocation territory.

## Valuation

Rule Breakers still uses the canonical `weighted-average`, but only TAM-capture is currently applicable, so the blend assigns it 100%. The exit-multiple component was removed: Q2 free cash flow was negative $68.2m, first-half free cash flow was negative $15.2m, GAAP earnings were negative and Yahoo's current FCF field is distorted by acquisition working capital. There is no defensible current owner-earnings anchor.

The TAM-capture survivor case reads **$13.44b** of 2036 HIMS revenue from the standardized [U.S. direct-to-consumer telehealth market model](../market-docs/us-direct-to-consumer-telehealth.md). Applying a **16% EBIT margin**, **15x EV/EBIT**, **$705m** net debt, **258.2m** current diluted shares and **3%/yr** dilution produces $31.56b of terminal equity, a **$90.93** terminal share price and **11.54%/yr** ROI from the **$30.51** entry price. Doximity remains the mature digital-health role model; the lower margin recognises Hims' pharmacy and fulfillment mix. Value creation contributes **14.89%/yr** and dilution subtracts **2.91%/yr**.

The model is deliberately conditional: today's 64% gross margin, negative owner cash flow and acquisition obligations do not yet establish the terminal EBIT economics. Mechanical scores are refreshed from the current traits and valuation; the agent score is lower because TAM-capture is a survivor path, not an unconditional expected return.

## Thesis Connections

Hims fits a consumerisation-of-healthcare thesis and a narrower AI-enabled-care thesis through personalization, clinical workflow and support automation. Neither thesis removes the need for regulatory compliance, durable retention or cash conversion.

## Watch

- Q3 U.S. organic revenue growth and whether the promised second-half acceleration appears without another acquired contribution.
- Gross-margin and Adjusted EBITDA bridges by branded GLP-1, personalized products and international mix; test whether 64% is a floor.
- GAAP operating cash flow and free cash flow after normalising Eucalyptus working capital, deferred consideration and restructuring.
- Subscriber retention, churn, CAC payback, NPS, reorder and cross-category adoption; subscriber growth alone is insufficient.
- Eucalyptus purchase-price allocation, organic revenue contribution, earn-out/deferred consideration and impairment indicators.
- Latest proxy: performance metrics, founder ownership after sales, holding requirements, SBC and fully diluted share count.
- Legal contingencies and regulatory changes affecting compounded or personalized products.

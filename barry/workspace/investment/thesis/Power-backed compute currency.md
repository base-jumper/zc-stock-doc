# Power-Backed Compute Currency

## Thesis

In a distant future where AI/compute becomes one of the dominant productive forces in the economy, claims on reliable power and compute may become a new form of hard monetary asset. The currency may not be literal kilowatt-hours, but tokenised claims that can be exchanged for compute. Compute consumes power, and power is ultimately scarce.

In this world, power providers or compute providers could effectively “mint” tokens backed by deliverable electricity or compute capacity. AI providers consume the tokens to run models and agents. Most people may not care about the underlying mechanics; they may simply trade the tokens for normal goods and services, much as people use money today without thinking about central-bank balance sheets.

This is a long-range, speculative FIT. It is not immediately investable, but it is worth monitoring because early monetary regime shifts can create enormous asymmetric opportunities. Bitcoin is the obvious historical analogy: dismissible at birth, but transformative for early believers if the thesis proved even partly right.

## Initial Probability

- Power becomes a strategic AI bottleneck: ~80%.
- Compute becomes a globally traded strategic commodity: ~50–60%.
- Energy/compute-backed tokens become a meaningful parallel monetary system: ~20–30% over a long horizon.
- Energy/compute-backed tokens displace fiat as a dominant settlement layer: low probability, but very high upside if it happens.

## Why This Could Happen

- AI labour may become a major input into economic output.
- AI labour requires compute.
- Compute requires electricity, chips, cooling, land, and networks.
- Electricity is harder to fake than fiat credit: it must be physically produced and delivered.
- If confidence in fiat currencies falls, markets may search for harder settlement assets.
- A token redeemable for compute or power could have intrinsic industrial utility.
- Energy-rich jurisdictions or companies may monetise power by exporting compute rather than electrons.
- AI agents may prefer programmable, instantly settled digital claims over traditional banking rails.

## Potential Monetary Logic

The chain is:

**Power → Compute → Intelligence/AI labour → Economic output → Monetary value**

If compute becomes a dominant form of productive capacity, then a credible claim on compute could become analogous to a claim on labour, energy, or industrial output.

This does not require every person to directly consume compute. People could hold and trade compute-backed tokens because others — especially AI companies, autonomous agents, governments, and corporations — need compute.

## Possible Forms

- Tokenised compute credits redeemable for GPU-hours or model inference.
- Energy-backed stablecoins redeemable for power or data-centre capacity.
- Sovereign compute credits issued by energy-rich nations.
- Hyperscaler-issued compute credits that trade externally.
- Commodity futures markets for compute capacity.
- AI-agent payment rails denominated in compute units.
- Bitcoin-like proof-of-work assets regaining relevance as energy-backed monetary assets.
- Data-centre capacity rights traded like oil/gas/LNG contracts.

## Catalysts

- Loss of confidence in USD or other major fiat currencies.
- Persistent US budget deficits and rising debt-service costs.
- Inflation volatility or financial repression.
- Geopolitical fragmentation of the dollar system.
- Sanctions encouraging alternative settlement networks.
- AI compute shortages becoming economically painful.
- Governments treating compute capacity as strategic national infrastructure.
- AI companies securing direct power ownership or sovereign-level energy deals.
- Tradable compute futures or tokenised compute marketplaces emerging.
- A major economy, energy producer, or hyperscaler issuing credible compute/power-backed credits.

## Broad Winners / Beneficiaries

This is not cleanly investable yet, but potential beneficiaries include:

- Owners of scarce, low-cost, reliable power.
- Energy-rich countries with political stability.
- Nuclear and hydro power owners.
- Data-centre operators with secured long-term power.
- Hyperscalers controlling compute markets.
- Tokenisation/payment infrastructure providers.
- Crypto networks that credibly connect energy, compute, and settlement.
- Exchanges and clearinghouses that list compute/energy derivatives.
- Companies building metering, verification, custody, and settlement infrastructure.

## Specific Areas To Watch

### Energy-Rich Compute Exporters

Countries or regions with abundant cheap power may export compute rather than raw energy. This could include hydro-rich, nuclear-rich, geothermal-rich, gas-rich, or solar-rich jurisdictions.

Watch for:
- National AI/data-centre strategies built around energy exports.
- Sovereign compute clouds.
- Government-backed compute credits.
- Data-centre special economic zones near power generation.

### Hyperscalers / AI Infrastructure Providers

Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Oracle, Meta, Nvidia-linked clouds, and specialist GPU clouds may become issuers or market makers of compute claims.

Watch for:
- Transferable compute credits.
- Compute reservation markets.
- External resale of committed GPU capacity.
- Power-backed AI infrastructure contracts.

### Crypto / Tokenisation Infrastructure

If a power/compute-backed monetary system emerges, it likely needs programmable settlement, custody, auditability, and cross-border transfer.

Watch for:
- Compute-backed stablecoins.
- Energy-backed tokens.
- Proof-of-work narratives re-emerging around energy monetisation.
- Tokenised commodity infrastructure.
- Regulatory acceptance of real-world-asset tokens.

### Market Infrastructure

Compute may eventually trade like a commodity.

Watch for:
- GPU-hour futures.
- Data-centre capacity derivatives.
- Power+compute bundled contracts.
- Exchange-listed compute products.
- Standardised measurement units for inference/training capacity.

## What To Monitor

### Monetary / Macro Signals

- US budget deficits.
- Debt-to-GDP and interest expense as percentage of tax revenue.
- Demand for US Treasuries.
- Foreign central-bank reserve composition.
- De-dollarisation moves.
- Gold and Bitcoin strength during fiscal stress.
- Inflation volatility.
- Capital controls, sanctions, or financial repression.
- Loss of confidence in central-bank independence.

### Compute / Power Signals

- Compute shortages.
- GPU-hour pricing.
- Cloud compute reservation markets.
- Hyperscaler PPAs and power acquisitions.
- AI companies buying or building generation assets.
- Energy-rich jurisdictions building compute export strategies.
- Data centres co-located with power generation.
- Compute futures or tokenised compute marketplaces.
- Major AI agents using programmable payment rails.

### Token / Market Structure Signals

- Launch of credible compute-backed tokens.
- Energy-backed stablecoins with audited reserves/deliverability.
- Exchanges listing compute derivatives.
- Tokenised power purchase agreements.
- Settlement between AI agents using non-fiat units.
- Regulatory frameworks for real-world-asset tokens.
- Institutional adoption of tokenised commodity claims.

## Risks / Bear Case

- Fiat currencies remain dominant despite deficits because network effects are overwhelming.
- Compute becomes abundant and cheap, weakening scarcity value.
- Energy efficiency improves fast enough to reduce power scarcity.
- Governments ban or heavily regulate private compute-backed money.
- Tokens fail due to redemption, custody, fraud, or standardisation problems.
- Hyperscalers keep compute credits closed and non-transferable.
- Power is too location-specific to become a clean global monetary base.
- Gold, Bitcoin, or existing fiat stablecoins absorb the hard-money demand instead.

## Investment Framing

This is a watchlist thesis, not an immediate portfolio-construction thesis.

Near-term actionable overlap belongs in the **Power Bottleneck** FIT: grid equipment, power generation, uranium/nuclear, data-centre infrastructure, and utilities.

For this longer-term thesis, the goal is to watch for early signs of a new monetary/commodity market forming around compute. If credible compute-backed or power-backed tokens emerge, the early winners may look strange or fringe at first — just as Bitcoin once did.

The key question to keep asking:

**Is compute becoming important enough, scarce enough, transferable enough, and trusted enough to function as collateral or money?**

## Monitor Log

### 2026-08-01 — Transferable inference claim emerges as a bearer compute asset

Material signal: Venice's DIEM is a transferable, ownable claim on future AI inference that an autonomous agent can hold and spend directly.

Evidence noted by the monthly FIT monitor:
- Galaxy Research's July 2026 review describes DIEM as a tradeable token granting $1 per day of Venice inference credits in perpetuity across the platform's models.
- DIEM can be sold outright, leased through secondary marketplaces, or held directly by an agent as a permissionless inference balance.
- Venice reported processing 100 billion tokens across its access points by June 23 and raised a $65 million Series A in July, giving the issuer/product more substance than a purely conceptual crypto project.
- The wider stack is converging: GPU price indices and exchange futures, GPU-backed onchain lending, tokenised inference access, and agentic payment rails now coexist and can potentially interoperate.

Why it matters:
- This directly hits the strongest tokenisation checkpoint yet: compute access has become an ownable, transferable bearer-like asset rather than merely collateral behind dollar credit.
- It also connects tokenised compute to AI-agent settlement because an agent can hold the asset and consume inference without an account-bound cloud reservation.
- Credibility remains early. DIEM is dollar-denominated rather than fixed in compute units, is an unsecured promise on Venice rather than physically collateralised capacity, and less than half of available inference was reportedly consumed weekly; most holders remain speculators.

Thesis update:
- Modestly raise “energy/compute-backed tokens become a meaningful parallel monetary system” from roughly 25–35% to roughly 30–40%.
- Do not raise the dominant-settlement-layer probability. This is a proto-monetary compute claim, but it lacks broad merchant/agent adoption, standardized redemption, issuer diversification, and money-like price stability.

### 2026-07-01 — GPU-backed onchain credit appears

Material signal: tokenised/onchain credit markets are starting to use GPU infrastructure and compute cashflows as collateral.

Evidence noted by the monthly FIT monitor:
- USD.AI docs describe non-recourse financing facilities for AI infrastructure operators, secured by GPU fleets and asset-level cashflows.
- Depositors mint USDai/sUSDai; yield is described as coming from GPU infrastructure loans and Treasury-bill reserves.
- Docs show deployed contract addresses, audits/bug bounty, proof-of-reserves/reserves pages, and governance over eligible hardware/collateral parameters.
- GPU Loans, powered by USD.AI, markets loans against AI-grade GPUs with quoted LTV up to 80% on new GPUs.

Why it matters:
- This directly hits the tokenisation/collateral checkpoint: compute assets are being packaged into liquid, composable onchain credit instruments.
- It is not yet compute-backed money in the strong sense, but it is a clearer step than simple AI-infra scarcity or cloud credits.
- Credibility is still early/DeFi-native; redemption, underwriting, collateral verification, liquidity, and counterparty risk remain major questions.

Thesis update:
- Modestly raise “energy/compute-backed tokens become a meaningful parallel monetary system” from roughly 20–30% to roughly 25–35%.
- Do not materially raise the dominant-settlement-layer probability. This is collateralised credit, not money-scale settlement.

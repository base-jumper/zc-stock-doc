# Financial Plan

The purpose of this document is to outline the financial objective we are trying to achieve.

## Goal
The overall goal is to grow a portfolio of assets where the overall portfolio in aggregate deliver a high rate of return. I am in the accumulation phase, and at the point where I have enough capital to make time & effort spent on investing a worthwhile pursuit (ie. the returns pay back the effort). My investment horizon is still at least a couple of decades. That gives plenty of time to ride out volatility, so there's room to take measured risks in pursuit of reward.

Stocks are my preferred asset class. I believe we're still in the early stages of an economic boom. AI is making it easier to build the next generation of technology, leading to an exponential growth trend. 

## Background
I'm an engineer with experience across software and hardware. I feel comfortable assessing new technologies, making judgement on technological motes, and differentiating between what is real versus hype. In the past I've worked with chips and products from semiconductor manufacturers whilst designing circuit boards. At present I work full-stack in software, and have exposure to cloud platforms, SaS and PaS services. 

## Diversification
The portfolio should remain diversified enough to benefit from the following:
* Increase the chances of capturing the upside of the small number of companies that turn out to 100-baggers over the long run
* Hold uncorrelated assets such that a fall in one geography or industry is at least partially offset by gains in other geographies or industries

However, it should not become too broad. Too many individual stocks will become a maintenance burden to stay on top of (and major toke-burn!). We could go the opposite route and invest everything in an index ETF, but I think we can do better being more selective. So far it has paid-off!

Less than 10 ETFs and 40 companies seems about right. 

## ETFs/LICs/LITs
Should be used for the following purposes:
* Default place to invest new funds when there are no compelling opportunities in individual companies at the time. Justification: it's better to collect the beta than sit in cash or invest in an individual company if I don't have high conviction in it.
* For broad exposure to themes where I either haven't been able to clearly identify individual winners, or don't have the knowledge required to assess companies in the space (eg. genomics ETFs such as ARKG)
* For broad exposure to economies as a whole that are likely to perform well (eg. exposure to )
* Access to alternative and unlisted assets (Regal RF1 is a great example). This can provide good diversification with low correlation to stocks.
* Leverage the expertise of fund managers with a solid track record.

We'll aim to keep 50% of our stock holdings in ETFs/LICs/LIts. A significant portion of this should be allocated to ETFs with limited correlation to the broader stock market.

## Bonds
We might park some money in bonds at times when we feel the market is getting overheated. But we prefer to keep our money deployed in growth assets as often as possible. No need to maintain a certain percentage of funds in bonds at all times. As a "safe" investment, prefer defensive companies over bonds (eg. healthcare, consumables, etc).

## Stock Picking Guidelines
We want a large portion of holdings to be quality compounders, that we hold over a period of 10-20 years.

Buying stocks that are already priced to the downside is attractive when we believe the downside is temporary or over-estimated by the market. If we are correct, this provides a low-risk entry point. The price is unlikely to fall further, so the main risk is one of short-term opportunity cost while waiting for the stock to recover. By contrast, a stock with upside already priced in can fall significantly if it fails to realize that upside. I am prepared to wait longer-than-most for a stock to recover if I believe its fundamentals are strong - this should be used to our advantage.

Buying cyclicals at a low point in a cycle is also a valid strategy. Again, I'm prepared to be patient.

Day trading or purchased based purely on technical analysis without regard for fundamentals is not a part of this portfolio.

Capital gains tax needs to be factored into any decision to rotate out of a stock.

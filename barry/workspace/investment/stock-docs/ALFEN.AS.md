# ALFEN.AS — Alfen N.V.

## Business Overview

Alfen is a Dutch electrical-equipment company serving the European energy transition. It designs, develops and produces **Smart Grid Solutions** (substations and grid-operator/project equipment), **EV Charging** equipment, and **Energy Storage Systems** (utility-scale, commercial/industrial and mobile battery solutions). The business is project/product-heavy rather than software-like: revenue depends on grid-operator rollouts, energy-storage project milestones, charger volumes, mix and execution.

2025 was a reset year. Revenue fell **10.7% to €435.6m**: Smart Grid Solutions was **€189.1m** (-10.2%), EV Charging **€120.8m** (-21.2%) and Energy Storage Systems **€125.6m** (+1.6%). Adjusted EBITDA was **€25.5m**, a **5.8%** margin, and net profit was roughly breakeven (-€0.2m). Management cut FTEs from 1,053 to 923, reduced personnel and operating costs, and began a company-wide transformation around customer centricity, product excellence and digitalisation.

The most recent operating signal is better but not clean. Q1 2026 revenue rose **25.0% YoY to €129.7m**, with growth in Energy Storage Systems and Smart Grid Solutions partly offset by EV Charging weakness. Adjusted EBITDA rose to **€8.2m** (6.3% margin). Management reiterated 2026 guidance of **€435m-€475m** revenue, **4%-7%** adjusted EBITDA margin and capex below 4% of revenue, while warning revenue is front-loaded in 2026.

Alfen and CATL agreed in July 2026 to target **5 GWh of sodium-ion battery-storage deployments in Europe**, extending their lithium-ion supply relationship. This could broaden Alfen's ESS offering, lower material-cost exposure and improve tender competitiveness, but no revenue, timing or firm-order economics were disclosed; it is strategic optionality rather than backlog today. No newer company operating release had been published as of **16 August 2026**; H1 results are due after market on **18 August**.

## Trait Assessment

Primary strategy: **Rule Breakers**. Alfen is not a cash cow: profits and free cash flow have been inconsistent and there is no dividend. It is not a wonderful-and-fair compounder yet: returns on capital collapsed after 2022 and the EV Charging business is under competitive pressure. It is also not a clean freeroll because there is no hard valuation floor near the current price. The best fit is a low-confidence Rule Breaker / turnaround-in-a-growth-wave: grid congestion, storage and electrification create a real runway, but Alfen still has to prove it can capture that wave profitably.

### Right Place and Time — 0.78 / 0.82

Alfen sits in a structurally attractive area: European electrification, grid congestion, distributed generation, energy storage, EV charging and energy security. Management’s Q1 2026 release points to permitting reforms for grid-critical infrastructure, Dutch DSOs’ 2026-2028 investment plans, strong European BEV registration growth, and continued demand for utility-scale and C&I storage. ACER also describes a major upscaling in European distribution-grid investment trends.

This is the strongest part of the case. The score is not higher because the opportunity is attractive for the whole industry, not uniquely for Alfen, and some demand is lumpy/project-based rather than smooth compounding.

### Top Dog — 0.45 / 0.65

Alfen has credible niches, especially Dutch grid equipment, Finnish Elkamo products, integrated storage projects and European EV chargers. It delivered **833 substations** in Q1 2026 and says transport-distribution-station deployments in the Netherlands have increased by roughly tenfold from its standardised turnkey approach.

But it does not look like a category-defining top dog. EV Charging revenue fell **19.0%** in Q1 2026 and **21.2%** in FY2025 under competitive pressure, with average sales price declining. Energy Storage is growing but project-based and competitive. Alfen is a relevant specialist, not an obvious winner-takes-most platform.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.42 / 0.62

Alfen’s advantages are practical rather than impregnable: European manufacturing, long grid-operator relationships, domain know-how, product certification, local service, and the ability to package grids, charging and storage into integrated solutions. Those matter in critical infrastructure where reliability and local execution count.

The July 2026 CATL sodium-ion agreement adds access to a differentiated chemistry and strengthens supplier/product breadth. It does not yet prove pricing power or exclusivity, and the disclosed 5 GWh deployment target is not the same as contracted Alfen revenue.

The moat is still narrow. Gross margins are decent but not exceptional, group adjusted gross margin was **28.1%** in 2025 and **26.2%** in Q1 2026, and EV Charging pricing pressure shows customers have alternatives. The storage business also carries lower margins when large projects dominate the mix. A well-funded competitor could pressure most lines.

### Evidence of Durable, Accelerating Growth — 0.50 / 0.78

The growth record is mixed. Revenue grew from **€439.9m in 2022** to **€504.5m in 2023**, then fell to **€487.6m in 2024** and **€435.6m in 2025**. Q1 2026 rebounded **25% YoY**, but management’s full-year guidance implies only **0%-9%** growth versus 2025 and a front-loaded year.

Interpretation: there is visible cyclical/project rebound and operating leverage, not yet durable compounding. Energy Storage backlog of **€130m** at 31 March 2026 (**€82m** for 2026, **€48m** for 2027) supports near-term revenue, but the broader engine still needs several quarters of evidence.

### Organic Growth over Acquisitive Growth — 0.82 / 0.75

The current thesis is mostly organic: product renewal, European expansion, local service teams, grid projects, ESS backlog conversion and charger feature rollout. Yahoo statements show negligible goodwill (€0.1m) and no large acquisition fingerprint. Diluted share count has also been broadly stable around 21.7m.

The caveat is that organic growth quality is not the same as organic growth itself. Alfen is not buying growth, which is good, but it still has to prove the organic initiatives can restore margins and sustained revenue growth.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.42 / 0.78

CEO Michael Colijn joined in late 2025 and has moved quickly: cost control, FTE reduction, a business-unit structure from May 2026, local-for-local sales/service expansion, and renewed focus on customer needs, product excellence and execution. The actions and language are sensible, but there is not yet a demonstrated operating record.

Leadership continuity is now a larger concern. The 2025 annual report records the exits of the former CEO and CCO, and CFO Onno Krap then stepped down effective **1 July 2026** after roughly two years at Alfen. Bart Meussen, an experienced transformation finance executive, became interim CFO while the board searches for a permanent successor. Not all departures imply dysfunction, but turnover across three senior roles during a difficult operating reset lowers the score. H1 execution and the permanent-CFO appointment are the next evidence points.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.42 / 0.80

The 2025 remuneration report supports a mixed rather than unknown assessment. On-target STI is **50% of base salary** (maximum 75%); 2025 financial measures were revenue, adjusted EBITDA and inventory, while non-financial measures covered emissions, safety, product compliance and IT. The 2025-2027 LTI grants conditional shares worth **100% of base salary** at target, measured over three years and followed by a two-year holding period. Its 65% financial weighting uses revenue growth and adjusted EBITDA margin, with the remainder tied to quality, customer satisfaction and emissions.

The multi-year equity and holding period are positives, as are explicit customer/quality measures and share-ownership guidelines. The soft spots are material: most financial incentive weight still rewards size and adjusted accounting profit rather than per-share value, FCF or ROIC; key management collectively held **no ordinary shares at 31 December 2025**, only conditional rights; and Yahoo's broader insider-ownership estimate remains about **0.5%**. No recent insider transactions were available in Yahoo's feed. Confidence is higher after reading the report, but the score remains low-middling until the new CEO/CFO build meaningful ownership and the 2026 plan demonstrates stronger per-share-value alignment.

### Customer Devotion — 0.48 / 0.45

There is some customer stickiness in grid and project work: Alfen cites longstanding Dutch grid-operator relationships, standardised turnkey deployments and local service expansion. For infrastructure customers, reliability, certification and delivery capability can matter more than brand love.

But customer-devotion evidence is weak. There are no retention, NPS, renewal, churn or clear customer advocacy metrics in the sources used. EV Charging price pressure and uneven public-order patterns argue against strong devotion in that segment. Score stays middling until customer evidence improves.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair fails on consistent earnings and durable returns: net income swung from **€53.0m** in 2022 to **€29.7m** in 2023, **-€27.0m** in 2024 and roughly breakeven in 2025, while ROIC fell from high levels to negative/near-zero. Cash-cow fails because FCF has been volatile and no dividend/buyback thesis exists. Freeroll has plausible catalysts, but downside support is weak at **2.1x book**, about **19x EV/EBITDA** on current depressed EBITDA, and no dividend or asset floor close to price.

## Overall

Alfen is an interesting but messy energy-transition specialist. The industry setup is good and Q1 2026 showed real improvement, especially ESS and SGS. The stock could work if the transformation lifts margins, ESS backlog converts cleanly, and EV Charging stabilises after product renewal.

The negatives are substantial: weak moat, EV Charging competition, lumpy ESS projects, low current profitability, recent losses, modest current returns on capital, senior-leadership turnover and little direct management ownership. This is not a high-conviction compounder yet. It belongs on watch as a recovery/growth-wave candidate, not near the top of the allocation list.

Canonical valuation uses **Rule Breakers weighted-average**, with **TAM-capture at 100% weight**. Exit-multiple is omitted because current earnings are roughly breakeven and FY2026 consensus EPS of about €0.20 would imply a noisy, not-yet-underwritable current P/E anchor. The **16 August 2026** quote was **€13.36**, only 0.4% below the model's €13.41 entry price, so the stored valuation inputs and ROI were left unchanged pending the H1 release.

TAM-capture inputs:

- Entry price: **€13.41**; shares: **21.74m**; current market cap about **€292m**.
- Terminal horizon: **7 years**.
- TAM: **€50b** addressable annual European opportunity across grid equipment, electrification infrastructure, EV charging and storage integration by the early 2030s. This is intentionally broad, so capture is kept modest.
- Capture: **1.5%**, implying **€750m** terminal revenue. That is roughly an 8% revenue CAGR from 2025, so it is not heroic if Alfen truly participates in the European electrification buildout.
- Margin: **8% EBITDA**, above today’s 5%-6% adjusted EBITDA margin but below high-quality electrical-equipment leaders.
- Exit multiple: **12x EBITDA**, using Eaton (ETN) only as a broad mature electrical-equipment reference, haircut for Alfen’s smaller scale, project mix and weaker moat.
- Dilution: **0.5%/yr**; terminal net debt held at **€20.7m**.

This produces a TAM-capture / weighted-average ROI of about **12.7% annualized** if the thesis works. The return is not bad, but it is highly execution-dependent: the model assumes Alfen nearly doubles revenue, expands EBITDA margin, and deserves a mature infrastructure-equipment multiple. Because those odds are far from certain, I keep **agent_score at 0.48** despite the double-digit scenario ROI.

## Thesis Connections

Potential FIT: European electrification and grid bottlenecks. Alfen gives exposure to grid reinforcement, distributed energy storage and charging infrastructure without betting on a single commodity. The connection is real, but current quality is not strong enough for a high-conviction thesis slot.

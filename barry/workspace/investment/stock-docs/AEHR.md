# AEHR — Aehr Test Systems, Inc.

## Business Overview

Aehr Test Systems sells semiconductor test and burn-in equipment, especially FOX wafer-level burn-in/test systems and Sonoma package-level burn-in systems. Its pitch is that AI accelerators, silicon photonics, automotive, industrial, and power semiconductors increasingly need reliability screening before expensive packaging or mission-critical deployment. Revenue is equipment/project driven, so orders and customer timing matter a lot more than a smooth subscription-like run-rate.

The current setup is a sharp rebound story priced as if the rebound already worked. At the 1 August close of **$79.98**, Yahoo values AEHR at **$2.61B**, **52x TTM sales**, with negative TTM EBITDA, net income and FCF. The 2026 10-K confirms FY26 revenue of only **$50.0M**, down 15%, and a $7.1m net loss, but year-end backlog reached **$80.6M** from $15.2m and net cash rose above $106m after a large at-the-market equity raise.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.85 / 0.85

The industry setup is genuinely attractive. AI accelerators, advanced packaging, high-power devices, silicon photonics, automotive and industrial semiconductors all raise the cost of field failures and of discovering bad die after packaging. The 10-K documents higher silicon-photonics system revenue, expanding FOX/Sonoma positioning for AI accelerators and HPC processors, and $80.6m of year-end backlog. Interpretation: the burn-in/reliability niche is in the right part of the semiconductor cycle if AI hardware spending persists.

The companion market analysis bounds the directly relevant global wafer/package/die burn-in and reliability-equipment market at **$0.85b in 2026** and **$2.4b in 2036**, deliberately excluding general functional ATE and outsourced test service. Confidence is not higher because diversified vendors do not disclose burn-in-only revenue and current peer shares are analyst estimates.

### Top Dog — 0.45 / 0.70

Aehr appears differentiated in wafer-level burn-in, combining FOX wafer-level systems with Sonoma package-level burn-in and automation. The 10-K reports thousands of systems installed and 125 active patents, but Aehr is still a small equipment vendor with lumpy revenue and larger competitors have deeper resources. The score reflects a plausible niche leader, not a proven category king.

Firm-up source: platform-level installed-base data and competitive win/loss commentary in future results or investor materials; the 10-K now supplies customer concentration.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.40 / 0.65

The advantage case is technical fit, 125 active patents, proprietary software/contactors and customer qualification: once a reliability solution is qualified into production, switching is not trivial. But the moat is not yet strongly evidenced in economics. FY26 gross margin fell to **35.3%** from 40.6% and 49.1% in the prior two years because of assembly, warranty, freight and tariff pressure, and price competition persists in less advanced systems.

Firm-up source: product-level gross margin and repeat-order evidence; especially whether the lead hyperscale PLBI customer converts forecasts into multi-system production orders without margin erosion.

### Durable Growth — 0.58 / 0.85

This remains the make-or-break trait. FY26 revenue fell **15.2%** to $50.0m after falling 10.9% in FY25, and gross margin compressed again. The positive leading evidence is much stronger: backlog rose to **$80.6m** from $15.2m, management expects a return to profitability in FY27, and Yahoo's three-analyst FY27 revenue consensus jumped to about **$136m** with the following year near $202m. Interpretation: a credible re-acceleration is ordered but not delivered; confidence is high in the setup, not in durable execution.

Firm-up event: FY27 quarterly conversion of the $80.6m backlog into revenue and recovery in gross margin and FCF.

### Organic Growth — 0.58 / 0.75

The core rebound thesis is organic demand for FOX and Sonoma systems, especially silicon photonics and AI/HPC. The Incal acquisition expanded packaged-part reliability exposure and FY25 comparisons, but Aehr is not a roll-up. FY26's $10.4m increase in wafer-level systems revenue was organic and offset part of a $19.9m wafer-contactor decline tied to EV softness.

Firm-up source: FY2026 annual filing revenue bridge and any split between legacy Aehr and Incal/Sonoma contribution.

### Management Backing — 0.58 / 0.70

Management is articulating the right markets and qualified a subcontractor to integrate, test and ship Sonoma systems directly, materially expanding capacity. The concern is per-share execution: FY26 remained loss-making and the company raised **$97.4m net through its ATM**, insulating the balance sheet by issuing equity before backlog conversion. The rating is neutral-positive, not a management premium.

Firm-up source: call transcript detail on capacity, lead-customer timing, and whether guidance is met without working-capital stress.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.25 / 0.85

Shareholders are paying a high price for a small, volatile business while dilution is material. FY26 stock-based compensation was **$6.76m**, or 13.5% of revenue, the equity-plan reserve increased by 2.5m shares, and the ATM supplied $97.4m of net financing. Shares outstanding reached **32.62m** by 20 July. Balance-sheet risk fell, but per-share value leaked before the business proved its ramp.

Firm-up source: 2026 proxy compensation targets and updated diluted share-count trend.

### Customer Devotion — 0.45 / 0.75

Repeat/follow-on orders, deposits and the backlog imply customers value the systems where burn-in prevents expensive downstream failures. Concentration remains high but improved: the five largest customers generated 70% of FY26 revenue, versus 77% and 93% in FY25/FY24; the top three were 26%, 14% and 11%. This is evidence of customer commitment, not broad independent devotion.

Firm-up source: named customer case studies, repeat-order history, and backlog conversion by customer cohort.

Other strategies lost because AEHR is not currently a wonderful-and-fair/cash-cow profile: earnings, FCF, margins, and return stability are negative. It is also not a clean freeroll because downside is not supported by balance-sheet value; the market cap is many times book/cash.

## Overall

AEHR has a real rule-breaker shape: small company, big technical wave, possible design-win inflection, and a product category that could matter more as AI hardware gets bigger, hotter, and more expensive to package. The 10-K makes the rebound more credible through $80.6m of backlog, capacity expansion and improved customer diversification. The problem is valuation and per-share discipline. At 52x TTM sales with negative earnings/FCF, the stock needs a very large FY27+ ramp, margin recovery and limited further dilution just to justify the quote.

My judgement: interesting business, bad risk/reward at this quote. Keep watching for execution proof or a much lower entry price.

# IFT.AX — Infratil Limited

## Business Overview

Infratil is a New Zealand infrastructure investment company listed on the NZX and ASX. It owns and actively manages a portfolio spanning digital infrastructure, renewable energy, telecommunications, airports, and healthcare. The largest value drivers are CDC data centres, One NZ, Longroad Energy, Wellington Airport, Qscan, Kao Data, and renewable-development platforms including Gurin, Galileo, and Mint.

The FY26 result is best read as a portfolio/NAV story rather than a conventional operating-company P&L. Infratil reported proportionate operational EBITDAF of NZ$989m, up 11%, total asset value of NZ$20.6b, and post-management-fee NAV of NZ$16.26/share. Growth is being pushed hardest into CDC and Longroad: CDC has more than 1GW contracted and management expects EBITDAF to exceed A$1b in FY28, while Longroad is targeting US$1b run-rate EBITDAF by CY29/30.

## Trait Assessment

### Downside Support / Valuation Floor — 0.62 / 0.72

The main floor is NAV rather than earnings. FY26 portfolio asset value was NZ$20.624b, with net asset value of NZ$16.26/share post management fees. At the ASX price of A$12.49 and NZD/AUD of ~0.8223, NAV is about A$13.37/share, so the stock traded at roughly a 6-7% discount to stated NAV.

That is real support, but not a hard floor. Much of NAV is based on private DCF valuations for assets such as CDC, Longroad, One NZ, Qscan and Wellington Airport, so discount rates, data-centre execution, renewable-development marks, and leverage can move the anchor. I used A$11.25 as the conservative downside floor: about a 16% haircut to FY26 NAV and ~10% below the current price.

Confidence is moderate because Infratil publishes independent valuation updates, but the private marks are still model-sensitive. Firmer evidence would come from realised asset sales near carrying value and the next CDC/Longroad valuation updates.

### Fundamental Stability / No Value Trap — 0.72 / 0.70

The portfolio is not a simple melting ice cube. FY26 proportionate operational EBITDAF rose 11%, FY27 guidance implies NZ$1.3-1.4b of proportionate operational EBITDAF excluding corporate costs, and several core assets have structural demand drivers: AI/data-centre demand for CDC, electricity demand and decarbonisation for Longroad, and recurring telecom cash flows from One NZ.

There are soft spots. One NZ's independent valuation fell 8.7% to NZ$3.4b, Galileo's value was marked down, RHCNZ EBITDAF slipped, and reported free cash flow is negative while Infratil continues heavy growth capex. Leverage is meaningful, but the BBB+ S&P rating and active divestments reduce near-term balance-sheet stress.

This scores as durable but not bulletproof: the asset mix is improving, yet the thesis depends on continued capital-market access and execution in private infrastructure assets. Confidence is not higher because the consolidated accounts obscure asset-level economics.

### Mispriced for an Identifiable Reason — 0.58 / 0.62

The likely mispricing is a holding-company/private-valuation discount plus execution scepticism. The market is being asked to capitalise long-duration data-centre and renewable-development growth, while also trusting private marks and funding requirements. That deserves some discount.

The variant view is that the discount may be too harsh if CDC's contracted capacity and Longroad's pipeline convert into the EBITDAF trajectory management has laid out. CDC's contracted capacity alone is expected to generate roughly A$2b of annualised contracted EBITDAF when fully deployed, and Longroad has 5.3GW total portfolio capacity with a path to 13.7GW by CY29 in management's forecast.

The edge is only moderate: the discount to NAV is not huge, the market already knows the AI/data-centre story, and the upside depends on execution rather than a hidden asset no one has noticed.

### Identifiable, Time-Bound Catalyst — 0.68 / 0.70

The catalysts are concrete but multi-step: CDC converting contracted capacity into billing/EBITDAF through FY27-FY28, Longroad bringing new renewable capacity online and progressing data-centre-adjacent opportunities, and capital recycling via divestments such as Contact Energy and the Qscan sale process. FY27 guidance provides a near-term scoreboard.

The window is 1-2 years for CDC EBITDAF step-up and FY27 guidance delivery, with a longer 3-4 year Longroad runway. The main derailers are build delays, customer activation timing, power/land constraints, higher discount rates, and funding/dilution if growth capital becomes expensive.

### Why the other strategies lost

Cash Cow loses because consolidated free cash flow is negative and the dividend yield is modest; this is not a mature distribution story. Wonderful and Fair loses because Infratil's consolidated ROIC/FCF profile is too lumpy and asset-valuation-led for a clean owner-earnings compounder. Rule Breakers has thematic exposure to AI infrastructure and renewables, but Infratil is a holding company rather than the direct top-dog operating platform.

## Overall

IFT.AX is a fair-quality infrastructure/NAV freeroll, not a screaming bargain. The attractive part is the combination of a modest discount to stated NAV, strong growth assets in CDC and Longroad, and identifiable FY27-FY28 catalysts. The detractors are private-valuation sensitivity, leverage, negative consolidated FCF during the investment phase, and only moderate upside from today's price unless CDC/Longroad marks keep stepping up.

Possible FIT connection: picks-and-shovels exposure to AI infrastructure and electrification through CDC, Kao Data, Longroad, and renewables. It is a diversified way to own power-plus-compute infrastructure, but with holding-company complexity and private-market valuation risk.

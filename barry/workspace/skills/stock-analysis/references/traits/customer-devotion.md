# Trait: Customer Devotion

**What we're looking for:**
The people who pay for this company — consumers, businesses, clinicians, developers — genuinely
love what it offers. They stick, they spend more over time, and they recommend it to others. This
is *demand-side devotion*, and it is the same signal whether the customer is a teenager or a
procurement committee; only the way it shows up changes.

**Why this matters:**
Customer enthusiasm is a leading indicator of business health. When customers love something they
churn less, expand spend, and recruit new customers for free, which lowers acquisition cost and
compounds growth. For an early-stage company this devotion is often the earliest hard evidence that
the product is winning before the financials fully show it.

**Love, not lock-in:**
This trait measures stickiness born of *preference*, not stickiness born of *captivity*. High
switching costs, contractual lock-in, or being the only option also keep customers from leaving —
but that is a moat, scored under [Sustainable Competitive Advantage](sustainable-advantage.md), not
devotion. The test is counterfactual: if leaving were easy and free, would they still stay and
expand? Customers who remain only because they are trapped score in the bottom band here, even
where that captivity is a genuine strength elsewhere.

**Signals to look for:**

The underlying signal is identical across business models; read whichever set fits the customer.

*Consumer (B2C):*

* High Net Promoter Score or equivalent satisfaction metrics
* Strong app-store ratings, review volumes, and engagement
* Organic social-media enthusiasm (not paid influencer campaigns)
* User communities that form around the product; word-of-mouth growth
* Customers visibly upset when they *cannot* use the product

*Business (B2B):*

* **Net revenue retention / net dollar retention above 100%** driven by voluntary expansion
  (more seats, modules, or volume from existing customers) — a reported, auditable number and the
  single strongest signal here
* Low **logo churn** and high renewal where switching is genuinely easy, not contractually forced
* Strong standing on verified peer-review platforms (G2, Gartner Peer Insights)
* **Named reference customers, case studies, and unprompted advocacy** — customers willing to sell
  the product for them
* Becoming the de-facto standard or default choice in their niche

**Key questions:**

* Do customers stay and spend more *by choice*, or only because leaving is painful?
* Is the product loved and recommended, or merely tolerated?
* Where would the company be if a customer's switching cost dropped to zero tomorrow?

**Scoring guidance:**

* **0.70–1.00** Passionate advocates; expansion and word-of-mouth; customers recruit other customers
* **0.40–0.69** Respected and useful, but does not inspire strong loyalty or voluntary expansion
* **0.00–0.39** Customers use it because they must (captive), or churn is structurally high

Score continuously within these bands. **Confidence is recorded separately** (see
[scoring.md](../scoring.md)): a young company with little retention history yet should be scored on
the evidence it has and flagged with low confidence — not marked down on score for being early.

**Documentation:**

* The retention / loyalty metric that fits the model — NRR or net dollar retention, logo churn, NPS,
  renewal rate, app-store ratings — with the value and source
* Evidence of voluntary expansion or word-of-mouth: cohort expansion, named references, user
  communities, unpaid social enthusiasm, peer-review standing
* An explicit note on whether stickiness is preference or lock-in, so it is not double-counted with
  [Sustainable Competitive Advantage](sustainable-advantage.md)
* Any first-hand or anecdotal use — clearly labelled as anecdote, not data

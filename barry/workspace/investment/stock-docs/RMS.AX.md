# RMS.AX — Ramelius Resources Limited

## Business Overview

Ramelius Resources is an Australian gold producer and explorer. It sells gold from Western Australian mining hubs, so revenue is driven mainly by ounces produced, realised gold price, all-in sustaining cost (AISC), reserve replacement, and project execution rather than by customer-level pricing power.

FY25 was a record year: 301,664oz of production at A$1,551/oz AISC, revenue of A$1.2b, underlying EBITDA of A$818.6m, statutory NPAT of A$474.2m, underlying free cash flow of A$694.9m, and cash/gold on hand of A$809.7m. Ramelius also completed the Spartan Resources combination in July 2025, expanding its Mt Magnet/Dalgaranga platform and targeting +500,000oz pa by FY30. The June 2026 update showed FY26 production guidance achieved at 192,182oz and A$649.6m cash/gold at 30 June 2026, before the July 2026 stamp duty payment.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.58 / 0.70

The earnings are real and cash-backed, but not highly predictable. Yahoo annual data shows positive net income in FY22-FY24 and cumulative FCF/net income of 1.44x; FY25 annual-report figures were materially stronger again, with NPAT up 119% to A$474.2m. The caveat is that gold miners are commodity-price and grade-cycle businesses: FY25 benefited from high-grade Cue/Penny ore, lower hedge commitments, and a much higher realised gold price. This is not the smooth, forecastable earning power of a classic wonderful business.

Confidence would firm up with a longer 7-10 year through-cycle margin, production, reserve-replacement and hedge-book table.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.78 / 0.75

This is the strongest part of the case. FY25 underlying FCF was A$694.9m, while Yahoo shows FY24 FCF of A$331.6m and TTM FCF around A$402-411m. The latest Yahoo TTM FCF margin is about 34%, and FY25 management reported a dividend payout ratio of 29% of FCF. The cash is not capital-light in the software/brand sense — mining needs sustaining and growth capex — but at current gold prices Ramelius is throwing off substantial real cash even while funding projects and exploration.

Confidence would firm up with the full FY26 cash-flow statement and a clearer sustaining-vs-growth capex split.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.52 / 0.65

The operating record is impressive but structurally cyclical. Ramelius has met production and cost guidance for six consecutive years and increased Mineral Resources to 12Moz and Ore Reserves to 2.4Moz at 30 June 2025. The Spartan integration, Dalgaranga, expanded Mt Magnet and Rebecca-Roe give it a credible multi-year production path. Against that, gold mining is a depleting-resource business: reserves must continually be replaced, grades can surprise both ways, and margins are exposed to gold price, labour, energy and contractor inflation.

This is durable for a miner, not durable like a branded consumer or software compounder.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.25 / 0.75

Ramelius is a price taker. Its advantage is operational execution, mine quality, reserve base, cost control and balance-sheet strength — not the ability to raise prices. FY25 AISC of A$1,551/oz and Mt Magnet AISC of A$1,314/oz were excellent, but the realised gold price was set by the market. That makes the moat narrower and more cyclical than the cash generation suggests.

### Capital Allocation — 0.72 / 0.75

Capital allocation has been disciplined. Ramelius kept a net-cash balance, paid a seventh consecutive dividend, added a maiden interim dividend, executed a A$250m buyback program with A$140.7m completed by the June 2026 quarter, and agreed to sell the non-core Edna May hub for A$300m while focusing on Mt Magnet/Dalgaranga and Rebecca-Roe. The Spartan transaction is strategically logical because Dalgaranga sits close to Mt Magnet and added high-grade Never Never/Pepper resources, but integration and project-delivery risk remain.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.58 / 0.65

Returns have improved sharply but are not yet proven through a full cycle. Yahoo-derived ROIC rose from 2.4% in FY22 to 6.8% in FY23 and 16.5% in FY24, with ROCE at 19.6% in FY24; FY25 profitability was stronger again. The latest level creates value, but the multi-year average is modest and gold miners can look exceptional near the top of a gold-price/grade cycle.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.95 / 0.90

The balance sheet is excellent. Yahoo shows A$658.7m cash versus A$88.0m debt, net cash of about A$570.7m, current ratio 3.3x and quick ratio 2.8x. FY25 annual report disclosed A$809.7m cash/gold at 30 June 2025 and the June 2026 update disclosed A$649.6m cash/gold at 30 June 2026. For a cyclical miner, net cash is exactly the posture we want.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.72 / 0.65

The company has long-serving leadership and strong execution under Mark Zeptner, but the assets, processes and operating hubs matter more than one individual. The Spartan integration adds depth but also transition complexity. This is moderate-low key-person risk, not founder-dependent fragility.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.70 / 0.60

The FY25 remuneration report ties incentives to safety, production, AISC and business outcomes, which are owner-relevant for a miner. There was some board discretion upward in FY25 after record performance and guidance outperformance; that is understandable but worth watching so pay does not become too forgiving. Insider ownership is modest at about 2.6% per Yahoo, so alignment is good but not owner-operator level.

### Why the other strategies lost

Wonderful-and-fair loses mainly on pricing power and through-cycle predictability: Ramelius is a well-run miner, not a structurally price-setting compounder. Freeroll has real catalysts — Dalgaranga/Mt Magnet integration, Rebecca-Roe approvals/development, Edna May sale, buybacks — but the price is not close enough to a hard floor; book value is around A$2.06/share versus a A$2.98 price, and net cash is only about 10% of price. Rule-breakers loses because gold mining is not an emerging, winner-takes-most market and there is no customer-devotion angle.

## Overall

Ramelius is a high-quality gold miner with a fortress balance sheet, strong cash generation, a credible growth pipeline and shareholder-friendly capital returns. The main detractors are commodity cyclicality, limited pricing power, reserve-depletion risk, and execution risk across Spartan/Dalgaranga, Mt Magnet expansion and Rebecca-Roe.

As a stock, this looks more like a cyclical cash generator with growth projects than a forever compounder. It can be attractive if bought with gold-price and execution risk respected, but the current valuation already capitalises a fair amount of the recent strength.

## Valuation

I valued RMS under the cash-cow exit-multiple lens using FCF because cash conversion is central and mining earnings are capex-sensitive. Inputs: A$2.975 price; A$0.218 current FCF/share, triangulated from Yahoo TTM FCF of roughly A$411m over ~1.89b shares; 8% aggregate FCF growth for five years, reflecting the +500koz FY30 ambition but haircut for project and gold-price risk; 10x exit P/FCF, below the implied entry multiple to avoid relying on rerating; -1.5% dilution for buybacks/share-count shrinkage; and 2.7% dividend yield.

This is deliberately conservative: a stronger gold price or successful Mt Magnet/Rebecca-Roe ramp would beat it, while lower gold prices or project slippage would compress both FCF and the multiple. The resulting ROI from the valuation script is recorded in front matter. My agent score is 0.58: good business execution and balance sheet, but not enough hard downside support or moat quality to score higher at this price.

## Thesis Connections

Possible fit as a gold exposure / inflation-hedge cash generator with disciplined capital returns and organic growth optionality. It does not obviously fit a structural technology or consumer adoption thesis.

## Watch

- July 2026 full quarterly activities report: final AISC, cash movement, hedge position and FY27 guidance.
- Dalgaranga/Never Never ramp and Mt Magnet mill expansion milestones, especially cost and schedule variance.
- Rebecca-Roe DFS/FID, approvals, capex and first-production timeline.
- Gold price sensitivity and hedge-book commitments.
- Reserve replacement versus depletion; grade reconciliation at Cue, Penny, Never Never and Rebecca-Roe.
- Completion of the Edna May sale and actual cash/share consideration received.
- Buyback discipline: repurchases should remain price-sensitive, not automatic.

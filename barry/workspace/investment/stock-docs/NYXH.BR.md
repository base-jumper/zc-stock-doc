# NYXH.BR — Nyxoah SA

## Business Overview

Nyxoah is a Belgian medtech company commercialising **Genio**, a leadless, battery-free hypoglossal neurostimulation system for obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). Revenue comes from implant systems and related disposable patches, with the important commercial inflection now being U.S. launch after FDA approval in August 2025.

The product is differentiated from the incumbent hypoglossal nerve stimulation model by being leadless and battery-free. Genio has had CE Mark since 2019; Nyxoah says it later expanded its CE indication to Complete Concentric Collapse patients, a group it says is contraindicated in competitor therapy. In the U.S., FDA approval covers a subset of adult moderate-to-severe OSA patients with AHI >=15 and <=65.

Financially this is still a very early commercial-stage company. FY25 net revenue was **€10.0m**, up **122%**, with Q4 net revenue **€5.6m** after the first full U.S. sales quarter. Q1 2026 global net revenue rose to **€6.4m**, including **€4.3m U.S. net revenue**; final Q2 net revenue was **€7.7m** globally and **€5.2m** in the U.S., up **21%** and **22%** sequentially. Management retained FY26 worldwide net revenue guidance of **€36m–€40m** and **60%–62%** gross margin. The cost is heavy: Q2 operating loss was **€20.6m**, H1 operating cash outflow was **€38.1m**, and H1 net loss was **€48.9m**. Q2 gross margin was **60%**, down from 63% a year earlier because of a higher U.S. mix, while commercial and R&D spend still dominate.

Balance-sheet risk improved materially in June 2026. Nyxoah raised **$95m** equity and received **$15m** from the European Investment Bank, for **$110m** aggregate new capital to accelerate U.S. commercialization. Cash, cash equivalents and financial assets were **€97.8m** at 30 June, about 2.6 times H1 operating cash burn. That came with major dilution: period-end shares were **100.1m**, versus 43.0m ordinary shares at December 2025 in Yahoo financial statements. At the 7 August 2026 close of **€1.268**, market cap is about **€127m**.

No universal disqualifier found in the sources reviewed. Inspire's 2025 patent suit seeks damages and an injunction, and Nyxoah has countersued, but the H1 filing says the matter is too early to predict or quantify and records no loss provision. It is a material risk to monitor, not yet evidence of a business-model kill-shot; immediate insolvency risk is also low after the June financing.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.75 / confidence 0.75

OSA is a large, under-penetrated clinical problem and hypoglossal nerve stimulation is already a validated category, helped by the existence of a much larger incumbent. Nyxoah now has the hard prerequisites for a U.S. medtech launch: FDA approval, trained surgeons, active accounts, reimbursement codes/approval experience, and new capital. Q1 2026 also reported 100% prior-authorization approval on reviewed submissions with commercial payers and the WISeR program.

The caveat is that the addressable market is not the entire OSA population. Genio's FDA indication is a subset of moderate-to-severe OSA patients, and adoption depends on surgeons, sleep physicians, payers, patient willingness for an implant, and competition from CPAP and other therapies. Confidence would improve with independent U.S. procedure-volume data and payer-denial trends.

### Top Dog — 0.38 / confidence 0.70

Nyxoah has a differentiated technology but is not the top dog economically. Inspire Medical Systems is the established HGNS incumbent, with Yahoo showing about **$915m** TTM revenue versus Nyxoah's **€15m** TTM revenue. Nyxoah's first full U.S. commercial quarter was encouraging, but it remains a challenger rather than the category leader.

The score is not lower because Genio has a credible wedge: leadless/battery-free architecture, CE indication expansion into CCC patients, FDA PMA approval, and early U.S. account activation. To firm this up, watch real U.S. share gains: implants per active account, surgeon repeat rates, and whether Genio wins accounts already familiar with HGNS.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.45 / confidence 0.75

The moat candidates are real but not yet proven: regulatory approvals, clinical evidence from BLAST/BETTER SLEEP/DREAM, surgical training, manufacturing know-how, reimbursement work, and product differentiation versus an implanted battery/lead design. Q2 showed distribution ramp progress: 55 more U.S. surgeons trained, 89 more active high-volume accounts added, and 427 patients in prior authorization entering Q3. Those are meaningful barriers for a tiny medtech.

The moat is also fragile. Large competitors have more reps, deeper payer relationships, existing physician mindshare, and more clinical data. Q2 gross margin fell three points year on year to 60% as U.S. mix rose, so pricing/unit economics are not yet proven, and the reciprocal patent litigation with Inspire puts part of the IP position under direct challenge. Genio may be better for some patients, but better architecture does not automatically become durable pricing power or share. This reduces the score slightly to **0.45** while the primary filing raises confidence to **0.75**; independent head-to-head evidence, repeat surgeon use and litigation milestones would firm it up further.

### Durable Growth — 0.72 / confidence 0.80

Revenue growth is finally visible. FY25 net revenue rose **122%** to **€10.0m**; Q4 2025 net revenue rose **347% YoY** to **€5.6m**; Q1 2026 global net revenue was **€6.4m**, up sharply from **€1.1m** in Q1 2025. Final Q2 net revenue was **€7.7m** globally and **€5.2m** in the U.S., with U.S. revenue up **22%** sequentially and full-year **€36m–€40m** guidance reiterated.

This is still off a tiny base and Q2 U.S. sequential growth landed just below the prior 25%–30% guide, so the launch needs several more clean quarters. The company needs many years of high growth to justify the commercial infrastructure, and losses mean growth must eventually translate into high-margin recurring/consumable revenue rather than just subsidised launch volume.

### Organic Growth — 0.80 / confidence 0.75

The thesis is primarily organic: one core product platform, new geographies, trained surgeons, activated accounts, payer access and manufacturing scale-up. Recent financing is being used to accelerate the Genio launch rather than to roll up unrelated assets.

The risk is not acquisitive growth; it is capital intensity and dilution during the organic ramp.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.55 / confidence 0.55

Management executed a difficult regulatory/commercial transition: CE Mark, Nasdaq/Euronext listings, DREAM/FDA work, U.S. launch, reimbursement groundwork and a June 2026 financing package. Insider participation in the equity raise is positive, and the EIB loan is a useful external validation signal.

The mark is capped because the business has consumed large amounts of capital, shareholder dilution has been severe, and the June 2026 release also announced a process to transition to a U.S.-based CEO. That CEO shift may be strategically sensible for U.S. commercialization, but it adds execution uncertainty. Confidence would improve after the new CEO is named and Q2/Q3 launch metrics show the expanded sales force is productive.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.65 / confidence 0.55

Yahoo shows high insider ownership/holdings, and the financing release highlighted strong insider participation. That supports alignment. The counterweight is dilution: the June 2026 share count of **100.1m** is far above the December 2025 ordinary share count, and there are also subscription rights and convertible-bond conversion rights outstanding. Per-share value creation must be strong enough to outrun future issuance.

Confidence is only moderate because I have not reviewed the detailed remuneration policy, option strike schedule, or post-financing insider ownership by person.

### Customer Devotion — 0.50 / confidence 0.55

Evidence is promising but still early. Management cited positive surgeon/patient feedback and rapid U.S. training/account activation: **145 surgeons / 57 accounts** by December 2025, **207 surgeons / 91 active high-volume accounts** by March 2026, and **262 surgeons / 180 active high-volume accounts** by Q2 2026. The July Investor Day recap also cited **123 ordering customers**, **427 patients in prior authorization**, early launched accounts reaching about **15% share** in their first six months, and physician commentary highlighting Genio's bilateral stimulation/no-chest-incision differentiation.

This is not yet customer devotion. It is early launch uptake. The score needs real-world repeat usage, patient satisfaction, explant/revision rates, and independent surgeon preference data before it can move higher.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair and cash-cow fail on profitability, returns on capital, earnings quality and free cash flow. Freeroll has some catalyst/downside-support elements after the financing, but the value rests mainly on commercial execution in a large market rather than asset-backed downside or a discrete mispricing catalyst.

## Overall

Nyxoah is a legitimate rule-breaker candidate, but a risky one. The attraction is clear: FDA-approved differentiated medtech, early U.S. commercial traction, large OSA/HGNS market, and a stock price/market cap that imply scepticism after heavy dilution. If Genio becomes a credible second major platform in HGNS, upside can be large from a roughly **€127m** equity value.

The negatives are just as obvious: not the incumbent, tiny revenue base, very high cash burn, still-evolving reimbursement/commercial evidence, patent litigation, CEO transition, and major dilution. This is not a quality compounder yet; it is a financed U.S. launch option.

## Watch

Checked through **10 August 2026**; the 5 August Q2/H1 filing is incorporated above and no later company release or SEC filing was identified.

- Q3 results (currently indicated for **5 November 2026**): trained surgeons, active/ordering accounts, implants per account, and conversion of the **427** prior-authorisation patients.
- Cash runway: quarterly operating cash burn versus **€97.8m** cash/financial assets and H1 operating outflow of **€38.1m**.
- Unit economics/manufacturing: gross margin versus the **60%–62%** FY26 guide, Q1 yield issues, U.S. mix and consumable-patch replenishment costs.
- CEO transition: identity, U.S. commercial experience, and whether key technical/regulatory talent stays.
- Reimbursement: final CMS 2027 OPPS/ASC rates versus proposed **+12%/+15%**, commercial payer approval rates, Medicare friction, denials, and coding/payment changes.
- Inspire/Nyxoah patent litigation: claim construction, trial timing, settlement, injunction risk and any loss accrual.
- Dilution: subscription rights, convertible conversion at **€1.48**, RSUs, and any additional capital needs.

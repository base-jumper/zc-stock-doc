## Reasoning

By 2013, US adult tablet ownership had risen from 3% in 2010 to 34%, indicating a rapid consumer-electronics adoption cycle. A 75% ceiling reflects the tablet's broad utility and falling hardware prices while allowing for adults adequately served by smartphones or computers and for non-users deterred by the cost of an additional device. DVD players provide a fast, recent consumer-electronics analog; VCRs provide a slower replacement-cycle analog; home computers capture higher-cost general-purpose hardware; and cable TV anchors recurring cost and structural non-adoption. The four-point target history fits cleanly but remains early enough that the analog prior retains material weight; the mechanical blend weight is therefore kept.

## Issues

No admissible analog closely matches a portable, touch-first secondary computing device. The short 2010–2013 history makes both the apparent speed and the 75% ceiling uncertain, particularly because overlap with smartphones and computers could either expand the addressable use cases or limit the need for a separate device.

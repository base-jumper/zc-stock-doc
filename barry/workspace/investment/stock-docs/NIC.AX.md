# NIC.AX — Nickel Industries Limited

## Business Overview

Nickel Industries is an ASX-listed Indonesian nickel producer. It owns controlling interests in four RKEF projects producing nickel pig iron/matte, the Hengjaya Mine, 10% of the Huayue Nickel Cobalt HPAL project, and a rising stake in the Excelsior Nickel Cobalt (ENC) HPAL project. The business is shifting from stainless-steel/NPI exposure toward Class 1 nickel products for the EV battery chain through HPAL, while trying to secure ore supply through Hengjaya, Sampala and Siduarsi.

Profit is driven by LME nickel and NPI/MHP payability, ore grade and ore availability, Indonesian RKAB quotas, coal/electricity and sulphur costs, debt costs, and the ramp-up of ENC and future HPAL assets. The company reports in USD; the stock trades in AUD.

## Trait Assessment

### Downside Support / Valuation Floor — 0.55 / 0.68

There is some floor, but not a hard one. At A$0.89, NIC trades close to its 200-day average and above the A$0.66 52-week low. Yahoo shows about 2.4x book and about 2.0x tangible book on a simple quote-currency basis, but the reported financials are USD and the ASX price is AUD, so the statement-derived per-share floor needs FX care. Using the 52-week low / nickel-downturn level of ~A$0.66 as a practical trading floor gives roughly 26% downside from the current quote. The dividend yield is about 4%, and recent FCF covered dividends in 2025, but payout reliability is commodity-cycle dependent.

Confidence is below 0.8 because a proper floor should reconcile USD tangible book, Indonesian asset marks, minority interests, and replacement value. That needs a cleaner asset-by-asset NAV than Yahoo provides.

### Fundamental Stability / No Value Trap — 0.52 / 0.74

The business is not structurally obsolete, but it is highly cyclical and currently in a weak part of the nickel cycle. Revenue fell from US$1.88B in 2023 to US$1.65B in 2025, operating margin compressed from 15.1% to 7.0%, and NIC posted attributable losses in 2024 and 2025. Offsetting that, FCF stayed positive in the annual cash-flow data, Q1 2026 operations rebounded sharply, and management says high-cost nickel peers have been forced to suspend while NIC remained operating.

The diagnosis is cyclical/industry oversupply rather than terminal decline, but leverage and commodity exposure mean the floor can erode if nickel prices or Indonesian policy move against the company.

### Mispriced for an Identifiable Reason — 0.60 / 0.65

The market appears to be pricing NIC as a levered, cyclical nickel/NPI producer exposed to weak nickel prices, Indonesian permitting, project execution and related-party complexity. The variant view is that the market may be under-crediting the 2026 catalysts: a much larger 14.3Mt Hengjaya RKAB quota, Q1 margin recovery, ENC commissioning/ramp-up, and the option value from Sampala/CNE/TMI-style HPAL integration.

That is a plausible edge, not a slam dunk. The same facts can be read as higher complexity and more execution risk rather than hidden value. Analyst targets are supportive — Yahoo shows a mean target of A$1.258 and median A$1.20 — but analyst targets are not a thesis by themselves.

### Identifiable, Time-Bound Catalyst — 0.76 / 0.78

The catalyst is unusually concrete for a miner: ENC commissioning was expected to start in May 2026, run through July, and reach nameplate by around late October 2026; the Hengjaya Mine received a 14.3Mt 2026 RKAB sales licence, roughly a 60% uplift; and Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA from operations rose to US$135.6M from US$37.3M in Q4 2025. The June 2026 CNE/Sampala transaction adds a second-stage HPAL/ore-integration catalyst, with CNE commissioning expected mid-2027, but that is more conditional and involves related-party approvals.

Confidence is just under 0.8 because execution risk is real: ENC ramp timing already moved, HPAL costs are exposed to sulphur prices, and a March 2026 contractor fatality temporarily suspended some Hengjaya-related activity.

**Why other strategies lost:** Cash-cow loses because earnings are volatile, debt service is heavy, and dividends are not the core reliable return. Wonderful-and-fair loses on low/falling ROIC, weak earnings quality, commodity pricing and leverage. Rule-breakers has some EV-battery/HPAL runway, but NIC is still a capital-intensive commodity producer rather than a dominant high-growth platform with customer devotion or a defensible demand-side moat.

## Overall

NIC is a medium-quality freeroll/special-situation idea: tangible operational catalysts, cheap-ish forward earnings if the ramp works, and meaningful upside to analyst fair value, but also a lot of ways to get hurt. The case rests less on owning a wonderful business and more on a 12–18 month rerating as ENC ramps and Indonesian ore control tightens the market.

Main positives: low-cost Indonesian footprint, large Hengjaya ore base, improving Q1 2026 margins, strong HPAL economics at HNC, and concrete Class 1 nickel diversification. Main detractors: commodity price exposure, high debt/low coverage at trough, recent losses, dilution/acquisition history, related-party complexity, and ESG/safety/policy risks.

## Valuation

I model this as a freeroll using the asymmetric-payoff method. Inputs are A$0.89 current price, A$0.66 floor, A$1.25 fair value, 60% catalyst probability and 1.5 years. The floor is the recent nickel-downturn/52-week-low area rather than a rigorous NAV. The fair value is close to Yahoo's analyst mean target of A$1.258 and assumes ENC ramp plus RKAB-driven ore sales are visible enough for the market to value NIC closer to its forward earnings and HPAL option value.

This gives roughly 26% downside to the floor and 40% upside to fair value, or about 1.6x upside/downside before probability weighting. The payoff is acceptable but not spectacular; dividends are excluded from the model and would add modest carry if maintained. My judgement score is 0.54: worth tracking and possibly attractive if the ENC ramp keeps hitting milestones, but not clean enough to treat as a high-conviction compounder.

# SGH.AX — SGH Limited (Seven Group Holdings)

## Business Overview

SGH Ltd is an Australian diversified industrial conglomerate, majority-controlled by the Stokes family (via Australian Capital Equity, ~68-73%). It generates ~85% of EBIT from three wholly-owned industrial businesses:

- **WesTrac** — the authorized Caterpillar dealer for Western Australia, NSW and the ACT; supplies and services heavy mining and construction equipment. FY26 revenue $5.8B (-6%), EBIT $647M (+1%), EBIT margin 11.2% (+76bp), ROCE 24.6%. A 6% rise in the annuity-style services business ($4.1B, +6% on parts volume and rebuild activity) offset a 26% normalisation in capital sales (machinery) to $1.6B.
- **Boral** — Australia's largest building-materials group (cement, concrete, quarries, asphalt). FY26 revenue $3.8B (+5%), EBIT $535M (+14%), EBIT margin 14.1% (+113bp), ROCE 19.5%. Volume growth (concrete +7%, quarry +1%, cement +2%) with 2-3% price gains.
- **Coates** — Australia's largest equipment-hire business. FY26 revenue $1.0B (-3%), EBIT $270M (-7%), EBIT margin 26.7% (-111bp), on competitive pressure and lower financial utilization.

It also holds a 30% stake in **Beach Energy** (BPT, oil/gas producer — FY26 contribution $107M EBIT, -18% on lower production/prices) and a ~20% stake in **Southern Cross Media** (media), plus a 15.5% interest in the **Crux LNG** backfill project (first gas H2 CY2027).

FY26 (12 months ended 30 June 2026): revenue $10,589M (-1.4%), underlying EBIT $1,554M (+1.1%), EBIT margin 14.7% (+40bp), underlying NPAT $920M (-0.4%), statutory NPAT $689.2M (+31.8% off FY25's high significant items), underlying EPS 226c. Operating cash flow $2.1B (99% of EBITDA). Net debt $3.7B (1.8x EBITDA, below 2.0-2.5x target). Share buyback of up to $500M announced; final dividend 32c full-franked, total FY26 64c (+3%), payable 9 Oct 2026.

FY27 guidance: EBIT growth of flat to low-single-digit, with a 5-6% FX headwind on WesTrac parts pricing in H1; WesTrac capital sales expected around same level (~$1.6B); Boral continues volume growth.

At A$40.92, market cap ~$16.6B, ~25.6x trailing statutory P/E (which is distorted), ~15.3x forward consensus EPS ($2.67), ~1.6% dividend yield.

### Freeroll — Downside Support / Valuation Floor — 0.45 / confidence 0.60

The stock's floor is a combination of asset and income backstops. Net tangible assets (NTA) improved to $7.33/share at 30 June 2026 (from $6.29), excluding goodwill that is largely Boral and WesTrac-acquisition related. Book equity is $12.6/share. The dividend (64c fully-franked ≈ 1.6% yield at $41) is covered and growing. More defensively, the shares have sold off nearly 28% from the 52-week high ($52) to ~$41, and the market has already absorbed the "cautious guidance" narrative; the 52-week low of $37.26 was visibly held.

The floor is not hard: at $41 the stock is not at NTA (far above), the dividend is not a 5-10% yield, and the businesses are cyclical. The realistic floor over a 1-2 year catalyst window is roughly $34-38 (a deep cyclical markdown below the 52-week low). Confidence is moderate because the actual binding floor (whether buyers re-appear near $37-38, well above NTA) has not been aggressively retested.

### Fundamental Stability / No Value Trap — 0.65 / confidence 0.80

The fundamentals are cyclical but not structurally eroding. FY26 revenue was flat (-1.4% on a continuing-ops basis), driven down by WesTrac capital-sales normalisation, but services and parts (the annuity), building materials, and the energy stake all held or grew. Boral grew volumes and EBIT; services at WesTrac grew 6%; the infrastructure contract pipeline (A$1.7T five-year program) supports demand.

The risks are cyclical (commodity/FX cycles, Coates pricing pressure in Queensland), not a melting-ice-cube. The structural risk is a coordinated downturn in Australian construction + mining CAPEX; that would cut SGH earnings materially, but the businesses (Caterpillar dealer, essential materials, hire) have long-duration demand and pricing. No evidence of technological disruption of the industrial portfolio. Score held at 0.65 (not higher) because of the ongoing building-sales normalisation and Coates pricing difficulty.

### Freeroll — Mispriced for an Identifiable Reason — 0.55 / confidence 0.65

The market's read in August 2026 was cautious: SGH delivered in line, expanded margins, and announced a $500M buyback, but fell ~10% on the day — the share price is down ~22% from its 52-week high as capital-sales normalisation and an FX headwind in H1 FY27 were extrapolated as a stall.

We think the market is anchoring to a soft near-term 12-month guide and discounting the portfolio's medium-term engines: (a) WesTrac services rebuild + record rebuild activity with mining customers; (b) Boral's path toward mid-teen margins (cost-out, value-led pricing, transport variabilisation); (c) Crux LNG first gas in H2 CY2027 → new FY28 earnings; (d) the $500M buyback at ~15x forward earnings; (e) the Ravenhall property JV (land + capital-light industrial). At ~15x FY27 consensus EPS of ~$2.68 plus a 1.6% yield, the market is paying little for the medium-term 10% EBIT/EPS CAGR goal and the FY28-29 pipeline that management explicitly flagged.

## Overall

SGH is a high-quality cyclical industrial franchise with genuinely market-leading positions (WesTrac, Boral, Coates) and a strong balance sheet, but the earnings power is cyclical and the return on equity isn't "wonderful" by a quality-compounder bar. The most compelling setup is the **freeroll**: the stock has corrected ~22% into a well-covered floor region, trades at a reasonable forward multiple with a substantial buyback announced, and has clear FY27-28 catalysts (deleveraging + buyback, Crux LNG first gas, FY28 capex/construction pipeline). Against that, the company's self-described 10% EBIT growth ambition is not yet showing in FY27 guidance (flat, FX headwind), Coates is under pricing pressure, and Beach/Southern Cross energy exposures carry commodity risk.

Score ~0.60 on the freeroll setup; the strongest alpha is a potential re-rate to ~$48 (consensus median $48.25, upside ~17%) against a floor near $35-38.

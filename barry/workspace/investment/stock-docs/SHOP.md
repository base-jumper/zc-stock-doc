# SHOP — Shopify, Inc.

## Business Overview

Shopify is the leading commerce operating system for independent merchants, powering storefronts, payments, shipping, fulfilment integrations, marketing analytics and an app ecosystem that merchants use to sell online, in-store and across channels. It generates revenue on two tracks:

1. **Subscription Solutions** — recurring monthly platform fees (MRR $221M in Q2 2026, up 19% YoY) across Basic/Advanced/Plus plans, including the higher-priced enterprise Plus tier.
2. **Merchant Solutions** — transactional revenue from Shopify Payments (68% of GMV in Q2 2026, vs 64% a year ago), capital lending, shipping/fulfilment, hardware and retail POS, apps and marketplaces. This is now ~78% of total revenue (Q2 2026), up from 76% a year earlier, and is the key growth engine.

The flywheel: merchants generate GMV on the platform, GMV drives payment and merchant-revenue share, deep integration raises switching costs, and the app ecosystem and scale deepen the moat. In Q2 2026 Shopify facilitated $115.6B of GMV (up 32% YoY), revenue grew 34% (33% constant currency) to $3.6B, and free cash flow margin hit 18%.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.90 / 0.85

E-commerce remains underpenetrated relative to total retail (roughly one-in-five online in the US), and the emergence of AI-assisted store creation, fulfilment/agentic commerce and cross-border commerce expands the opportunity. Shopify itself highlighted Q2 as "a monster quarter" with >30% growth across GMV, revenue, gross profit and FCF, with growth across all merchant sizes, channels and geographies. The addressable market — global online retail and the connected commerce software layer — remains large and expanding.

### Top Dog — 0.82 / 0.80

Shopify is the clear category leader in independent e-commerce enablement (vs. Amazon marketplace, which is a marketplace not a merchant OS), with roughly a mid-teens share of US online retail and leadership in SMB commerce software. The enterprise Plus tier is growing faster than the base, and Shopify Payments (68% of GMV) deepens its hold on the transaction value chain. It is not a monopoly (WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Salesforce Commerce and Adobe Commerce compete), so leadership rather than winner-take-most. Confidence capped by no single verified market-share source.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.82 / 0.75

Deep moat: strong switching costs (merchants route their storefront, payments, inventory, and app integrations through Shopify; migrating is costly), an ecosystem (app store, theme ecosystem, partners) with high engagement, and scale economics in payments/lending/fulfilment. Payments penetration rising from 64%→68% shows the value chain being captured. But multi-homing is possible and category incumbents (WordPress/WooCommerce, BigCommerce) remain. The moat is real but not unassailable, hence the partial score/confidence.

### Durable Growth — 0.90 / 0.85

Reliable accelerating growth is the strongest trait. Revenue grew 26%, 26%, 30% in 2023, 2024 and 2025 (per Yahoo: $7.06B, $8.88B, $11.56B), and Q2 2026 grew 34% YoY with GMV up 32%. The growth script's summary reads **accelerating** (recent 30.1% vs a 27.3% window CAGR) with FCF closely tracking net income (1.63× latest). Free cash flow margin hit 18% in Q2 2026. This is a maturing compounder with strong cash conversion, not a cash-burning growth story.

### Organic Growth — 0.82 / 0.75

Growth is overwhelmingly organic: new merchants, Plus upgrades and payments attach (Payments penetration rose 64→68%). Acquisitions are small, sensible bolt-ons (goodwill only 3.2% of assets, cumulatively ~$3.1B over the last four years vs. $11.6B 2025 revenue), and share count rose only ~3% over the last four years. The franchise is built, not bought. Confidence is moderate because Yahoo doesn't disclose clean ex-acquisition organic revenue.

### Management Backing — 0.72 / 0.70

Tobias Lütke remains co-founder and CEO, providing founder-led, decade-long orientation and a track record from a bootstrapped startup to a NASDAQ large-cap. The 2022 growth-overspending correction (layoffs, divestiture of logistics) showed a willingness to prioritise quality over scale. The team is deep. Slightly below 0.70-1.00 exceptional because the CEO is a founder-operator rather than a proven capital allocator over many cycles, and there is a broad but large HQ.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 / 0.65

Managed-by-committee with meaningful insider ownership (~73% institutions; founder Lütke indirectly holds ~641k shares) and a $5B buyback is a positive capital-return signal. However, roughly $260M YTD and $150M/quarter stock-based compensation is a real owner cost (though buybacks now roughly offset it), and the compensation package/warrant structures aren't as transparent as ideal; the alignment is decent but not exceptional. The founder not maintaining a founder-class stake (only ~0.6% indirectly) is a modest ding.

### Customer Devotion — 0.85 / 0.75

Shopify merchants show strong retention and expansion depth. Payments penetration rising (64→68% in a year) is a loyalty signal: merchants route more volume through Shopify's ecosystem even when switching is technically feasible — that is preference-plus-integration devotion. Strong app-marketplace engagement. Confidence moderate: no clean net-revenue-retention figure is disclosed; the evidence is directional.

**Why the other strategies lost.** Wonderful-and-fair loses on a short record of consistent GAAP earnings (2022 loss; 2026 GAAP net income inflated/exposed to equity-investment fair-value swings) and low historical ROIC (~11%). Cash-cow loses because the business still has a wide reinvestment runway — free cash is being reinvested in AI infrastructure rather than durably distributed. Freeroll fails on no valuation floor and no time-bound catalyst ($146 is 75× FCF; nothing defines a protected floor). No universal disqualifier was identified: strong balance sheet ($4.9B cash, $178M debt), no accounting-integrity issue, no kill-shot.

## Overall

Shopify is a premier long-duration commerce compounder: the undisputed leader in independent e-commerce software, with an accelerating growth engine, deep payment-driven moat, strong cash conversion and a fast-appending AI angle. The main detractors are: (1) GAAP earnings quality is inflated by non-operating equity-investment fair-value gains (Affirm, Klaviyo, Global-E) and persistent stock-based compensation; (2) the valuation is rich (~76× trailing FCF); (3) merchant-solutions mix growth structurally compresses gross margin over time; (4) lending/payment losses rose 76% in Q2; and (5) competition and regulatory change. It is a wonderful company, but at the current price the forward return is only modest.

## Valuation

The canonical Rule Breaker valuation uses only the bottom-up exit-multiple lens. A TAM-capture block is not used: no existing market doc contains Shopify as a player, so an internally estimated TAM would be fabricated precision (this follows the workspace precedent set for DASH).

The 21 August price is $146.57 (Yahoo). The model anchors on **$1.93 of trailing free cash flow per diluted share** — using the latest trailing FCF of ~$2.07B and ~1.21B diluted shares, with the FCF margin 18% in Q2 2026. I use `metric: FCF` because GAAP net income is heavily distorted by unrealized equity-investment fair-value gains each quarter and stock-based comp, whereas cash conversion is clean.

Growth fades from 25% to 9% over ten years — comfortably below the current 30%+ run-rate and below the consensus forward-growth estimates (Yahoo +1y revenue growth ~25%). Exit multiple set at 28× P/FCF, around today's forward P/E anchored but below the ~76× entry multiple, recognising a maturing compounder. Dilution modeled as −0.8%/yr: Shopify is now buying back ($1.4B in Q2, $5B authorization) roughly offsetting the ~$150M/quarter stock-based comp.

The script produces a **5.5% annualised ROI** (1.7× over ten years): business growth contributes ~15.6% annualised, the ~0.8% buyback tailwind, but multiple compression from 75.9× to 28× deducts ~9.5% annualised. A 10-year operating case at 5.5% a year is a modest reward for the risk — the quality is real, but the entry price already capitalises a lot. Agent score 0.62: this is a genuinely wonderful business whose quality, growth and cash conversion justify a healthy rating, I intend the rating to reflect that the forward return is only modest at today's price.

## Thesis Connections

Shopify is clearly a picks-and-shovels play in the "single-person companies" thesis — if AI enables more solo e-commerce brands, Shopify supplies the storefronts, payments, fulfilment and marketing rails (see thesis: *Single person companies*). The Q2 result — GMV growth across all merchant sizes, including small — is direct evidence for that thesis. Also a beneficiary of agentic-commerce rails (Visa/Mastercard/PayPal agentic payments; Shopify is a feedback partner).

## Watch

- **GMV and merchant count growth by segment**, especially SMB vs Plus/enterprise; drive the whole merchant-solutions engine.
- **Shopify Payments penetration** (68% Q2, 64% a year ago) — the biggest per-volume lever; whether take-rate (payment fee / GMV) stays stable or is pressured.
- **Revenue-mix gross-margin compression** as merchant solutions (lower gross margin) displace subscription revenue; watch the consolidated gross-margin trend.
- **Earnings quality**: whether GAAP net income is increasingly dominated by unrealised equity fair-value gains and private-equity marks (Level-3, $1.5B) — which would inflate reported profitability. Prefer operating measure / FCF.
- **Lending/transaction credit losses** rose 76% in Q2; watch Shopify Capital / payments risk costs as merchant mix broadens.
- **Stock-based compensation vs. the $5B buyback** each quarter; require genuine positive per-share dilution or repurchase-accretive FCF.
- **AI adoption:** AI features for store creation, customer service, marketing automation; and agentic-commerce rails translating into GMV/attach.
- **Currency** (EUR/GBP/CAD) and tariff exposure to reported FX.

# FDS — FactSet Research Systems Inc.

## Business Overview

FactSet sells subscription financial data, analytics and workflow software to investment professionals. Its workstation, portfolio analytics, enterprise data feeds/APIs, trading and managed-service products sit inside research, portfolio construction, execution, performance, risk and reporting workflows. CUSIP Global Services supplies security identifiers used in front-, middle- and back-office systems. At August 2025 FactSet served 8,996 clients and 237,324 users; 82% of organic annual subscription value (ASV) came from the buy side. The Americas generated 65% of FY2025 revenue, EMEA 25% and Asia-Pacific 10%.[1]

The model is predominantly recurring subscription revenue. Switching is costly because clients embed FactSet's data, analytics and proprietary portfolio information into critical workflows. The counterweight is formidable competition: Bloomberg, S&P Global Market Intelligence and LSEG Data & Analytics are larger peers, while MSCI, Morningstar, BlackRock Aladdin and specialist vendors compete in narrower workflows. FactSet's current growth vectors are deeper enterprise penetration, wealth and private-market workflows, cloud/API delivery and AI-enabled products.[1][3]

## Trait Assessment

No universal disqualifier was found. There was no disclosed restatement, fraud allegation, solvency threat, material hidden liability, abusive controller or imminent business-model prohibition. The unresolved IT-control material weakness is serious, but management and the auditor concluded the published FY2025 accounts were fairly presented and the May 2026 10-Q reported no identified misstatement; it therefore reduces confidence rather than disqualifying the company.[1][2]

### Earnings Quality — 0.82 score, 0.78 confidence

FactSet has a long, unbroken profit record. SEC XBRL data show positive net income in each of the 12 fiscal years through FY2025; revenue rose every year in the available 2018–2025 series. FY2022–FY2025 net income increased from $396.9 million to $597.0 million, while cumulative FCF was 1.15 times cumulative net income. Receivables remained near 10–12% of revenue. These are strong consistency, predictability and cash-backing signals.[4][5]

The blemish is reporting control. The IT-general-control material weakness affecting revenue, receivables and deferred revenue processes persisted at May 2026, although redesigned controls were being tested and no restatement was reported. Confidence would rise with the FY2026 10-K's ICFR assessment and auditor opinion confirming full remediation; this is also a Watch item.[1][2]

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.90 score, 0.90 confidence

TTM FCF was approximately $708 million, or a 29% margin. FY2022–FY2025 FCF margins held around 26–28%, capex consumed only 9–15% of operating cash flow, and cumulative FCF exceeded net income. This is a genuinely capital-light cash engine, not a one-year working-capital release.[4][5]

Capex has run at 39–58% of D&A, but much of D&A is acquisition-intangible amortisation rather than wear on physical assets; FactSet also capitalises internal-use software. The apparent capex shortfall therefore does not by itself indicate asset harvesting. SBC was roughly 10% of FCF in FY2025 and remains a real owner cost, although repurchases more than offset dilution.[1][4]

### Fundamental Stability — 0.86 score, 0.90 confidence

The recurring revenue base is durable: annual ASV retention exceeded 95% in FY2025 and remained above 95% in Q3 FY2026; client retention was 91% in FY2025. Q3 organic revenue and organic ASV grew 7.0% and 7.1%, respectively, with no client representing more than 3.5% of revenue in the first nine months of FY2026. The business is healthy rather than a melting ice cube.[1][2][3]

Structural threats are real but not yet biting: cheaper data, generative/agentic AI, the shift from active to passive investing, customer consolidation and larger rivals could pressure seats or pricing. Current retention, broader user counts and accelerating ASV argue that these remain risks rather than observed secular erosion.[1][3]

### Pricing Power — 0.80 score, 0.88 confidence

The moat comes from embedded workflows, proprietary/normalised data, client portfolio integrations, trusted identifiers and high switching risk. FactSet reported more than 95% ASV retention and Q3 enterprise renewals that were 30% longer on average. FY2018–FY2025 operating margins generally held around 27–32%, with FY2025 at 32.2%, while organic ASV continued to grow.[1][3][4]

This is strong retention and margin evidence, though it does not isolate price from seats, modules or mix. The moat is stable, not impregnable: Bloomberg, S&P and LSEG are well-capitalised, and free/AI-enabled information sources could reduce willingness to pay.[1]

### Capital Allocation — 0.70 score, 0.82 confidence

Over FY2022–FY2025 FactSet generated about $2.30 billion of FCF, returned $1.31 billion through dividends and buybacks, and spent about $2.35 billion on acquisitions. The 2022 acquisition cycle pushed net debt/EBITDA to 2.43 times, but debt was subsequently repaid and leverage fell to 1.07 times by FY2025. The dividend has risen for 27 consecutive years.[3][5]

Recent allocation has improved. In Q3 FY2026 FactSet repurchased 926,370 shares at an average $219.21—below the $282.95 analysis price—and returned $243.4 million in total. Diluted shares fell roughly 6% year over year in the quarter. However, the record includes heavy acquisition dependence: FY2025's $243.2 million LiquidityBook and $120.2 million Irwin purchases created about $256 million of goodwill, and goodwill reached 30% of assets. The strategic fit is credible, but the eventual returns are not yet proven.[1][3][5]

### Returns on Capital — 0.68 score, 0.86 confidence

ROIC including acquired goodwill rose from 12.9% in FY2022 to 18.1% in FY2025; ROCE rose from 13.4% to 20.5%. That is value-creating and improving, but the four-year average ROIC of 16.3% sits below the default 20% "high" anchor. ROE near 27% overstates the operating economics because leverage and a smaller equity base create a roughly nine-point ROE–ROIC gap.[5]

Goodwill was 36% of invested capital in FY2025, so returns on the acquired capital matter. The script's very high recent incremental ROIC is directionally encouraging but unstable over a short window; the score therefore recognises improving returns without calling them exceptional.[1][5]

### Conservative Debt — 0.78 score, 0.90 confidence

TTM net debt/EBITDA was 1.36 times and EBIT covered interest 15.4 times. At FY2025, leverage was 1.07 times versus a 2.43-times peak in FY2022, and near-term debt was covered about 29 times by cash plus one year of FCF. At May 2026, $499 million was current, but FactSet also had strong FCF and a revolving facility; $395 million of debt was floating-rate.[2][5]

For a stable, capital-light subscription business this is sound rather than pristine: net cash would be ideal, but leverage is modest and falling with ample coverage. No off-balance-sheet financing beyond ordinary-course letters of credit was reported.[2]

### Key-Person Risk — 0.76 score, 0.78 confidence

FactSet's advantage is institutional—data, workflow integrations, client service and a 47-year operating history—not concentrated in one person's relationships or IP. Founder dependence is absent and the board separates chair and CEO roles.[1][6]

There is nevertheless transition risk. Sanoke Viswanathan became CEO in September 2025 and Joshua Warren became CFO in April 2026, so two central leaders are new. This uncertainty is irreducible for now; evidence after two full reporting cycles under the new team, including execution against FY2027 targets and senior-management retention, would raise confidence.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.64 score, 0.90 confidence

The FY2025 annual incentive was 80% company performance—two-thirds ASV growth and one-third adjusted operating margin—and 20% individual goals. Long-term awards used five-year options and three-year PSUs tied equally to cumulative adjusted operating earnings and revenue. The company has ownership guidelines, clawbacks, no option repricing without shareholder approval and prohibits hedging and pledging.[6]

Alignment is only mixed because incentives still lean on size and adjusted metrics rather than ROIC or per-share FCF. The new CEO owned no shares at October 2025 and received a large one-time option package that vests if the stock reaches 150% of the grant-date VWAP; that creates useful upside alignment but begins with granted rather than purchased ownership.[6]

Cash Cow wins mechanically at 0.781 quality with 0.855 confidence. Wonderful-and-fair is a credible secondary fit (0.729/0.823), but the only moderate reinvestment runway and acquisition reliance make the cash-return framing cleaner. Rule Breakers loses on mature-industry growth and M&A-heavy growth quality; Freeroll loses because there is no hard valuation floor or identifiable, time-bound catalyst.

## Overall

FactSet is a high-quality cash generator with unusually sticky revenue, strong margins, improving ROIC, conservative leverage and a shareholder-return record that includes 27 straight dividend increases. The current price is materially below the prior year's range and recent repurchases were executed well below today's quote.

The reasons it is not top-tier are equally clear: organic growth is mid-single-digit rather than exceptional, the business has needed meaningful M&A to expand its workflow footprint, ROIC including goodwill is good rather than elite, the new leadership team is unseasoned in-role, and the unresolved control weakness deserves respect. AI is both an upsell opportunity and a threat to the scarcity value of financial information. The stock looks sensible at $282.95, but the conservative return model produces only a high-single-digit annual return.

## Valuation

The exit-multiple model uses FCF because cash conversion is central and acquisition amortisation makes GAAP earnings noisier. Current owner earnings are estimated at $19.92 per share from approximately $708 million of TTM FCF divided by 35.6 million shares. At $282.95, that is a 14.20-times entry P/FCF.[4][5]

The five-year model fades aggregate FCF growth from 6.0% to 4.0%, uses 1.5% annual net share-count reduction, a 1.63% starting dividend yield and a 14.0-times exit P/FCF. The exit multiple is slightly below entry, so the case does not depend on re-rating. These assumptions are conservative against Q3 organic ASV growth of 7.1%, but also recognise a mature end market and acquisition dependence.[3]

The model produces an 8.1% annualised ROI and a 1.47-times total-return multiple. Annualised attribution is approximately 5.0% business growth, 1.5% buyback lift, negative 0.3% multiple change and 1.8% income. `overall.agent_score` is 0.58: business quality is strong and downside is moderated by cash generation, but an 8% expected return plus the control and AI risks does not warrant a high-conviction rating.

## Watch

- FY2026 10-K: confirm the IT-control material weakness is fully remediated and review the auditor's ICFR opinion.
- Track organic ASV growth and >95% retention; investigate if ASV growth falls below 5% or retention breaks trend.
- Review performance under CEO Sanoke Viswanathan and CFO Joshua Warren after two full reporting cycles, including senior-team turnover.
- Measure organic growth separately from acquisitions and track returns/impairments for LiquidityBook, Irwin and the large FY2022 acquisition base.
- Track AI monetisation versus cannibalisation: client adoption, incremental ASV, pricing and any seat compression.
- Revisit buyback assumptions if repurchases occur materially above intrinsic value or the share count stops shrinking.

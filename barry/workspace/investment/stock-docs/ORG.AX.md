# ORG.AX — Origin Energy Limited

## Business Overview

Origin Energy is a large Australian integrated energy company. Its core business is **Energy Markets**: retail electricity, gas, broadband and adjacent customer solutions, plus generation and contracting assets. Origin also owns a major interest in **Australia Pacific LNG (APLNG)**, **22.7% of Octopus Energy**, and a **19.6% direct stake in Kraken Technologies** plus 3.1% indirect exposure through Octopus after their July 2026 legal separation.

FY26 statutory profit rose 6% to **$1.574b**, but underlying EBITDA fell 6% to **$3.220b** and underlying profit fell 22% to **$1.159b** as lower APLNG pricing, volumes and trading gains outweighed stronger Energy Markets and Octopus/Kraken performance. Energy Markets EBITDA rose 21% to **$1.701b**; Integrated Gas EBITDA fell 26% to **$1.620b**; and Origin's share of Octopus/Kraken EBITDA improved from an $88m loss to an **$8m loss**.

The thesis is not a clean hyper-growth case or a pure utility bond-proxy. Origin is a **mature, cash-generative energy-transition incumbent with embedded growth options** in Kraken/Octopus, customer solutions, batteries, VPPs and firming assets. FY26 added 243,000 customer accounts, put 1.3GW of 1.8GW of committed large-scale battery capacity into revenue service, completed the Kraken separation and produced **$2.074b of adjusted free cash flow**. The legacy asset base supports the dividend; any further re-rating now depends on operational delivery because the cleanest corporate catalyst has already fired.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.60 / confidence 0.94

Origin has real but cyclical earning power. FY26 statutory profit rose 6% to **$1.574b**, while underlying profit fell 22% to **$1.159b** and underlying EPS was 67.4 cents. The gap reflects significant and fair-value items, including accounting around the Octopus/Kraken separation; commodity-linked Integrated Gas earnings also move materially between years. Audited segment disclosure and strong cash backing support the score, but the reliance on underlying measures and volatile statutory-to-underlying reconciliations prevent a high predictability rating.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.65 / confidence 0.92

FY26 cash conversion was strong: operating cash flow was **$1.917b**, capex fell to **$969m**, APLNG distributed **$911m**, and adjusted free cash flow rose 72% to **$2.074b**. The $0.60 fully franked dividend consumed 50% of that measure. This is meaningful surplus cash, but it is not fully clean: adjusted FCF excludes $484m of Eraring and Mortlake battery capex and reverses futures-collateral and Queensland bill-relief timing effects. The battery program should reduce major capex as projects finish, while APLNG decline and future transition investment keep the cash stream cyclical.

### Fundamental Stability / No Value Trap — 0.72 / confidence 0.94

Origin does not look like a melting ice cube. Energy Markets EBITDA rose 21% to **$1.701b**, customer accounts increased 243,000 and cost to serve fell; FY27 segment guidance of **$1.55–1.85b** implies resilience as battery ramp-up offsets lower wholesale tariffs. Kraken contracted accounts reached 95m, Octopus had about 19m retail accounts and the combined equity-accounted EBITDA loss narrowed to $8m.

The weakness is cyclical and depletion-driven rather than terminal, but it is real. FY26 APLNG production was 668PJ and Integrated Gas EBITDA fell 26%; FY27 production guidance is **625–670PJ** while capex and opex rise to $3.0–3.3b. The data-security incident also creates unresolved trust, remediation and regulatory risks. These threats cap the score despite the durable domestic franchise.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.62 / confidence 0.85

Origin's advantage is portfolio flexibility rather than unconstrained retail pricing. Its generation, gas contracts, storage, hedging and customer scale let Energy Markets manage procurement costs across volatile conditions; FY26 wholesale electricity, gas and LPG performance lifted segment EBITDA materially. Regulation, default-market offers, affordability pressure and customer switching limit pass-through, while APLNG prices are externally set. FY27 evidence that battery earnings offset lower wholesale tariff components would firm up the durability of this advantage.

### Capital Allocation — 0.66 / confidence 0.92

Allocation was broadly rational in FY26. Origin completed major batteries on time and on budget, had 1.3GW of 1.8GW committed capacity generating revenue, paid a covered $0.60 dividend, kept leverage below target and invested another **$210m** in Kraken's externally priced raise after year-end. The dividend reinvestment plan is satisfied on market, avoiding new-share dilution. The deductions are continued commodity and transition capital intensity, no buyback support at low prices, and Yanco Delta economics that remain challenged even with government support.

### Returns on Capital — 0.56 / confidence 0.95

Rolling 24-month underlying ROCE was **12.6%**, down from 14.6%. That is respectable for a heavy integrated utility but not an exceptional compounding spread, and it weakened even as cash generation improved. Future returns depend on batteries, customer solutions and Kraken/Octopus earning adequately on the capital committed while APLNG declines. Project-level battery returns and a stabilisation of group ROCE would justify a higher score.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.78 / confidence 0.95

Adjusted net debt to adjusted underlying EBITDA was **1.6x**, below Origin's 2–3x target range, and Moody's rating remained Baa2 stable. Management expects leverage to move only into the lower end of the target during FY27 as remaining battery capex, leases, the Kraken investment and lower LNG trading gains absorb cash. This is conservative for the asset mix and leaves capacity to ride commodity volatility, though APLNG distributions and excluded battery leases make the adjusted definition worth monitoring.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.84 / confidence 0.88

Origin's retail franchise, generation fleet, trading systems, APLNG interest and strategic stakes are institutional assets rather than dependent on CEO Frank Calabria. The FY26 report shows planned internal succession across Energy Supply and Integrated Gas, and the board added two independent directors. A leadership change could disrupt execution, but it would not break the business model.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.74 / confidence 0.96

The audited FY26 remuneration report materially improves the evidence. Executive STI is generally 60% financial and 40% strategic, with half paid in cash and half deferred in shares. LTI is split equally between three-year relative-TSR rights and restricted rights subject to a board underpin, with releases extending to five years. The CEO must hold 2.5 times fixed remuneration and actually held **9.3 times** at year-end. Malus and clawback apply.

The board also cut the CEO's FY26 incentives by **$357,000** after the data-security incident, reducing both STI and a vesting LTI award before investigations finish. That is credible accountability. The soft spot is that holistic restricted-right underpins and strategic metrics such as asset replacement or transaction completion can reward delivery without directly requiring high per-share returns.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.62 / confidence 0.88

Origin has more runway than a pure cash cow: 1.8GW of committed batteries, the ~1.5GW Yanco Delta option, customer electrification, and 22.7% economic exposure to both Octopus and Kraken. The runway is not clearly high-return. Yanco economics are challenged, APLNG needs rising spend to slow decline, and project-level battery returns are undisclosed. This makes Wonderful-and-Fair a genuine near fit but not the primary framing.

**Why the primary strategy won:** Cash Cow leads mechanically at **0.671 score / 0.922 confidence**, with Wonderful-and-Fair a genuine near-tie at **0.659 / 0.914**. Cash Cow fits better because current owner returns are carried by covered dividends and harvested legacy cash, while reinvestment returns remain only moderate. Rule Breakers loses because mature energy retail, generation and LNG dominate; Kraken/Octopus is an embedded option rather than the whole business. Freeroll loses because the price is no longer near the floor and the cleanest catalyst has already fired.

## Overall

Origin delivered a solid FY26 result: Energy Markets strengthened, adjusted free cash flow reached $2.074b, leverage fell and the Octopus/Kraken loss nearly disappeared. Those gains were partly offset by lower Integrated Gas earnings, declining APLNG production and the unresolved data-security incident.

At **$11.86**, however, the stock is no longer a compelling Freeroll. The price sits almost at conservative fair value and about 20% above the estimated floor, while the clearest catalyst is complete. The business remains worth watching, but a fresh entry needs either a materially lower quote or new evidence that raises fair value.

## Valuation

The canonical Cash Cow valuation uses a five-year **exit-multiple** model on normalised FCF per share. FY26 adjusted FCF was about $1.20 per share; I haircut that to **$1.05** for unusually low tax, excluded timing items and likely APLNG pressure. The model uses the **$11.86** quote, 1% aggregate annual FCF growth, no dilution, a **10x** exit P/FCF versus the implied 11.3x entry multiple, and the current **5.1%** dividend yield. The script produces about **4.0% annualised total ROI**: 1.0% business growth and 5.4% income are partly offset by 2.4% annual multiple compression. The return is income-carried, but low.

The old Freeroll lens now fails decisively. A **$9.50** yield/SOTP floor leaves 19.9% downside, while Yahoo's **$12.00** median target leaves only 1.2% upside; at 55% success probability over one year, the price-only expected ROI is about **-8.3%** and asymmetry only **0.06x**. `overall.agent_score` falls to **0.43**: FY26 improves confidence in the cash stream, but the current quote offers little prospective return and no margin of safety.

## Thesis Connections

Connected to FIT through **energy transition infrastructure, electrification, grid firming, virtual power plants and customer energy software**. Origin’s most interesting exposure is Kraken/Octopus plus its domestic customer-solutions platform; the legacy coal/gas generation and LNG assets are the cash bridge funding the transition.

## Watch

- Post-separation Kraken: account growth beyond 95m contracted accounts, recurring-revenue conversion and any liquidity path for Origin's 19.6% direct stake.
- Octopus/Kraken contribution: move from the FY26 **$8m loss** to sustained positive EBITDA while continuing international investment.
- FY27 Energy Markets EBITDA against **$1.55–1.85b** and whether battery earnings offset lower wholesale tariffs.
- Cost to serve per account: hold steady in FY27 after FY26 reductions and acquired-customer growth.
- APLNG FY27 production against **625–670PJ** guidance and capex/opex against **$3.0–3.3b**; determine whether higher oil-linked pricing offsets natural field decline.
- Data-security incident: final affected-customer count, information types accessed, remediation cost, regulator response, customer churn and any class-action exposure.
- Dividend coverage by adjusted FCF after excluded growth capex and timing adjustments; FY26's $0.60 dividend was 50% of adjusted FCF.
- Eraring extension to April 2029: operating reliability, closure cost, emissions/regulatory backlash and no further major overhaul requirement.
- Remaining battery delivery and early flexibility earnings; 1.3GW of 1.8GW committed capacity was revenue-generating at FY26 year-end.
- Re-entry discipline: require a quote near the $9.50 floor or evidence that supports fair value materially above $12.00.

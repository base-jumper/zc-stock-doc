# ZEAL — ZEAL Network SE

## Business Overview

ZEAL is Germany's leading online lottery broker, chiefly through Lotto24 and Tipp24. It earns brokerage revenue on state-lottery ticket billings and increasingly sells higher-margin proprietary charity lotteries, online games and raffles. Lotteries supplied 91% of H1 2026 revenue; Games supplied 6%. The core economics are unusually capital-light: customer acquisition and software are the main reinvestment needs, while physical capex is negligible.

H1 2026 revenue rose 20% to €121.8m, EBITDA 10% to €38.9m and net profit 12% to €21.8m. Lottery monthly active users rose 9% to 1.65m and billings per user 4%; the lottery gross margin increased from 17.3% to 18.2%. Results still vary with jackpot conditions, but customer growth and proprietary products are reducing that sensitivity. In July 2026 ZEAL acquired UK prize-draw operator SevenCanyon for £33.8m plus up to £4.8m, funded mainly by a new €40m loan.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.70 / 0.82 confidence

ZEAL was profitable in each of 2022–2025, but reported net income was not smooth: €16.0m, €12.8m, €58.4m and €41.7m. The 2024 peak included an unusually favourable tax result, so it is a poor base year. Cash backing is strong: cumulative 2022–2025 FCF was about 1.45× cumulative net income, receivables fell from 13.0% to 11.6% of revenue, and H1 2026 net profit grew 12%. This clears the cash-reality test but the short Yahoo history and tax-driven volatility keep the score from exceptional.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.90 / 0.90 confidence

FCF was €27.1m, €25.1m, €74.5m and €60.6m in 2022–2025; the latest four-year average FCF margin was 28.7%. Capex consumed only 0.8%–5.0% of operating cash flow, consistent with a software-and-marketing-led model, and cumulative FCF/net income conversion was 1.45×. The very low capex/D&A ratio reflects amortisation of acquired lottery intangibles rather than neglected physical assets, although working-capital movements make half-year cash flow noisy.

### Fundamental Stability — 0.86 / 0.90 confidence

The franchise is expanding rather than melting: 2022–2025 revenue more than doubled, gross margin stayed near 90% on the accounting presentation, and operating margin rose from 21.6% to 27.6%. H1 2026 revenue, active users and lottery take rate all increased. Structural risks are regulation, lottery substitution and jackpot variability; the mitigating evidence is ZEAL's licensed position, 44% share of German online lottery brokerage, and diversification into proprietary products, games and UK prize draws.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.82 / 0.85 confidence

ZEAL combines regulatory licences, leading distribution and customer data/scale. It reports a 44% share of German online lottery brokerage, while online penetration is only about 29%. H1 2026 lottery gross margin expanded 0.9 percentage points to 18.2% while MAU rose 9%, evidence that monetisation did not cost volume. The moat is stable-to-widening, but state lotteries control the underlying product and regulation constrains economics, so ZEAL is not fully sovereign over price.

### Capital Allocation — 0.68 / 0.82 confidence

The company returned €202m in dividends and €45m in buybacks during 2022–2025, shrinking diluted shares by 5.4%. Recent payouts were covered by FCF, though large earlier special dividends exceeded annual cash generation and partly explain the move from net cash to debt. SevenCanyon appears disciplined at roughly 3.4× last-year EBITDA before earn-out and adds a related profitable market, but it is ZEAL's first material recent acquisition and its purchase-price allocation is unfinished. The allocation record is shareholder-friendly, not spotless.

### Returns on Capital — 0.62 / 0.88 confidence

ROIC rose from 4.8% in 2022 to 14.2% in 2025 and ROCE reached 17.5%, but four-year average ROIC was only 9.4%. Goodwill equalled about half of invested capital, depressing the acquisition-inclusive return; the capital-light underlying operations are stronger than the consolidated percentage suggests. Recent improvement is meaningful, but a full-cycle record above a reasonable cost of capital is not yet demonstrated.

### Conservative Debt — 0.82 / 0.90 confidence

At June 2026 ZEAL held €98.9m cash against €81.3m interest-bearing loans, with 51.4% equity and strong historical interest coverage. The €40m SevenCanyon facility announced after period-end turns that into modest pro-forma net debt, still below roughly 0.4× combined annual EBITDA before earn-out and transaction costs. Five- and seven-year terms reduce refinancing concentration. Acquisition integration, not solvency, is the balance-sheet issue.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.78 / 0.65 confidence

ZEAL is professionally managed rather than dependent on a founder or star operator; its brands, licences, platform and customer base are institutional assets. CFO succession in 2025 was internal, supporting bench depth. Confidence would rise with the 2025 annual report's succession and executive-responsibility disclosures; until then, the absence of concentrated operational dependence is clearer than the depth of the bench.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.58 / 0.60 confidence

Management has delivered per-share shrinkage, FCF-backed distributions and a conservatively priced acquisition, which are aligned outcomes. Yahoo reports high insider ownership, and a director-linked vehicle disclosed receipt of 749,999 shares in February 2026, but that transaction was an accrual rather than open-market buying. The score remains provisional because the current remuneration report's performance metrics, vesting periods and dilution have not been reviewed. The specific firm-up source is the FY2025 remuneration report and 2026 AGM materials.

Cash Cow wins because high FCF margins, negligible capex and large distributions outweigh the remaining reinvestment runway. Wonderful-and-Fair loses on only moderate acquisition-inclusive ROIC and a runway increasingly reliant on adjacencies/M&A; Rule Breakers loses because this is a mature regulated category rather than an emerging winner-takes-most market; Freeroll loses because tangible downside support and a discrete rerating catalyst are weak.

## Overall

ZEAL is a high-quality regulated digital tollbooth with a leading position, strong cash conversion and credible mid-teens revenue ambitions. The main deductions are jackpot sensitivity, only moderate reported ROIC, regulation, and the untested integration of SevenCanyon. The acquisition may improve diversification, but it also shifts the thesis from pure cash harvesting toward execution-dependent expansion.

## Valuation

At €43.40, the shares trade at about 20.9× trailing earnings and 15.2× normalised current FCF. The exit-multiple model uses current owner earnings of €2.85 per share (about €60.2m TTM FCF divided by 21.1m shares), 7% aggregate FCF growth, 0.5% annual buyback accretion, a 3.23% dividend yield, and a five-year 15× exit P/FCF. The growth assumption is deliberately below management's mid-teens revenue target because it excludes a heroic SevenCanyon contribution and allows for jackpot volatility. The model returns **10.71% annualised** (1.66× over five years): approximately +7.00% from business growth, +0.50% from buybacks, -0.30% from multiple compression and +3.49% from income. This supports an agent score of 0.67: attractive quality, but only a low-double-digit expected return and meaningful new integration risk.

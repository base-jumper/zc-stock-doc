# Trait: Brand / Reputation Moat

**Model role:** scored candidate trait; not selected by the current production model.

**What we're looking for:**
A demand-side *preference* moat: customers choose the leader out of trust, habit, or perceived
quality/safety, and would keep choosing it even with no lock-in and no other users to benefit from.
This is the intangibles leg of the classic moat set — the pull that concentrates share on a name
rather than on a mechanism.

**Why it pushes `s1` up:** when buyers default to the trusted brand and treat rivals as riskier or
inferior, spend piles onto the leader and challengers must discount or out-spend to be considered at
all. It is the dominant concentrator in markets with no network or switching mechanics: beverages
and staples, luxury, payment-scheme and platform trust, and reputation-gated services (credit
ratings, audit, advisory) where a wrong choice carries career or safety risk.

**Boundary tests — keep this distinct from its neighbours:**

* **vs. [[switching-costs]]** — brand is why a customer *picks* you with nothing sunk; switching cost
  is why they *stay* once leaving would be costly. "I prefer Coke" is brand; "re-platforming our ERP
  is a two-year project" is switching cost. Score the free-choice preference here.
* **vs. [[network-effects]]** — brand preference holds even if the customer is the only user; a
  network effect needs *other* users to create the value. If the pull disappears when no one else is
  on the product, it belongs in network-effects.
* **vs. [[data-scale-advantage]]** — brand is *perceived* superiority (reputation, trust, identity);
  data-scale is *measurably* better output from a feedback loop. Score the reality where it exists,
  the perception here.

**Weakeners to check:**

* **Objective, spec-driven buying** — where buyers compare on measurable price/performance
  (commodities, many procurement and B2B-infra deals), brand does little to concentrate share.
* **Cheap, credible entry signals** — retailer private label, influencer-native and DTC brands, or
  review platforms that let unknowns prove quality quickly all erode an incumbent name's advantage.
* **Reputation is losable** — unlike sunk capital, a brand moat can collapse on a scandal or quality
  failure; weight its *durability* over the 10-year horizon, not just today's strength.

**Scoring guidance (0–1, how strongly brand/reputation concentrates share):**

* **0.70–1.00** Buyers default to the trusted name and pay a premium for it; unknowns struggle to be
  considered even at lower price
* **0.40–0.69** Brand tilts choice but a credible challenger competes on merit within a cycle
* **0.00–0.39** Near-commodity or spec-driven; the name barely moves the purchase

**Documentation:** the concrete source of preference (trust/safety, habit, identity/status,
certification-by-reputation), any evidence of a realized price premium or unaided-preference gap, and
why the reputation should persist over the horizon.

# MCY.NZ — Mercury NZ Limited

## Business Overview

Mercury NZ is a New Zealand integrated electricity generator-retailer. Its generation assets are 100% renewable across Waikato hydro, geothermal and wind; it also retails electricity, gas, broadband and mobile to mass-market, SME, commercial and industrial customers. FY2025 revenue was NZ$3.498b and EBITDAF was NZ$786m, with generation/wholesale contributing NZ$837m of segment EBITDAF and customer contributing -NZ$51m. FY2025 was a tough hydrology year: hydro generation fell to 3,410GWh, the fourth-lowest Waikato scheme year since 1980, and net profit fell to NZ$1m.

The current investment case is not explosive growth. It is a mature infrastructure-like renewable utility with a dividend record, essential demand, and a large development/renewal programme. The main tension is that Mercury looks like a cash-cow candidate on stability and dividends, but the cash is being heavily recycled into growth and asset renewal while leverage is elevated.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.45 / 0.75

Mercury has real operating earnings, but accounting profit is not clean or especially predictable year to year. FY2025 EBITDAF was NZ$786m, down from NZ$877m in FY2024, while reported NPAT dropped from NZ$290m to NZ$1m due to weaker EBITDAF and unrealised financial-instrument movements. Yahoo shows TTM net income to common of about NZ$88m on NZ$3.407b revenue, a low 2.6% margin.

Interpretation: the underlying utility cash engine is real, but hydrology, wholesale-price exposure, derivatives and depreciation/finance costs make headline earnings noisy. For a cash-cow strategy that wants dependable distributable profits, this is only a partial pass. Firm-up: use FY2026 audited results to separate normalised EBITDAF/NPAT from derivative and hydrology effects.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.42 / 0.75

Mercury generated NZ$483m of operating cash flow in FY2025. Management's own five-year table reports free cash flow of NZ$345m and ordinary/special declared dividends of NZ$337m, but the statutory cash flow statement also shows NZ$437m of PPE capex plus NZ$30m of intangibles, so free cash after total growth spend is thin. Yahoo's TTM free cash flow estimate is about NZ$193m versus market cap of about NZ$9.78b.

Interpretation: cash generation exists, but it is not a clean surplus-cash machine right now. Growth projects and hydro refurbishment are consuming a lot of cash, and the dividend relies on management's policy definition of free cash flow over time rather than a large current excess. Firm-up: track FY2026 operating cash, stay-in-business capex versus growth capex, and dividend coverage after the current build cycle.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.78 / 0.80

The business supplies essential electricity and related services in New Zealand, with 906k total customer connections/products disclosed in FY2025 and 586k electricity ICPs in the Q3 FY2026 update. Generation is renewable and long-lived, and the company has nine hydro stations, five geothermal stations and five wind farms. Q3 FY2026 showed stronger hydrology and YTD generation of 6,726GWh, up 824GWh on the prior comparable period.

Interpretation: demand durability is strong and the asset base should remain relevant in an electrifying economy. The risk is not demand disappearance; it is weather variability, regulation, execution and commodity/wholesale price exposure.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.62 / 0.70

Mercury owns scarce renewable generation and has integrated retail load, which gives it a defensible position in a market where new firm renewable supply is difficult and slow to build. It was able to implement average electricity price increases of about 9.7% from 1 April 2025, though management attributed most of that to regulated lines and transmission costs rather than pure retail pricing power.

Interpretation: the moat is real but mixed. Scarce hydro/geothermal/wind assets matter; retail energy remains competitive and politically sensitive, and electricity affordability limits how aggressively pricing can flow through. Firm-up: compare Mercury's retail churn and gross margins against Contact, Meridian and Genesis after FY2026 results.

### Capital Allocation — 0.60 / 0.75

Mercury has grown ordinary dividends for 17 consecutive years, declared 24.0cps in FY2025, and guided to 25.0cps in FY2026. It targets ordinary dividends at 70–85% of free cash flow on average over time. At the same time, it is investing about NZ$1b across Ngā Tamariki expansion, Kaiwera Downs 2 and Kaiwaikawe, expected to add about 1.1TWh of renewable output; Q3 FY2026 said Ngā Tamariki OEC5 opened on time/on budget and Kaiwera Downs Stage 2 first generation started ahead of schedule.

Interpretation: management is doing sensible utility things: maintain the dividend, reinvest into renewable generation, and refurbish core hydro assets. The concern is the dividend plus growth-capex combination while leverage is high. This is rational if project returns clear the cost of capital, but it is not a pure cash-return story yet.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.35 / 0.75

Returns are modest. Yahoo reports TTM ROE of about 1.9% and ROA of about 3.0%; FY2025 reported NPAT was only NZ$1m against NZ$4.903b of equity. Even allowing for noisy derivative effects and a bad hydrology year, Mercury is an asset-heavy utility rather than a high-ROIC compounder.

Interpretation: this is the weakest quality trait. Stable infrastructure can still be investable, but the cash-cow score should not pretend Mercury earns software-like returns. Firm-up: use normalised FY2026 earnings and invested capital after the new generation assets contribute for a cleaner cycle-adjusted ROIC.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.38 / 0.80

Balance-sheet resilience is acceptable for a regulated/infrastructure-style utility but stretched for a cash-cow. FY2025 net debt was NZ$2.183b, gearing was 30.8%, and management's debt/EBITDAF was 2.5x, inside its 2.0–3.0x target range. Yahoo's TTM snapshot shows total debt of about NZ$2.397b, cash of NZ$94m and net debt/EBITDA around 3.0x; the statement-scorer view using FY2025 EBIT/EBITDA showed weak EBIT interest coverage and high net debt/EBITDA during the low-earnings year. Mercury had NZ$570m of undrawn borrowing facilities at 30 June 2025 and was in compliance with covenants.

Interpretation: not a distress case, but not conservative. The payout and growth programme leave less margin for another bad hydrology/price year. Firm-up: monitor FY2026 net debt/EBITDAF and interest cover after the guidance upgrade.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.80 / 0.65

Mercury is asset- and process-driven, not founder-led. The CEO changed in FY2025 and the business continued to execute major projects and operations. The infrastructure, customer book and governance structure matter more than any one individual.

Confidence is only moderate because this analysis did not deeply review the bench, operational leadership or project-delivery dependencies.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.60 / 0.55

The disclosed strategy and dividend policy are generally shareholder-relevant: EBITDAF growth, renewable development, customer outcomes, and long-term asset resilience. But this pass did not fully parse the remuneration scorecard, executive share ownership, or whether incentives are per-share-value focused versus growth/scale focused.

Firm-up: review the FY2025 remuneration report in detail and compare incentive metrics against ROIC, free cash flow per share, leverage and dividend coverage.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair lost mainly on returns on capital and only moderate reinvestment runway economics; Mercury is durable but not a high-return compounder. Freeroll lost because there is no obvious cheapness/catalyst setup: the stock trades around 2.0x book and roughly 24x forward earnings, not near a hard downside floor. Rule-breakers lost because Mercury is a mature utility, not a fast-growing emerging winner.

## Overall

Mercury is a decent-but-not-cheap renewable utility, best framed as a low-confidence cash-cow/infrastructure income idea rather than a compounding growth stock. Strengths are essential demand, long-lived renewable assets, dividend consistency, and visible near-term operational momentum from FY2026 generation recovery and project delivery. The main detractors are weak FY2025 accounting earnings, modest returns on capital, elevated leverage, and a dividend/growth-capex mix that makes current free cash flow coverage less clean than a classic cash cow.

I would keep it watched, but not prioritise fresh capital at NZ$6.90 unless the goal is defensive NZ utility income and the buyer is comfortable with low expected total return.

## Valuation

The cash-cow valuation uses an exit-multiple earnings model because current statutory FCF is distorted by heavy growth capex and accounting earnings are at least a visible market anchor. Inputs: NZ$6.90 price; NZ$0.275 current EPS fundamental, triangulated from Yahoo FY2026 consensus EPS of NZ$0.2745 and the improved FY2026 guidance backdrop; 2% annual earnings growth; 22x exit P/E versus an entry multiple of about 25x; 3.6% dividend yield; five-year hold.

The script result is about 3.2% annualized ROI. The attribution is mostly income: business growth contributes about 2.0% p.a., multiple compression from 25x to 22x subtracts about 2.6% p.a., and dividends add about 3.9% p.a. That is not terrible for a defensive utility, but it is too thin for a high-conviction opportunity. My agent score is 0.38: stable and useful to watch, but valuation leaves little margin for hydrology, leverage or execution disappointment.

## Thesis Connections

- Renewable-electrification exposure: Mercury benefits if New Zealand power demand rises with electrification, data/AI demand and industrial decarbonisation.
- Defensive income: potential fit for a lower-volatility dividend sleeve, not for aggressive compounding.
- Infrastructure scarcity: hydro/geothermal assets are hard to replicate, but that scarcity is already substantially reflected in the share price.

## Watch

- FY2026 result: confirm whether upgraded EBITDAF guidance of NZ$1.05b converts into NPAT, operating cash and debt reduction.
- Dividend coverage: track ordinary dividend versus free cash flow after separating stay-in-business capex from growth capex.
- Leverage: net debt/EBITDAF and interest cover after Ngā Tamariki, Kaiwera Downs 2 and Kaiwaikawe move from construction to contribution.
- Project execution: budget/schedule and output from Ngā Tamariki OEC5, Kaiwera Downs Stage 2, Kaiwaikawe and the hydro refurbishment programme.
- Regulation/politics: affordability pressure, market-design changes and security-of-supply interventions.
- Remuneration: review whether executive incentives reward per-share value and balance-sheet discipline, not just EBITDAF and project growth.

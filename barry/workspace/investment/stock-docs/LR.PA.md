# LR.PA — Legrand SA

## Business Overview

Legrand is a French electrical and digital-building infrastructure group. It sells wiring devices, cable management, power distribution, connected-building products, datacenter racks/power systems and energy-efficiency solutions across residential, non-residential and datacenter end markets. The company reports customers in around 170 countries, with 2025 sales split roughly **38% Europe**, **42% North and Central America**, **20% rest of world**, and by end market about **one-third residential**, **~40% non-residential**, and **26% datacenters**.

The business is a mature, cash-generative industrial that also has a real new growth leg in datacenter and energy/digital infrastructure. Mechanically it scores as a **Cash Cow / Wonderful-and-Fair dual-fit**, with Cash Cow narrowly ahead because current FCF generation is excellent. In 2025 Legrand reported **€9.48b sales**, **20.7% adjusted operating margin**, **€1.3b FCF / 14.0% of sales**, and **13.1% sales growth at constant FX**, driven by **+7.7% organic growth** and **+5.1% acquisition contribution**. Q1 2026 stayed strong: sales **€2.54b**, organic growth **+9.3%**, acquisition growth **+8.2%**, adjusted operating margin **20.7%**, and FCF **€221m**, with 2026 guidance for **+10–15% constant-FX sales growth** and **20.5–21.0% adjusted operating margin**.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.83 / confidence 0.82

Legrand has demonstrated clean, repeatable earning power over the Yahoo-visible period. Net income rose from **€1.00b in 2022** to **€1.24b in 2025**, with no loss years in the four-year data, low net-income growth dispersion, and cumulative FCF/net-income conversion of **1.15×**. EPS also rose from **€3.73** to **€4.71**. Gross margins stayed around **50%**, operating margins rose to **19.1%**, and receivables/revenue moved only modestly from **11.5%** to **12.9%**.

The caveat is data depth: yfinance only serves about four annual years here, not the full 7–10 year cycle the rubric wants. Confidence would firm up with a longer through-cycle table from Legrand filings and a deeper check on adjusted items, but the evidence available is clearly quality-positive.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.76 / confidence 0.82

Legrand is strongly cash-generative. Local `free_cash_flow.py` shows latest FCF of **€1.33b**, TTM FCF about **€1.36b**, latest FCF margin **14.0%**, four-year average FCF margin **15.0%**, and cumulative FCF/net-income conversion of **1.15×**. Capital intensity is low: capex consumed only **15.8%** of operating cash flow in 2025 and **~14–17%** across the four-year window.

The main caveat is capex below D&A, with capex/D&A around **0.61×** in 2025. For a specialist industrial with acquisitions and intangible amortisation that is not automatically harvesting, but it does require watch-list discipline. Confidence would improve with a clean split of maintenance versus growth capex.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.83 / confidence 0.82

The fundamentals are stable-to-improving, not a melting ice cube. Over 2022–2025 revenue, operating margin, EPS and FCF all moved upward; local `fundamental_stability.py` shows latest revenue, operating margin and EPS all at their four-year peaks. 2025 growth was supported by both organic demand and acquisitions, and Q1 2026 confirmed momentum with **+9.3% organic growth** despite weak Europe and parts of Asia-Pacific.

The main structural threat is building-cycle weakness, especially in Europe, plus the risk that datacenter demand normalises after a capex boom. For now, datacenter power infrastructure and energy-efficiency demand look like structural offsets rather than temporary patches.

### Returns on Capital — 0.58 / confidence 0.75

Returns are value-creating but not spectacular on a goodwill-inclusive basis. Local `returns_on_capital.py` shows ROIC around **9.8–11.0%** over 2022–2025, ROCE around **12.5–13.8%**, and incremental ROIC around **12.3%**. That is a positive spread for a steady industrial, but below the 20%+ level that would make this an obvious high-return compounder.

The main distortion is acquisition goodwill: goodwill is **~57% of invested capital** in the latest year and **43% of total assets**, so reported ROIC includes a large price-paid component from the acquisition strategy. The underlying branded/specialist product economics are better than the headline ROIC suggests, but the goodwill load is real and keeps the score moderate.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.78 / confidence 0.78

Legrand has a real moat from specialist product breadth, brand/channel position, standards/certifications, installed-base familiarity, and scale. The 2025 Universal Registration Document says **two-thirds of sales are generated by leading market positions**, the group has **~3,500 active patents in 61 countries**, and it employs **~2,800 people in R&D**. Financially, the moat shows up in **~50% gross margins** and a resilient **20.7% adjusted operating margin** despite mixed building markets.

The moat is not unassailable: electrical products are competitive, many categories face Schneider, ABB, Siemens, Eaton, Hubbell and local specialists, and datacenter growth attracts capable competitors. Still, the evidence points to a durable, broad, stable-to-widening advantage rather than a commodity industrial.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.74 / confidence 0.75

Legrand has a credible medium-term runway. Its 2030 ambition is about **€15b sales**, implying nearly **+10% average annual sales growth excluding FX**, and management targets **nearly €10b FCF over 2025–2030** with FCF averaging **13–15% of revenue**. The key runway is energy/digital transition: datacenters, energy transition and digital lifestyles represented **53% of 2025 sales**, and datacenters alone are now **26%** of revenue.

This is not an open-ended hypergrowth runway. Europe and parts of Asia/LatAm were weak in Q1 2026, and a material part of the runway comes through bolt-on acquisitions rather than purely organic expansion. The runway is good enough for Wonderful-and-Fair, but not pristine.

### Capital Allocation — 0.63 / confidence 0.72

Capital allocation is mostly rational but acquisition-heavy. Legrand combines low sustaining capex, a covered dividend, selective buybacks and a long-running bolt-on M&A engine. The local `capital_allocation.py` summary shows 2022–2025 cumulative dividends of **€2.07b**, buybacks of **€0.64b**, acquisitions of **€3.15b**, a latest payout ratio around **46%**, and dividend FCF cover of **2.31×**. The share count fell about **0.7%** over the period.

The concern is that external growth is central: Legrand completed **31 acquisitions from 2020–2025**, invested about **€4.3b**, and announced seven deals in 2025 representing **€500m annual revenue**, followed by four more Q1 2026 deals adding **~€275m annual revenue**. That is coherent and strategically focused, but goodwill is high and acquisition discipline has to remain excellent.

### Conservative Debt — 0.70 / confidence 0.80

The balance sheet is acceptable for a stable industrial, though leverage has risen with acquisitions. The 2025 URD reports net debt at **1.9× EBITDA**; Q1 2026 reports **2.1× EBITDA**. Local script output shows 2025 net debt/EBITDA **1.67×**, EBIT interest cover **20.2×**, and near-term debt cover **6.8×**. Liquidity looks fine: Yahoo shows current ratio **1.96** and quick ratio **1.26**.

The rating is not higher because debt and acquisition commitments are moving up; Yahoo’s TTM snapshot shows net debt/EBITDA around **2.17×**. A maturity-ladder and fixed/floating debt check from the URD would improve confidence further.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / confidence 0.65

This is an institutional business, not a founder cult. The advantage sits in product families, channel relationships, standards, brands, patents, acquisitions/integration process and a global operating system. CEO Benoît Coquart matters, but the investment case does not depend on one irreplaceable individual. Legrand also added three Executive Committee members in early 2026, suggesting bench depth.

Confidence is only moderate because I did not do a full succession and board-depth analysis beyond the URD excerpts.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.64 / confidence 0.70

The incentive framework is directionally aligned but not perfect. CEO compensation includes fixed pay, annual variable pay and long-term variable compensation. The disclosed measures include organic sales growth, adjusted operating margin before/after acquisitions, external sales growth, CSR roadmap achievement, and relative share-price performance versus the EURO STOXX Industrial Goods & Services index.

That rewards growth, margin discipline and relative shareholder return, which is good. The soft spot is that sales/external-growth metrics can still encourage M&A volume if not balanced by return-on-capital discipline. I did not find enough evidence on personal capital at risk or post-vesting holding to score this higher.

**Strategy fit:** Cash Cow narrowly wins mechanically on durable earnings, strong FCF conversion, pricing power and a covered dividend. Wonderful-and-Fair is a genuine near-tie: the reinvestment runway is real, but goodwill-heavy ROIC and acquisition dependence keep it from being the cleaner primary frame. Freeroll loses because there is no hard valuation floor at today’s price: Yahoo shows **4.8× book**, negative tangible book per share, net debt, and only a **~1.7% dividend yield**. Rule Breakers loses because Legrand is not an early-stage disruptor; it is a high-quality incumbent participating in a structural datacenter wave, with growth helped materially by acquisitions.

## Overall

Legrand is a good company at a not-cheap price. The business has strong margins, excellent cash conversion, a credible datacenter/energy-transition runway and a focused acquisition machine. The main negatives are valuation, goodwill-heavy returns on capital, and dependence on continuing acquisition discipline. I would rather own it than ignore it, but at **~24× my current EPS anchor** and **~19.5× EV/EBITDA**, the valuation leaves less room for mistakes than the quality deserves.

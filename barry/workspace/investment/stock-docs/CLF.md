# Cleveland-Cliffs (CLF) — stock analysis

## Verdict

Cleveland-Cliffs is an unloved cyclical steel turnaround, not a quality compounder. The stock has real upside if U.S. steel pricing, automotive demand, trade enforcement, cost cuts and a possible POSCO transaction all line up. But the balance sheet is heavy, free cash flow has been ugly, and the business has almost no margin for another weak steel cycle.

At **$11.93** (2026-07-24 close), the Q2 earnings reaction has removed some of the former price asymmetry. The operating evidence is materially better, however: Q2 adjusted EBITDA tripled sequentially to **$286M**, free cash flow turned positive, and management guided Q3 adjusted EBITDA to about **$575M**. I’d still classify CLF as a **speculative Freeroll / cyclical rebound watch**, not a core buy: the recovery is now visible, but leverage remains high and part of Q2 cash generation came from higher payables.

**Rating:** watch / small speculative only. Q3 delivery against the **$575M** EBITDA guide and sustained free cash flow matter more now than POSCO, which remains under discussion but has no deadline and no agreed valuation or structure.

## Business snapshot

Cleveland-Cliffs is a vertically integrated North American steel producer focused on value-added sheet products, especially automotive. The chain runs from iron ore mining, pellets and direct reduced iron through primary steelmaking, finishing, stamping, tooling and tubing. It employs about **25,000** people across the U.S. and Canada.

The mix is meaningfully cyclical. FY2025 steel product volume was **16.2m net tons**: roughly **40% hot-rolled**, **28% coated**, **15% cold-rolled**, **5% plate**, **3% stainless/electrical**, and **9% other/slabs**. End markets were diversified but still highly macro-sensitive: infrastructure/manufacturing, distributors/converters, direct automotive customers, and steel producers.

## Why it fits “Freeroll”

CLF is not a Rule Breaker and not a Wonderful-and-Fair compounder. It is asset-heavy, cyclical, leveraged, union/labour/energy exposed, and dependent on steel spreads. The right frame is: **is the market over-discounting a trough, and is there a catalyst to close the gap?**

The bull case has four legs:

- **Trough earnings:** FY2025 was brutal: revenue **$18.6B**, GAAP net loss about **$1.4B**, adjusted EBITDA only **$37M**, and operating cash flow **-$462M**. That is a depressed base, not a normal year.
- **Sequential recovery:** Q2 revenue rose to **$5.2B**, average selling price reached **$1,124/ton**, steel cash margin increased to **$349M**, and adjusted EBITDA rose to **$286M** from **$95M** in Q1. Operating cash flow was **$230M** and capex **$157M**, implying about **$73M** of simple free cash flow.
- **Visible company guidance:** management guided Q3 adjusted EBITDA to about **$575M**, expects Q4 to exceed Q3, and maintained 2026 expectations for **16.5–17.0m tons** of shipments and about **$700M** capex.
- **Catalysts:** Q3 margin expansion, about **$400M** of contracted property-sale proceeds expected mainly in H2, higher fixed-price contract resets that management estimates can add **$500M** of 2027 EBITDA, trade enforcement, and cost reductions. POSCO is now optional upside rather than the base catalyst: discussions remain friendly, but management says valuation and structure must improve and it has no deadline.

The bear case is equally blunt: CLF had **$7.7B** long-term debt against only **$70M** cash at June 2026, and debt remained above the 2025 year-end level despite a **$63M** Q2 ABL repayment. If Q3 guidance misses or steel prices roll over, equity value gets squeezed fast.

## Trait scoring

- **Downside support — 0.55 (confidence 0.72):** June equity was **$5.8B**, roughly **$10/share**, and the revised **$8.50** floor sits below book value and the current quote. But it is a soft cyclical floor: debt is large, asset values are earnings-sensitive, and the stock traded below it within the last year. Confidence would firm with sustained debt reduction and a full-cycle asset-value reconciliation.
- **Fundamental stability — 0.60 (confidence 0.78):** the trough now looks more cyclical than terminal. Q2 EBITDA rose to **$286M** and simple FCF turned positive, with management guiding a further sharp Q3 step-up. The score stays below clear-pass territory because the quarter remained loss-making and higher payables more than explained the positive FCF. Q3 cash conversion will firm this up.
- **Mispricing — 0.58 (confidence 0.68):** the market had over-discounted the 2025 trough, but the **27%** price rise from the prior $9.40 valuation input to $11.93 has recognized part of the recovery. Upside remains if Q3/Q4 EBITDA and 2027 contract resets land, but this is no longer a near-floor entry. Confidence would improve with externally observable contract resets and durable mid-cycle cash earnings.
- **Catalyst — 0.75 (confidence 0.78):** the catalyst has shifted from a hoped-for POSCO deal to visible operating delivery: **$575M** Q3 EBITDA guidance, further Q4 improvement, approximately **$400M** of contracted property sales, and higher 2027 fixed-price contracts. POSCO remains friendly but open-ended. Q3 results and disclosed asset-sale closings should resolve the remaining uncertainty.

## Valuation

Freeroll/asymmetric payoff model:

- Entry price: **$11.93** — 2026-07-24 close from Yahoo Finance
- Downside floor: **$8.50/share** — a conservative blend of stressed trading history and discounted June book value, not a liquidation value
- Fair value / sell target: **$18/share** — assumes mid-cycle EBITDA recovery and a market willing to re-rate CLF as leverage and cash flow improve; reduced from $20 for POSCO timing slippage and still-unproven FCF turn
- Catalyst probability: **60%** — Q2 delivered the first cash-flow proof and Q3 guidance is explicit, partly offset by execution and steel-price risk
- Window: **1.5 years**

This gives:

- Downside to floor: **-29%**
- Upside to fair value: **+51%**
- Upside/downside asymmetry: **~1.8x**
- Probability-weighted annualized ROI: **~12.3%**

The higher price reduces the freeroll geometry, but the operating probability has improved enough to lift my judgement score to **0.62**. It is still investable only if Nick specifically wants cyclical steel exposure. The downside floor is soft and debt can turn a normal cyclical miss into real equity pain.

A more bullish case is possible: if normalized EBITDA returns toward **$2B+**, debt comes down, and the market prices CLF at a more confident mid-cycle multiple, **$20–25** is still plausible. The bear case is also obvious: if steel prices roll over or POSCO/FCF catalysts fade, CLF can stay in or below single digits.

## Key risks

1. **Steel cycle risk:** HRC/steel spreads drive the thesis. CLF is operationally levered to pricing.
2. **Balance sheet:** Q2 2026 long-term debt was **$7.7B**. Liquidity was **$3.1B**, but most is credit availability, not cash.
3. **Automotive exposure:** weak auto production hurt 2025. A prolonged auto slump would damage volumes and mix.
4. **Policy/trade risk:** tariffs and import restrictions help CLF, but policy can change and retaliation can hurt customers.
5. **Execution risk:** cost cuts, footprint optimization, Stelco integration benefits, and any POSCO deal all need execution.
6. **Environmental/labour/capex intensity:** steel is capital-intensive, regulated, energy-heavy and labour-sensitive.

## Watch list

- Q3 2026: delivery against approximately **$575M adjusted EBITDA**, and whether Q2's positive free cash flow persists without another payable build.
- Q4 2026: management's claim that EBITDA should exceed Q3, plus evidence the recovery survives normal seasonality.
- Average steel selling price and cash margin per ton.
- Whether 2026 shipment guidance of **16.5–17.0m tons** holds.
- Net debt reduction, revolver borrowings, interest expense, and credit ratings.
- Closing and proceeds of the approximately **$400M** contracted property sales; confirm proceeds go to debt.
- Evidence behind the estimated **$500M** 2027 EBITDA uplift from fixed-price contract resets.
- POSCO MoU: treat as optional unless valuation and structure become shareholder-accretive; no deadline is currently in place.
- U.S./Canadian steel trade enforcement and import volumes.
- Automotive production volumes and contract pricing.

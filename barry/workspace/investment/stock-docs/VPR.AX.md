# VPR.AX — Volt Group Limited

## Business Overview

Volt Group is a micro-cap Australian industrial technology company selling proprietary equipment and service solutions into mining, resources and energy customers. It is not a single-product speculative developer: FY25 was profitable, cash-generative, and FY26 now includes four operating/growth pillars:

- **Wescone** — OEM of the W300 sample crusher, used mainly in iron ore and assay laboratories. This is the legacy profitable niche asset with patent protection and long operating history.
- **4D Delta** — acquired in January 2026; digital asset inspection and condition-monitoring services using 3D laser scanning, proprietary processing software and a customer portal. Customers referenced by Volt include Rio Tinto, BHP, Alcoa, South32, Ok Tedi, and North American cement/lime producers.
- **EcoQuip** — mobile solar light, communications and surveillance towers for remote resource sites. Westgold now has 37 towers; six units remain at Chevron's Gorgon project after 47 were returned at the end of a five-year deployment.
- **ATEN/HYTEN** — earlier-stage waste-heat-to-power and waste-heat-to-hydrogen technology. Potentially valuable, but not yet the underwriting anchor.

FY25 revenue was $5.10m, adjusted EBITDA $1.40m and net operating cash flow $2.09m. HY26 strengthened after 4D Delta: ordinary revenue receipts reached $5.1m, up 134% on HY25; receipts excluding 4D Delta rose 15% to $2.52m; and adjusted net operating cash flow was $1.85m, excluding $0.52m of one-off acquisition costs. Cash was $3.63m at 30 Jun 2026, and management said FY26 revenue guidance of $11.0–12.2m and EBITDA guidance of $4.1–4.8m remained generally on track. The latest quote used here is $0.105 (Yahoo close, 7 Aug 2026), with 163.6m quoted ordinary shares, market cap roughly $17.2m and EV about $13.6m, or ~2.8–3.3x guided FY26 EBITDA. Volt's announcement index showed no filing after the 30 Jul Q2 update when checked on 9 Aug 2026.

No universal disqualifier found on current evidence: the company is profitable, had cash, modest borrowings/facilities, positive operating cash flow, and no identified fraud/solvency/business-model kill-shot in the primary filings reviewed.

## Trait Assessment

### Downside Support / Valuation Floor — 0.50 / confidence 0.70

Facts: Volt had $3.63m cash at 30 Jun 2026 versus a ~$17.2m market cap at $0.105/share. FY25 net assets were $6.88m, or roughly $0.042/share on the current share count. The stronger support is earnings: HY26 adjusted net operating cash flow was $1.85m, and FY26 EBITDA guidance of $4.1–4.8m remains on track according to management.

Interpretation: the floor is **not** a hard asset floor. It is an earnings-supported floor, which remains weaker because it depends on FY26 execution. I use **$0.10/share** as a rough downside case: 4.8% below the latest quote, above book/cash-only liquidation value, and supported if the business stays profitable but the market discounts guidance. Confidence would firm up with reported HY26 EBITDA and segment-level cash generation; receipts and management-adjusted operating cash flow are useful but not a substitute.

### Fundamental Stability / No Value Trap — 0.75 / confidence 0.75

Facts: Volt is not loss-making: FY25 statutory profit was $0.45m, adjusted EBITDA was $1.40m, and operating cash flow was positive. HY26 delivered $5.1m of receipts and $1.85m of adjusted operating cash flow; even excluding 4D Delta, receipts grew 15%. Cash was $3.63m at half-year end, and ASX removed Volt's requirement to file Appendix 4Cs after consistent positive cash flow.

Interpretation: earning power appears to be improving rather than structurally eroding. The main caveat is that FY26 growth is acquisition-boosted and several divisions are still scaling. This is small-company execution risk, not a classic melting-ice-cube value trap on current evidence. Reported HY26 EBITDA and full cash-flow reconciliation would firm this up.

### Mispriced for an Identifiable Reason — 0.70 / confidence 0.65

Facts: the stock trades around ~2.8–3.3x guided FY26 EV/EBITDA, assuming guidance is delivered. FY25 looked optically weak because revenue and EBITDA declined, partly due to Wescone's Africa distributor transition and acquisition/non-recurring costs. VPR is tiny and illiquid: several July/August sessions traded no shares, so institutional demand and price discovery are naturally limited.

Interpretation: the likely mispricing is simple: the market is discounting a micro-cap with messy historical numbers, acquisition integration and poor liquidity. HY26 receipts, cash flow and 4D Delta delivery give better grounds to disagree with that discount, but management guidance still does substantial valuation work and there is little independent coverage. Reported HY26 EBITDA would close the key evidence gap.

### Identifiable, Time-Bound Catalyst — 0.80 / confidence 0.75

Facts: the first catalyst checkpoint was positive: HY26 receipts reached $5.1m, adjusted operating cash flow was $1.85m, 4D Delta delivered $1.28m of Q2 receipts in line with its acquisition budget, and platform assets grew 21% to 883. Wescone HY26 receipts rose 68% to $1.73m. EcoQuip was mixed: 29 more towers went to Westgold, but Q2 receipts fell 38% to $0.32m after Chevron returned 47 towers following a five-year deployment; founder Dave Sharp also departed and the division was restructured.

Interpretation: the re-rating catalyst is increasingly concrete, with the next 6–18 months centred on reported HY26/FY26 EBITDA and free-cash-flow conversion. The 4D Delta/Wescone evidence raises the odds of guidance delivery; EcoQuip redeployment and management transition are the clearest swing factors. Confidence would firm up with reported HY26 earnings rather than receipts and management-adjusted cash flow.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.62 / confidence 0.75

Facts: the strongest positive is real insider ownership. In the FY25 annual report, Adam Boyd and related entities were disclosed with **28.4m shares / 17.42%** of issued capital, and Simon Higgins and related entities with **8.48m / 5.19%**. Directors also participated in the January 2026 placement at **$0.135/share**: Boyd's disclosed holdings rose across direct/related entities, Bill Johnston acquired 148,148 shares, and Peter Torre's related holder acquired 370,270 shares. The FY25 remuneration report says there is **no short-term incentive scheme** currently, and variable pay is mostly options/performance rights under the Employee Incentive Plan. Vesting conditions are mixed but directionally useful: revenue up 150% from FY24 revenue, 24-month service plus **60-day VWAP >= $0.40**, first ATEN construction start, and EcoQuip deployment hurdles. James Garwood's June 2026 performance rights used similar conditions plus a 150-unit EcoQuip deployment hurdle. The securities trading policy prohibits directors/KMP hedging company securities, including vested/unvested incentive securities. The 4D Delta vendors received **26.85m shares at $0.135/share escrowed until 6 July 2027**, plus earn-outs tied to CY26/CY27 EBITDA above $1.5m and capped at $2.25m, with Volt able to pay up to 50% in shares. The FY26 AGM remuneration report vote was strongly supportive: **99.81% for / 0.19% against** on poll.

Offsets: the system is not clean enough for a high score. Volt does **not** have a separate remuneration committee; remuneration is handled by the full Board. Some hurdles reward **revenue or deployment counts**, not per-share value, return on capital, or free cash flow per share, so they can still encourage growth-for-growth's-sake. Non-executive directors receive performance rights, which is common in micro-caps but weaker governance than plain fees plus personally bought shares. The unquoted overhang is material for a company this size: the Q2 FY26 report's issued-capital summary showed **10.5m unlisted options and 6.2m performance rights**, or roughly **16.7m potential shares**, before any future earn-out shares. Q1 FY26 also disclosed **$206,848** of related-party director-service payments, including a **$77,000 bonus** to the Executive Chairman, and the annual report showed directors were owed **$767,917** of unpaid director fees at 31 Dec 2025. Related-party office consumables were tiny and disclosed, but the unpaid fees/bonus warrant monitoring.

Interpretation: **0.62 / 0.75** reflects meaningful founder/director ownership, no current STI, anti-hedging language, shareholder-approved equity incentives, director placement participation, and escrowed/EBITDA-linked 4D Delta consideration. These point management broadly in the same direction as shareholders. The score stays in the low 0.60s because dilution is non-trivial, some targets are imperfect, and governance is founder/micro-cap style rather than a best-practice owner-operator setup.

**Strategy fit:** This is a genuine near-tie between Freeroll and Wonderful-and-Fair. Freeroll wins mechanically because the near-term FY26 delivery catalyst is the cleanest way the market gap closes; Wonderful-and-Fair remains a plausible secondary frame if Volt proves durable high-return compounding over the next 1–2 reporting periods. Rule Breaker lost more clearly: Volt has attractive niches, but it is not yet an obvious category-defining top dog in one explosive market, and growth is partly acquisition-led.

## Overall

VPR is interesting. It is a profitable micro-cap with proprietary niche products, a much larger FY26 earnings base after 4D Delta, positive cash flow, low balance-sheet stress, and a valuation that does not look demanding if FY26 guidance is credible.

The clean bull case is simple: FY25 was a transition year, 4D Delta adds a higher-growth inspection/software-enabled services platform, Wescone/EcoQuip provide industrial niches, and HY26 receipts/cash flow indicate management's FY26 guidance is plausible. The market is still valuing the group at only ~2.8–3.3x guided FY26 EBITDA. If guidance converts into reported earnings and cash, this can re-rate quickly.

The bear case is also simple: it is tiny, illiquid, acquisition-adjusted guidance is doing a lot of work, segment economics are not yet transparent, and the company has several moving parts for a business of this size. EcoQuip must redeploy 47 returned Chevron towers while absorbing its founder's departure. The stock is not backed by a hard asset floor; it still needs FY26 delivery.

Current rating: **7.2/10** — the HY26 checkpoint improves the execution case and the lower price improves asymmetry, but conviction still depends on reported EBITDA/free cash flow and EcoQuip redeployment.

## Watch

- EcoQuip: redeployment economics/timing for the 47 returned Chevron towers, Westgold fleet utilisation, post-founder management execution, and fleet-growth capital needs.

- Dilution: options/performance rights/earn-out shares and any further capital raises.
- Segment disclosure: next update should extract revenue, EBITDA and capital employed by Wescone / 4D Delta / EcoQuip / ATEN if available.
- Liquidity: daily trading volume is thin and recent price moves have occurred on very low volume; valuation may be less actionable than the headline upside suggests.

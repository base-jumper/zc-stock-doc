# DASH — DoorDash, Inc.

## Business Overview

DoorDash operates local-commerce marketplaces (DoorDash, Wolt and Deliveroo) and a Commerce Platform for merchants' own channels. It earns mainly from merchant commissions and consumer fees net of courier payouts, plus memberships, advertising and merchant software/fulfilment. In Q2 2026 its marketplaces operated in more than 40 countries; 970 million orders generated $33.1 billion of marketplace gross order value (GOV) and $4.45 billion of revenue. The flywheel is broader merchant selection attracting consumers, order density improving fulfilment economics, and those audiences supporting ads and merchant services.

Reported Q2 growth included Deliveroo, acquired for $3.72 billion in October 2025, but the legacy business remained strong: excluding Deliveroo, orders grew 17%, GOV 23% and revenue 24%. Q2 GAAP net income was $200 million, adjusted EBITDA was $914 million and free cash flow was $742 million. DoorDash ended June with $6.2 billion of cash and investments against $2.75 billion of 2030 convertible notes.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.84 / 0.82

Local commerce continues shifting online, while advertising, grocery/retail delivery and merchant software expand the opportunity beyond restaurant delivery. Q2 marketplace GOV rose 36% year on year, or 23% excluding Deliveroo, and the platform remains underpenetrated relative to total local-commerce spend. SevenRooms venues signed rose more than 100% and digital-ordering revenue rose more than 40%, while DoorDash is also investing in AI-assisted discovery and autonomous delivery. Structurally competitive economics and labour/regulatory exposure prevent an exceptional score.

### Top Dog — 0.86 / 0.88

DoorDash is the scaled US category leader and now has a material international position through Wolt and Deliveroo. Q2 orders grew 27% to 970 million, providing density, consumer reach and merchant distribution that smaller rivals struggle to match. Uber remains a formidable, better-diversified competitor, so this is leadership rather than winner-take-all certainty.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.82 / 0.84

The moat combines two-sided marketplace scale, delivery density, consumer memberships, merchant integrations and a growing advertising layer. These reinforce selection, fulfilment speed and unit economics, but multi-homing is easy and rivals can subsidise both sides. DoorDash says older US consumer cohorts show rising DashPass penetration, order rates and adjusted gross profit per monthly active user; Wolt's month-three and month-six cohort order rates also improved year on year alongside unit economics. This is stronger evidence that scale and membership deepen engagement, though confidence would rise further with a consistent quantified retention series and country-level share.

### Durable Growth — 0.89 / 0.92

DoorDash is a maturing growth platform, so revenue, orders, GOV and cash conversion are the right measures. Revenue grew 31%, 24% and 28% in 2023–2025, with gross margin rising each year. Q2 revenue grew 36%, orders 27% and GOV 36%; excluding Deliveroo, those rates were still 24%, 17% and 23%. GAAP net income was $200 million, Q2 FCF rose to $742 million, and trailing FCF reached $2.14 billion. Growth remains high and cash conversion is improving, although management expects year-end merchant-payment timing to reduce reported 2026 FCF by $700–800 million.

### Organic Growth — 0.68 / 0.92

The core grew strongly before the latest deals, and Q2 finally separates much of the acquired contribution: excluding Deliveroo, orders grew 17%, GOV 23% and revenue 24%. That is strong organic evidence. Still, the $3.72 billion Deliveroo purchase created $1.99 billion of goodwill and $1.50 billion of acquired intangibles, while SevenRooms and Deliveroo materially broadened capabilities and geography. Growth is predominantly organic in the current quarter but capital allocation is no longer purely organic.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.82 / 0.82

Co-founder Tony Xu remains CEO and DoorDash has expanded from US restaurant delivery into a global local-commerce platform while improving GAAP profitability. Q2 provides early positive integration evidence: Deliveroo accelerated MAU and order growth and exceeded management's profit expectations, while Wolt cohorts improved engagement and unit economics. DoorDash is consolidating international technology and functions, with the global platform targeted for full rollout in the first half of 2027. Longer-term Deliveroo returns and segment economics remain the test, but execution evidence is improving.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.50 / 0.94

Founder leadership supports long-term orientation, but the dual-class structure concentrates voting power and equity compensation remains expensive. DoorDash expects $1.2–1.3 billion of 2026 SBC. Against that, it repurchased 5.7 million shares for $887 million in Q2 and $1.0 billion in the first half; weighted-average diluted shares were 439 million, roughly flat with 438 million a year earlier. The repurchases are now large enough to offset dilution, but they still use cash to neutralise a real owner cost rather than create clean per-share growth.

Governance weakened further on 6 August: holders controlling **54.2% of voting power** approved by written consent a reincorporation from Delaware to Nevada, without a public-holder vote. The final charter and bylaws will arrive with the Schedule 14C, so the precise change in minority-holder protections is not yet reviewable, but the process reinforces the control discount and lowers the score.

### Customer Devotion — 0.83 / 0.84

Membership programs now provide direct evidence of voluntary expansion rather than just habitual use. DoorDash added more US paid DashPass members in the 12 months through Q2 than in the prior 24 months combined. Management reports that older US cohorts show rising DashPass penetration, order rates and adjusted gross profit per MAU; DashPass members placed about 75% of US grocery and retail orders. New SevenRooms venues more than doubled and digital-ordering revenue grew over 40%. Stickiness is partly preference—members choose to deepen use—but also reflects affordability and network availability, so it is not pure product love. The remaining evidence gap is a quantified retention/churn series or independent longitudinal satisfaction data.

Rule Breakers wins mechanically because DoorDash combines a strong industry runway, leadership and durable growth. Wonderful-and-fair loses on the short record of consistent GAAP earnings and low historical ROIC; cash-cow loses because surplus cash is still being reinvested and diluted rather than durably distributed; freeroll lacks a hard valuation floor and time-bound re-rating catalyst. No universal disqualifier was identified: filings show ample liquidity, positive cash flow and no disclosed kill-shot liability or accounting-integrity issue.

## Overall

DoorDash is a high-quality growth platform with category leadership, strong organic growth beneath the Deliveroo contribution, improving cash generation and better evidence that membership deepens cohorts. The main deductions are weak historical ROIC, costly SBC, founder voting control, acquisition/integration risk, competition and courier regulation. At $209.86, the business still looks better than the prospective return: the price capitalises substantial continued growth and margin execution.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a long-duration digital-local-commerce thesis: marketplace density, memberships, ads and merchant software can compound together. It does not fit a near-term value or income thesis.

## Watch

- Track legacy growth excluding Deliveroo and whether Deliveroo continues to accelerate MAUs/orders while exceeding profit expectations; monitor integration costs and the $1.99 billion goodwill balance.
- Track GOV growth versus net revenue margin and GAAP operating margin; scale should eventually produce clearer operating leverage.
- Track SBC, diluted shares and net repurchases each quarter; require genuine per-share FCF growth.
- Seek quantified DashPass/Wolt+/Deliveroo Plus retention or churn and independent longitudinal satisfaction data; management's directional cohort disclosure is encouraging but not a full retention series.
- Monitor regulatory classification and courier-cost changes across the US, EU and UK.
- Global platform rollout in H1 2027: look for product velocity, redundancy savings and measurable international unit-economics benefits.
- Review the Schedule 14C, Nevada articles and bylaws when filed; compare director/officer liability, fiduciary-duty and minority-holder protections with the Delaware documents.

# INTU — Intuit Inc.

## Business Overview

Intuit is a US financial-software platform built around TurboTax, Credit Karma, QuickBooks, Mailchimp, ProTax and Intuit Enterprise Suite. From FY2026 it reports two segments: **Consumer** (TurboTax, Credit Karma and ProTax) and **Global Business Solutions** (QuickBooks, Enterprise Suite, payroll, payments, capital and Mailchimp). The model is predominantly recurring/service software revenue, with very high gross margins and low physical capital intensity.

The current operating story is straightforward: QuickBooks/Global Business Solutions is the steadier B2B compounding engine; Consumer is tax-season-heavy but highly cash-generative; Credit Karma adds personal-finance distribution; and management is pushing the portfolio toward an “AI-driven expert platform” with AI agents plus human experts. In Q3 FY2026, revenue grew 10% to $8.6b, Consumer grew 8%, Global Business Solutions grew 15%, and Online Ecosystem grew 19%. FY2026 company guidance is for revenue of $21.341–$21.374b, up about 13–14%, and non-GAAP EPS of $23.80–$23.85.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.84 / 0.78

Intuit has a clean recent record of profitability and cash conversion. Yahoo-derived annuals show net income rising from $2.1b in FY2022 to $3.9b in FY2025, no loss years in the four-year data window, and cumulative FCF conversion of about 1.7x net income. FCF was $6.1b in FY2025 versus $3.9b of net income; TTM free cash flow is reported around $7.7b. Receivables as a percentage of revenue were stable-to-improving around 2.8–3.5% in the available annuals.

The caveat is stock-based compensation: SBC was $2.0b in FY2025, roughly 32% of FCF, so adjusted profit needs some skepticism. Confidence is below 0.8 because the automated Yahoo window only covers four complete fiscal years; a deeper 7–10 year filing pull would firm up through-cycle consistency.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.72 / 0.76

Returns are good and improving rather than elite on a goodwill-inclusive basis. The scorer output shows ROIC rising from 9.1% in FY2022 to 15.8% in FY2025, ROCE rising from 10.9% to 19.1%, and a high incremental ROIC estimate. ROE is not being massively flattered by leverage: latest ROE minus ROIC is only about 3.8 points.

The main wrinkle is goodwill. Goodwill was $14.0b in FY2025, about 54% of invested capital, so returns including acquisition goodwill understate the economics of the core software franchise but also remind us that prior M&A capital must earn its keep. A filing-level tangible-capital and acquisition-return bridge would firm this up.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.82 / 0.68

The moat is real: TurboTax and QuickBooks are category-defining products with brand, trust, workflow integration, switching costs and data advantages. The financial signature backs that up: gross margin is around 80%, operating margin has expanded materially, and FY2026 guidance implies continued double-digit revenue and EPS growth. Q3 FY2026 Global Business Solutions growth of 15% and Online Ecosystem growth of 19% suggests QuickBooks still has pricing/mix/attachment power.

The risks are also real. Tax software is politically and regulatorily exposed, Credit Karma is more macro/credit-cycle sensitive, and AI could lower software switching friction over time. Confidence is modest because I have not pulled cohort retention, churn, NPS or segment-level pricing/volume disclosure.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.76 / 0.66

Intuit still has a long runway in small/mid-market business workflows, payments/payroll/capital attachment, assisted tax, and AI-enabled expert services. Management is specifically leaning into mid-market businesses, Intuit Enterprise Suite and AI agents; Q3 FY2026 commentary says mid-market is growing north of 30%. That is a credible organic reinvestment route on top of a large installed base.

This is not an early-stage blank canvas: consumer tax is mature, QuickBooks is already large in its core markets, and some expansion depends on cross-sell and adjacencies rather than pure greenfield adoption. Confidence would improve with an investor-day TAM bridge and penetration data by QuickBooks Online, payments, payroll and Enterprise Suite.

### Capital Allocation — 0.66 / 0.74

Recent allocation is mostly sensible but not pristine. The core business requires little capex, the balance sheet is conservative, the dividend is well-covered, and shareholder returns are meaningful: from FY2022–FY2025 the company paid $3.9b of dividends and repurchased $8.6b of stock, while the latest dividend was covered more than 5x by FCF. In Q3 FY2026 management repurchased $1.6b of stock and the board approved a new $8b authorization, which is potentially attractive if repurchases occurred near the depressed 2026 price.

The mark-down is M&A. The Credit Karma/Mailchimp era left very large goodwill, and FY2022 acquisition spend was $5.7b. That does not make Intuit a bad allocator, but it means per-share compounding has been partly diluted by expensive strategic deals. Share count has barely fallen over the last four fiscal years despite large buybacks, partly because SBC absorbs some of the repurchase spend.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.92 / 0.86

This is a major strength. Latest snapshot net debt is essentially flat at about $0.1b, with net debt/EBITDA around 0.02x and EBIT interest coverage around 87x. Annual data show leverage falling from 1.22x net debt/EBITDA in FY2022 to 0.52x in FY2025, and FY2025 near-term debt coverage is extremely strong. There is no solvency concern.

Confidence is high, though a debt-maturity ladder and fixed/floating split from the 10-K would tighten the refinancing-risk read.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.80 / 0.68

The thesis is about the franchise, customer base, data, brands and product ecosystem, not a single irreplaceable founder. Sasan Goodarzi is important and appears to have kept the company focused on platform expansion and AI, but Intuit is a 40-year-old institution with deep product and go-to-market systems. The 2026 proxy says the board maintains both long-term succession planning and emergency short-term plans. Loss of one executive would be disruptive, not thesis-breaking.

Confidence remains below 0.8 because the proxy does not name a tested successor or provide enough detail to judge bench depth; a future CEO transition would be the cleanest test.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.68 / 0.82

The 2026 proxy closes the largest evidence gap. About 96% of FY2025 named-executive direct compensation was performance-based; the CEO's $34.6m target equity grant was 50% three-year relative-TSR PSUs, 25% four-year RSUs and 25% options. Target PSU vesting requires 60th-percentile TSR versus the S&P 500, and the CEO's RSUs and PSUs carry an additional one-year post-vesting hold. Intuit also requires CEO ownership of 10x salary, prohibits hedging and pledging, and applies clawbacks to incentive compensation. Yahoo reports insiders hold about 2.35% of shares, and a director bought 1,750 shares around $309 in May 2026.

The plan is still mixed rather than excellent. Annual cash incentives rely on revenue and non-GAAP operating income, which can reward size and excludes real SBC expense; the CEO's equity quantum is large; and FY2025 SBC of $2.0b was roughly 32% of FCF while the diluted share count barely fell despite substantial buybacks. Long horizons, relative TSR hurdles and holding/clawback rules point management broadly toward owners, but dilution and adjusted metrics keep the score below the strong-alignment band.

### Why the other strategies lost

**Cash Cow** actually screens as a genuine near-tie because Intuit is exceptionally cash-generative and returns capital; I am framing the doc as Wonderful and Fair because the reinvestment runway in QuickBooks, Enterprise Suite, money/payroll and assisted/AI tax still matters more than pure harvest-and-return. **Rule Breakers** gets credit for durable growth, top-dog products and AI optionality, but Intuit is already a mature, profitable compounder rather than an emerging category disruptor. **Freeroll** fails structurally: the stock may be cheaper than usual, but there is no hard asset/income floor near the current price and no specific ≤4-year catalyst that forces a re-rating.

## Overall

Intuit is a high-quality compounder at an undemanding current quote. The business has category-leading software assets, high margins, low capital intensity, strong cash conversion, conservative leverage and still-plausible reinvestment runway in small business, payments, payroll, assisted tax and AI-enabled workflows.

The main detractors are not existential: heavy SBC and mixed pay metrics, prior expensive M&A, goodwill-heavy capital, regulatory/political exposure in tax preparation, and incomplete evidence on customer devotion. At the current price, the valuation is doing a lot of work in the positive direction; if the quote normalizes materially higher, the thesis becomes more “wonderful” than “fair”.

# VWS.CO — Vestas Wind Systems A/S

## Business Overview

Vestas is a global wind-energy equipment and services company headquartered in Denmark. It designs, manufactures, installs and services onshore and offshore wind turbines. The business has two main economic engines: Power Solutions, where orders and execution drive turbine revenue but margins are cyclical and project-execution sensitive; and Service, where the installed base creates recurring contractual revenue at structurally higher margins.

The FY2025 result showed the recovery: revenue of EUR 18.822bn, EBIT margin before special items of 5.7%, order intake of 16.3 GW, and combined Power Solutions plus Service backlog of EUR 71.9bn. Q1 2026 continued that direction: revenue of EUR 3.966bn (+14.4% YoY), EBIT margin before special items of 3.2%, turbine order intake of 4.504 GW (+44% YoY), and combined backlog of EUR 76.1bn. Management maintained FY2026 guidance for EUR 20-22bn revenue and 6-8% EBIT margin before special items.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.45 score, 0.75 confidence

Earning power is real but not yet reliably predictable enough for a high wonderful-and-fair score. Yahoo financials show a EUR 1.572bn net loss and negative EUR 1.014bn FCF in 2022, then recovery to EUR 77m net income in 2023, EUR 499m in 2024 and EUR 778m in 2025. FCF has backed the recovery well in the last two full years: EUR 1.177bn in 2024 and EUR 1.008bn in 2025 versus net income of EUR 499m and EUR 778m. The problem is consistency, not cash conversion in the recovery years: turbine OEM margins remain exposed to supply chain, warranty, execution, commodity and policy cycles.

Confidence is below high because Yahoo only supplies roughly four annual years here; a full 7-10 year through-cycle filing read would firm up how abnormal 2022 was and how durable the recent margin recovery is.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.62 score, 0.75 confidence

Returns have recovered sharply but are not yet proven durable. The derived Yahoo metrics show ROIC of -49.7% in 2022, 8.5% in 2023, 17.8% in 2024 and 23.0% in 2025, with ROCE of 13.3% in 2025. That latest ROIC is high for an industrial business and not flattered by ROE leverage, but the average across the window is distorted by the 2022 loss. Goodwill is material at about 39% of invested capital in 2025, so tangible and incremental-return checks matter.

Confidence would improve with a full annual-report return bridge, including goodwill/tangible-capital treatment and explicit cost-of-capital estimate.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.45 score, 0.65 confidence

Vestas has scale, technology depth, installed-base/service relationships and a top-tier brand in wind, but turbine manufacturing is still a hard, competitive industrial market. Gross margin improved from 0.8% in 2022 to 13.3% in 2025, and Q1 2026 EBIT margin was the best first quarter since 2018, suggesting execution and pricing discipline have improved. Still, the absolute margin level is modest and OEMs compete on project economics, reliability, financing and execution rather than pure brand pricing power.

A firmer score needs peer margin/share data and evidence that recent order pricing is structurally better rather than cyclical backlog repricing.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.72 score, 0.70 confidence

The runway is the strongest part of the thesis. Wind power remains a large structural decarbonisation and energy-security market, and Vestas is already operating at global scale. The backlog supports multi-year visibility: combined backlog was EUR 71.9bn at FY2025 and EUR 76.1bn at Q1 2026, including EUR 39.8bn of expected contractual service revenue. Revenue guidance of EUR 20-22bn for 2026 implies continued growth from the FY2025 base.

The caveat is that runway only compounds shareholder value if turbine and offshore ramp-up capital earns acceptable incremental returns. That looks plausible after the 2024-26 recovery, but not yet proven across a clean cycle.

### Capital Allocation — 0.62 score, 0.70 confidence

Allocation looks mostly sensible: management is funding capex for the offshore/manufacturing ramp, while acquisitions have been immaterial in recent Yahoo cash-flow data (EUR 95m cumulative acquisition spend across 2022-25). Balance-sheet cash exceeds debt, and capital returns resumed after profitability recovered: FY2025 dividend proposal of DKK 0.74/share, EUR 150m buyback initiated after FY2025 results, and a further EUR 100m buyback announced with Q1 2026. The 2025 dividend was well covered by FCF.

The main caution is whether buybacks are value-accretive at the current multiple and whether offshore ramp-up investments meet return targets. That needs tracking rather than a high-conviction score today.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.82 score, 0.80 confidence

The balance sheet is a strength. Yahoo-derived annual metrics show net cash in 2025: cash and short-term investments of EUR 4.531bn versus total debt of EUR 3.374bn, or net debt/EBITDA of -0.52x. Current debt of EUR 782m was covered about 7.1x by cash plus one year of FCF, and TTM data still show a modest net cash position. This gives Vestas room to absorb working-capital swings and manufacturing-ramp volatility.

Confidence is not perfect because the debt maturity ladder, fixed/floating split and committed facilities should be checked directly in the annual report.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.80 score, 0.65 confidence

The investment case is not dependent on one irreplaceable person. Vestas' advantage is institutional: installed fleet, engineering base, manufacturing footprint, supplier/customer relationships and service contracts. CEO Henrik Andersen is important, but the company is not founder-led and the thesis should survive ordinary executive succession.

Confidence is moderate because I did not inspect the detailed succession/bench disclosures.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 score, 0.55 confidence

Alignment appears adequate but not clearly owner-operator strong. Yahoo shows negligible insider ownership and 52.7% institutional ownership. Governance risk scores are low overall, but the available data do not establish meaningful personal capital at risk, long holding periods, or a compensation design tied strongly to per-share intrinsic value and returns on capital.

This needs the 2025 remuneration report/proxy details to firm up: STI/LTI metrics, vesting periods, shareholding requirements, and equity dilution.

### Why the other strategies lost

The strategy leaderboard was close, not a blowout: wonderful-and-fair won on confidence-adjusted score, with cash-cow a near-tie and rule-breakers close behind. I kept wonderful-and-fair as primary because the case depends on a proven leader restoring returns while reinvesting into a structural runway. Cash-cow is a partial fit thanks to recovered FCF and capital returns, but loses on inconsistent profitability, modest FCF margin and a still-material reinvestment/offshore ramp rather than surplus-cash harvesting. Rule-breakers is directionally relevant because wind is a large structural-growth market and Vestas is a top-tier player, but Vestas is a mature cyclical industrial rather than an early-stage disruptor, and the thesis is not a winner-takes-most hyper-growth outcome. Freeroll loses because there is no hard valuation floor near the current price and no specific ≤4-year re-rating catalyst; the case depends on operational recovery and compounding, not a defined asymmetric floor-to-fair-value setup.

## Overall

Vestas is a watchable but not high-conviction wonderful-and-fair candidate. The positives are clear: global wind leader, strong order/service backlog, structural renewable-energy runway, recovering margins, and a net-cash balance sheet. The detractors are equally important: the recent history includes a deep 2022 loss, turbine manufacturing is competitive and execution-sensitive, FCF margin is not yet cash-cow quality, and current valuation already prices a meaningful recovery.

Potential FIT: energy transition, electrification and energy-security infrastructure. Vestas offers direct exposure to wind build-out plus a growing service backlog, but it is a manufacturing/execution story rather than a pure software-like compounder.

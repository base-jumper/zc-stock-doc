# CEG — Constellation Energy Corporation

## Business Overview

Constellation is the largest US nuclear operator and, after the January 2026 Calpine acquisition, a roughly **55 GW** diversified competitive-power platform spanning nuclear, gas, geothermal, hydro, wind and solar. It also supplies around **2.5 million** retail customer accounts, including about 80% of the Fortune 100.

The business earns from generation, capacity, retail/commercial supply, hedging, long-term power contracts and clean-energy support programs. Nuclear is the core moat: existing licensed plants provide scarce, reliable, low-carbon output that is difficult and slow to recreate. Calpine adds flexible gas, geothermal and retail scale, but also acquisition integration, commodity and leverage risk.

Q2 2026 adjusted operating EPS rose to **$2.55** from **$1.91**, although GAAP EPS fell to **$1.42** from **$2.67** because derivatives, decommissioning and acquisition accounting remain noisy. Management raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance from **$11.00–$12.00** to **$11.50–$12.50**. It also signed another **920 MW** of 15–20 year nuclear PPAs beginning in 2029–2032, advanced the Crane restart through FERC and NRC approvals, and agreed to sell the **606 MW** Brazos Valley gas plant for **$860M** before adjustments.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.70 / confidence 0.80

Adjusted operating earnings are useful and now supported by a raised full-year range, but the bridge to GAAP remains large. Q2 adjusted EPS of **$2.55** added back fair-value losses, Calpine integration costs and acquired-contract amortisation, while GAAP EPS was **$1.42**. First-half operating cash flow was **$1.553B**, roughly flat year over year despite the much larger acquired platform, and working-capital/collateral movements remain material.

**Interpretation:** underlying earnings are credible, but accounting and cash conversion are too complex for a higher score. **Firm-up:** review FY2026 cash flow before growth and the full-year reconciliation after Calpine purchase accounting settles.

### Returns on Capital — 0.62 / confidence 0.70

The legacy nuclear fleet has strong scarcity economics, but this is still an asset-heavy generator with large maintenance, fuel, decommissioning and growth requirements. Calpine materially enlarged the asset and goodwill base, so the acquisition's incremental returns cannot yet be observed cleanly.

**Firm-up:** compare FY2027 post-synergy operating profit and FCF before growth with the final Calpine purchase price, assumed debt and retained asset base.

### Pricing Power — 0.80 / confidence 0.85

Nuclear scarcity, capacity markets, clean/firm attributes and the nuclear PTC underpin pricing. The new **920 MW** of 15–20 year PPAs with investment-grade customers is direct evidence that buyers will contract scarce output well ahead of delivery, while all eligible CEG PJM plants cleared the 2028/29 auction at **$325/MW-day**.

**Interpretation:** CEG has genuine bargaining power, though contract prices remain undisclosed and regulation still mediates market economics.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.80 / confidence 0.85

The runway now has several concrete legs: the Crane restart targeted for 2027, nuclear uprates, **920 MW** of newly signed PPAs starting 2029–2032, powered-land/data-centre arrangements, and Calpine integration. FERC's interconnection-rights waiver and the NRC fuel-license approval materially reduce Crane execution risk, while the Walmart/Dresden agreement supports a **30 MW** uprate.

**Interpretation:** this is a strong and increasingly evidenced runway, but the economics and timing of several projects are still not fully disclosed.

### Capital Allocation — 0.74 / confidence 0.80

Management raised guidance, continued dividends and buybacks, and is completing required Calpine divestitures. The agreed **$860M** Brazos Valley sale and the previously announced **$5.0B** PJM asset sales should help fund deleveraging; about **$2.8B** of repurchase authority remained at filing. The tension is that first-half repurchases of **$1.971B** occurred while debt and short-term borrowings were elevated.

**Firm-up:** track how divestiture proceeds split between debt reduction, growth and buybacks through FY2026.

### Conservative Debt — 0.50 / confidence 0.85

At June 30, CEG carried **$19.111B** of long-term debt plus **$363M** due within a year and **$5.226B** of short-term borrowings, against **$697M** cash. Liquidity is substantial — around **$7.0B** of credit-facility capacity — and ratings remained **BBB+/Baa1**, but the balance sheet is not conservative after Calpine. A loss of investment grade would have triggered an estimated **$3.4B** incremental collateral requirement.

**Interpretation:** manageable, investment-grade leverage is not the same as balance-sheet strength. Expected asset-sale proceeds help, but deleveraging needs to be demonstrated.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.70 / confidence 0.68

The CEO and CFO matter, but the licensed asset base, operating systems, technical workforce and long-lived contracts make the franchise institutionally durable. **Firm-up:** succession depth and operating-leadership retention after Calpine integration remain irreducible until the next proxy and integration updates.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.72 / confidence 0.72

Proxy incentives include free cash flow before growth, relative TSR and a credit-ratings negative modifier, which aligns management with per-share outcomes and balance-sheet discipline. **Firm-up:** the 2027 proxy should show whether post-Calpine incentives put enough weight on deleveraging and acquisition returns rather than adjusted EPS growth alone.

### Why the other strategies lost

CEG is not an early-stage category creator, so Rule Breakers is a poor framing. It lacks a hard valuation floor and discrete asymmetric catalyst for Freeroll, while its modest dividend and long reinvestment runway do not fit Cash Cow. Wonderful and Fair best captures the scarce assets, pricing power and reinvestment opportunity, with leverage and valuation as the main constraints.

## Overall

Q2 strengthened the thesis: adjusted EPS grew, guidance rose to **$11.50–$12.50**, another **920 MW** of long-term PPAs validated demand, and Crane crossed meaningful regulatory milestones. The main offset is a balance sheet that is more stretched than the prior short summary conveyed, plus unchanged complexity in GAAP-to-adjusted earnings and cash conversion.

At about **$270**, CEG remains a high-quality but fully valued power compounder. The evidence now supports a **7.1/10** valuation-aware view, up modestly because guidance and contracting improved, but the stock is still a watch rather than a chase until deleveraging and Calpine cash conversion become clearer.

## Thesis Connections

- **Power bottleneck:** direct exposure to scarce firm capacity and higher reliability needs.

## Watch

2. **Crane restart:** remaining NRC approvals, capex, schedule and 2027 commissioning after the FERC CIR waiver and fuel-license amendment.

5. **Cash conversion:** reconcile raised adjusted EPS with FCF before growth after working capital, collateral, integration costs and capex.

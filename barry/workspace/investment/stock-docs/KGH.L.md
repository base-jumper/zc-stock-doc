# KGH.L — Knights Group Holdings plc

## Business Overview

Knights is a UK-listed legal and professional services group focused on regional markets outside London. It operates a corporatised, one-team model rather than the traditional partnership model: central business services, a single technology platform, regional client teams and selective recruitment/acquisitions of legal professionals. Its services span real estate, dispute resolution, private wealth, corporate/commercial, residential property, employment, remortgage services, clinical negligence and IFA advice.

FY2026 was a step-up year. Underlying revenue rose 28.2% to £207.7m, with 7.1% organic growth and the rest mainly from acquisitions; underlying PBT rose 18.6% to £33.2m; underlying FCF was £40.3m; and net debt excluding leases was £65.4m after acquisition spend and capex. The company says it has about 5% share of a c.£4bn regional UK legal services market, 29 locations, 1,220 average fee earners and 29 acquisitions completed over 14 years.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.54 / confidence 0.88

Knights has real earning power and strong cash backing, but the annual report makes the accounting-quality caveat harder to dismiss. Reported net income was negative in FY2022, then £7.9m, £9.8m and £7.6m in FY2023-25; FY2026 reported PAT was only £4.7m versus underlying PAT of £24.7m. Reported PBT of £10.2m was adjusted by £5.3m of acquired-intangible amortisation, £8.1m of contingent acquisition payments treated as remuneration and £9.8m of other non-underlying costs to reach underlying PBT of £33.2m. Yahoo shows cumulative FCF/net income of 2.44x across FY2022-25, and FY2026 underlying cash conversion was 163%, so the issue is not lack of cash but how much acquisition cost is excluded from headline earnings. Confidence is now higher after the full annual-report bridge; a 7-10 year bridge and deal-cohort outcomes would still firm up normalised earning power.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.68 / confidence 0.86

Cash generation is good for a professional-services roll-up, though not elite on a clean reported basis. Yahoo shows TTM FCF of about £24.6m; FY2025 reported FCF was £13.4m, while the company’s FY2026 underlying operating cash flow after tax was £40.3m, helped by a £2.4m working-capital inflow. Cash discipline is a genuine strength: reported lock-up was 84 days, inside the 90-day target, but the annual report also discloses 112 days when long-duration work excluded from the KPI is included. Net capex fell to £6.0m from the unusually high £12.1m in FY2025. I am not treating peak FY2026 cash conversion as normalised because working-capital release and the gap between reported and underlying costs matter. A longer cash-conversion history and maintenance-vs-growth capex split would firm this up.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.72 / confidence 0.78

The business is not a melting ice cube. Revenue has grown from £125.6m in FY2022 to £207.7m in FY2026, FY2026 organic growth was 7.1% and second-half organic growth was 11.8%. Demand for legal and professional services is economically sensitive in places, especially real estate/corporate work, but diversified service lines and private-client/disputes/clinical negligence exposure soften that. The bigger durability question is talent retention: legal services revenue walks out the door if senior professionals leave. Knights reported qualified-fee-earner churn of 10% and eNPS of +46 in FY2026, both supportive, but this remains the trait to watch.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.62 / confidence 0.70

Knights has a narrow-to-moderate moat, not a fortress. The moat sources are a premium regional brand, national breadth, centralised support, technology and the ability to offer senior lawyers a corporate alternative to traditional partnerships. FY2026 fees per fee earner rose to £170k, up c.18% in two years, and management cited pricing discipline as a driver of organic growth; gross profit margin was high at 49.4%. But legal services remains relationship-heavy and competitive, and individual lawyers can take clients elsewhere. Confidence would firm up with client retention, price-vs-volume and win/loss data.

### Capital Allocation — 0.55 / confidence 0.84

Allocation is coherent but acquisition-heavy. Knights reinvests in senior hires, offices and technology, pays a modest progressive dividend targeting about 20% of underlying PAT, and uses acquisitions as a core growth pillar. The FY2026 dividend was 5.63p, covered by cash flow. Four FY2026 acquisitions carried £12.5m of accounting consideration before up to £5.1m of employment-contingent payments; total acquisition-related cash outflow in the year was £19.5m, and management estimates another £17.1m across FY2027-29. Goodwill and acquired intangibles rose to £112.2m. The £1.22m purchase of own shares was for the employee benefit trust, not a capital return to outside owners. Management has a credible integration playbook, but contingent payments, recurring integration costs and no disclosed deal-cohort ROIC prevent a stronger score. Post-deal returns and earn-out outcomes remain the key evidence gap.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.43 / confidence 0.76

This is the main limiter. Yahoo-derived ROIC was 2.3%, 8.3%, 9.3% and 6.6% across FY2022-25, with ROCE 7.8% in FY2025. FY2026 underlying PBT margin of 16.0% is healthy, and the scorer script estimated incremental ROIC around 15.6%, but consolidated returns including goodwill are not high enough for a wonderful-business score. The business may earn attractive returns on organic hiring and integration, but acquisition goodwill absorbs much of that value at group level. Confidence would improve with return-on-invested-capital data excluding leases and by acquisition vintage.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.61 / confidence 0.86

Debt is acceptable but not conservative enough to ignore. Covenant leverage was 1.50x at 30 April 2026, net debt excluding leases was £65.4m and the post-year-end RCF was increased to £159m and committed to July 2029. The annual report confirms pricing at SONIA plus 1.65%-2.65%, dependent on leverage, with covenants broadly unchanged. Lease liabilities were £51.1m and total FY2026 lease payments £8.9m, so a lease-inclusive view is materially tougher. Interest and lease costs are manageable while cash conversion stays high, but floating-rate exposure and acquisition commitments would reduce flexibility if organic growth or collections weakened. Exact covenant thresholds remain undisclosed.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.50 / confidence 0.78

The model is institutionalising, but people still matter. CEO David Beech remains closely associated with the strategy and owns 22.0% of the company; the remuneration committee explicitly justified his 26% FY2027 salary increase partly on retention. Legal-services revenue also depends on many partner and senior-associate relationships. Knights has an expanded management team, including the CFO, a CTO and 16 Client Services Directors, plus a central platform that reduces dependence on any one lawyer. Board-level succession is reviewed continuously, but no specific CEO succession plan is disclosed. The thesis therefore still carries meaningful founder and senior-fee-earner dependence.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.43 / confidence 0.90

Alignment is mixed. CEO David Beech owns 18.9m shares, or 22.0%, and does not participate in equity plans, so his wealth remains substantially tied to the share price. The CFO’s 2028 performance shares use underlying-EPS hurdles from 27.26p to 43.62p and the plans include malus/clawback. Against that, the annual bonus is up to 100% of salary and is driven mainly by annual adjusted PBT; FY2026 paid 70% despite reported PBT falling. From May 2026 the CEO’s salary rose 26% and the CFO’s 11%, versus 4% for the workforce. The largest concern is a related-party 25-year lease from a CEO-controlled property company at current rent of £801k a year. The remuneration committee is independent on paper, but short-term adjusted-profit incentives, pay quantum and self-dealing exposure keep the score near the bottom of the mixed band.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair loses on low consolidated ROIC and acquisition-heavy growth. Rule-breakers loses because this is a mature professional-services consolidator, not a dominant player in a new, fast-expanding category, despite useful technology/AI optionality. Freeroll loses because there is no hard valuation floor and no concrete ≤4-year catalyst; the case is cheap cash generation plus execution, not an event-driven re-rate.

## Overall

Knights is a decent cash-generative small-cap compounder/cash-cow hybrid: cheap on normalised FCF and forward EPS, with strong collections and a credible regional consolidation playbook. The weaknesses are clearer after the annual report: reported earnings require large recurring acquisition adjustments, deal-related cash obligations extend through FY2029, debt is not trivial once leases are included, consolidated ROIC is modest, and governance is only mixed. The stock looks investable only if management keeps organic growth positive, avoids overpaying for firms, narrows the reported-to-underlying gap and turns technology/platform investment into operating leverage.

The current fundamental remains £0.32/share of normalised FCF, triangulating FY2026 underlying operating cash flow after tax of £40.3m, Yahoo TTM FCF around £24.6m, underlying EPS of 28.14p and consensus next-year EPS around 32.4p. ## Thesis Connections

Fits a regional-services consolidation thesis: fragmented UK regional legal services, growing technology/compliance burdens for small firms, and a central platform that can improve acquired practices’ lock-up, cross-selling and recruitment. The AI/technology angle is practical rather than moonshot: better document management, onboarding, compliance and productivity inside a professional-services platform.

## Watch

- FY2027 organic growth: whether the FY2026 H2 acceleration continues without needing more acquisitions.
- Lock-up and cash conversion: track both the headline KPI (84 days) and the 112-day figure including excluded long-duration work.
- Acquisition discipline: purchase multiples, £17.1m of estimated FY2027-29 deal payments, integration outcomes and post-deal returns.
- Net debt/covenant leverage after further acquisitions, especially with floating-rate debt and lease liabilities.
- Reported vs underlying earnings: contingent remuneration and recurring “non-underlying” costs should narrow rather than reset higher with each deal.
- Qualified-fee-earner churn and senior-hire productivity; this is the operating heart of the model.
- The 29 September 2026 AGM vote and future disclosure on executive pay, the CEO-controlled property lease and succession.

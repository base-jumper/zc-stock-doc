# 9502.T — Chubu Electric Power Company, Incorporated

## Business Overview

Chubu Electric Power is a major Japanese utility group serving the Chubu region. The operating core is retail/power services through Miraiz and regulated transmission/distribution through Power Grid; earnings are also materially exposed to JERA, the fuel and thermal-generation joint venture accounted for by the equity method. Other activities include renewables, nuclear, global, business development and a new real-estate division.

The investment case is not a growth-stock case. It is an essential-service utility with below-book asset backing, stable dividends, fuel-cost/time-lag volatility and meaningful leverage. FY2025 revenue fell 3.4% to JPY 3,546.0bn, mainly from lower fuel-cost adjustment charges, while ordinary profit rose 5.3% to JPY 291.0bn as JERA’s coal procurement competitiveness improved and Miraiz benefited from procurement-portfolio restructuring. FY2026 earnings guidance was left “undetermined” because fuel and wholesale-power price assumptions were too uncertain, while the annual dividend forecast remained JPY 70/share.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.58 / confidence 0.75

Chubu has real earning power, but the path is not smooth enough for a high quality score. Yahoo’s four-year annual set shows positive net income in each available year: JPY 38.2bn in FY2022, JPY 403.1bn in FY2023, JPY 202.1bn in FY2024 and JPY 227.8bn in FY2025. EPS similarly swings from JPY 50.55 to JPY 533.18 to JPY 301.56. The company’s FY2025 financial report confirms profit attributable to owners of parent of JPY 227.8bn, up 12.7% YoY.

Cash backing is acceptable on the available Yahoo convention, with cumulative FCF/net income around 1.46x, but this is not clean owner earnings for a capital-intensive utility. Chubu’s own presentation reports operating cash flow of JPY 334.4bn and investing cash flow of -JPY 350.8bn in FY2025, so free cash flow after investment was -JPY 16.3bn. Confidence would improve with a longer 7-10 year bridge separating fuel time-lag effects, Hamaoka/nuclear items and recurring maintenance/growth capex.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.42 / confidence 0.70

This is the weak point for a cash-cow framing. Yahoo’s FCF series is positive and stable because it maps close to operating cash flow: JPY 295.8bn, JPY 344.1bn, JPY 301.3bn and JPY 334.4bn over FY2022-FY2025, with an average margin around 8.6%. But Chubu’s own cash-flow presentation defines free cash flow as operating cash flow plus investing cash flow, and that was negative in both FY2024 (-JPY 90.4bn) and FY2025 (-JPY 16.3bn). In plain English: the operating business produces cash, but the grid/utility asset base consumes most or all of it.

The dividend is currently covered by accounting earnings and operating cash flow, but this is not a surplus-cash machine in the strict sense. The score is therefore only partial: stable operating cash generation, offset by heavy investment needs and limited genuinely distributable cash.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.68 / confidence 0.80

The demand base is durable because electricity transmission/distribution and retail energy are essential services in the Chubu region. FY2025 Miraiz electricity sold increased 1.0% to 109.0 TWh, while Power Grid area demand slipped only 0.5% to 123.9 TWh. That looks stable, not structurally eroding.

The caveat is commodity/regulatory volatility rather than secular decline. FY2025 revenue fell because fuel-cost adjustment charges declined, not because the franchise collapsed. Management explicitly flagged fuel prices, wholesale electricity prices and Middle East risk as reasons FY2026 income guidance was undetermined. Hamaoka nuclear review issues also produced contract-termination costs. Those are meaningful risks, but they are not evidence of a melting-ice-cube utility franchise.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.60 / confidence 0.70

Chubu’s moat is mostly regulatory/essential-infrastructure rather than brand-led pricing power. The Power Grid business is a natural-monopoly style network with stable ordinary profit of JPY 47.5bn in FY2025 despite slightly lower area demand. Miraiz faces retail competition, but its FY2025 ordinary profit rose as procurement restructuring offset pressures.

The score stays moderate because Japanese utilities do not have unconstrained pricing power. Fuel-cost adjustment mechanisms help pass through commodity costs, but regulatory/public pressure, wholesale-market movements and government support measures constrain economics. Gross/operating margins around 6.5% in FY2025 are stable but not high.

### Capital Allocation — 0.58 / confidence 0.70

Management is behaving like a utility steward: maintain supply reliability, invest in the asset base and pay stable dividends. FY2025 dividends were JPY 70/share, a 23.2% reported payout ratio and 23.9% payout ratio excluding time-lag effects. The stated shareholder-return policy targets a consolidated payout ratio over 30%, while still investing in plant, equipment and growth sectors.

The positives are dividend stability and low buyback/dilution noise; the share count is essentially flat. The negatives are heavy debt and investment demands. Net financing cash flow was positive in FY2025 and outstanding interest-bearing debt rose by JPY 155.5bn to JPY 3,233.4bn. The allocator may be rational, but shareholders are not yet seeing a high proportion of cash returned.

### Returns on Capital — 0.35 / confidence 0.85

Returns are low in absolute terms, even if acceptable for a regulated utility. Yahoo metrics show ROIC of 4.3% in FY2025 and a four-year average around 4.4%, with FY2024 unusually high at 8.0% and FY2022 depressed at 1.4%. Chubu’s own FY2025 presentation reports ROIC of 4.4%, above the medium-term target of 3.2% or more, and ROE of 7.5%.

That meets the company’s utility-style hurdle, but it is not a wonderful-business return profile. Incremental ROIC may be better in the latest period, but the large regulated asset base and high debt mean this is a modest-spread infrastructure return, not a high-return compounder.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.45 / confidence 0.80

Debt is meaningful even for a utility. Chubu reports outstanding interest-bearing debt of JPY 3,233.4bn at March 2026; Yahoo’s annual statement gives net debt of JPY 2,598.3bn and EBITDA of JPY 479.2bn, or net debt/EBITDA around 5.4x. The four-year peak was materially worse in FY2022 at about 9.5x. Interest coverage is much better: FY2025 EBIT covered net interest about 10.3x, and current debt coverage by cash plus a year of FCF was around 2.5x.

For a stable utility, this is stretched rather than dangerous. The trend has improved since the weak FY2022 year, and equity ratio improved to 41.0%, but leverage would constrain shareholder returns if commodity prices or regulatory outcomes turn against them.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / confidence 0.65

The thesis is institutional rather than dependent on one visionary. Chubu’s value sits in regional infrastructure, regulated networks, operating licences, JERA exposure and long-lived assets. President Kingo Hayashi matters, but losing any single executive should not break the franchise. Confidence is moderate because this was assessed from governance/structure rather than a deep succession review.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.58 / confidence 0.50

No obvious misalignment appeared in the quick pass, and governance data from Yahoo shows low audit, compensation and shareholder-rights risk scores, though board risk is higher. The company’s stated medium-term targets include ROIC, ROA, ROE and equity-ratio discipline, which are more owner-relevant than pure size targets.

Confidence is deliberately low because I did not review the Japanese securities report/proxy compensation detail. Firm this up next time by checking executive pay metrics, ownership guidelines, director independence and whether incentives use per-share value/ROIC rather than revenue or absolute earnings growth.

**Why the other strategies lost / near-tie:** Wonderful-and-fair is a mechanical near-tie because Chubu has a stable utility moat, but I do not frame it there: returns on capital are too low and the reinvestment runway is too limited for a quality-compounder thesis. Rule-breakers fails because this is a mature regulated utility with no durable high-growth engine. Freeroll has some downside support from P/B of 0.77x and a covered dividend, but the catalyst score is weak: there is no clearly time-bound re-rating trigger while FY2026 guidance is undetermined.

## Overall

Chubu is an acceptable but unexciting utility note: stable essential demand, below-book valuation, a covered dividend and moderate institutional resilience. The drawbacks are material: low ROIC, stretched debt, capex-heavy cash flows and limited growth. It looks more like a hold/watch utility cash-yield candidate than a compelling allocation candidate at the current price after a strong 52-week move.

Inputs: price JPY 3,208; current EPS/fundamental JPY 301.57 from FY2025; five-year holding period; 1.0% annual earnings growth; 10x exit P/E, slightly below the current 10.64x entry multiple; 0% dilution; and 2.18% dividend yield from Yahoo. The return is mostly dividend income. Business growth adds only ~1% and the assumed mild multiple compression offsets it. At this price, the visible expected return is too low for the leverage/capex risk, so my agent score is 0.40.

## Thesis Connections

Potential FIT connection: grid stability, electrification and data-centre/industrial power demand in Japan. But this note is not a clean data-centre power compounder. It is primarily a mature regional utility with JERA/fuel-market exposure and low return on capital.

## Watch

- FY2026 earnings guidance: management left it undetermined; update once fuel and wholesale-price assumptions are publishable.
- True free cash flow after investing cash flow; the cash-cow score improves only if post-investment FCF becomes sustainably positive.
- Interest-bearing debt and net debt/EBITDA; keep an eye on whether leverage moves below ~5x or worsens again.
- Dividend policy vs payout target over 30%; current JPY 70/share is covered by EPS but not by much true free cash after capex.
- Hamaoka nuclear review status and any further costs or regulatory delays.
- JERA contribution, coal/LNG procurement competitiveness, and fuel time-lag gains/losses.

# XRO.AX — Xero Limited

## Business Overview

Xero is a cloud small-business financial platform. It started in online accounting, then widened into payroll, payments, practice tools, bank feeds, apps, analytics and AI-enabled workflows for small businesses and their advisors. The core model is subscription SaaS, with payments now a larger part of the mix after the October 2025 Melio acquisition.

FY26 was strong operationally but messy optically: operating revenue rose 31% to NZ$2.753b, or 21% organically excluding Melio; customers rose 11% to 4.92m, or 10% organically; AMRR rose 37% to NZ$3.27b, or 25% excluding Melio. Free cash flow was NZ$554m with a 20.1% FCF margin; organic FCF excluding Melio was NZ$640m, up 26%. Gross margin fell from 89.0% to 83.9% because Melio payments carry structurally lower gross margins; excluding Melio mix, gross margin stayed at 89.0%.

The business is no longer a tiny hyper-growth disruptor, but it still fits best as a Rule Breaker: a category-defining compounder in a large, underpenetrated cloud-accounting / small-business-finance market, now trying to use payments and AI to expand the job-to-be-done from accounting software into a financial operating system.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.86 / confidence 0.82

The setup is attractive. Xero says its opportunity is an untapped ~US$100b TAM across small-business accounting, payroll, payments and adjacent financial workflows. The industry shift is structural: SMB back-office work is moving from desktop/manual workflows to cloud platforms, embedded payments, automation and AI assistants. Xero also has a credible claim that AI increases the addressable job-to-be-done because small businesses can get capabilities previously reserved for larger companies.

This is not a brand-new industry, and competitive intensity is real, especially in the US against Intuit. But the market remains large, fragmented and underpenetrated outside the most mature geographies. Industry economics are attractive where the product is subscription SaaS: Xero-only gross margin stayed ~89% in FY26.

### Top Dog — 0.72 / confidence 0.72

Xero is a leader in ANZ and has a substantial UK position, including more than one million UK customers. Globally it had 4.92m customers at FY26 year-end and continues to add customers organically. Its position is strongest where accountant/bookkeeper channels and cloud penetration already favour Xero; in the US it is still more challenger than category king, and Intuit/QuickBooks remains the formidable incumbent.

The score is strong but not exceptional because Xero is not the undisputed global top dog. The bull case needs Xero to keep deepening ANZ/UK penetration while using Melio/payments and AI to improve its US competitiveness. Confidence would improve with third-party market-share data by region rather than company-reported customer counts.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.78 / confidence 0.78

Xero has multiple moat sources: workflow switching costs, accountant/bookkeeper channel effects, integrations through the app ecosystem, bank-feed connectivity, brand trust in core markets, and data/workflow depth that should help AI features become more useful. The financial evidence is consistent with a moat: Xero-only gross margin remained 89.0%, ARPC rose, and LTV/CAC improved to 5.9x.

The moat is not unassailable. Accounting software is sticky but not impossible to switch, and Intuit has more scale in the US. Payments also lowers reported gross margin and exposes Xero to payment-network economics. Overall, the advantage looks stable-to-widening in ANZ/UK and still being built in the US.

### Durable Growth — 0.86 / confidence 0.88

Growth is still robust for a business of this size. FY26 operating revenue grew 31% headline and 21% organically; AMRR grew 37% headline and 25% excluding Melio; customers grew 11% headline and 10% organically; ARPC grew 23% headline and 14% organically. Free cash flow remained strongly positive at NZ$554m despite the Melio drag, and organic FCF rose 26%.

This is the trait that keeps Xero interesting after the share-price collapse. The growth rate is not early-stage explosive, but it is durable, profitable growth with operating leverage underneath. Watch the second derivative: organic growth and pro-forma Rule of 40 need to keep improving after the Melio integration, not just headline numbers.

### Organic Growth over Acquisitive Growth — 0.55 / confidence 0.88

This is the main quality scar. Xero still generated 21% organic revenue growth and 10% organic customer growth in FY26, so the core is clearly not stalled. But the Melio deal materially changed the profile: Melio added NZ$205m operating revenue from acquisition date, drove the payments mix shift, created large goodwill, and contributed a NZ$111.7m net loss in FY26. The annual report says goodwill from Melio was about NZ$4.2b.

The acquisition may be strategically right, especially if it gives Xero a credible US payments wedge. But from a quality-of-growth perspective, a large, loss-making acquisition funded with capital/debt is a real penalty. Confidence is high because the organic/headline split and Melio impact are explicitly disclosed.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.70 / confidence 0.70

Management under CEO Sukhinder Singh Cassidy is clearly growth-oriented and coherent: the 3x3 strategy, AI/JAX, partner ecosystem and Melio payments push all fit the ambition to become a small-business financial operating system. FY26 also showed discipline in core operations: Xero FTE was broadly flat excluding Melio, revenue per FTE rose 21%, and organic adjusted EBITDA grew 30%.

The offset is capital allocation risk. Melio is a bold move, not a small bolt-on, and it temporarily worsens reported margins and FCF. The score stays in the strong-but-not-exceptional zone until integration progress proves the deal was worth the complexity. Confidence would improve with more post-acquisition execution evidence over FY27/FY28.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.72 / confidence 0.76

Executive incentives are reasonably aligned. FY26 STI uses a 70% financial / 30% non-financial split, with financial metrics tied to group net new MRR and operating-expense ratio; non-financial metrics include partner NPS, small-business NPS, churn, employee engagement and strategic progress. LTI metrics include operating revenue CAGR, FCF margin and relative TSR. That is a sensible blend of growth, cash discipline, customer quality and shareholder outcome.

The soft spots: Xero does not prospectively disclose some LTI targets, individual performance multipliers remain somewhat opaque, and RSU-based compensation creates dilution risk. The announced buyback to offset share-based compensation is shareholder-aware, but it also highlights that SBC is material enough to need active management.

### Customer Devotion — 0.72 / confidence 0.65

The best hard evidence is retention economics rather than a published NPS figure. Xero-only churn was 1.14% monthly in FY26, up from 1.03%, while LTV/CAC improved to 5.9x and LTV per customer rose to NZ$4,304. ARPC expansion also indicates existing customers are accepting price increases and/or adopting more products.

This is more “highly useful and sticky” than visibly cult-loved. Accountants and small businesses often stay because workflows, data and advisor relationships make switching painful; that is a moat, not pure devotion. Confidence would firm up with reported NPS values, cohort retention / net revenue retention, and third-party customer review data.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-Fair is also plausible because Xero is profitable, capital-light and moat-like, but its earnings record is less clean after Melio and the big acquisition/goodwill risk makes “wonderful at a fair price” less clean than a growth compounder frame. Freeroll loses because there is no hard valuation floor and no specific ≤4-year catalyst; the setup is quality/growth repricing, not downside-protected special situation.

## Overall

Xero is a high-quality Rule Breaker that has fallen far enough to become interesting again. The core business is still excellent: strong organic revenue growth, high Xero-only gross margin, positive FCF, improving ARPC, strong LTV/CAC and a large AI/payments-enabled runway.

The bear case is not stupid: Melio is large, lower-margin, loss-making and expensive; US competition is brutal; churn ticked up; and the stock is still not obviously cheap on reported FCF if growth fades. But at ~28x reported FCF after a deep drawdown from the 52-week high, the valuation is no longer absurd for a business that can plausibly compound FCF per share at low-to-mid teens.

My current view: worth watching closely / potentially buyable for a growth sleeve, but not a pound-the-table bargain. The thesis needs FY27 evidence that Melio expands US payments/ARPC without permanently degrading Xero’s economics.

**Exit-multiple component.** Metric is FCF / owner earnings. Fundamental today is **A$2.65/share**, based on FY26 reported FCF translated to AUD and divided by the implied share count. Assumptions: **A$144B** maturity-year TAM, based on Xero’s cited ~US$100B opportunity translated at roughly current FX; **4.5%** terminal capture; **32% EBITDA margin**; **22x EV/EBITDA** terminal multiple; **1.5%** annual dilution; and zero terminal net debt. ## Thesis Connections

Xero connects to FIT themes around AI-enabled single-person / small-team companies. If JAX and embedded financial workflows reduce admin load for small businesses, Xero could become part of the infrastructure that lets very small teams operate with less back-office friction. It is also adjacent to “single person companies” because accounting, payments, payroll and compliance automation are core constraints for tiny firms.

## Watch

- FY27 organic revenue growth and AMRR growth excluding Melio; watch for sustained deceleration below the high-teens. No FY27 trading update was found on Xero’s financial-information page as of 9 Jul 2026.
- Melio integration: payments attach, US ARPC, direct customer growth, gross margin mix and whether Melio losses narrow.
- Pro-forma Rule of 40 trajectory toward management’s FY28 aspiration; FY26 pro-forma was 36.0%.
- Churn: FY26 Xero-only churn increased to 1.14% monthly; confirm this stabilizes or improves.
- SBC/dilution: check whether the A$550m buyback genuinely offsets RSU dilution or merely masks it.
- Independent regional market-share data, especially US position vs Intuit/QuickBooks.
- Any goodwill impairment risk from the NZ$4.2b Melio goodwill if US payments disappoint.
- Maria Dew KC external review into Xero’s handling of historic 2017 complaint matters; monitor for findings that change the governance/culture risk assessment.

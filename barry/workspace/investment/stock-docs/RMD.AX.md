# RMD.AX — ResMed Inc.

## Business Overview

ResMed is a global sleep and respiratory-health company. It sells cloud-connected flow generators, masks and accessories for sleep apnea and respiratory insufficiency, plus out-of-hospital software for providers and residential-care operators. The core profit engine is still the recurring device/mask/accessory ecosystem: installed PAP users consume masks and accessories over time, while connected devices, remote monitoring and software increase adherence, provider workflow value and switching costs.

FY2026 revenue rose 10% reported / 8% constant currency to US$5.65b. Sleep and Breathing Health grew 10%, led by masks and accessories up 13%; Residential Care Software grew 5%. GAAP gross margin expanded 170bp to 61.1%, non-GAAP operating margin rose 180bp to 36.1%, and non-GAAP EPS increased 17% to US$11.17. FCF remained strong at US$1.65b. ResMed also agreed to sell MatrixCare, acquired Noctrix Health, and enters FY2027 planning to return more than US$1.85b through buybacks and dividends.

## Trait Assessment

Best-fit strategy: **Cash Cow**. ResMed also has wonderful-and-fair qualities, but the current evidence is strongest for a mature, durable healthcare franchise throwing off large amounts of surplus cash while still growing modestly.

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.90 / 0.90

ResMed has clean, growing earning power. FY2026 GAAP net income rose 9% to US$1.52b and non-GAAP net income rose 16% to US$1.63b. FCF of US$1.65b exceeded GAAP net income and was approximately flat despite higher capex and working-capital needs. The main adjustment was a US$41.9m Astral field-safety notification charge; GAAP still remained strongly profitable.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.89 / 0.90

ResMed is strongly cash-generative. FY2026 operating cash flow was US$1.81b and FCF was US$1.65b, a 29.2% revenue margin and 1.08x GAAP net income. Capex rose to US$156m from US$90m but remained only 8.7% of operating cash flow. This confirms that the high FY2025 cash output was not a one-year anomaly.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.90 / 0.88

The fundamentals are rising, not eroding: FY2026 revenue grew 8% constant currency, GAAP operating margin rose to 33.4%, diluted EPS increased 10%, and FCF held at US$1.65b. Q4 demand remained broad, with devices up 8%, masks and accessories up 11%, and software up 3%. The main structural threat remains GLP-1-driven weight loss reducing sleep-apnea demand; the FY2026 result still shows no erosion.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.87 / 0.88

ROIC has been high and improving: ~19.3% in FY2022, 16.8% in FY2023, 20.0% in FY2024 and 21.2% in FY2025. ROE was ~23.5% in FY2025 without financial leverage doing the work, and operating margins expanded to 32.8% in FY2025. This is the quantitative fingerprint of a real moat: ResMed earns strong returns while carrying low net leverage.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.85 / 0.82

The moat comes from brand/reputation in sleep therapy, clinician/provider channels, connected-device data, patient adherence tooling, regulatory requirements, and replacement-mask/accessory recurrence. FY2026 non-GAAP gross margin expanded 240bp to 62.4% while constant-currency revenue grew 8%; masks and accessories grew faster than devices in both regions. That combination supports durable economics, though part of the margin gain reflects manufacturing productivity rather than pure pricing power.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.76 / 0.78

The runway is real but not explosive: obstructive sleep apnea and home respiratory care remain underdiagnosed globally, and ResMed can keep reinvesting in connected devices, masks, diagnostics/access, AI/digital health and software. FY2026 included new AirSense/AirCurve/AirTouch launches, an Oura partnership, and the Noctrix wearable-therapy acquisition. However, the base is already large and mature, and FY2027 consensus revenue growth is around 7%.

Confidence is 0.78 because TAM and diagnosis-penetration data were not deeply sourced in this pass. Firm-up source: ResMed investor deck / FY2026 10-K market-size discussion and independent sleep-apnea prevalence/diagnosis-rate data.

### Capital Allocation — 0.82 / 0.85

Capital allocation strengthened. ResMed returned about US$1.05b in FY2026 through US$700m of buybacks and US$350m of dividends, up more than 70%, while funding US$351m of acquisitions. It plans to return more than US$1.85b in FY2027 and raised the quarterly dividend 10% to US$0.66. The MatrixCare sale is a useful portfolio-discipline signal, while the Noctrix and VirtuOx acquisitions keep M&A execution relevant.

The higher score reflects explicit per-share capital returns backed by FCF and a net-cash balance sheet. The FY2026 10-K's sale terms and acquisition purchase-price allocations would firm the remaining uncertainty around portfolio returns.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.93 / 0.90

The balance sheet is very strong for a medical-device compounder. At FY2026, cash was US$1.47b versus US$659m of total debt, leaving roughly US$810m net cash; current assets of US$4.32b covered current liabilities of US$1.40b. Debt is not a constraint on reinvestment or the announced FY2027 capital return.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / 0.65

Mick Farrell is a long-tenured CEO and part of the Farrell family legacy, so leadership matters. But the thesis rests far more on an institutionalized global franchise — products, provider relationships, regulated quality systems, installed base and software workflows — than on one individual's unique judgment. The score is high for business resilience, but confidence is modest because succession planning and bench depth were not sourced.

Firm-up source: latest proxy statement / annual report governance section for named executives, succession planning disclosure and board independence.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.70 / 0.55

The available evidence is directionally acceptable but incomplete. ResMed has long-term management continuity and has not materially diluted shareholders; diluted shares are roughly flat over FY2022–FY2025 despite stock-based comp. The capital-return pattern also suggests per-share discipline. I have not yet reviewed the proxy remuneration metrics, ownership guidelines, CEO holdings, performance hurdles or clawbacks, so this remains a mixed-to-good placeholder rather than a fully evidenced score.

Firm-up source: latest ResMed proxy statement / DEF 14A remuneration report, specifically LTIP metrics, ownership requirements, executive holdings and equity-dilution tables.

### Why the other strategies lost

Wonderful-and-fair is a genuine near-tie secondary lens (0.838 score / 0.806 confidence versus cash-cow at 0.862 / 0.831), but the cash-cow frame wins because current cash generation is the clearest investable feature and a meaningful share of surplus cash is already returned through dividends and buybacks. Freeroll is the wrong frame because there is no hard valuation floor or time-bound re-rating catalyst; book value, net cash and dividend yield do not provide a strong floor. Rule Breakers is also a weaker fit: the company is innovative and growing, but it is already a mature profitable leader rather than an emerging hypergrowth disruptor.

## Overall

ResMed is a high-quality cash-cow / mature compounder: strong returns on capital, expanding margins, excellent FCF generation, a net-cash balance sheet, and a durable healthcare need. FY2026 strengthens the thesis and the planned FY2027 capital return should accelerate per-share growth. The main detractors are valuation sensitivity, the Astral safety charge, and execution on portfolio changes. At about 16x forward earnings on Yahoo's ASX line, the price is fair-to-attractive if EPS compounds around high single digits.

## Valuation

I used an exit-multiple model on ASX CDI economics, anchored on FCF because the cash-cow thesis is about distributable owner earnings. Inputs: 11 August price A$31.06 per CDI; A$1.55 of current FCF per CDI, supported by FY2026 FCF of US$1.65b and the 10:1 CDI ratio; five years; 6% aggregate FCF growth; 2% annual net buybacks; 1.3% dividend yield; and a 17.5x exit P/FCF. The buyback assumption is above FY2026's roughly 1% diluted-share reduction but below the first-year pace implied by FY2027's >US$1.85b capital-return plan.

The model produces 6.8% expected annualized ROI: +6.0% from business growth, +2.0% from buybacks, +1.5% from income, partly offset by -2.7% annualized multiple compression from a 20.0x entry to 17.5x exit. My agent score is 0.80: visible business quality and capital returns are excellent, but the higher post-result price keeps modeled returns moderate rather than exceptional.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a durable healthcare compounder thesis: chronic disease, home-based care, connected medical devices, recurring consumables and workflow software. It is also a beneficiary of better diagnosis and adherence in sleep apnea rather than a one-product cyclical device story.

## Watch

- FY2027 delivery against >US$1.85b of planned capital returns; monitor actual share-count reduction and repurchase prices.
- GLP-1 / weight-loss impact on sleep-apnea incidence and PAP demand; watch for evidence of reduced patient starts versus improved diagnosis awareness.
- MatrixCare sale terms and closing; Noctrix/VirtuOx integration should support growth without goodwill impairments or margin drag.
- Astral field-safety notification: scope, cash cost, remediation, and any recurrence beyond the US$41.9m FY2026 charge.
- Proxy/remuneration review to firm up incentive alignment and succession/key-person risk.
- Market-share and replacement-mask/accessory trends; a slowdown here would weaken pricing power and runway.

# NEX.PA — Nexans S.A.

## Business Overview

Nexans is a French electrification pure play that designs, manufactures, and installs cable systems and services across the power value chain: generation and high-voltage transmission, grid distribution, building/usage cables, and industry solutions. The company has deliberately refocused away from lower-value complexity toward electrification markets where grid investment, renewable generation, interconnectors, data-center power demand, and building electrification should keep demand structurally healthy.

In 2025 Nexans reported €6.10bn of standard sales, +10.1% reported and +8.3% organic growth; adjusted EBITDA rose 27.3% to €728m, with an 11.9% adjusted EBITDA margin on standard sales. Free cash flow was €344m and year-end net debt was €266m, or 0.36x adjusted EBITDA (Yahoo's larger €464m FY25 FCF figure was inflated by transmission down-payments, as flagged previously).

**H1 2026 (reported July 29)** confirmed the trajectory and raised guidance. H1 standard sales were €3,248.6m, +5.0% including +1.5% organic and +3.8% from acquisitions; Electrification grew +4.5% organically. Group adjusted EBITDA was €387.7m, +4.3% yoy, an 11.9% margin on standard sales; Electrification adjusted EBITDA rose +5.2% with a 13.2% margin (vs 13.3% FY25), still affected by an adverse mix. H1 net income was €105.9m (€122.8m from continuing operations, vs €143.3m in H1 2025, with Lynxeo/AmerCable/Autoelectric treated as discontinued under IFRS 5). H1 FCF was €165.5m, a 42.7% cash-conversion ratio, with no maturities before 2027 and financial leverage at 1.4x — the first reported post–Republic Wire balance sheet. FY26 guidance was **raised**: adjusted EBITDA €770–840m (previously €730–810m) and FCF €235–325m (previously €210–310m), excluding Great Sea Interconnector execution in 2026 but including MI-line loading at year-end and Republic Wire from June 1.

The €680m Republic Wire acquisition completed June 1, adding a U.S. low-voltage platform with about €520m of current annual sales, entering the dynamic U.S. market and positioning for data-center demand. It improves the North American growth runway but raised leverage to 1.4x. At €138.10/share on August 19, Yahoo showed a market cap of about €6.04bn — up ~3% from the July 23 view of €5.87bn at €134.10.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.62 / 0.78

Nexans remains profitable: €245m net income in 2022, €221m in 2023, €279m in 2024, €352m in 2025, and €105.9m in H1 2026 (€122.8m from continuing operations). The H1 print is informative on quality: it was distorted by IFRS 5 discontinued operations (Lynxeo, AmerCable, Autoelectric divestments), and the continuing-operations line fell ~14% yoy — partly divestiture-related, partly mix. Revenue remains lumpy due to project timing, metal prices, scope changes and transmission down-payments.

The score slips slightly because H1 confirms that reported FCF is materially lumpy: the €464m Yahoo FY25 FCF figure (which anchored the prior view) is not a normal run-rate — H1 2026 FCF of €165.5m annualizes below that, and FY26 guidance of €235–325m is the better earnings-quality read. Cash backing is still real (H1 cash conversion 42.7% of adjusted EBITDA), so this stays a mid-score, not a derating.

### Returns on Capital — 0.40 / 0.80

This remains the main quality weakness. The helper script shows ROIC of 16.7% in 2022, 9.4% in 2023, 5.9% in 2024, and 6.9% in 2025, with ROCE of 8.9% in 2025. Average ROIC across the window is roughly 9.7%, only modestly above cost of capital. ROE looks better at 17.6% in 2025, but the ROE-minus-ROIC gap suggests leverage and capital structure flatter the equity-return view.

Incremental returns are hard to read because the company is actively investing in PWR-Transmission capacity, including the Electra cable-laying vessel and Charleroi extension. The 13.2% H1 Electrification margin (vs 13.3% FY25) shows mix/execution holding but not yet re-rating. To firm this up, use Nexans' segment invested-capital disclosure and post-expansion returns once the new assets ramp.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.55 / 0.60

Nexans has a real but not pristine moat. Positives: century-plus technical expertise, high-voltage project credentials, cable-laying capability, customer-critical infrastructure, and management's stated focus on selectivity and value-driven growth. H1 continued to show margin resilience (Electrification 13.2% despite adverse mix) and PWR-Transmission's MI line is loaded up until mid-2028 — evidence of multi-year demand visibility in the highest-value segment.

Negatives: cable manufacturing still has commodity and project-execution characteristics, gross margin is only about 11–13% in Yahoo data, and competition in electrical equipment is substantial. The moat is narrower than a software or branded-consumer compounder; strongest in complex high-voltage transmission, weaker in standard usage cables.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.65 / 0.65

The runway is attractive: electrification requires more generation, transmission, distribution, and usage infrastructure, and Nexans is positioned across that chain. H1 Electrification organic growth of +4.5% sits in the high range of CMD guidance, with PWR-Transmission leading. Management is spending to expand transmission capacity and is entering the U.S. data-center cabling opportunity via Republic Wire.

The reason this is not higher is the return-on-capital evidence. A runway only compounds if incremental capital earns well; current reported ROIC is mediocre and acquisitions contribute to growth. The opportunity is real, but the financial proof of high-return reinvestment is not yet strong.

### Capital Allocation — 0.62 / 0.68

Allocation looks sensible and H1 supports a modest upgrade. The Republic Wire deal closed early June (value-accretive per management, expanding U.S. footprint and data-center exposure), leverage came in at 1.4x — well-controlled and below the level the pre-deal uncertainty allowed for — and the group retains "financial flexibility to execute a disciplined and value-creating M&A strategy," with M&A in PWR-Grid and PWR-Connect at the core. The dividend of €2.90/share (2026) was confirmed and paid in May.

The caution is acquisition and capex intensity: cumulative acquisition spend in Yahoo's four-year window was €1.17bn before Republic Wire, goodwill had already risen to €680m, and FY26 guidance still implies FCF well below the FY25 reported figure. Deal synergies and post-acquisition returns need to stay visible before awarding a high allocator score.

### Conservative Debt — 0.65 / 0.72

The post-deal balance sheet is now reported and it is fine, not pristine. Financial leverage is 1.4x net debt/adjusted EBITDA at H1 2026 (pro-forma of Republic Wire), up from 0.36x at FY25 pre-deal but comfortably within the investment-grade envelope; there are no maturities before 2027 and the debt profile is well diversified. The earlier uncertainty — deal financing and post-deal leverage — is resolved.

The score sits in the conservative band but slightly below the pre-deal 0.72 because the 1.4x starting point plus a still-active M&A pipeline means less downturn optionality than the FY25 snapshot implied.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.78 / 0.55

The thesis is not dependent on a single charismatic founder. Nexans' advantage sits in assets, project references, engineering know-how, customer relationships, and global operations across 41 countries rather than one individual. Confidence is only moderate because I did not review the latest succession and senior-management bench disclosures in detail.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.72 / 0.82

Unchanged from the prior review: the 2026 CEO plan is reasonably well aligned. Annual variable pay is capped at 150% of fixed compensation and is 65% collective / 35% individual, with a collective scorecard including ROCE (20%), EBITDA (30%), FCF (30%) and organic growth (10%). Long-term awards are capped at 150% of fixed pay using a three-year 2028 test: 40% relative TSR, 40% EBITDA margin subject to cash-conversion and organic-growth qualifiers, and 20% CSR. The CEO must retain 25% of vested performance shares, subject to a 15,000-share minimum.

It falls short of exceptional because much ownership is granted rather than purchased, annual EBITDA remains prominent, and acquisition integration appears in individual objectives. The remuneration report provides good plan detail; actual hurdle difficulty and Julien Hueber's eventual open-market ownership record remain the main uncertainties.

### Why other strategies lost

Cash-cow scores marginally higher mechanically (0.6155), but wonderful-and-fair remains the chosen framing because the business is still reinvesting heavily and FCF margins are moderate rather than fat; this is not yet a mature low-capex harvest. Rule-breakers has some fit because electrification is a strong secular wave, but Nexans is a proven industrial rather than an emerging exponential disruptor. Freeroll loses because there is no hard valuation floor near the current price: price/book is about 2.8x, price/tangible book is higher, net debt is positive (1.4x), and the dividend yield is only about 2.1%.

## Overall

Nexans is a good electrification infrastructure business, and H1 2026 was operationally solid: guidance raised, leverage controlled at 1.4x post–Republic Wire, Electrification margins holding at 13.2% despite adverse mix, and the MI line loaded to mid-2028. The main detractors are unchanged and now clearer: reported ROIC is not high enough yet to call it a first-class compounder, and the FCF anchor is materially lower than the FY25 reported figure suggested once transmission down-payments normalize — the company's own FY26 FCF guidance (€235–325m) is the honest run-rate, roughly 40% below the €464m the previous valuation anchored on.

My view: still worth watching for the transmission-capacity investments to convert into higher sustainable ROIC, but the valuation no longer screens as an investable quality bargain. At €138.10 the entry multiple is ~21.6x normalized FCF, and the base-case expected return has fallen to low single digits — the market has already re-rated the good news. This is now a watch-and-wait situation rather than a buy.

## Valuation

I refreshed the wonderful-and-fair exit-multiple method on FCF per share. The anchor is now **€6.40/share**, down from €10.75, because the previous anchor relied on Yahoo's €464m FY25 FCF which the doc itself flagged as inflated by transmission down-payments — H1 2026 confirms the normalization: FY26 company guidance midpoint of €280m FCF ÷ 43.65m ordinary shares ≈ €6.40/share. This is a guidance-based run-rate anchor, not a trailing figure.

At €138.10/share (Aug 19), the entry multiple is about 21.6x normalized FCF (vs 12.5x on the old inflated anchor). I assume 9% aggregate FCF growth for five years (guided EBITDA growth plus mix improvement, consistent with the €770–840m FY26 EBITDA midpoint and consensus next-year EPS growth ~30% off a depressed base), a 15x exit P/FCF (holding the prior exit-multiple premium as the transmission mix improves), no dilution, and a 2.10% dividend yield (€2.90/share on €138.10).

Those assumptions produce a **4.0% annualized five-year ROI**: 9.0% from business growth, about −7.0% from multiple compression (21.6x entry → 15x exit), and about 2.6% from income, with no dilution assumed. The return is almost entirely carried by the dividend and growth; the multiple re-rating is a significant drag because the stock is no longer cheap on normalized FCF. Agent score drops to 0.42: operationally sound and balance-sheet fine, but a low expected return at the current price.

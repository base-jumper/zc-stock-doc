# ENR.DE — Siemens Energy AG

## Business Overview

Siemens Energy is a German energy-technology group serving utilities, grid operators, industrial customers, and renewable-power developers. Its core businesses are Gas Services, Grid Technologies, Transformation of Industry, and Siemens Gamesa/Wind Power. The company sells large engineered equipment and lifecycle services: gas and steam turbines, generators, compressors, grid products, transformers, high-voltage direct-current systems, substations, industrial decarbonisation equipment, and wind turbines/services.

The investment case is no longer a simple Siemens Gamesa turnaround. The current engine is the combination of grid electrification, power-system reliability, data-centre and industrial power demand, and a very large backlog. Q3 FY2026 strengthened that case: orders reached €17.9bn, book-to-bill was 1.57, backlog rose to €162bn, and comparable revenue grew 18.5% to €11.4bn. Profit before special items more than tripled to €1.62bn, while free cash flow pre-tax rose to €2.32bn, partly helped by customer advances. Management kept FY2026 guidance at 14–16% comparable revenue growth, a 10–12% profit margin before special items (now expected toward the upper end), net income around €4bn, and FCF pre-tax around €8bn. Siemens Gamesa posted its first positive quarterly result since FY2022.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.90 / 0.82

Electricity grids, gas-generation reliability, industrial electrification, and renewables integration remain in a favourable demand window. Q3 orders of €17.9bn, book-to-bill of 1.57, and €162bn backlog show demand is not theoretical; Gas Services orders rose 61.9% comparably, with U.S. data-centre demand among the drivers, while Grid Technologies orders rose 27.6%. Interpretation: Siemens Energy is well placed in a structurally expanding energy-infrastructure cycle, though the project cycle remains exposed to execution, supply-chain, policy, and permitting risk.

### Top Dog — 0.72 / 0.65

Siemens Energy is a global scaled supplier in gas turbines, grid technologies, and wind power, and it has the service footprint needed for long-lived installed-base economics. The score is below elite because Siemens Gamesa's quality issues damaged the wind franchise, and grid/gas equipment is competitive rather than winner-take-all. To firm this up, pull market-share data by Grid Technologies/HVDC, Gas Services, and offshore/onshore wind.

### Sustainable Advantage — 0.66 / 0.65

Advantages are mostly scale, technical qualification, installed-base service relationships, engineering know-how, and the ability to deliver large bankable projects. Q3 added evidence that the backlog has a better margin profile: Gas Services margin before special items reached 17.3% and Grid Technologies 19.9%, both four points above the prior-year quarter. Those advantages are real but not invulnerable: customers run tenders, projects are lumpy, execution mistakes are expensive, and the wind business has shown that technical problems can destroy economics. Confidence remains capped until the margins persist through more of the backlog.

### Durable Growth — 0.84 / 0.85

Revenue grew from €29.0bn in FY2022 to €39.1bn in FY2025, a roughly 10% CAGR in the helper data. Q3 comparable revenue then grew 18.5%, all segments grew, profit before special items more than tripled, and cash conversion was strong. The €162bn backlog provides unusually good visibility, although customer advances flatter current cash flow and the growth still has to survive a full project-execution cycle.

### Organic Growth — 0.72 / 0.70

Recent growth is primarily operating/backlog driven rather than acquired: helper output shows only ~€0.5bn cumulative acquisition spend over FY2022–FY2025 while revenue rose by ~€10bn. Goodwill as a percentage of assets fell from about 20.5% to 16.0%. Share count rose materially versus FY2022, so per-share compounding has been diluted by crisis-era capital needs.

### Management Backing — 0.72 / 0.70

Management has stabilised the balance sheet, exited the federal guarantee early in June 2025, lifted cash flow, expanded capacity, and materially improved project margins. Q3 is the strongest turnaround evidence yet: record group orders/revenue/profitability and Siemens Gamesa's first positive quarter since FY2022. The score is still capped because one profitable wind quarter does not erase the 2023 quality failure or prove that the huge backlog can be converted without another provision cycle.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.62 / 0.70

The FY2025 compensation report says the February 2025 compensation system was approved with 97.81% support. Variable pay resumes in FY2026 after the federal-guarantee restrictions; annual bonus metrics include profit margin and comparable revenue growth, and long-term equity awards remain part of pay. Positives: equity-based LTIs and malus/clawback. Caveat: one-off retention/equity/early-exit components after the guarantee period and target shifts reduce cleanliness.

### Customer Devotion — 0.50 / 0.40

There is indirect evidence of customer demand in orders, backlog, and service relationships, but no direct NPS/retention/customer satisfaction source was reviewed. For a B2B capital-equipment group, backlog is useful but not the same as customer love. Firm-up source: customer concentration, repeat-order/service-retention metrics, and any disclosed customer satisfaction metric in FY2026 compensation reporting.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair is more plausible after the Q3 margin and cash-flow step-up, but not yet the primary frame because consistent earnings quality and returns on capital still need a longer post-2023 record. Cash-cow loses because the business is reinvesting into a capacity/backlog growth cycle and pays only a small dividend, not a mature high-payout harvest. Freeroll loses because the share is not cheap versus asset/earnings floors and there is no obvious downside floor near the current price.

## Overall

This is a high-quality turnaround-growth story in a strong demand cycle, but at a price that already capitalises a lot of success. The best evidence is the €162bn backlog, 18.5% Q3 comparable revenue growth, sharply higher segment margins, strong FCF, and Siemens Gamesa's return to quarterly profit. The biggest detractors remain execution risk in large projects, a still-short clean margin record, advance-payment support in cash flow, and valuation: at €158.56, Yahoo shows ~25.7x forward EPS and ~3.2x trailing sales.

My valuation-aware judgement is positive on the business but guarded on the stock. Q3 de-risked the operating thesis, yet the current valuation leaves little room for merely good execution and a long-horizon model exposes meaningful multiple compression. This remains watchlist quality rather than an obvious entry.

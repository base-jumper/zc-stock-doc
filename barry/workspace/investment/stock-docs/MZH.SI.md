# MZH.SI — Nanofilm Technologies International Limited

## Business Overview

Nanofilm is a Singapore-listed advanced materials and surface-engineering group. Its core business is applying proprietary vacuum-coating technologies to customer components, mainly through the Advanced Materials Business Unit (AMBU). FY2025 revenue was S$244.6m, up 19.7% YoY, with AMBU contributing S$206.9m or 84.6% of group revenue. The other units are Industrial Equipment, Nanofabrication, and the small Sydrogen hydrogen-energy business.

The company’s pitch is technically interesting: in-house R&D, process engineering, self-built equipment, and coating know-how that can be scaled into high-volume manufacturing for 3C/consumer electronics, automotive, precision engineering, semiconductor, optics, and new energy applications. The financial reality is more mixed. Revenue recovered strongly in FY2025, but margins and returns remain far below the 2022 level, and free cash flow has been negative for three straight years because capex has remained heavy.

## Trait Assessment

### Downside Support / Valuation Floor — 0.20 / 0.75

There is no clean freeroll floor. At about S$1.19/share and roughly S$776m market cap, Nanofilm trades around 1.77x book and about 2.15x tangible book. Book value per share is roughly S$0.67, tangible book roughly S$0.55, and the company is not net-cash-backed after including debt and leases. The balance sheet is not distressed — FY2025 cash and bank balances were S$84.6m versus S$92.4m bank loans and S$15.7m lease liabilities — but this is not an asset-value setup where the downside is obviously protected.

Interpretation: the only plausible floor is balance-sheet / replacement-value support, and it is a weak one because the share price is well above book and the assets are specialised manufacturing assets that could earn poor returns if utilisation disappoints. I used S$0.67/share book value as the downside floor in the valuation, but that is more a reference point than a hard floor.

### Fundamental Stability / No Value Trap — 0.45 / 0.80

The business has stabilised from the FY2023 trough, but not yet to a high-quality level. Revenue rose from S$177.0m in FY2023 to S$204.3m in FY2024 and S$244.6m in FY2025. FY2025 gross margin was about 36.2%, operating margin about 6.7%, and net margin about 4.8%, all materially below FY2022 levels. Net income improved to S$11.8m in FY2025, but ROIC was only about 2.7% and free cash flow was still negative at about -S$14.5m.

Management says the major capex cycle is largely completed and the focus is shifting to asset utilisation, cash-flow management, productivity, and reducing losses in newer segments. That gives the recovery thesis some credibility, but the current numbers still look like an under-earning capital-intensive business rather than a proven compounder.

### Mispriced for an Identifiable Reason — 0.30 / 0.55

The market’s likely concern is straightforward: Nanofilm has good technology and a recovered revenue line, but capital intensity, customer concentration risk in consumer electronics, weak FCF conversion, and low ROIC make the quality of earnings questionable. That concern is not obviously wrong. The share price has already recovered sharply over the last year, and the stock trades on rich-looking trailing multiples: around 59x trailing earnings and about 16.6x EV/EBITDA, though the forward PE is nearer 22x if the earnings recovery continues.

Interpretation: there may be a mismatch if FY2025 is only the early stage of a multi-year utilisation recovery, but this is not a classic “cheap because misunderstood” freeroll. The current valuation already prices in a decent rebound.

### Identifiable, Time-Bound Catalyst — 0.40 / 0.65

The catalyst is operational rather than corporate: major capex largely complete, higher utilisation of existing infrastructure, China-plus-one footprint in Vietnam and India, Germany expansion through AxynTeC and EuropCoating, continued AMBU growth, and improved Industrial Equipment deliveries. If these convert into positive FCF and higher ROIC over FY2026–FY2027, the market could re-rate the earnings base.

The weakness is that the catalyst is not hard-dated. There is no announced sale, buyback, special dividend, litigation outcome, or clear capital return event. The thesis depends on management execution and demand staying healthy.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Cash-cow:** fails because FCF has been negative for three consecutive years and the dividend is not FCF-covered.
- **Wonderful-and-fair:** fails on returns and FCF quality; ROIC is currently low-single-digit and cash conversion is poor.
- **Rule-breakers:** has some proprietary-technology flavour, but revenue growth is not durable enough, the addressable-market evidence is not strong enough, and profitability/FCF do not support a high-conviction growth thesis.

## Overall

Nanofilm is a watchlist turnaround, not an attractive buy on current evidence. The technology platform and FY2025 revenue recovery are real positives, and the balance sheet gives management room to keep executing. But the stock is not cheap against current earnings or asset value, free cash flow is still negative, and the best catalyst is a soft operational improvement cycle. That is not enough for a high-quality score or a compelling freeroll.

The key question is whether FY2026 shows operating leverage and positive FCF now that the major capex cycle is supposedly behind it. Until then, this is a “prove it” situation.

## Thesis Connections

Potentially relevant to a manufacturing automation / advanced materials / Asia industrial technology thesis, but only if Nanofilm proves it can turn proprietary process know-how into sustainably high returns on capital. Current numbers do not yet show that.

## Watch

- FY2026 interim results: positive free cash flow would materially improve the thesis.
- ROIC and operating margin: evidence that asset utilisation is rising after the capex cycle.
- AMBU customer/program concentration: whether growth is broadening beyond key consumer-electronics programmes.
- Sydrogen losses and capital demands: whether the newer hydrogen segment remains a drag.
- China-plus-one execution in Vietnam and India, and whether Europe acquisitions add profitable revenue rather than complexity.
- Dividend cover: current dividend is modest but should be judged against FCF, not accounting earnings.

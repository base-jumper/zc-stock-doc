# SSE.L — SSE plc

## Business Overview

SSE is a UK-listed electricity infrastructure group focused on regulated electricity networks, renewables and flexible generation. The portfolio is increasingly a grid-and-clean-power infrastructure business: in FY2025, Networks and Renewables produced 87% of adjusted operating profit, while thermal flexibility declined as spark-spread volatility normalised. In FY2026 SSE reported £2.237bn adjusted operating profit, 153.5p adjusted EPS, a 68.7p dividend, £3.586bn adjusted investment/capex and 14.5TWh renewable generation output.

The main driver is reinvestment: SSE says it invested a record £3.6bn in FY2026 and has a five-year £33bn investment plan to 2030, primarily focused on strengthening the electricity network in northern Scotland. Its July Q1 trading statement showed networks investment up **83%** year on year to about **£0.9bn**, renewables output up **31%**, and unchanged adjusted-EPS guidance of **168–193p** for FY2027 and **225–250p** for FY2030. This is capital-heavy and depresses free cash flow, but the spend is tied to regulated networks, renewables and system flexibility that should be needed for the UK clean-power transition.

Universal disqualifiers screened: no evidence found of fraud, insolvency, hidden-liability kill-shot, value-leaking control, or a business-model kill-shot. The live risks are execution, regulation/planning delays, financing cost and shareholder dilution rather than outright ineligibility.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.55 / confidence 0.75

SSE has real earning power, but the line is not as clean or predictable as a classic consumer/asset-light compounder. FY2026 adjusted EPS was 153.5p versus FY2025 adjusted EPS of 160.9p; reported EPS was 105.5p in FY2026 and 108.2p in FY2025. Yahoo-derived reported net income shows one loss year in the four-year annual window served by yfinance, then positive profits in FY2024-FY2026, but reported growth is lumpy because energy markets, hedges, tax and exceptional items move the accounting result around.

Cash backing is the main caveat. The free-cash-flow script shows cumulative FCF / net income of -0.16 over FY2023-FY2026 and negative FCF in FY2025 and FY2026 because capex exceeded operating cash flow. I do not treat that as an accounting-quality fraud signal — it is largely deliberate infrastructure build-out — but it means owner earnings are not currently close to reported earnings. Confidence would firm up with a longer ten-year adjusted EPS and operating-cash conversion bridge from the annual reports.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.55 / confidence 0.75

Returns are acceptable for a regulated/infrastructure utility, not exceptional in an absolute sense. The returns script shows ROIC of 6.4% in FY2026, 7.0% in FY2025, 10.5% in FY2024 and 0.9% in FY2023; average ROIC over the available window is 6.2%. For an asset-heavy regulated utility, a mid-single-digit ROIC can still create value if the allowed returns and inflation linkage sit above the cost of capital, but it is not a high-return compounder in the Terry Smith sense.

The positive read is that incremental ROIC in the script is high and the company is deploying large sums into regulated networks where returns should be more stable than merchant generation. The negative read is simple: this is capital-intensive, debt-funded infrastructure. Returns are capped by regulation and financing costs matter.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.70 / confidence 0.75

The moat is structural rather than brand-based: regulated monopoly network assets, grid connection scarcity, scale, planning/consent complexity and long-lived infrastructure. SSE's own FY2025 result noted that Networks and Renewables became 87% of adjusted operating profit, increasing the proportion of regulated/contracted or policy-supported earnings. Regulated networks do not have free pricing power in the consumer-brand sense, but the price-control framework gives more defensible and often inflation-linked revenue than an unregulated commodity business.

The risk is political/regulatory. The same regulation that protects returns can also cap them, delay them, or shift value from shareholders to consumers. Merchant renewables and thermal flexibility add commodity/weather exposure. This is a good moat for durability, not a licence to earn software-like margins.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.80 / confidence 0.85

This is the strongest trait. SSE is positioned directly in grid reinforcement, electrification, renewables and system flexibility. FY2026 annual-report highlights cite £3.6bn of investment in the year and a five-year £33bn investment plan to 2030, primarily focused on the northern Scotland electricity network. FY2025 sources cited the prior £17.5bn five-year programme to 2027 and named projects including Dogger Bank A, Shetland HVDC/Viking, and Eastern Green Link 2.

The runway is credible because electricity demand growth, renewable integration and transmission bottlenecks require physical grid investment. Q1 added concrete delivery evidence: networks investment rose **83%**, work advanced on ASTI/LOTI, Dogger Bank and Western Isles projects, and NESO identified more than **£12bn** of additional potential SSEN Transmission investment, taking Future Uncertainty Mechanism opportunities above **£17bn**, subject to approvals. The catch is that reinvestment is not automatically high-return: regulated allowed returns, construction execution, supply-chain costs and planning delays determine whether the runway compounds per-share value or mostly grows the asset base.

### Capital Allocation — 0.65 / confidence 0.70

Management is allocating heavily toward the right strategic areas: networks, renewables and flexibility, while signalling capital discipline as policy/planning delays and macro conditions changed. The FY2025 release explicitly said the capital programme was revised to reflect macro conditions and delays; FY2026 frames the funding route as supporting long-term value creation, earnings growth and a progressive dividend.

The caveat is funding. The capital-allocation script shows capex at 126% of operating cash flow in FY2025 and 129% in FY2026; FCF was negative both years, dividends were still paid, and diluted shares rose about 6.5% over the four-year Yahoo window. In Q1 SSE raised **£1.1bn** of hybrid debt at an average 4.6% cost and 6.6-year tenor plus **£1.3bn** of senior debt at an average 5.1% cost and 8.7-year tenor. That diversifies funding but makes the cost-of-capital hurdle explicit. If equity or debt funds projects at returns only modestly above that hurdle, headline asset growth may not translate into strong per-share returns.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.70 / confidence 0.75

Debt is meaningful but broadly appropriate for regulated infrastructure. The debt script shows FY2026 net debt / EBITDA of 2.6x, down from 2.8x in FY2025 and far below the distorted FY2023 peak. Interest coverage was strong at 32.9x EBIT in FY2026, and Yahoo's snapshot shows net debt / EBITDA around 3.17x. This sits in the conservative-to-adequate range for a regulated utility, assuming maturities are laddered and credit-market access remains open.

The issue is liquidity/refinancing during a heavy capex cycle: FY2026 FCF was negative and near-term debt cover in the script was only 0.4x without considering undrawn facilities. Confidence would firm up by reading the debt maturity ladder, fixed/floating split, facilities and credit-rating commentary in the annual report.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.85 / confidence 0.65

SSE is an institutional utility/infrastructure company, not a founder-led personality bet. The assets, licences, engineering capability, regulatory relationships and project pipeline matter far more than any one executive. Martin Pibworth has been CEO since July 2025, succeeding Alistair Phillips-Davies; the transition does not appear to have disrupted strategy or delivery. The thesis should survive normal leadership changes if the board preserves capital discipline and regulatory execution.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.65 / confidence 0.85

The FY2026 remuneration report makes the alignment mixed but credible. Half of the 2026-29 Performance Share Plan is relative TSR (20% against the FTSE 100 and 30% against European utilities), 20% is adjusted EPS, and 30% is split between strategy and sustainability. Awards vest after three years and carry a further two-year holding period; executive shareholding requirements are 275%-300% of salary and continue for two years after departure. Malus and clawback provisions apply.

The soft spot is that neither ROIC nor return on regulatory equity is an explicit metric. The strategy component rewards Networks RAV and renewables capacity growth, while the annual incentive includes capital delivery, so management can benefit from growing the asset base even if incremental per-share returns are mediocre. TSR, per-share EPS, long holding periods and meaningful ownership requirements partly counter that risk. The overall design points management broadly toward shareholders, but not as cleanly as a return-on-capital or owner-earnings plan.

### Why the other strategies lost

Cash Cow lost because free cash flow is currently negative and capex-heavy; SSE may pay a dividend, but it is not a low-reinvestment surplus-cash harvester today. Freeroll lost because the stock is not obviously at a hard floor with a discrete ≤4-year re-rating catalyst; it is a long-duration infrastructure compounder. Rule Breakers has a strong industry wave, but SSE is a mature, regulated utility rather than an emerging winner-takes-most disruptor.

## Overall

SSE is a decent wonderful-and-fair candidate with an unusually visible reinvestment runway but only moderate underlying returns. The bull case is that the UK grid/clean-power build-out gives SSE years of regulated and contracted asset growth, supporting EPS and dividend growth while reducing exposure to volatile merchant energy profits. The bear case is that the economics are capped and capital-hungry: high capex, debt, possible equity issuance and regulatory/political constraints can absorb much of the value before it reaches shareholders.

My view: quality is solid, runway is strong, valuation is not obviously cheap but still plausible if FY2027-FY2030 EPS targets land. This is more of a steady infrastructure compounder than a high-octane compounder.

## Watch

- FY2026/27 adjusted EPS guidance: Q1 reiterated **168–193p** and the FY2029/30 **225–250p** ambition; track winter delivery, weather and plant availability.
- Funding route for the £33bn plan: equity issuance, scrip take-up, debt ratings, and whether dilution stays modest.
- Regulated networks: allowed returns, Ofgem price-control decisions, planning/consent timing and delivery on major ASTI/LOTI/EGL2 projects.
- Free cash flow: whether negative FCF remains purely growth capex or starts pressuring dividend/debt capacity.
- Incentives: watch whether future remuneration policy adds ROIC/return-on-regulatory-equity measures; the current plan still rewards RAV/capacity growth alongside TSR and adjusted EPS.

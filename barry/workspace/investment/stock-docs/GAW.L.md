# GAW.L — Games Workshop Group PLC

## Business Overview

Games Workshop designs, manufactures, markets and sells the Warhammer tabletop hobby: fantasy/sci-fi miniatures, rules, books, paints, hobby tools and related products. Its core business sells through three channels: Trade independent retailers, owned Retail stores, and Online. FY2025 core revenue was £565.0m, with Trade the largest route to market, plus 570 owned stores in 24 countries and 8,100 independent retailers in 71 countries.

The second profit engine is licensing Warhammer IP, mainly PC/console games today, with longer-term optionality from media. FY2025 licensing revenue was £52.5m and licensing operating profit was £49.5m; H1 FY2026 licensing stepped down to £16.0m revenue after the prior-year Space Marine 2 spike, highlighting that licensing is attractive but lumpy. The core miniatures business is the durable engine: H1 FY2026 core revenue grew 17.3% to £316.1m and core operating profit grew to £126.1m.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.90 / 0.86

The earnings base is unusually clean. Yahoo/YFin annuals show four consecutive profitable years, FY2022-FY2025 revenue rising from £414.8m to £617.5m, operating margin rising to 42.3%, FY2025 net income of £196.1m, and cumulative free-cash-flow conversion of roughly 1.02x net income. H1 FY2026 added £105.5m profit attributable to owners, keeping TTM earnings around £206m / £6.25 per share.

The main blemish is licensing cyclicality: FY2025 benefited from unusually strong video-game royalties, while H1 FY2026 licensing revenue fell from £30.1m to £16.0m. I still score earnings quality high because the core business alone is highly profitable and growing. Further confidence would come from multi-year constant-currency channel data and a better decomposition of recurring licensing versus one-off minimum guarantees.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.92 / 0.86

The business turns accounting profit into cash. FY2025 operating cash flow was £247.4m and free cash flow was £206.5m after £40.9m capex. The helper script showed latest FCF margin of 33.4%, four-year average FCF margin of 30.2%, rising FCF margin trend, and cumulative FCF conversion around 1.02x net income. Capex is meaningful because Games Workshop is investing in manufacturing, warehousing and IT, but it is not consuming the economics.

### Fundamental Stability — 0.88 / 0.84

The last four reported annual periods show no drawdown in the headline fundamentals: revenue, operating margin, diluted EPS and free cash flow all reached new highs in FY2025. The helper script classified revenue, operating margin, EPS and FCF as rising, with FY2025 revenue £617.5m, operating margin 42.3%, diluted EPS £5.935 and FCF £206.5m. The main stability caveat is licensing volatility, not core miniatures demand.

### Returns on Capital — 0.96 / 0.90

This is the standout trait. The helper script calculated latest ROIC at about 70% and four-year average ROIC at about 61%, with incremental ROIC around 148%. Those figures fit the business model: valuable owned IP, premium physical products, direct-to-consumer stores, disciplined trade terms, and limited acquisition/goodwill drag. FY2025 operating profit was £261.3m on a balance sheet with net cash rather than debt.

### Pricing Power — 0.86 / 0.78

Games Workshop sells proprietary hobby products tied to IP it owns, so customers cannot simply swap to a commodity equivalent. FY2025 gross margin was 72.1% on reported revenue and core operating profit margin was 37.5% of core sales. Management also disclosed average RRP increases of about 5% on new products in FY2024/25 and expects tariff costs to pressure gross margin by about 2 percentage points, with a plan to offset via efficiencies and pricing on new products.

Confidence is below 0.8 because the annual report does not quantify unit volumes, customer churn, elasticity, or price/mix separately. Watch future releases for whether price rises continue without damaging volume or hobby engagement.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.78 / 0.72

The runway is real but not unlimited. Management's stated strategy is geographic spread across Trade, Retail and Online, and FY2025 still had only 570 owned stores and 8,100 independent retailers globally. H1 FY2026 Trade grew 25.2% at actual exchange rates and the number of trade outlets rose by about 500 to 8,600, suggesting underpenetrated markets remain. Licensing and the Amazon Warhammer 40,000 film/TV partnership add optionality, but management explicitly says there will not be significant news in the short term.

Confidence is limited by lack of market-size disclosure and by operational constraints: capacity investment, IT replacement scheduled through 2028/29, US tariff disruption, and the fact that Warhammer is a niche hobby rather than a universal consumer product.

### Capital Allocation — 0.84 / 0.82

Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-friendly. Management targets a three-month cash buffer, raised to £85m in FY2025, invests in manufacturing/warehousing/IT capacity, pays staff profit share, and returns surplus cash as dividends. The helper script showed four-year cumulative dividends of £539.7m, about 87% of cumulative FCF, no meaningful acquisitions, and only about 0.5% share-count growth. This is exactly the behaviour expected from a high-ROIC cash generator with limited need for M&A.

The only reason the score is not higher is valuation-linked: buying back shares at ~32x earnings would likely be unattractive, but the company mainly pays dividends rather than forcing repurchases.

### Conservative Debt — 0.96 / 0.90

The balance sheet is very strong. FY2025 cash was about £132.6m at year-end in the annual cash-flow statement, and Yahoo/YFin's latest snapshot showed total cash £171.1m, total debt £49.8m, net cash about £121m, current ratio 3.83, and quick ratio 3.06. The helper script showed negative net-debt/EBITDA and near-term debt cover of about 30x. Solvency risk is not the issue here.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.78 / 0.70

Warhammer is bigger than any one executive: the IP library, product design process, store/trade network, and community flywheel are institutional assets. CEO Kevin Rountree and the senior team matter, but the company has a long operating history and deep hobby culture. The risk is that creative stewardship is hard to measure externally; poor release cadence, lore mishandling, or community alienation could impair the moat before it shows up in financials.

Confidence would improve with deeper evidence on design-studio succession, creator retention, and whether customer engagement is broadening beyond legacy fans.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.70 / 0.65

The culture has good signs: staff profit share, emphasis on cash discipline, and plain-spoken long-term language in annual reports. But insider ownership is low on Yahoo/YFin at roughly 0.5%, and FY2025 introduced proposed share-based awards after shareholder engagement. That may improve alignment, but it also creates monitoring risk because the remuneration committee itself calls it a major policy change.

Confidence is modest until the new incentive plan has a track record and we can see whether targets reward per-share value creation rather than size or short-term profit.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair is a very close runner-up, but the mechanical scorer slightly preferred cash-cow because GAW's FCF generation, fundamental stability and surplus-cash dividend policy are central to the current thesis. Rule-breakers scores well on IP dominance and customer devotion, but the company is already highly profitable and mature rather than a speculative hypergrowth breaker. Freeroll loses decisively: the balance sheet is strong, but the share price has little asset floor or mispricing support at ~32x earnings.

## Overall

Games Workshop is a genuinely excellent cash cow: owned IP, cult-like customer devotion, extreme ROIC, high margins, high cash conversion, net cash, and disciplined capital returns. The problem is not quality; it is entry price. At about £200.76 per share / ~32x TTM earnings, the market is already paying up for the quality and some media/licensing optionality. This remains worth watching, but I would need either a pullback or evidence that core growth can stay in the low-teens for longer to get more excited.

## Thesis Connections

Potential FIT angle: durable niche IP compounder with global fan/community economics and licensing/media optionality. It is not a cheap cash cow today; it is a premium-quality consumer/IP asset where the key question is whether Warhammer can keep compounding beyond the tabletop core.

## Watch

- H2 FY2026 core growth: whether Trade strength persists and Retail/Online accelerate or lag.
- Licensing normalization after Space Marine 2: track royalty income, receivables and new game/media milestones.
- Amazon Warhammer 40,000 project: no short-term news expected, but a confirmed production/release path would matter.
- US tariffs: management estimated about £12m FY2025/26 PBT hit if unmitigated and ~2pp gross-margin pressure.
- IT systems replacement through 2028/29: watch execution risk and capex/opex drag.
- Price elasticity/community health: evidence that RRP increases are accepted without damaging volume or sentiment.
- Revisit valuation if price falls below ~£150 or if evidence supports sustained >10% core EPS growth.

# MSGE — Madison Square Garden Entertainment Corp.

## Business Overview

MSG Entertainment is a live-entertainment and premium-hospitality company built around a small collection of scarce, world-famous New York City venues plus the Radio City Rockettes' **Christmas Spectacular**. Its venues are Madison Square Garden (The Garden), the Infosys Theater at MSG, Radio City Music Hall, the Beacon Theatre, and the Chicago Theatre. Revenue comes from:
- **Entertainment offerings** (FY26: $810M of $1.06B) — concerts, special events, family shows and marquee sports at its venues, plus the Christmas Spectacular.
- **Food, beverage and merchandise** (FY26: $164M) — in-venue concessions largely tied to events, including a share of profits at Knicks/Rangers games per its Arena License Agreements with MSG Sports (a sibling Dolan company, now splitting Knicks/Rangers).
- **Arena license fees and other leasing** (FY26: $86M) — a cash component (~$47M guided FY27, growing 3%/yr) plus revenue subject to shared economics with MSG Sports (suites, sponsorships, signage).

The economics are concentrated: The Garden and the Christmas Spectacular drive a significant majority of adjusted operating income (per management on the FY26 call). During fiscal 2026 MSGE welcomed ~6.4M guests across ~960 events; the Christmas Spectacular sold 1.2M tickets across 215 shows, a 25-year attendance high and a record ~$195M of revenue. FY26 revenue was $1.06B (+13%), operating income $141.5M (+16%), and adjusted operating income (AOI) $262.2M (+18%). It is asset-heavy (owns The Garden and the theater properties) and carries ~$579M of gross debt.

## Trait Assessment

Primary strategy: **Cash Cow**. The leaderboard picked cash-cow (score 0.59 / conf 0.66) with **wonderful-and-fair** as a genuine near-tie dual-fit (0.53 / 0.66); both are committed, and both use the exit-multiple valuation. Cash-cow is the honest primary: MSGE is a mature, durable cash-generation business with scarce-venue pricing power and limited high-return reinvestment runway that now returns cash via buybacks rather than compounding. The near-tie with wonderful-and-fair reflects the genuinely high quality of its franchise moat; they are resolved together because both anchor on owner FCF.

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.45 / 0.70

The earnings are real and cash-backed but **erratic and lumpy** rather than consistent. FY26 net income was $66.2M, up from $37.4M FY25 — but FY24 was $144M, FY23 $77M, and FY22 was a $133M loss. Year-on-year net-income growth oscillates violently (record shows −74%, +88%, +157%), and the income statement carries large swings in tax provisions and unusual items. FCF conversion is strong (cumulative FCF/net income 3.0x over the window), but the underlying volatility is a real caveat for a "dependable cash cow" thesis — this is a high-fixed-cost asset business (venues, union labor, large event-driven SW/C) where results swing with event mix and the sports calendar. Confidence is 0.70: the full 7–10 year cycle record lives in the 10-Ks, not Yahoo's 4-year window.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.65 / 0.70

FY26 operating cash flow was a strong $351.4M, but that is flattered by working-capital timing. The cash balance of $294M includes a large amount "due to promoters" from the surge of booked concerts — management explicitly warns of a partial reversal in FY27 as that timing unwinds. Normalized OCF is better anchored near the AOI of $262M minus the ~$32M net interest and full-cash income taxes. Capital intensity is genuinely low (capex/OCF ~19–22%, capex/D&A ~0.4x), and FY26 FCF margin was ~31% (TTM), well above the 15% strong bar — but the "forward" free cash that recurs to owners is structurally thinner once working-capital swings and the full-cash-tax position are normalized. Score 0.65: strong generation with real timing/tax caveats.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.72 / 0.70

This is not a melting ice cube. Live events, premium venue experiences, and the Christmas Spectacular have proven durable for decades (93rd season), and The Garden is effectively irreplaceable — it is the only venue able to host the NBA/NHL seasons and a large portion of NYC's arena concerts. FY26 revenue was a record, and management guided to solid AOI growth in FY27. The main structural overhang is the **Penn Station redevelopment** and the proposed transfer of the Infosys Theater to the master developer — a strategic/redevelopment risk, not a demand-kill. (Recall this is the sibling Dolan company; the Knicks/Rangers spinoff is MSG Sports, not MSGE.) Confidence 0.70 — durability is real but the Penn Station process and the arena's role in it are still in flux.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.78 / 0.70

The moat is the scarcity and prestige of the venues. The Garden is a single irreplaceable arena in the world's largest media market; Radio City Music Hall and the Rockettes/Christmas Spectacular have a 90+ year brand. The company raised suite fees, sponsorship, and Christmas Spectacular ticket yields year over year (revenue per event, per-concert revenue, and premium-hospitality renewals all up), demonstrating pricing power without losing demand — the majority of concerts sold out and the Spectacular sold a record 1.2M tickets. The direction is stable/widening, though the moat is tied to specific physical assets and management's skill in keeping the calendar full. The main threat is a shift in how music/demand flows to venues (touring concentration, streaming-era artist economics) rather than a direct competitive attack — hence 0.70 rather than higher. Confidence 0.70.

### Capital Allocation — 0.60 / 0.65

This is the weak spot and a large part of why the near-zero ROI is what it is. MSGE has returned ~$205M via buybacks since the 2023 spinoff (6.1M shares, ~2% of the float per share), including ~$25M in FY26 and a shrinking share count (−5.5% over the window). That is shareholder-friendly. But the balance sheet is meaningfully levered (~$579M gross debt, net debt ~$556–882M depending on measurement) for an asset-heavy business, the company pays no dividend, and the biggest "allocation" question is the **Dolan control structure**: James Dolan is Executive Chairman and CEO, the family holds the Class B super-voting shares, and capital decisions (buybacks, the Infosys Theater sale, reinvestment) flow through that concentrated control. Rational-ish capital return, but a controlled-company structure and thin reinvestment options cap the score. Confidence 0.65 — buyback valuation-at-price is a judgement Yahoo can't fully supply.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.60 / 0.65

Read carefully for a cash cow: the equity is thin/negative (Yahoo shows negative common equity and books on it), which is an artifact of the heavy leverage and buybacks, not necessarily a broken business. ROIC has been improving: ~15.6% FY25 (latest reported), up from ~10% average, with 4-yr average ~10% and a rising trend. That is decent but not elite — this is an infrastructure-style asset business earning a modest-to-decent return on a heavy sunk base, which is normal for the type. On a 20% operating-company bar it is high-end "decent/mixed," not "high"; the leverage flatters the profit-to-equity optics while the true return on total capital is the honest read. Score 0.60.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.55 / 0.60

This is the main financial risk. Net debt/EBITDA is ~3.2x (through-cycle peak 3.55x, rising), interest coverage ~2.4x EBIT — **stretched** for a business whose cash hangs on the event calendar and consumer/sponsor demand. The movie is not insolvency (self-funding ~2.1x, cash + undrawn facilities are adequate, FY26 cash jumped to $294M), but leverage around 3x for an asset-heavy cyclical-ish entertainment business is at the "stretched" end of the bands, not conservative. It also means the ~$4/share normalized owner-FCF is net of a real interest drag. Confidence 0.60 — the maturity ladder and fixed/floating split need the 10-K.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.60 / 0.60

Scored as resilience. James Dolan is prominent and chairs/CEOs the whole Dolan complex, and the venues' success does lean on his relationships and the family's long-term ownership. But The Garden, Radio City, and the Rockettes brand are institutional assets that would survive his absence; there is a functioning management team (CFO David Collins, etc.). Moderate concentration — the business would survive a key-person departure but with real disruption given the entrepreneurial/controlled culture. Confidence 0.60; a tested succession path would firm this.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.35 / 0.60

This scores poorly, and it is structural: the Dolan family holds the Class B super-voting shares (10 votes each, electing up to 75% of the board) and James Dolan is both Executive Chairman and CEO, sitting atop both MSGE and MSG Sports (and previously the wider Dolan complex). This is a **controlled company** by NYSE definition. Pay and governance are therefore not primarily aligned to arm's-length minority owners — the family can set the agenda, and the only true checks are the Class A holders electing 25% of the board and the audit/governance machinery. It is not the abusive/gaming extreme (that would be disqualifying), but the incentive system is not one that makes minority shareholders confidently rich in lockstep with management. Score 0.35 with confidence 0.60; an independent-board or sunset read would change it.

## Overall

MSGE is a high-quality, durable live-entertainment business with genuinely scarce venues and a legendary holiday production, currently executing well (record FY26, 13% revenue and 18% AOI growth, strong buyback program). The detractors are structural: **meaningful leverage (~3.2x ND/EBITDA)**, a **controlled-company structure with weak minority incentive alignment**, **erratic/lumpy earnings**, and a **thin normalized owner-FCF** once interest, full-cash taxes, and working-capital timing are factored in. The quality (rising, decent returns on mostly-fixed assets) is real but the price no longer reflects a bargain — the stock has roughly doubled in a year on the Knicks championship halo, Penn Station redevelopment optionality, and buybacks. It is a decent cash cow at a fair-to-full price, not a mispriced one.

## Thesis Connections

MSGE sits in the Dolan corporate family tree alongside MSG Sports (Knicks/Rangers, splitting into MSGK/MSGR) and Sphere Entertainment (SPHR). The live-entertainment and premium-hospitality thesis — scarce venues, iconic productions, pricing power on fixed assets — is a distinct and attractive niche. It does **not** depend on the sports-spinoff machinery (that is MSGS), but the family's serial restructuring and concentrated control are a standing overhang on how minority value is realized.

# EVO.ST — Evolution AB

## Business Overview

Evolution supplies live-casino and RNG games to online-gambling operators, earning B2B licensing revenue from more than 870 customers. Its global studio network, content catalogue and operator integrations underpin unusually high margins. Q2 2026 revenue was EUR 517.8m: Europe returned to sequential growth, North America grew 9.5% year on year and Latin America 26.3%, while cybercrime disruption kept Asia volatile. The latest quarter confirms a mature, cash-rich franchise rather than a current high-growth rule breaker.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.76 / 0.86 confidence

Evolution remained profitable throughout the four-year Yahoo window. Net income rose from EUR 843m in 2022 to EUR 1,062m in 2025, although 2025 fell 14.6%; cumulative FCF equalled 98% of cumulative net income. H1 2026 profit was flat year on year despite a 1.4% revenue decline. The cash backing is excellent, but recent revenue stagnation and regulatory/cyber disruptions reduce predictability.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.95 / 0.92 confidence

2022–25 FCF margins averaged 56%, with capex only 8–11% of operating cash flow. 2025 FCF was EUR 1.12bn and conversion was 105% of net income. This is exceptional, durable, capital-light cash generation; capex was also close enough to D&A to avoid an obvious harvesting flag.

### Fundamental Stability — 0.62 / 0.83 confidence

The business remains highly profitable and constant-currency Q2 revenue grew an estimated 2.4%, but reported revenue has stalled: FY2025 was up 0.2%, H1 2026 fell 1.4%. Regulation, channelisation and cybercrime are persistent constraints, particularly in Europe and Asia. Growth in regulated North America and Latin America provides an offset, so this looks pressured rather than structurally broken.

### Pricing Power — 0.82 / 0.78 confidence

Evolution's moat rests on studio scale, premium content, local-language tables, distribution across 870 operators and exclusive brands such as Monopoly. Adjusted EBITDA margins stayed near 66% through the slowdown, strong evidence of economic differentiation. Direct contract pricing and operator churn are not disclosed; confidence would improve with operator-level renewal and take-rate data, which is not publicly available.

### Capital Allocation — 0.88 / 0.90 confidence

In 2025 the company returned EUR 1.07bn through EUR 572m of dividends and EUR 500m of buybacks, broadly matching EUR 1.12bn of FCF. Shares fell from 213.6m at end-2023 to 199.2m at end-2025. Acquisitions were immaterial in 2025, and the Galaxy transaction was explicitly described as non-material.

### Returns on Capital — 0.88 / 0.91 confidence

Yahoo-derived ROIC was 24–31% in every year from 2022 to 2025, with 2025 at 26.2%. The company carries net cash, so leverage does not flatter returns. ROIC including the large acquired-goodwill balance remains well above a reasonable cost of capital, although the recent decline suggests incremental economics should be watched.

### Conservative Debt — 0.96 / 0.94 confidence

At FY2025 Evolution held EUR 818m cash against EUR 91m of lease debt, or net cash equal to roughly 0.5 times EBITDA. Current and quick ratios were about 2.0 and annual FCF vastly exceeded current debt. Solvency and refinancing risk are negligible on disclosed figures.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.62 / 0.68 confidence

CEO Martin Carlesund has led Evolution since 2015 and remains closely identified with product and regulatory execution. The global studio network and catalogue are institutional assets, but public evidence on tested succession depth is limited. Confidence would improve from the FY2026 governance report's succession disclosures.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.68 / 0.65 confidence

Large insider ownership and aggressive, FCF-funded repurchases align management with per-share outcomes. The evidence reviewed here does not fully resolve incentive metrics, vesting hurdles or whether concentrated holdings are actively controlled. Confidence would improve by reviewing the 2026 remuneration report and insider-transaction history.

Cash-cow wins because exceptional FCF, returns and shareholder distributions coexist with limited current growth. Wonderful-and-fair loses on the now-uncertain reinvestment runway; rule-breakers loses on durable growth; freeroll lacks a discrete, time-bound catalyst despite a modest valuation.

## Overall

Evolution is an exceptional cash machine whose growth engine has temporarily stalled. The balance sheet, margins and capital returns provide resilience, while North America and Latin America preserve optionality. Regulation, weak channelisation and cybercrime mean the old straight-line growth case should not be assumed. No universal disqualifier was found; the UK licence review concluded with a GBP 4.75m settlement and no broader pattern identified.

## Valuation

The SEK 691.40 price is about 11.3× normalised current FCF/share. The FCF anchor is SEK 61: FY2025 FCF of EUR 1.12bn divided by 202.8m diluted shares and translated at EUR/SEK 11.03, rounded down for current softness. The five-year model assumes 3% aggregate growth, 3% annual net buybacks, a 12× exit multiple and a 4.4% dividend yield. These deliberately treat Evolution as a cash cow and do not require a return to historical growth. The agent score is 0.78: compelling cash economics at a modest multiple, tempered by regulatory and Asian execution risk.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a high-ROIC, capital-light cash-compounder thesis, but should be sized as a regulated-content platform rather than a clean secular grower.

## Watch

- Track quarterly constant-currency revenue and whether Asia returns to sustained growth.
- Track European channelisation and any new regulator action after the concluded UK review.
- Confirm buybacks remain FCF-funded and genuinely reduce diluted shares.
- Review FY2026 governance and remuneration reports for succession and incentive-plan detail.

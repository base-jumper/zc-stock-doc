# TA.TO — TransAlta Corporation

## Business Overview

TransAlta is a Canadian independent power producer with a diversified fleet across hydro, wind, solar, gas, energy transition assets and energy marketing in Canada, the U.S. and Western Australia. The company makes money by generating and marketing electricity, with a mix of merchant exposure, hedged production and long-term contracted/tolling assets.

The current story is a transition from volatile Alberta merchant power toward more contracted cash flows. 2025 results were resilient in cash-flow terms despite softer Alberta prices: adjusted EBITDA was **C$1.104b**, free cash flow was **C$514m / C$1.73 per share**, and operating cash flow was **C$646m**. 2026 guidance is lower at **C$950m–C$1.05b adjusted EBITDA** and **C$350m–C$450m FCF / C$1.18–C$1.51 per share**, with management citing softer market conditions but reaffirming guidance after Q1.

Recent growth is contract-heavy: TransAlta signed an Alberta data-centre MOU with CPPIB/Brookfield for an initial ~230 MW PPA and potential up to 1 GW load; acquired Far North's 310 MW Ontario gas capacity; and announced the acquisition of two fully contracted Colorado gas peakers totalling 318 MW with 25–30 year tolling agreements, expected to add about **US$80m EBITDA** and **US$33m FCF** annually, funded with assumed project debt and a **C$350m** equity offering.

No universal disqualifier found in the sources reviewed. The main risk is not fraud or insolvency; it is that leverage, dilution and commodity/merchant exposure could absorb too much of the cash-flow upside.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.45 / confidence 0.75

Accounting earnings are not clean or predictable enough for a high cash-cow score. Yahoo annuals show net income swung from **C$695m in 2023** to **C$229m in 2024** and a **C$138m net loss in 2025**; EPS moved from **C$2.33** to **C$0.59** to **-C$0.64**. The stock-analysis earnings-quality script found one loss year in the four-year Yahoo history and high net-income growth dispersion.

The offset is cash conversion: 2025 FCF was positive and management's adjusted metrics show operating performance better than IFRS net income. I score this mid-low because the cash stream is real, but reported profits are volatile and heavily adjusted. Confidence would improve with a longer cycle including older Alberta power-price regimes and a cleaner bridge between IFRS earnings, adjusted EBITDA and maintenance FCF.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.72 / confidence 0.80

This is TransAlta's best cash-cow trait. Company-reported FCF was **C$514m / C$1.73 per share** in 2025, above the midpoint of guidance, and Q1 2026 produced **C$102m / C$0.34 per share** despite lower production and softer power prices. Yahoo/statement-derived FCF was positive in 2023–2025 after negative FCF in 2022, with FCF margins around the mid-teens on annual figures and a stronger TTM snapshot.

The score is not higher because the business remains capital intensive and cyclical. 2026 guidance steps down to **C$350m–C$450m** FCF, and the Colorado deal adds long-duration cash flow but with substantial assumed project debt and fresh equity. This is a cash generator, but not an effortless asset-light one.

### Fundamental Stability — 0.55 / confidence 0.75

The asset base is essential infrastructure and increasingly contracted, which supports durability. Operational availability was **92.3%** in 2025 and **93.8%** in Q1 2026, and long-term tolling/hedging reduces some merchant exposure. The Colorado assets are 100% contracted with investment-grade customers for 25+ years, and fuel/O&M/capital cost pass-through improves predictability.

The mark-down is the falling recent financial trend: 2025 revenue fell to **C$2.405b** from **C$2.845b** in 2024; adjusted EBITDA fell from **C$1.255b** to **C$1.104b**; Q1 2026 adjusted EBITDA fell to **C$204m** from **C$270m**. Alberta prices and production still matter, and management explicitly frames near-term Alberta headwinds as material. Stability is improving structurally, but the recent numbers are not stable enough for a high score.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.45 / confidence 0.55

TransAlta has some structural advantages: a diversified fleet, hydro optionality, energy-marketing capability, deep operating history, and contracted/tolling assets where costs can pass through. Those features make cash flows more defensible than a pure merchant generator.

But this is not a classic pricing-power business. A meaningful portion of economics depends on commodity power markets, hedges, asset availability and contract terms rather than the ability to raise prices independently. Confidence is moderate because a fuller asset-by-asset contracted/merchant split and contract tenor schedule would firm up how much of EBITDA is genuinely protected.

### Capital Allocation — 0.55 / confidence 0.65

Management is returning some cash: the board raised the dividend 8% to **C$0.28 annualized**, the seventh consecutive annual dividend increase, and the payout is well covered by 2026 guided FCF. The Colorado transaction is strategically consistent — long-term contracted, investment-grade customers, pass-through costs, and expected FCF-per-share accretion.

The caveat is balance-sheet and dilution discipline. The Colorado acquisition uses **US$750m** assumed project debt plus a **C$350m** bought-deal equity issue at **C$19.20**, adding about **18.2m** shares before any over-allotment. The assets may be good, but shareholders are paying with leverage and dilution. Score: middling-positive, not clearly excellent.

### Returns on Capital — 0.38 / confidence 0.75

Returns are too low and too volatile for a high-quality compounding or pristine cash-cow read. The returns script shows ROIC falling from **22.8% in 2023** to **8.1% in 2024** and **2.3% in 2025**, with ROA/ROE also weak or negative in 2025. Utility/infrastructure assets naturally earn lower returns than software-like businesses, but even on that basis the latest spread looks thin.

I score this low-mid rather than failing because 2023 showed the asset base can earn strong returns in better market conditions, and contracted additions should stabilise future returns. Still, the current return profile does not justify a high multiple.

### Conservative Debt — 0.30 / confidence 0.80

Leverage is the main red flag. Yahoo's current snapshot shows total debt around **C$4.46b**, cash around **C$274m**, and net debt/EBITDA about **5.5x**. The debt script's 2025 annual view showed net debt/EBITDA **5.67x**, EBIT interest coverage **0.6x**, and net debt/FCF **10.4x**. The Colorado assets add project-level non-recourse debt, which is contract-backed and amortising, but still adds financial complexity.

The business is not necessarily distressed — assets are essential, FCF is positive, and management expects credit metrics to improve — but this is not a conservative balance sheet. Any cash-cow thesis has to watch debt service and refinancing risk closely.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.75 / confidence 0.55

This is an asset/operator business rather than a founder-led product company. CEO succession occurred in 2026, with Joel Hunter succeeding John Kousinioris and Kousinioris staying as a strategic adviser for six months. The fleet, contracts and operating systems should matter more than one individual.

Confidence is moderate because I did not do a deep management-bench review beyond the company releases.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 / confidence 0.45

Evidence is mixed and not fully reviewed. Yahoo shows low insider ownership and high institutional ownership; governance data flags elevated shareholder-rights risk. On the positive side, management is talking in per-share FCF terms and the dividend/buyback discipline is shareholder-relevant.

Confidence is low because I did not parse the latest proxy compensation tables in this run. Next update should review whether pay is tied to per-share FCF/ROIC/deleveraging rather than size or EBITDA growth alone.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful and Fair fails on returns-on-capital and leverage; this is not a high-ROIC compounder. Rule Breakers fails durable-growth/top-dog setup: TransAlta is an established utility/infrastructure operator, not an emerging dominant disruptor. Freeroll has some catalyst-like elements in contracted acquisitions/data-centre load, but downside support is weak at ~12x book value and negative tangible book; there is no hard floor near the current price.

## Overall

TransAlta is best framed as a leveraged, improving cash-flow infrastructure name rather than a clean compounder. The positives are real: essential power assets, growing contractedness, positive FCF, covered dividend, and several long-duration growth/contracting opportunities. The negatives are just as real: reported earnings volatility, falling recent EBITDA, high leverage, equity dilution, and limited genuine pricing power.

At **C$19.45**, the stock is not obviously cheap enough to offset those risks. It may work if contracted growth lands, Alberta power prices recover, and leverage falls, but the current setup is more “watch carefully” than “high-conviction buy.”

# RELX — RELX PLC

## Business Overview

RELX is a UK-headquartered global provider of information-based analytics and decision tools for professional customers. The group is organised around Risk, Scientific, Technical & Medical, Legal, and Exhibitions. This is the former Reed Elsevier business after years of mix shift away from print toward workflow software, databases, analytics and AI-enabled decision tools. The NYSE ticker `RELX` trades in USD, while the company reports in GBP.

H1 2026 revenue was £4.87b, up 7% underlying, adjusted operating profit was £1.73b, up 9% underlying, and adjusted operating margin improved 70bp to 35.5%. Underlying revenue grew across every division: Risk 8%, STM 6%, Legal 10% and Exhibitions 6%. Adjusted EPS rose 11% at constant currency to 68.6p, and management reaffirmed its full-year outlook for strong underlying revenue, adjusted operating profit and adjusted EPS growth.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.83 / confidence 0.82

RELX has the earnings profile I want in a compounder: consistently profitable, cash-backed, recurring and not obviously cyclical. Yahoo annual data shows net income rising from £1.63b in 2022 to £2.07b in 2025, with no loss years in the available four-year window and low dispersion in net-income growth. Cumulative FCF/net income was about 1.13x. H1 2026 reinforced that read: reported net profit was £1.17b, FCF was £1.16b and adjusted cash conversion was 98%. The remaining confidence limit is history depth: the readily available series is shorter than a full cycle, and adjusted-versus-reported items still warrant periodic review.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.86 / confidence 0.82

This is a very cash-generative business. FY2025 operating cash flow was £2.84b and FCF was £2.31b, giving a 24.1% FCF margin and 1.12x FCF conversion versus net income. H1 2026 FCF increased to £1.16b from £1.15b despite higher capex, cash interest and tax; adjusted cash conversion remained 98%. FCF margins across 2022-25 were consistently strong at roughly 21.6-24.1%, and stock-based compensation is small relative to FCF. Confidence remains short of perfect because RELX does not split maintenance from growth capex.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.84 / confidence 0.82

The reported fundamentals are unusually stable: revenue, operating margin, EPS and FCF all hit new highs in 2025 in the available Yahoo series, and H1 2026 extended the pattern with 7% underlying revenue growth, 9% adjusted operating-profit growth and a 35.5% adjusted margin. Demand is diversified across compliance/fraud/identity, scientific publishing and analytics, legal research/workflows and exhibitions. Exhibitions adds some cyclicality, but embedded professional information products drive most of the value. Confidence would improve with a longer segment-level series spanning multiple cycles.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.84 / confidence 0.78

RELX has real pricing power because its products sit inside professional workflows where data quality, coverage, reliability and switching costs matter. H1 2026 provides better evidence that AI is strengthening rather than merely defending the moat: Legal grew 10% underlying with strong renewals and new sales, driven by adoption of Lexis+ with Protégé, while Risk grew 8% using proprietary and contributory datasets. Group margin expanded 70bp. The caveat is that RELX still does not disclose a clean price/volume or churn bridge; STM also faces open-access and research-integrity pressure, and AI-native competition remains live. The moat looks stable-to-widening, but explicit retention and pricing data would firm this up.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.79 / confidence 0.78

The runway is good, not infinite. H1 2026 showed repeatable organic product extension across the portfolio: Lexis+ with Protégé drove double-digit growth in Law Firms & Corporate Legal; STM's new AI workspace LeapSpace and other tools supported a step-up to 6% growth; Risk's financial-crime, fraud/identity and insurance products sustained 8%. Two small acquisitions cost only £103m, so the current growth engine is mainly organic. Scale caps the score: RELX is already a roughly £10b-revenue company, and management returns substantial cash because not all earnings can be reinvested internally. Specific adoption, usage and retention metrics would firm the runway further.

### Capital Allocation — 0.72 / confidence 0.86

Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly but more aggressive than the prior view implied. RELX completed £1.75b of its £2.25b 2026 buyback in H1 and another £100m after June; shares outstanding fell 3.8% from December to 1.75b. It also raised the interim dividend 7%, while H1 acquisitions were only £103m. The concern is funding: buybacks exceeded H1 FCF of £1.16b and drove net debt up £1.53b to 2.3x EBITDA. Management explicitly uses a 2.0-2.5x leverage range and treats buybacks as the balancing item. That is rational if repurchases occur below intrinsic value, but it sacrifices balance-sheet optionality and prevents a higher score.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.82 / confidence 0.76

Yahoo-derived ROIC improved from 17.2% in 2022 to 24.0% in 2025, with a four-year average around 20.9%; ROCE rose from 21.6% to 34.5%. These are excellent returns and line up with the qualitative moat. The main blemish is goodwill: goodwill is over 50% of assets and around 83% of capital in the script output, so returns on tangible capital would look different and historical acquisition prices matter. Still, the recent direction is good: margins are rising, buybacks are reducing share count and reported earnings/FCF are growing.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.66 / confidence 0.90

Debt is serviceable but stretched for a capital-light compounder. Net debt rose from £7.20b at December 2025 to £8.73b at June 2026, or 2.3x EBITDA, primarily because of accelerated buybacks. Interest cover remains excellent: H1 adjusted operating profit covered adjusted net interest about 12.5x. Refinancing risk is manageable rather than absent: bank and bond debt has a 3.7-year weighted average life, 54% is fixed after derivatives, and $4.1b of committed facilities were undrawn, including $3.5b maturing in 2030. Strong recurring cash flows and liquidity keep this well away from a solvency issue, but leverage above the strategy's conservative band constrains the score.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / confidence 0.70

The thesis is not dependent on one founder or star operator. RELX's assets are databases, content, customer relationships, workflow integrations, brands and product organisations spread across several divisions. CEO Erik Engstrom's execution has been excellent, particularly the long mix shift into analytics and decision tools, but the business should survive leadership transition. Confidence is limited because I did not do a deep board/succession review.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.80 / confidence 0.90

The 2025 remuneration report materially firms this up. The 2026 LTIP is weighted 40% to ROIC, 40% to adjusted EPS growth and 20% to relative TSR, measured over three years with a further two-year holding period; malus and clawback apply. The annual incentive is split equally among revenue, adjusted net profit after tax and cash flow. At year-end 2025, the CEO and CFO held shares worth 2,569% and 1,066% of salary respectively, far above the then-applicable requirements. The main soft spots are large maximum award quantum and adjusted EPS being partly buyback-sensitive, but the multi-year ROIC/TSR design and substantial holdings align management well with per-share owners.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Cash-cow is credible because RELX has high FCF margins and a growing dividend, but the better framing is wonderful-and-fair: high returns, pricing power and a reinvestment runway matter more than yield. Rule-breakers is less apt because RELX is a mature incumbent, not an early hyper-growth disruptor. Freeroll loses because there is no hard downside floor or near-term catalyst; this is a quality compounding case, not a cheap special situation.

## Overall

RELX remains a high-quality compounder at a fair rather than cheap price. H1 2026 strengthened the operating thesis: every division grew organically, group margin expanded, and Legal's acceleration gives concrete evidence that RELX can monetise AI inside trusted workflows. The offset is a deliberately more aggressive balance sheet: management used debt to front-load buybacks and lifted leverage to 2.3x EBITDA. The current view is therefore slightly sharper—business quality has improved, while financial optionality has weakened. AI competition, STM policy/research-integrity risk, goodwill and the ADR/GBP currency bridge remain the main uncertainties.

## Thesis Connections

Strong FIT for a wonderful-and-fair/global compounder bucket: professional information assets, workflow lock-in, high cash conversion, rising margins, high returns on capital and a credible AI-enabled product runway. It is less attractive for a pure value or income bucket because the dividend yield is modest and tangible asset backing is poor.

## Watch

- Whether H2/FY2026 sustains H1's 7% underlying revenue, 9% adjusted operating-profit and 11% constant-currency adjusted-EPS growth, particularly Legal's 10% and STM's 6% revenue growth.
- AI monetisation in Legal and STM: seek disclosed adoption, usage, retention, ARPU or price/volume data for Lexis+ with Protégé, LeapSpace and related tools.
- STM policy/open-access pressure and research integrity issues.
- Completion price and net share-count effect of the remaining £400m 2026 buyback; test repurchases against intrinsic value, not the prior share-price high.
- Net debt/EBITDA after the buyback completes: require a credible path back toward 2x, with the fixed-rate share and maturity profile remaining comfortable.
- Segment margins, especially whether adjusted operating profit continues to grow faster than revenue.
- Any change to the 2026-28 LTIP's ROIC/EPS/TSR hurdles, executive shareholdings or remuneration dilution.

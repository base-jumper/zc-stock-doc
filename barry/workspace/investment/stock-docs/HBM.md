# HBM — Hudbay Minerals Inc.

## Business Overview

Hudbay is an Americas-focused copper miner operating Constancia in Peru, Snow Lake in Manitoba and Copper Mountain in British Columbia. Copper is the primary product, with gold an important earnings and cost credit (39% of Q1 2026 gross revenue), plus zinc, silver and molybdenum. The growth pipeline is led by Copper World and the newly acquired Cactus project in Arizona; management targets copper production rising from roughly 125kt today to more than 250kt by 2030, and later above 350kt with staged Cactus development.

## Trait Assessment

The leaderboard narrowly selects **wonderful-and-fair** on confidence-adjusted quality, but the 0.60 quality score is mixed rather than an endorsement. Rule-breakers is a genuine near-tie because the Arizona pipeline creates an unusual growth path for a mature miner.

### Earnings Quality — 0.62 / 0.93 confidence

FCF was positive in each of 2022–2025 ($179m, $196m, $317m and $238m), and the first half of 2026 added more than $200m despite over $200m of sustaining capital. Q2 produced $101.8m FCF, $321.2m adjusted EBITDA and $113.5m adjusted earnings attributable to owners. Cash conversion is real and improving; predictability remains only moderate because metal prices and shipment timing still move results.

### Returns on Capital — 0.52 / 0.88 confidence

ROIC improved from roughly 5% in 2022–2023 to 8% in 2024 and 15% in 2025, but this follows stronger prices and is not yet a proven through-cycle high-return record. Copper World/Cactus could raise returns, but large development capex can also dilute them.

### Pricing Power — 0.25 / 0.95 confidence

Hudbay is a price taker. Asset quality and by-product credits lower unit costs but do not let the company set copper or gold prices; this is the clearest mismatch with a classic compounder.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.83 / 0.90 confidence

The runway is substantial: brownfield expansions, Copper World, Cactus, Mason and exploration around existing mills. Management targets copper output above 250kt by 2030 versus about 125kt currently, with later potential above 350kt. The question is return on that runway, not its existence.

### Capital Allocation — 0.64 / 0.84 confidence

Debt fell sharply, Mitsubishi funded 30% of Copper World, and first-half FCF funded sustaining capital while Hudbay retired the April notes. Offsetting this, Cactus consideration issued 46.8m shares, second-half Cactus work will cost about $30m, British Columbia growth capex guidance rose $30m to $115m, and the renewed 5% NCIB follows a prior authorisation under which no shares were bought. The Copper World DFS, updated Cactus PFS and evidence that project-level returns survive current capex assumptions remain the firm-up evidence.

### Conservative Debt — 0.87 / 0.95 confidence

At 30 June 2026 Hudbay held $890.9m cash, $1.045bn total liquidity and **negative $80.5m net debt**, or -0.1x net debt/adjusted EBITDA, after repaying $472.5m of April notes with cash and a $272m revolver draw. About $334.5m of cash is restricted to Copper World, but the remaining balance sheet is still resilient for a cyclical miner; project sanctions will consume liquidity.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.78 / 0.72 confidence

Three operating regions and an established development organisation reduce reliance on one person. The 2026 management circular's succession discussion would firm up evidence on leadership depth.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.58 / 0.72 confidence

Insiders hold about 3.6% and the board received very strong 2026 election support, but ownership alone does not establish that incentives reward per-share returns over empire building. The 2026 management circular's scorecard, performance-share metrics and ownership requirements would firm this up.

**Why the others lost:** Rule-breakers nearly tied, supported by copper's demand backdrop and pipeline growth, but Hudbay is neither a category disruptor nor a dominant producer and customer devotion is inapplicable to exchange-priced metal. Cash-cow lost on cyclical earnings and heavy reinvestment; freeroll lost because $20.36 is far above tangible book value and lacks a defensible near-price floor despite clear project catalysts.

## Overall

Hudbay combines good jurisdictions, a quarter-end net-cash position, three operating assets and an unusually deep copper growth pipeline. Q2 reaffirmed 2026 production guidance, improved consolidated cash-cost guidance to **$(0.45)–$(0.25)/lb**, and produced another $102m of FCF. The counterweight is fundamental: Hudbay sells commodities without pricing power, British Columbia costs are above guidance, returns remain cycle-sensitive, and the June Cactus acquisition added 46.8m shares before project economics have been updated to Hudbay's own assumptions.

## Valuation

The exit-multiple model uses normalised current FCF of $1.05/share, close to Q2-updated Yahoo TTM FCF of about $1.01/share and supported by two consecutive ~$102m FCF quarters, at the 1 August close of **$22.75**. Aggregate FCF growth remains 8%, 10%, 12%, 12% and 8% as operating improvements and Arizona development advance, with no further net dilution, a 14x terminal P/FCF and the 0.12% current dividend yield. The result is **0.94% annualised ROI** over five years: +9.99% from business growth, -8.36% from multiple compression and +0.16% from income. A 14x exit is well below the 21.67x entry multiple and forces business growth—not re-rating—to carry the case. `overall.agent_score` remains 0.55: Q2 improved operating evidence and balance-sheet strength, but the higher quote keeps modelled return poor and exposed to metal prices, execution and the post-acquisition share base.

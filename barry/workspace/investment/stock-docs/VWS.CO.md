# VWS.CO — Vestas Wind Systems A/S

## Business Overview

Vestas is a global wind-energy equipment and services company headquartered in Denmark. It designs, manufactures, installs and services onshore and offshore wind turbines. Power Solutions supplies most revenue but carries cyclical project, manufacturing and warranty risk; Service monetises the installed fleet through long-duration contracts at structurally higher margins.

The recovery strengthened materially in Q2 2026. Revenue rose 26.1% year on year to EUR 4.723bn and EBIT margin before special items reached 9.4%, versus 1.5% a year earlier. Power Solutions revenue rose 36.8% to EUR 3.827bn and its margin reached 10.4%; Service revenue fell 5.5% to EUR 896m while its margin remained within plan at 16.6%. Wind-turbine orders rose 67% to 3.349 GW, and the combined turbine and Service backlog reached EUR 76.9bn. Management kept revenue guidance at EUR 20-22bn but raised FY2026 EBIT-margin guidance to 7-9% from 6-8%.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.50 score, 0.85 confidence

Earning power is real but still too cyclical for a high wonderful-and-fair score. The annual record includes a EUR 1.572bn net loss and negative EUR 1.014bn FCF in 2022, followed by net income of EUR 77m in 2023, EUR 499m in 2024 and EUR 778m in 2025. Q2 2026 added convincing recovery evidence: net profit was EUR 285m versus EUR 34m a year earlier, LTM EPS reached EUR 1.11, and LTM adjusted FCF was EUR 943m. Cash conversion is not uniformly smooth, however: adjusted FCF was EUR 94m in Q2 but negative EUR 439m for H1 because the seasonally weak first half absorbed working capital.

Accounting quality is adequate rather than pristine. The Q2 result included EUR 27m of special items, mainly for the Operating Model Reset, and management emphasises EBIT before special items. Warranty expense was still material at EUR 141m, or 3.0% of revenue. The recovery is now well supported by profit and cash, but turbine OEM earnings remain exposed to project timing, supply chains, warranty outcomes and policy cycles.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.66 score, 0.85 confidence

ROCE is the appropriate primary measure for this capital-intensive industrial business. Vestas reported LTM ROCE before special items of 16.5% at Q2 2026, up from 11.8% in FY2025, 8.0% in 2024, 2.9% in 2023 and negative 18.5% in 2022. The current return is value-creating and the direction is strong, but it remains below the strategy's roughly 20% high-return anchor and has not yet been sustained through a cycle.

The balance sheet is not flattering the result: net interest-bearing debt/EBITDA was 0.0x at quarter-end. Q2 Power Solutions margin of 10.4% provides evidence that recent manufacturing and offshore investment is beginning to earn better incremental returns, but one strong quarter is not enough to prove durability. A clean period at or above the 20% long-term ROCE ambition would justify a higher score.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.48 score, 0.78 confidence

Vestas has real scale, engineering depth, installed-base relationships and a leading Service footprint, but turbine manufacturing remains a competitive industrial market. Q2 gross margin rose to 17.0% from 11.1%, and Power Solutions margin improved by 10.8 percentage points. New-order ASP was EUR 1.00m/MW versus EUR 1.11m/MW a year earlier; management attributed the decline to a higher mix of low-scope Americas projects and described the overall pricing environment as stable rather than weakening.

The Service backlog adds a narrower form of pricing protection: EUR 1.3bn of the year-on-year backlog increase came from contractual indexation. Even so, Service revenue fell and its margin eased to 16.6%, while OEM customers remain price-sensitive. The moat is narrow-to-moderate, not absent. Confidence would firm up with comparable peer margin/share data and evidence that order pricing remains above cost inflation through a weaker demand period.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.74 score, 0.82 confidence

The opportunity remains large and underpenetrated. Vestas cites an addressable onshore market of 65 GW and an offshore market outside China of 11 GW of annual installations by 2030. Its turbine backlog reached EUR 36.0bn, Service backlog EUR 40.9bn, and development pipeline 24.4 GW. Q2 deliveries increased 25%, including offshore deliveries rising from 320 MW to 776 MW.

Organic routes include onshore capacity, the V236-15 MW offshore platform, Service contracts across 166 GW, cost-out and project development. The caveat is returns: some investment is still completing the offshore manufacturing ramp, and ROCE has not yet reached the 20% long-term ambition. The runway is long, but the amount that can be reinvested at demonstrably high returns remains less certain than the market opportunity itself.

### Capital Allocation — 0.68 score, 0.85 confidence

Allocation is mostly sensible. Management is funding the offshore/manufacturing ramp, keeps acquisition activity limited relative to the group, targets net interest-bearing debt/EBITDA between -1x and +1x, and intends to return at least 40% of annual net profit through dividends and buybacks. The Board announced a new buyback of up to DKK 3.0bn (about EUR 400m) after Q2, following the EUR 100m programme completed in August; the new programme can repurchase up to 34m shares and runs through 16 December 2026.

The buybacks should more than offset incentive dilution at current execution, supporting a -1% annual share-count assumption in valuation. The caution is price discipline: DKK 204.50 is around 19x the normalised earnings anchor, so repurchases look fair rather than clearly cheap, and the new programme will move a small net-cash position toward modest net debt. A longer record of counter-cyclical repurchases and demonstrated offshore returns is needed for the high band.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.80 score, 0.88 confidence

The balance sheet remains resilient for a cyclical industrial. At 30 June 2026, Vestas held EUR 3.462bn of cash against EUR 3.535bn of interest-bearing debt, leaving a positive net interest-bearing position of EUR 92m and net debt/EBITDA of 0.0x. The company issued a EUR 500m bond due 2033 and used the proceeds to repay the EUR 500m bond maturing in Q2, removing the immediate refinancing wall. Moody's Baa2 rating carried a stable outlook.

The EUR 400m buyback will consume most of the net-cash cushion, but projected leverage remains within management's -1x to +1x range. The score stays below the top end because earnings are cyclical, solvency ratio was only 14.8%, and leases, guarantees and project obligations still matter.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 score, 0.80 confidence

The advantage is institutional rather than dependent on one founder: installed fleet, engineering base, factories, supply relationships, service contracts and global customer coverage. CEO Henrik Andersen is important, but the thesis should survive ordinary succession. The August appointment of Anna Mascolo, formerly Shell's EVP of Low Carbon Solutions, to lead Northern & Central Europe and Global Offshore—with an orderly handover from Nils de Baar—adds evidence that major operating roles can transition without putting the business case at risk.

This remains a business bet rather than a person bet. Confidence is not higher because the internal CEO succession bench and contingency plan are not publicly tested.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.68 score, 0.88 confidence

The 2025 remuneration report materially improves the evidence. The annual incentive was tied to Group EBIT margin before special items (50%), adjusted FCF (30%) and Service EBIT margin (20%), with no payout if the Group EBIT threshold failed. The 2025 long-term award runs for three years to 2027 and uses EPS (60%), ROCE (30%) and avoided emissions (10%); the assessment adjusts for buybacks and dividends to prevent a mechanical windfall. Executive share ownership must reach one year's fixed salary within five years. CEO Henrik Andersen held 352,819 shares worth 435% of salary at year-end and sold none in 2025.

The system is broadly aligned but not exemplary. Most ownership came through grants rather than open-market purchases, EPS remains the dominant LTI metric, target CEO LTI value was 155% of salary, and compensation is deliberately positioned near the upper quartile. These soften an otherwise sensible multi-year, returns-and-cash-oriented design.

### Cash-Cow secondary fit — FCF generation and stability

Cash-cow is a genuine secondary fit but not the primary lens. LTM adjusted FCF of EUR 943m, a 16.6% Service margin and substantial buybacks support it; negative H1 adjusted FCF, elevated reinvestment and a recent loss cycle do not. Free-cash-flow generation therefore remains only 0.45, while fundamental stability is 0.70: the recovery is credible, but the business has not become a low-volatility harvesting machine.

### Why the other strategies lost

Wonderful-and-fair remains the primary strategy because the case centres on a global leader restoring returns while reinvesting into a long structural runway. Cash-cow is the closest secondary fit, but loses on inconsistent through-cycle cash generation and the still-heavy offshore/manufacturing reinvestment phase. Rule-breakers captures the structural wind-growth and leadership elements, but Vestas is a mature cyclical industrial rather than an early-stage disruptor or winner-takes-most platform. Freeroll loses because the share price is near its 52-week high, there is no hard valuation floor, and the thesis depends on sustained execution rather than a defined re-rating catalyst.

## Overall

Vestas is a credible but not high-conviction wonderful-and-fair candidate. Q2 supplied the most persuasive recovery evidence yet: double-digit revenue growth, a 9.4% group margin, a 10.4% Power Solutions margin, strong orders, rising backlog, positive quarterly FCF and raised guidance. The Service backlog, improving ROCE and balance-sheet resilience are meaningful positives. The remaining constraints are through-cycle inconsistency, competitive OEM economics, offshore execution risk, first-half cash absorption and a share price that already discounts much of the recovery.

## Thesis Connections

Potential FIT: energy transition, electrification and energy-security infrastructure. Vestas offers direct exposure to wind deployment plus a long-duration Service backlog, but it remains a manufacturing and execution story rather than a software-like compounder.

## Watch

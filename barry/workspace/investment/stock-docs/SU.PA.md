# SU.PA — Schneider Electric S.E.

## Business Overview

Schneider Electric is a French energy-technology and industrial-automation company. It sells an integrated portfolio across **Energy Management** and **Industrial Automation**, serving Data Center & Networks, Buildings, Industry and Infrastructure. The profit drivers are electrical distribution, critical power, automation hardware, software, digital services and field services; the strategic through-line is electrification, automation and digitalisation. Its pending **$3.1b all-cash acquisition of industrial-data/AI platform Cognite** would deepen AVEVA's software stack but raises the bar for acquisition returns.

FY2025 was a strong base year: revenue crossed **€40.2b**, up **+8.9% organic**; adjusted EBITA was **€7.5b** at **18.7%** margin; and free cash flow was a record **€4.6b**. Q1 2026 confirmed momentum with **€9.8b** revenue and **+11.2% organic growth**; Energy Management grew **+12.8%**, led by data centers, while Industrial Automation grew **+4.4%** as discrete automation recovered. Management reaffirmed FY2026 targets of **+7–10% organic revenue growth** and **+50–80 bps** organic adjusted EBITA margin improvement.

The current case is a high-quality compounder exposed to structural demand: data-center power, grid expansion, electrification, industrial automation, software and services. The obvious risk is not business quality; it is paying too high a multiple for that quality.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.78 / confidence 0.78

Earnings are consistent and cash-backed. Yahoo shows positive net income every available year from 2022–2025: **€3.48b, €4.00b, €4.27b, €4.16b**, with cumulative FCF about **1.04×** cumulative net income. The reported EPS line dipped in 2025, but adjusted EPS still rose to **€8.59** and the company generated record FCF with **111%** cash conversion from net income. Receivables/revenue improved from about **22% to 18%** over the Yahoo series, which does not suggest aggressive revenue recognition.

The score is high but not perfect: the history available through Yahoo is only four years, and Schneider reports adjusted profit measures with restructuring/acquisition costs in the bridge. Confidence would firm up with a deeper 7–10 year statutory and adjusted EPS bridge through the pandemic/inflation cycle.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.66 / confidence 0.75

Returns are good, not spectacular. Yahoo-derived ROIC rose from **10.1% in 2022** to **12.2% in 2025**, with ROCE rising from **13.0% to 15.3%** and an estimated incremental ROIC around **24.5%**. That is value-creating for an industrial technology business, and the trend is improving. The drag is the balance sheet: goodwill is large at roughly **60% of invested capital**, so reported ROIC is below the clean “20%+” compounder ideal and acquisitions matter to the read.

The positive interpretation is that the moat is real and incremental returns look stronger than average returns. The negative interpretation is that the acquired base caps reported returns. Confidence would improve with a clean returns-on-tangible-operating-capital bridge from the Universal Registration Document.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.76 / confidence 0.72

Schneider has a structural moat from installed base, electrical-distribution scale, mission-critical power reliability, software/workflow integration, channel depth and customer switching costs. FY2025 gross margin was about **42.1%** and the adjusted EBITA margin improved organically despite FX/gross-margin headwinds. Q4 commentary showed products growth balanced between volume and price, with North America pricing responding to tariffs and other geographies offsetting inflation.

The moat is strongest in medium-voltage/electrical distribution, data-center infrastructure, field services, AVEVA/ETAP/RIB software, and customer lifecycle relationships. It is weaker in more commoditised residential/buildings hardware and cyclical discrete automation. The score is high but not maxed because some segments remain cyclical and hardware competition can pressure price.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.82 / confidence 0.78

The runway is long. Schneider is directly exposed to data-center power demand, grid expansion, electrification, energy decentralisation, efficiency retrofits and industrial automation. Q4 2025 demand accelerated across Data Center & Networks and Infrastructure; Q1 2026 delivered **+11.2%** organic group growth, and management’s 2026–2030 Capital Markets Day target is **+7–10% organic revenue CAGR** plus cumulative margin expansion. Software & Services were **19%** of 2025 revenue with an ambition to reach about **25%** by 2030, adding a recurring-revenue layer.

The key caveat is that this is not a capital-light software pure-play: growth needs capacity, supply-chain execution and working capital. Still, the market opportunity appears large, underpenetrated and durable enough to reinvest for years.

### Capital Allocation — 0.67 / confidence 0.78

Capital allocation has generally been rational. Schneider reinvests in the business and pays a progressive dividend; from 2022–2025, cumulative acquisition spend was about **€2.6b** versus cumulative FCF around **€16.6b**, the dividend consumed about **46%** of FCF and was covered roughly **2.1×** by 2025 FCF. Share count was broadly flat to slightly down.

The new concern is Cognite: Schneider agreed on 30 June to pay **$3.1b cash** for a business with 2025 revenue above **$170m**—roughly **18× revenue**—and integrate it into AVEVA. Cognite's 36% ARR-bookings growth and strategic fit are attractive, but the price embeds substantial execution and synergy expectations. This is too material and richly priced to call recent M&A plainly disciplined; the score moves into the upper “mostly sensible with a real lapse/risk” band until Schneider discloses economics and demonstrates returns. Confidence would firm further with the deal presentation, financing details and future acquired-revenue/margin reporting.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.78 / confidence 0.78

The balance sheet is resilient for a stable industrial technology company. Yahoo-derived annual net debt/EBITDA was **1.58×** in 2025, up from **0.98×** in 2024 but still moderate. EBIT interest coverage was **17.3×**, and near-term debt cover was about **3.2×** using cash plus one year of FCF against current debt. The snapshot including more recent Yahoo debt fields gives net debt/EBITDA around **1.9×**, still within a sensible range.

The score is high, with a penalty for rising leverage and acquisition/goodwill exposure. The pending $3.1b all-cash Cognite purchase is manageable against 2025 FCF of €4.6b but could lift leverage depending on funding; the score stays unchanged pending transaction financing details. Confidence would improve with the debt maturity ladder, fixed/floating split and committed facilities from the URD.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / confidence 0.70

This is an institutional franchise, not a single-person thesis. CEO Olivier Blum matters, but Schneider’s advantage sits in product breadth, channel relationships, installed base, engineering depth, software assets and global execution systems. The 2026 CFO transition from Hilary Maxson to internal candidate Nathan Fast appears orderly, with Fast having long internal finance experience and investor-relations visibility.

The score is high because no one person appears existential. Confidence is capped because a fuller succession/bench read would require governance and remuneration detail from the URD.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.62 / confidence 0.55

The incentives appear acceptable but not deeply audited here. Insider ownership is modest at about **3.5%** per Yahoo, institutions hold roughly **52.5%**, and governance risk is mixed: Yahoo shows board risk lower than audit/shareholder-rights risk. The company’s public targets emphasize organic growth, margin expansion, FCF and sustainability, which are broadly owner-relevant, but this run did not inspect the remuneration report in detail.

Confidence is deliberately low. To firm this up, review the URD remuneration section for metrics, vesting periods, shareholding requirements, pay quantum, dilution and clawbacks.

### Why the other strategies lost

**Cash cow** scores well on cash generation and stability, but it is the wrong framing: Schneider still has a substantial reinvestment runway in electrification, data centers, grid infrastructure, automation and software. **Rule-breakers** captures the structural wave and top-dog qualities, but Schneider is already a proven large-cap compounder, not an emerging hyper-growth disruptor. **Freeroll** is a poor fit because there is no hard valuation floor or time-bound re-rating catalyst; the stock is expensive, not obviously mispriced.

## Overall

Schneider is a high-quality, strategically well-positioned compounder. The strongest positives are structural demand, improving margins, excellent cash conversion, a defensible installed-base moat, a long reinvestment runway and a balance sheet that can support the plan. The main constraints are valuation and the fact that reported ROIC is good rather than elite once goodwill and capital intensity are included.

This is a stock to keep watching closely, not a screaming bargain. Business quality is strong enough to deserve a premium, but at roughly **25.5×** the current owner-earnings estimate and **~23×** forward Yahoo EPS, the forward return depends on Schneider sustaining high-single-digit growth and not suffering a meaningful de-rating. Cognite adds strategic upside but also introduces a clear capital-allocation test at a full purchase price.

## Thesis Connections

Schneider connects cleanly to long-running electrification and power-infrastructure themes: data-center energy intensity, grid expansion, automation, electrical distribution, energy efficiency and software-defined industrial systems. It is a broad “picks and shovels” beneficiary rather than a single-product AI/datacenter bet.

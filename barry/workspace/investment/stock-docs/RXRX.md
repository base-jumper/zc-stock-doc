# RXRX — Recursion Pharmaceuticals, Inc.

## Business Overview

Recursion is a clinical-stage biotechnology company that uses the Recursion OS—automated wet labs, proprietary multimodal biological and chemical data, machine learning, and clinical-development tools—to discover and develop medicines. It monetizes the engine in two ways: wholly owned drug candidates can create product economics if approved, while collaborations with Roche/Genentech and Sanofi provide research revenue, milestones, and potential royalties. The company has no approved products and no product revenue.

The August 2026 portfolio contains five clinical-stage internal assets plus REC-7735, whose IND was cleared for a Phase 1/2 start in 2H26. The lead program is REC-4881 for familial adenomatous polyposis. Partner validation is more concrete than commercial validation: Roche/Genentech exercised the first option on an unexplored neuroscience target, and cumulative inflows reported across all partnerships exceed $500m. Revenue is milestone-driven and lumpy rather than recurring.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.82 / 0.78

AI-enabled drug discovery is a structurally expanding field because pharmaceutical R&D productivity remains poor while multimodal data, foundation models, and automated experimentation are improving quickly. Recursion operates across target discovery, molecule design, and clinical execution rather than supplying a narrow point tool. The attraction is real but industry economics remain unproven: clinical attrition is still the binding constraint and well-funded biopharma, technology companies, and private TechBio firms are competing. Firm-up: the companion market doc provides the current boundary-matched ten-year sizing; refresh as realized collaboration and royalty pools become measurable.

### Top Dog — 0.72 / 0.70

Recursion is a leading contender, not a demonstrated dominant player. It reports more than 50 petabytes of multimodal data, five clinical-stage programs, more than $500m of cumulative partner inflows, and a first Genentech neuroscience target option. Its combined platform after acquiring Exscientia spans phenomics, chemistry, and clinical development. Yet Isomorphic Labs, Schrödinger, Insilico Medicine, Generate Biomedicines, Relay Therapeutics, large CROs, and internal pharma teams provide credible alternatives, and boundary-matched market shares are not public. Firm-up: comparable industry-wide program, partnership-inflow, and validated-target data.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.73 / 0.75

The moat case rests on proprietary data and an integrated lab-in-the-loop. Recursion reports more than 50PB of multimodal data; the Roche/Genentech neuroscience work used over one trillion internally manufactured neurons and genome-scale maps, while REC-7735 moved from first hit to development candidate in ten months and 242 compounds. Genentech's option is an external mechanism check that the maps can surface and validate novel biology. The advantage is not yet proven durable in approved medicines, frontier models are broadly available, and competitors can generate or license data. Firm-up: repeated partner options and evidence that platform-derived candidates beat conventional programs in clinical success, not only speed.

### Durable Growth — 0.62 / 0.86

For this clinical-stage company, pipeline and partner progression are more informative than reported revenue. Genentech advanced the first neuroscience target, REC-7735 received IND clearance, REC-4881 is producing Phase 2 evidence, and Sanofi programs are approaching candidate milestones. Against that, Q2 revenue fell 60% to $7.7m, H1 operating cash burn remained $187m, and only one program has Phase 2 human efficacy evidence. The engine is advancing, but growth is lumpy and has not yet compounded into product sales.

### Organic Growth — 0.30 / 0.92

Growth is materially acquisition-assisted. Recursion issued about 102.1m shares and transferred roughly $630m of consideration for Exscientia in November 2024, adding chemistry capability, collaborations, and several current clinical assets. It had already acquired Valence in 2023. Shares outstanding rose from 234.3m at FY2023 to 528.2m at FY2025, also reflecting equity raises and compensation. Internal programs and the original phenomics platform are organic, but the current end-to-end story and pipeline cannot be described as overwhelmingly built rather than bought.

### Management Backing — 0.68 / 0.78

Founder Chris Gibson built the original platform and remains chair and adviser, but Najat Khan became CEO in January 2026 after leading R&D strategy and portfolio organization at Janssen. The team has reduced 2026 cash-operating-expense guidance to below $375m, roughly 40% below pro-forma 2024, while preserving the main pipeline and hiring senior AI and drug-design leaders. NVIDIA, ARK, BlackRock, Vanguard, and DCVC are notable backers. Khan's CEO record is short, and the value created by the Exscientia integration is not yet established. Firm-up: delivery of the 2026–27 clinical and partner milestones within the lower spend envelope.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.38 / 0.90

Alignment is weak despite some improvements. FY2025 bonuses used pipeline, partner, platform, people/process, and integration objectives, with an 80% payout; long-term awards vest over four years, and clawback and anti-hedging policies exist. However, H1 2026 stock compensation was $41.3m against only $14.1m revenue, 27.5m RSUs remained outstanding, and large equity issuance has overwhelmed per-share ownership. CEO Najat Khan beneficially owned about 0.17% at the April record date, mostly options and near-term RSUs. The founder's ten-vote Class B shares converted in 2026, removing the dual-class voting overhang.

### Customer Devotion — 0.62 / 0.72

The relevant customers are pharmaceutical partners. Roche/Genentech's first target option, $216m of cumulative inflows under that collaboration, $134m from Sanofi, and repeat milestones indicate that sophisticated buyers see value in the platform. The evidence remains concentrated in a handful of contracts and does not establish voluntary expansion across a broad customer base; Q2 collaboration revenue fell sharply as project phases ended. Firm-up: additional target options, program expansions, renewed partnerships, or disclosed partner retention and economics.

Wonderful-and-fair and cash-cow lose mechanically because Recursion has never demonstrated earnings or positive free cash flow. Freeroll has concrete 2026–27 catalysts but no durable floor: June net cash covers only about one-third of the $3.22 share price and is being consumed rapidly, while clinical assets remain binary.

No universal disqualifier is triggered. June cash and restricted cash of $556.8m and lower spending guidance provide runway into early 2028 without additional financing, so there is no imminent solvency event. Disclosure controls remain ineffective because acquired Exscientia controls have not operated long enough to remediate identified material weaknesses; this is a serious watch item, but no restatement, fraud allegation, or evidence that the current financial statements are unusable was found.

## Overall

Recursion has one of the more credible integrated AI-drug-discovery platforms, and Genentech's first target option is a meaningful external proof point. The platform also has a plausible data flywheel and enough liquidity to reach several near-term clinical and partner milestones.

The case is nevertheless speculative. The platform has not produced an approved medicine, revenue is falling and milestone-lumpy, acquisition and financing have driven severe dilution, incentive alignment is weak, and cash burn implies another financing before product commercialization. The November REC-4881 update and Sanofi/Genentech milestones can materially change the evidence, but the current stock should be sized as an option on platform and clinical validation rather than a proven compounder.

## Thesis Connections

Recursion fits an AI-enabled drug-discovery thesis: proprietary biological data, automated experiments, and frontier models could reduce discovery time and create multiple independent medicines. The critical distinction is whether this becomes a durable product engine with repeatable clinical success, rather than a research platform that generates interesting targets but consumes shareholder capital.

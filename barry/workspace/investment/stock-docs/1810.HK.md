# 1810.HK — Xiaomi Corporation

## Business Overview

Xiaomi is a China-headquartered consumer-technology group that generates revenue across four pillars: (1) **smartphones and connected devices** (the core: smartphones under Xiaomi/Redmi/Poco, plus TVs, wearables, and a large Xiaomi AIoT/IoT ecosystem), (2) an **internet-services and software layer** (HyperOS operating system, ads, and value-added services over its connected base), and (3) a fast-ramping **electric-vehicle (Xiaomi Auto) business** launched in 2024, plus (4) an intensifying **artificial-intelligence push** (MiMo large-language models, and a stated multi-year AI investment program). Smartphones remain the profit anchor and roughly the majority of revenue; the IoT ecosystem and internet services are the higher-margin growth layer, and EVs are the newest, capital-intensive, low-margin but strategically important bet.

FY2025 (year ending 31 Dec 2025) revenue was CN¥457.3b, up ~25% YoY (from CN¥365.9b in FY2024), with gross margin of ~22% and net income of CN¥41.6b (basic EPS CN¥1.62, diluted CN¥1.56). Yahoo's TTM snapshot shows revenue CN¥438.1b, gross margin ~21%, net margin ~7.5%, and ROE ~12.6%. Balance sheet is conservatively financed: cash + short-term investments of ~CN¥30b against total debt of ~CN¥42b, net debt ~CN¥9b, D/E ~15%, with net cash after debt. FCF has been volatile (CN¥21.4b in FY2025, negative in FY2022) reflecting EV inventory build and heavy investment, but the core is cash-generative.

## Trait Assessment

*Winning strategy: rule-breakers (score 0.636, confidence 0.655).*

### Right Place and Time — score 0.78, confidence 0.70

Xiaomi sits in multiple structurally expanding waves at once. The EV business rides EV adoption in China and globally (a large, underpenetrated shift), the IoT/AIoT "smart everything" wave is still mid-S-curve, and the AI/LLM buildout is an enormous emerging opportunity. Xiaomi's internet-services revenue is tied to a 754m-global-MAU connected base that gives it distribution anchored in an installed user base that keeps growing. The industry economics have a catch — hardware (especially EVs) is structurally lower-margin and price-competitive — which is why this is a strong-but-not-exceptional score rather than a top-band one. Confidence is moderate: the EV and AI revenue trajectories are early, and the durability of Chinese consumer spending is a macro question.

### Top Dog — score 0.52, confidence 0.68

Xiaomi is a credible contender, not a clear global leader in its anchor category. In smartphones it is roughly the #3 seller globally (behind Apple and Samsung) and has strong share in China and premium ambition, but it is not the category-definer. In EVs it is an aggressive late entrant challenging BYD, Tesla, and Chinese rivals — compelling on price-performance, but not yet a leader. In AI LLMs it is a follower behind the frontier labs. Its edge is distribution, price-performance and an enormous connected-device base rather than category dominance. The next update should track EV share-of-sales, smartphone premium-share, and MAU growth materially.

### 3. Sustainable Competitive Advantage — score 0.45, confidence 0.55

Xiaomi's moat is real but thin and hard to call durable. Its strengths are a powerful brand/value proposition, a huge sticky ecosystem (AIoT device lock-in), scale in manufacturing, and a data distribution flywheel from its connected products. But in smartphones and increasingly EVs it faces formidable, better-capitalised rivals (Apple, Samsung, Huawei, Huawei-backed competitors, BYD, Tesla) with comparable or better ecosystems, so competitive positioning is contested. The guard is thin on the hardware side; the moat is most evident in the sticky AIoT/internet-svcs layer. Confidence is held at 0.55: differentiating moat durability (gross-margin trend of the IoT/services mix vs pure hardware) would firm it up.

### 4 — Evidence of Durable, Accelerating Growth — score 0.62, confidence 0.68

Revenue grew 25% YoY in FY2025, and ~18% CAGR over the 2022–25 window, with the EV business contributing meaningfully. The mix is deceptive: the EV ramp is low-margin and inventory-heavy; profit has not grown at the same rate as revenue (net income +8% and below in 2025 vs revenue +25%). FCF is volatile and did not closely track reported net income in FY2025 (ratio 0.5x), flagged by the durable-growth script — a sign the profit base is being reinvested into EV/AI rather than converting cleanly to cash. The second derivative is decelerating: revenue growth 35%→25%. Growth is durable in the sense of multiple large TAMs, but it is not yet compounding cleanly at high rates. Score 0.62 reflects real growth with a caveat on quality.

### 5 — Organic Growth over Acquisitive Growth — score 0.82, confidence 0.75

Xiaomi's growth is overwhelmingly organic. Goodwill is tiny (CN¥1.7b, ~0.3% of assets, flat), cumulative acquisition spend over four years is ~CN¥0.67b (negligible vs revenue), and there is no history of large or overpriced acquisitions or goodwill impairments. Growth is built by winning customers (phones, IoT, EV) with its own engine and ecosystem. No acquisitions of size. Confidence is solid, and a 1 (never any acquisitions is not claimed because of history (e..g. Deepmotion, Holitech stakes), but the conclusion — organic — is unambiguous.

### 6 — Great Management & Smart Backing — score 0.75, confidence 0.68

Founder Lei Jun remains chairman & CEO and is the archetype of founder-mentality leadership, with a long track record of building niche/category players. The backing is strong: major reputable institutional holders include Temasek, IDG, Qiming, and cross-listed index funds (e.g. Vanguard Total International, iShares emerging-markets). Communication on the EV/AI strategy is clear and outward-facing. Confidence is not higher because recent leadership turnover in the auto/EV vertical adds some execution uncertainty, and the sheer breadth of concurrent bets (phones + IoT + EVs + AI) makes delivery the real risk.

### 7 — Incentive Alignment — score 0.55, confidence 0.55

Alignment is mixed. Lei Jun's own-wealth (founder ownership ~20%) is genuinely aligned with long-term per-share value, which is a positive. But Xiaomi issues meaningful equity compensation (SBC was CN¥5.4b in FY2025, and there is gradual diluted-share creep — diluted shares rose from ~25.5b in FY2024 to ~26.7b in FY2025) and pays some tiered bonuses that can reward size-driven targets. The plan points at long-run value (founder-led) but is diluted by equity comp and growth-at-scale metrics. This is the honest mixed read.

### 8 — Customer Devotion — score 0.65, confidence 0.60

Xiaomi has credible customer-loyalty signals: an enthusiastic global fanbase ("Mi Fans"), high review volumes, strong app-store/community engagement, and a genuinely-loved value ecosystem that creates word-of-mouth. AIoT/device ecosystems produce lock-in, so some stickiness is captivity as much as free-choice devotion — but the fan-led traction is real, especially in China and emerging markets. Volume and satisfaction are strong; hard NPS or retention numbers are not public, capping confidence.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Wonderful & Fair (0.54):** Xiaomi has growth, runway, and competent management, but its hardware/EV margins are structurally modest, pricing power is weak (0.38), returns on capital (0.42) are unexciting, and earnings quality (0.55) is compressed by EV losses & margin mix. It is not a "wonderful" high-returns compounder — it earns its growth, not a moat-driven super ROI.
- **Cash Cow (0.51):** Xiaomi throws off real cash but FCF is volatile and materially below net income on the pure-EV hikks, margins are low, and it is not a mature, no-runway franchise — it has a huge reinvestment runway (0.72), the exact thing a cash cow must lack.
- **Freeroll (0.41):** Xiaomi is not cheap or out-of-favor below a hard floor; downside support is weak (0.25 — no conservative NAV undervalue), and there is no clear beaten-down re-rating "catalyst in 1-4 years" — the re-rating thesis (EV/AI) is a growth story, not a deeper-value one.

## Overall

Xiaomi is a high-quality, founder-led consumer-hardware-and-EV growth story in large, expanding markets (phones, AIoT, EVs, AI). Its strengths are real: durable organic growth, strong Indian/Chinese distribution and brand, a genuine scale ecosystem, a huge runway and founder stewardship. The principal detractors that prevent a much higher score: hardware margins are structurally thin and prices-competitive (weak pricing power, 0.38); growth is decelerating and FCF is not cleanly tracking profit as EV investment absorbs cash; it is a follower-not-leader in both premium handsets and EVs; and a broad set of new businesses fragments focus. Rule-breaker quality is clear (0.64) but the strategy's build-on-multiple-TAMs bet is precisely why it is a solid, not top-band, holding.

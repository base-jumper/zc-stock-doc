# FMG.AX — Fortescue Ltd

## Business Overview

Fortescue is an Australian iron ore miner and emerging energy/metals company. The Pilbara hematite business is the cash engine: record FY26 shipments of 201.3 Mt at an industry-leading C1 unit cost of US$18.74/wmt, producing revenue of US$17.0 billion, underlying EBITDA of US$8.6 billion (51% margin) and underlying NPAT of US$3.5 billion. Hematite realised price was US$90.7/dmt (88% of the Platts 62% Fe index). Iron Bridge is a higher-grade (67% Fe) magnetite JV with Formosa, still ramping and loss-making. Growth ambitions span Belinga iron ore (Gabon), copper exploration (Cañariaco in Peru, plus Argentina, Kazakhstan, Canada), a "Green Grid" of 2.4 GW renewable build-out in the Pilbara, green metal/hydrogen pilots, and Fortescue Zero battery-electric haulage. The company is led by executive chairman and ~30%+ owner Andrew Forrest, with Dino Otranto (Metals & Operations CEO) and Gus Pichot (Growth & Energy CEO).

FY26 statutory NPAT of US$2.9 billion included a US$750 million pre-tax (US$525 million after-tax) non-cash impairment at Iron Bridge and a US$73 million compensation-claim expense. Free cash flow was US$3.2 billion (operating cash flow US$6.8 billion less US$3.6 billion capex). The balance sheet is strong: US$5.1 billion cash, US$0.9 billion net debt, gross debt/EBITDA 0.7x. FY27 guidance: 197–207 Mt shipments (including 11–14 Mt Iron Bridge), C1 cost US$20.50–21.75/wmt, metals capex US$3.7–4.7 billion and energy opex ~US$300 million. A fully franked final dividend of A$0.46 brings FY26 to A$1.08/share (65% payout).

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.42 / confidence 0.85

Earnings are real but violently cyclical. Yahoo's four-year series shows US$4.8 billion (FY23), US$5.7 billion (FY24), US$3.4 billion (FY25), US$2.9 billion statutory (FY26), with year-on-year growth swings of +18%, −41%, −15% — a high-dispersion commodity path, fully inconsistent with a smooth compounder. No loss years in the available window, but the deep record includes the FY16 iron-ore crash when Fortescue posted a large attributable loss. Cumulative FCF/net income conversion is ~0.98, so reported profits do convert to cash. Statutory figures lean on recurring "underlying" adjustments (FY26 excluded the Iron Bridge impairment, exploration and legal costs), so headline quality is discounted. Iron ore prices, realised prices and volumes are set by the market, not the company.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.55 / confidence 0.75

At the peak of the cycle Fortescue throws off enormous cash: FY24 FCF of US$5.1 billion at ~28% margins. But forward capital intensity is high: FY26 capex of US$3.6 billion consumed over half of the US$6.8 billion operating cash flow, including US$848 million for decarbonisation, US$2.1 billion sustaining/hub development, US$368 million exploration. Company-defined FY26 FCF was US$3.2 billion (21% margin), ahead of FY25's US$2.6 billion. The four-year margin average is ~24% but declining as the mine plan and Green Grid build-out take more capital. Sustaining capex is now materially above the ~US$2–2.5 billion range of recent years, and FY27 guidance points to further capital spend (US$3.7–4.7 billion metals + US$150m energy). This is a high-cash business at times and a heavy-reinvestment business at others — not a pure surplus machine.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.50 / confidence 0.80

The demand base is structurally challenged. Chinese steel output is plateauing; long-term iron ore demand is forecast to decline as the world transitions away from blast-furnace steel. BMI recently cut its 2026 iron ore forecast to an average US$99/t amid weak Chinese construction activity and steel margins. Fortescue's low-cost position and record volumes show resilience, but the market is not growing — this is a mature, cyclical, structurally-contested commodity exposed to Chinese policy and steel-sector health, with Fortescue's higher-cost Iron Bridge and a new-entrant supply wave (Simandou) adding pressure. There is a real chance of a melting-ice-cube over a 10-year horizon. This is not stable like BHP or Rio; it's more cyclical and lower-cost-positioned than them.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.45 / confidence 0.75

Fortescue is a price taker in seaborne iron ore; most pricing is indexed to Platts 62% Fe and other market benchmarks. Hematite realised price was US$90.7/dmt (88% of Platts). There is no brand or customer lock-in. The structural advantage is a low-cost pit and integrated infrastructure (mine–rail–port), which lets it out-earn marginal producers and stay profitable through cycles, but it cannot raise prices without the market moving. The supposed "moat" is a cost-position moat, not pricing power, and the 2013–2016 history shows what happens when the benchmark collapses. Iron Bridge's magnetite economics are unproven (impairment on the ramp) — the higher-grade product exists but has yet to earn a premium.

### Capital Allocation — 0.40 / confidence 0.80

Mixed record. Dividends are disciplined: 65% payout, eighth consecutive year at 65%+. FY26 dividends of A$1.08/share (~A$3.3 billion) were covered by FCF. The share count is stable (no meaningful buybacks, no big dilution) — the flip side is that scarce cash has been directed into high-cost growth satellites: US$848 million of decarbonisation capex in FY26, a US$6.2-billion green-truck/fleet program spanning multiple years, an Energy segment that is still a drag on opex, the opportunistic Alta Copper acquisition, the Iron Bridge overrun/impairment, and Belinga studies. Executives describe a "pivot" to energy/copper/green metal. Some of these (Green Grid) could be value-accretive in time; several (Iron Bridge, large-scale green hydrogen economics) have already destroyed value or remain unproven. The mix of base-cash returns plus heavy strategically-optional spending is rational-at-times and speculative-at-times — a mid-score.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.58 / confidence 0.80

ROIC/ROCE has been strong but is declining from a commodity peak. Yahoo-derived ROIC was 21.4% in FY23, 23.7% in FY24, 13.7% in FY25, 11.4% in FY26; ROCE from 26.9% to 30.6% to 18.2% to 15.6%. The four-year average ROIC ~17.5% is decent but the trend is down as iron ore normalises and as growth capital (Belinga, copper, Green Grid) accumulates before contributing. The mechanical incremental-ROIC is negative (capital up, profits down). Returns on the core hematite asset remain well above cost of capital, but the marginal dollars being invested today are at lower expected returns and are unproven. This is a "decent, cyclical" return, not a durable "wonderful" one.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.85 / confidence 0.90

The balance sheet is conservative. Net debt/EBITDA is ~0.1x (US$0.9 billion net debt on US$8.2 billion EBITDA at June 2026), interest coverage is strongly positive, and the company self-funds capex. Gross debt/EBITDA 0.7x, gearing 23%, within the stated <2x and <40% through-cycle thresholds. Debt structure was embellished with a RMB14.2 billion term loan and liability management; maturities are spread. Reported interest-coverage metrics that appear negative are an artifact of net interest being reported netted with income; the actual story is minimal net debt. This is a resilient balance sheet that can ride a cycle.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.55 / confidence 0.75

Andrew Forrest remains executive chairman and ~30% owner, and is the driving force behind the energy pivot — the green strategy is closely identified with him. The company has operated smoothly operationally under Dino Otranto and the new CEO structure (Metals vs Growth & Energy), with a broad team. But the strategic direction — especially the multi-billion-dollar green investments and CMRG negotiations — is heavily Forrest-loaded. If he stepped away, the strategic ambition could be clawed back, which is a real (if partial) concentration. Institutional advantage lies in the mine/rail/port infrastructure, not a single head; moderate key-person risk.

### Incentive Alignment / Stewardship — 0.48 / confidence 0.80

Mixed. The dividend policy is strong (shareholder-friendly payout, buyback-neutral). Long-term remuneration does exist but the picture is diluted by the founder's all-consuming green, strategic, and copper ambitions, which carry execution risk for minority holders. There have been governance and cultural issues — the MinterEllison misconduct investigation referenced on the call (11 dismissals disclosed for FY26) and the contested history with the Yindjibarndi people (which produced a record A$150m native-title compensation award in May 2026). Pay quantum and the amount of "big vision" spending tilt incentives away from pure per-share value. No outright self-dealing found; moderate negative.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.32 / confidence 0.80

As a pure iron ore miner, the future reinvestment runway is negative: the addressable market is shrinking. The "runway" depends on unproven adjacencies — green hydrogen/steel, copper exploration, Belinga Gabon (far from development, no study finalized), Green Grid monetisation to third parties (inflated data-centre power). None of these has proven economics. The cash cow's classic no-runway read is present. The runway from the company's legacy business is absent — which is why this scores low.

## Overall

Fortescue is a high-quality, low-cost iron ore cash machine with a conservative balance sheet, an 8-year 65%-plus dividend record, and one of the world's best-cost positions in its commodity. The core hematite franchise at A$17.75 offers a 6% fully franked yield and a meaningful surplus that returns to shareholders.

It is not, however, a wonderful-business compounder. It is a cyclical, price-taking, commodity business with high capital requirements and a shrinking addressable market, run by a founder-led team that continues to redirect cash into unproven energy/metals/copper/steel projects, several of which have expressly impaired (Iron Bridge, hydrogen). The valuation is not demanding at 12.2x normalised FCF and 6% yield, but the expected annualized ROI is only ~3.6% under conservative assumptions because the entry multiple is already fair and no re-rating is assumed. The cash yield plus modest growth and no multiple help gives a modest income-carried return — a classic cow, not a get-rich-quick.

## Thesis Connections

This is primarily a thesis test for the "commodity cyclical into the Green transition" theme: Fortescue is an iron ore cash cow into a structurally declining steel/supply market, trying to be an energy/copper/green-metals pioneer. The thesis connection is weak-to-moderate: it is a high-yield dividend holding, but not a structural growth compounder. Copper optionality (Cañariaco, Argentina, Kazakhstan) and third-party Green Grid power sales could be multi-year call options, but they are unproven and not central to the thesis.

## Watch

- **Iron ore price / Chinese steel margin** — the key driver of both earnings and dividend. Watch 62% Fe CFR breaching US$90; BMI has already cut 2026 forecasts to ~$99. If spot drops below ~$85 sustained, FY28 dividends will be cut (65% payout on lower underlying NPAT).
- **CMRG talks / China trade friction** — a resolution or escalation; watch inventory levels at Chinese ports and statement tonnage. Could be a material re-rating event (positive on settlement, negative on further blockage).
- **Iron Bridge ramp:** watch 11–14Mt FY27 guidance, cash-cost path and the JV's own cash break-even versus fixed 70/30 cost split. Another impairment would hit confidence.
- **Green capex drift:** the US$6.2bn decarbonisation/green fleet program in FY27-29, US$0.9–1.3bn/yr decarb capex, and the Energy opex run rate. Watch for capital discipline and any sign of non-economic green commitments.
- **Dividend coverage:** 65% payout policy; the FY27 dividend is fully covered only if underlying earnings hold the cycle base case. Watch the ex-div date 1 Sep 2026 (A$0.46) and the FY27 interim.
- **People/stewardship:** CEO transitions, cultural/governance, the MinterEllison misconduct investigation (referenced on the FY26 call), and any escalation of Yindjibarndi native-title claims beyond the A$150m compensation award.
- **Capital returns vs growth:** will the board maintain 65%+ payout while still funding Belinga/copper/Green Grid, or will the payout be reduced to fund the pivot?

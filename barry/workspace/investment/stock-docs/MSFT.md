# MSFT — Microsoft Corporation

## Business Overview

Microsoft is a global software, cloud, gaming, search, professional-networking and AI infrastructure company. The investable core is the enterprise platform stack: Microsoft 365, Dynamics, LinkedIn, Azure, GitHub, security, databases, developer tooling and Windows/Server ecosystem. Revenue is mostly recurring or usage-based, with the strongest growth currently coming from Azure, cloud AI services and Copilot-adjacent adoption.

FY2026 was another record year: revenue was **$331.8b**, up **18%**; operating income was **$155.2b**, up **21%**; and adjusted net income excluding OpenAI investment effects was **$128.8b**, up **22%**. Q4 revenue was **$90.0b**, up 18%, with Microsoft Cloud revenue of **$59.3b**, Azure and other cloud services growth of **43%**, and commercial remaining performance obligations up 84% to **$678b**. Azure surpassed **$100b** of annual revenue and Microsoft 365 Copilot reached more than **30m paid seats**.

Current Yahoo snapshot at this update: **$464.72/share**, market cap about **$3.45t**, trailing P/E **25.1x**, forward P/E about **20.0x**, trailing P/S **10.4x**, and dividend yield about **0.81%**. The post-results price is no longer unusually cheap; the valuation now requires sustained double-digit earnings growth while the AI buildout absorbs exceptional capital.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — score 0.86, confidence 0.88

Microsoft has the track record this trait wants. Yahoo annual data for FY2023–FY2026 shows net income of **$72.4b, $88.1b, $101.8b and $133.7b**, with no loss years. FY2026 diluted EPS was **$17.95**, but OpenAI investment effects added **$0.67**; adjusted EPS was **$17.28**, up 22%. The operating earnings base is still advancing rapidly, while the explicit adjustment prevents the non-operating investment gain from being mistaken for recurring product economics.

Cash backing has weakened materially. FY2026 operating cash flow rose to **$182.9b**, but additions to property and equipment nearly doubled to **$115.9b**, leaving about **$67.0b** of free cash flow, a **20.2% margin** and only **0.50x** reported net income. Four-year cumulative cash conversion is about **0.69x**. This still looks like growth investment rather than accounting smoke, but owner earnings are far below GAAP earnings while the AI buildout is heavy. Confidence would firm further only with a reliable maintenance-versus-growth capex split, which Microsoft does not disclose.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — score 0.88, confidence 0.87

Returns remain excellent for a company this large. The refreshed returns script shows FY2023–FY2026 ROIC of **29.2%, 28.3%, 26.8% and 28.2%**, averaging 28.1%. FY2026 ROCE was **28.7%**, and the ROE-minus-ROIC gap was only 2.0 points, so leverage is not flattering the result.

The watch item is incremental return. The script estimates recent incremental ROIC at **27.2%**, helped by the FY2026 earnings acceleration. That is encouraging, but property and equipment rose to **$313.1b**, and returns on today's huge datacenter commitments will only be visible with a lag. If AI capex keeps rising without commensurate Azure/Copilot monetisation, this score should come down.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — score 0.86, confidence 0.78

Microsoft has multiple reinforcing moats: enterprise switching costs, identity/security integration, developer ecosystem, Office file/workflow standards, cloud scale, distribution through existing enterprise relationships, and increasingly AI infrastructure breadth. Gross margin has held around 68–70% despite a massive AI infrastructure buildout, and operating margin rose to 45.6% in FY2025. Q3 FY2026 revenue and operating income growth also show pricing and mix are still strong enough to offset infrastructure pressure.

The moat is not invulnerable. Azure competes directly with AWS and Google Cloud; AI model and agent economics remain unsettled; and regulators continue to scrutinise platform bundling. Still, customer workflows are deeply embedded, and the stack is getting more—not less—integrated.

### Reinvestment Runway — score 0.84, confidence 0.82

Microsoft still has unusual reinvestment runway for a mega-cap. Cloud migration is not done, security remains a large and growing enterprise spend category, AI infrastructure demand is pulling Azure capacity, and Copilot/agent monetisation creates a new layer over the existing Microsoft 365 and developer base. Azure's first **$100b** revenue year, **43%** Q4 growth, **$678b** commercial RPO and more than **30m** paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats provide stronger evidence that demand is scaling with the infrastructure.

The mark-down is that the opportunity is extremely capital-intensive. Capex/operating cash flow reached **63.4%** in FY2026, capital spending was more than 3x depreciation/amortisation, and Microsoft disclosed **$34.6b** of construction commitments plus **$194.1b** of purchase commitments primarily related to datacenters. The runway is real; utilisation, pricing and the incremental return on that runway are the key uncertainties.

### Capital Allocation — score 0.74, confidence 0.82

Capital allocation is mostly rational but now hinges on infrastructure discipline. FY2026 capex was **$115.9b**, dividends **$26.4b**, and buybacks **$22.3b**; FCF still covered the dividend about 2.5x and diluted share count fell modestly. The company had **$40.6b** remaining on its repurchase authorisation at year-end.

The main blemishes are M&A price discipline and the sheer size of forward infrastructure obligations. Activision's long-run per-share value creation remains unproven—Q4 XBOX content and services revenue fell **10%**, and the quarter included severance and impairment charges in XBOX. Buybacks around a mid-20s current-earnings multiple are also less powerful than the headline dollars imply.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — score 0.94, confidence 0.85

The balance sheet remains very strong. At June 2026 Microsoft held **$76.8b** of cash and short-term investments versus **$40.3b** of stated current and long-term debt; Yahoo's broader debt measure, which includes additional lease obligations, is about $128.8b. Liquidity is ample relative to debt maturities and annual operating cash flow, though the large datacenter lease and purchase commitments are more economically important than bond leverage alone.

Microsoft is still self-funding the AI buildout while retaining substantial liquidity. The missing detail is the fixed/floating mix across the full lease-and-debt stack, but nothing in the filing suggests near-term solvency risk.

### Key-Person Risk — score 0.80, confidence 0.72

Satya Nadella is important, but the thesis is not a one-person bet. Microsoft’s advantages are institutional: enterprise distribution, product breadth, partner channels, cloud infrastructure, developer mindshare, brand, and balance sheet. The senior bench is deep, and CFO Amy Hood has been central to the capital allocation and margin discipline for years.

The mark-down is that Nadella’s strategic judgment around cloud, GitHub, OpenAI partnership, and AI product integration has been unusually valuable. A poor successor could misallocate the AI capex cycle or weaken the culture, but the business would not break overnight.

### Incentive Alignment — score 0.74, confidence 0.65

The available proxy/reporting evidence suggests broad alignment: CEO compensation is heavily equity/performance-based, with outside reporting on the FY2025 proxy putting Satya Nadella’s total compensation at $96.5m, roughly 87% stock awards and over 95% performance-based. That structure points management toward long-term shareholder value more than cash salary does.

Confidence is only moderate because this update did not fully parse the 2025 proxy metrics, performance hurdles, holding requirements, and clawbacks. The next refresh should verify whether incentives are tied to per-share value/returns rather than revenue scale, AI narrative milestones, or easily gamed adjusted metrics.

### Why the other strategies lost

Cash-cow also scores well: Microsoft is massively profitable, cash-generative, financially conservative and returns capital. But it is not primarily a harvest case. The better frame is wonderful-and-fair because the reinvestment runway in Azure, AI, security and enterprise software is still material, and the valuation should be judged on whether those reinvested dollars earn attractive returns. Freeroll is a poor fit: there is no hard valuation floor near the current price and no discrete ≤4-year catalyst/re-rating setup. Rule-breakers has some conceptual overlap through AI, but Microsoft is a proven compounder, not an emerging asymmetric disruptor.

## Overall

Microsoft remains a high-quality compounder, but the latest filing sharpens both sides of the thesis. Azure, Cloud RPO and paid Copilot seats show unusually strong demand and ROIC remains high. Against that, FY2026 capex nearly doubled, FCF fell despite 18% revenue growth, the datacenter commitment stack is immense, and the share price rebounded sharply after results.

At about 20x forward EPS the stock can still compound acceptably if current growth persists, but the margin of safety is thinner than at the prior update. The main underwriting question is whether AI infrastructure produces durable per-share cash earnings rather than revenue scale and accounting profit alone.

## Valuation

I used the exit-multiple method with `metric: Earnings`, because Microsoft's operating earnings remain recurring while current FCF is depressed by growth capex. The current fundamental is **$18.40/share**, triangulated between FY2026 adjusted EPS of **$17.28** and current FY2027 consensus of about **$19.50** rather than using the OpenAI-inflated GAAP figure or a pure forward estimate.

Assumptions refreshed 1 August 2026: **$464.72** entry price, five-year hold, aggregate earnings growth fading from **13% to 7%**, **22x** exit P/E, **-0.20%** annual net dilution from buybacks, and **0.81%** dividend yield. The implied entry multiple is about **25.3x**, so the model assumes moderate multiple compression. The return must be carried by earnings growth, not re-rating.

The model produces an **8.17% annualised ROI** (1.48x over five years). Annualised attribution is about **9.98% business growth, 0.20% buyback lift, -2.72% multiple compression and 0.97% income**. This should be read as a quality-compounder return with significant execution sensitivity, not a deep-value return. Agent score is **0.68**: exceptional franchise strength, tempered by the higher entry price, sharply weaker cash conversion and the risk that datacenter economics settle below historic software returns.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a high-quality compounder / AI infrastructure thesis: Microsoft owns distribution, cloud capacity, enterprise workflow, developer tooling and a credible application layer for agentic AI. It is one of the few companies that can both fund the AI buildout internally and monetise it across multiple existing customer surfaces.

## Watch

- AI capex productivity: Azure growth, paid Copilot seats/usage, the $678b commercial RPO conversion, Microsoft Cloud gross margin and incremental ROIC must justify the datacenter buildout.
- FCF conversion: watch whether FY2026's 0.50x FCF/net-income ratio recovers as AI capacity matures or remains structurally depressed.
- Commitments: track utilisation, timing and returns against $34.6b of construction and $194.1b of mostly datacenter purchase commitments.
- Copilot/agent monetisation: look for hard adoption, retention, ARPU uplift and customer ROI evidence, not just product announcements.
- Regulatory pressure: bundling, cloud licensing, AI partnerships and platform control remain live antitrust risks.
- Activision/gaming: monitor whether the large acquisition creates durable per-share value or becomes goodwill drag.
- Incentives: next update should read the latest proxy in detail for metrics, hurdles, holding requirements and dilution.

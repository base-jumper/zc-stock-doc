# GQG.AX — GQG Partners Inc.

## Business Overview

GQG Partners is a listed active equity manager. It earns almost all net revenue from management fees charged on funds under management (FUM), with a small performance-fee contribution. At FY2025, FUM was US$163.9B, split across International, Emerging Markets, Global and US equity strategies; management fees were 98.3% of net revenue, which makes the model recurring but still exposed to market levels, fund performance and client flows.

FY2025 was a mixed but still profitable year. Closing FUM rose 7.1% from US$153.0B to US$163.9B, helped by US$14.8B of investment performance, but net flows were negative US$3.9B. Average FUM rose 10.8% to US$164.3B while the average fee realisation rate slipped from 49.6bp to 48.4bp. The July 2026 ASX update confirmed that the near-term picture is still deteriorating. FUM ended July at US$156.4B versus US$163.9B in December 2025. July brought another US$4.5B of net outflows across every strategy, lifting year-to-date outflows to US$19.6B; US$5.0B of July investment performance merely held total FUM roughly flat. The business remains high-margin and cash-rich, but its fee base is shrinking beneath favourable markets and is not a low-volatility bond proxy.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.78 / confidence 0.75

GQG’s reported earnings are clean for an asset manager: fee revenue, high operating margins, low capex and strong conversion of accounting profit into cash. Yahoo annual data shows net income rising from US$238M in 2022 to US$282M in 2023, US$432M in 2024 and US$463M in 2025, with no loss years in the available four-year set. The stock-analysis script calculated cumulative FCF/net income of about 1.01x across 2022–2025, so earnings are substantially cash-backed.

The weakness is predictability, not accounting quality. GQG’s revenue base depends on FUM, which moves with equity markets, strategy performance and client flows. FY2025 also showed the problem directly: FUM grew, but net flows turned negative and performance-fee revenue fell to US$13.8M from US$24.6M. I score the accounting quality high, but not “utility-stable.”

What would firm this up: a filing-level 7–10 year series including strategy performance, flows by channel and fee-rate history through weaker markets.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.82 / confidence 0.75

This is the strongest part of the case. GQG is capital-light: FY2025 net income attributable to GQG Inc. was US$463.3M and Yahoo-derived FCF was US$480.0M on US$779.3M reported revenue, a 61.6% FCF margin. The company reported cash of US$133.4M and no debt at year-end 2025, and said the primary use of cash remains working capital and dividends. Capex is tiny relative to revenue and profit.

The caveat is that “free cash” is not immune to FUM pressure. A market drawdown or sustained underperformance can reduce management fees quickly while compensation and distribution costs do not fall one-for-one. Even then, the structural cash conversion is excellent.

What would firm this up: quarterly FCF/working-capital detail and a better view of compensation flexibility in a drawdown.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.50 / confidence 0.85

The business remains durable enough to analyze as a cash cow, but the operating trajectory has deteriorated. GQG has a large global client base, four main equity franchises and diversified channels. July FUM of US$156.4B was 4.6% below December 2025 despite US$12.2B of favourable year-to-date investment performance. Net outflows reached US$19.6B through July and remained broad: US$6.0B International, US$7.5B Emerging Markets, US$3.5B Global and US$2.5B US equities. July alone lost US$4.5B, worse than June's US$3.2B, so the watch item for moderating withdrawals was not met.

The stability risk is active-management cyclicality. Poor relative performance, fee compression, consultant downgrades or client concentration can pressure flows for several years. The scale, breadth and July acceleration of withdrawals make a purely temporary diagnosis less comfortable; the still-large franchise argues against calling it structural decline yet. The cash stream is attached to client confidence, not just market beta.

What would firm this up: a sustained turn in monthly net flows, FUM retention by channel, institutional mandate wins/losses, and one-year/three-year/five-year strategy performance versus benchmarks and peers.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.58 / confidence 0.68

GQG has some moat: investment track record, brand with allocators, global distribution and capacity in large-cap global/emerging-market equities. The firm still generates elite margins, which suggests clients are willing to pay for the product.

But pricing power is only moderate. Average fee realisation fell from 49.6bp in 2024 to 48.4bp in 2025, which management attributed to strategy and vehicle mix. First-half 2026 outflows across all four strategy groups show that strong historical economics do not create meaningful switching friction when relative performance weakens. Active equity management faces structural fee pressure from passive products, model portfolios and institutional bargaining power. The moat is performance-and-trust based rather than contractual.

What would firm this up: fee-rate trend by strategy/vehicle and evidence that new products are not diluting economics.

### Capital Allocation — 0.72 / confidence 0.65

Capital allocation fits the cash-cow strategy. GQG returns most surplus cash: the board declared a 4Q25 dividend of US$0.0365 per share, representing a 90% quarterly payout, and total 2025 dividends/dividend equivalents paid were US$439.3M. Yahoo shows a very high trailing dividend yield around 14.7% at A$1.44, with an indicated payout ratio around 79%; the stock-analysis downside script estimated dividend/FCF cover at about 1.09x.

The good news is that the payout is funded by cash generation, not leverage. The question is whether reinvestment and diversification spend remain disciplined. PCS/private-capital initiatives, ETFs and managed accounts may help diversify, but could also distract if they are scaled for empire-building rather than returns.

What would firm this up: board capital-return policy over a full cycle, PCS economics, and management compensation metrics tied to per-share value rather than FUM growth alone.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.78 / confidence 0.70

Returns are very high because the business is capital-light. Yahoo shows trailing ROE around 108% and ROA around 81%; annual data shows ROE above 100% in 2024 and 2025. On a traditional industrial ROIC lens the data is less clean, but the economic reality is straightforward: a scalable fee business needs little tangible capital to generate profit.

The durability caveat is that high returns in asset management can be competed away by performance decay, fee compression or star-manager/key-person risk. The current return profile is excellent; the question is its persistence.

What would firm this up: returns through a poor investment-performance cycle and evidence that fee rates and margins stay resilient when flows are negative.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.90 / confidence 0.80

The balance sheet is a major strength. The FY2025 annual report states GQG had US$493.4M of total assets, US$133.4M cash excluding restricted cash, and no debt at 31 December 2025. Yahoo’s snapshot shows only lease-like debt of US$26.8M against US$133.4M cash, quick ratio of 10.9 and current ratio of 12.1. Net cash and low capex give the dividend real resilience.

The score is not 1.0 only because the real leverage is operating leverage to FUM and reputation, not financial debt.

What would firm this up: committed liquidity lines and any contingent obligations tied to seed investments or private-capital products.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.45 / confidence 0.60

This is the main quality blemish. GQG has built a broader institution, but the investment identity remains closely associated with co-founder/CIO Rajiv Jain and the central investment culture. Active managers can lose client trust quickly if key portfolio leaders leave, performance slips, or succession looks uncertain.

Management explicitly says it is trying to build an institution that outlives its founders, and the strategy/channel diversification helps. Still, for a cash-cow dividend thesis, a key-person shock could hit flows and the valuation multiple at the same time.

What would firm this up: named succession depth, portfolio-manager ownership/retention, and client concentration by lead PM relationship.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.65 / confidence 0.55

The compensation language is directionally positive: the annual report says compensation aims to align employees with clients, shareholders and company objectives, and GQG uses investment-alignment plans and seed investments in GQG-managed funds. The high dividend policy also disciplines surplus cash.

Confidence is only moderate because I did not fully review the remuneration report and insider ownership. Incentives in asset management can drift toward asset gathering if pay is tied too heavily to FUM rather than long-term client outcomes and per-share cash generation.

What would firm this up: the full remuneration scorecard, insider ownership, employee fund co-investment by seniority, and any clawback/deferral mechanics.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair is plausible on quality but loses on reinvestment runway and key-person durability: GQG is better framed as harvesting a cash-generative franchise than underwriting a long compounding runway. Freeroll has cheapness and yield, but lacks a clean time-bound catalyst beyond “flows recover / market re-rates.” Rule-breakers does not fit: this is a mature profitable asset manager, not an emerging category creator.

## Overall

GQG is a high-quality but cyclical cash cow whose near-term fundamentals have weakened. The attraction is capital-light economics, very high cash conversion, no real debt, and a dividend yield that is unusually high if earnings and FUM stabilise. The detractors are now more tangible: US$19.6B of net outflows through July, no monthly moderation, broad strategy underperformance, active-manager fee pressure, market exposure and key-person/reputation risk.

At A$1.47, the stock still prices in scepticism, but the higher entry price and worsening flows reduce the margin of safety. I would not treat the dividend as risk-free: the valuation partly reflects a smaller fee base and the risk that persistent outflows eventually reduce earnings and distributions. The balance sheet and cash conversion keep the valuation interesting, but evidence of flow stabilisation is now the key condition.

## Thesis Connections

Fits an income/cash-yield thesis: buy a capital-light, net-cash asset manager when the market is pricing flow/performance risk harshly, collect a large covered dividend, and reassess if FUM retention or fee rates deteriorate.

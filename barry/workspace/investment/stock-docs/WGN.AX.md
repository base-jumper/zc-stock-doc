# WGN.AX — Wagners Holding Company Limited

## Business Overview

Wagners is an Australian construction-materials and services group. FY2025 revenue was $431.3m: Construction Materials contributed $257.0m, Project Services $105.7m, and Composite Fibre Technologies (CFT) $68.4m. Construction Materials is vertically integrated across cement, concrete, quarries and reinforcing steel; Project Services supplies mobile concrete, crushing, haulage and precast work; CFT manufactures composite power poles, crossarms and infrastructure products in Australia and the US. FY2025 EBIT was concentrated in Construction Materials ($39.5m) and CFT ($9.8m), partly offset by corporate costs and a small EFC loss. [1]

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.58; confidence 0.88

Profitability is genuine but not yet predictable enough for a high score. FY2025 operating EBIT rose 9% to $41.8m and NPAT more than doubled to $22.7m, while FY2022–24 net income ranged from $3.1m to $10.3m. Project revenue can swing sharply when large contracts finish: its FY2025 revenue fell 49% after Sydney Metro work completed. Operating cash flow of $55.5m exceeded NPAT in FY2025, supporting cash quality, but working-capital movements and project timing make a single year a weak normalisation anchor. [1][2]

### Returns on Capital — 0.38; confidence 0.88

ROIC is the appropriate measure for this asset-heavy operating company. Yahoo-derived ROIC was only 5.6%, 3.5% and 7.2% in FY2022–24, below a reasonable cost of capital; the recent earnings uplift likely improves the run-rate but does not yet establish durable value creation. FY2025 net debt fell, so leverage is not the explanation for improving earnings, and goodwill is immaterial relative to the asset base. Incremental returns from new concrete sites and CFT automation remain unproven. [1][2]

### Pricing Power — 0.62; confidence 0.80

Vertical integration, owned quarry/cement capacity and specialised composite know-how provide a real but narrow moat. FY2025 cement revenue rose 3% on stable volume and margin improved by two percentage points through pricing and efficiency; CFT margins improved across product lines. However, bulk construction materials remain regional and cyclical, customers can tender competitively, and the group has not disclosed a long history separating price from mix and input inflation. Confidence would improve with price/volume/mix bridges across a full construction cycle; revisit after FY2026 results. [1]

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.68; confidence 0.78

There is visible room to deploy capital: three concrete-plant sites were acquired for $15m, the batch network was expanding from seven to nine sites, CFT pole capacity was being scaled toward 8,000 units in FY2026, and Queensland housing/infrastructure demand includes the Brisbane 2032 build. These routes are organic and adjacent to proven operations, but low historical ROIC and TTM capex of $42.9m mean the next-dollar economics are not yet demonstrated. Confidence would improve with plant-level utilisation, invested capital and returns after the new sites ramp; revisit after FY2027 results. [1][2]

### Capital Allocation — 0.62; confidence 0.82

Management reduced FY2025 net debt by $13.6m to $34.0m, invested in concrete capacity, and restored a modest, covered dividend. The trade-off is sensible given the runway, although shares outstanding subsequently rose to about 199.7m and TTM cash-flow data records $28.7m of stock issuance, creating material dilution. The score therefore recognises balance-sheet repair and organic investment but withholds credit for per-share compounding until new capital earns attractive returns. [1][2]

### Conservative Debt — 0.62; confidence 0.90

FY2025 borrowings were $57.0m excluding leases and net debt was $34.0m, down from $47.6m. All covenants were met, and the $145m banking facilities then matured in July 2026; current Yahoo data shows $30.1m cash and $158.3m total debt including lease-style obligations. Liquidity appears adequate (current ratio 1.51), but the refinancing date and capital intensity prevent a stronger score. [1][2]

### Key-Person Risk — 0.58; confidence 0.76

The Wagner family remains influential through founder/chairman Denis Wagner and substantial insider ownership, while operations are led by managing director Cameron Coleman and a broader executive team. Institutionalised segment operations reduce single-person dependence, but family influence and specialised CFT know-how are meaningful concentrations. Confidence would improve with a current succession plan and divisional leadership retention data from the FY2026 governance disclosures. [1][3]

### Incentive Alignment — 0.78; confidence 0.90

Insiders hold about 38.9% of shares and Denis Wagner directly owned 23.3m shares after an April 2026 purchase. Executive pay includes short- and long-term incentives, with performance rights designed to require shareholder-value outcomes. High ownership strongly aligns economic exposure, though related-party governance and dilution from performance rights deserve monitoring. [1][2][3]

Wonderful-and-fair is the best fit, but only a mixed one: the company has a credible organic runway and improving margins, while weak historical ROIC prevents a strong compounder score. Cash-cow is a genuine near-fit because cash conversion and dividends have recovered, but ranks second because free cash flow is volatile and growth capex remains substantial. Rule-breakers loses because most revenue is mature, cyclical materials rather than an emerging winner-takes-most market; freeroll loses because the shares trade around 4.1× book with no defensible valuation floor or time-bound rerating catalyst. No universal disqualifier was identified in the FY2025 audit, balance sheet, ownership or current disclosures reviewed.

## Overall

Wagners has real operating momentum: its integrated Queensland materials footprint is scaling, CFT has moved from near break-even to meaningful profit, and the balance sheet has improved. The central objection is that the market already capitalises this progress at roughly 25.6× trailing earnings and 4.1× book while historical ROIC and free cash flow are inconsistent. This is a promising execution story, not yet a proven high-return compounder.

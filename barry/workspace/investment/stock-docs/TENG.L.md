# TENG.L — Ten Lifestyle Group Plc

## Business Overview

Ten Lifestyle Group is a London-based (AIM: TENG) global concierge and customer-experience platform. It partners with financial institutions and premium brands to operate loyalty, lifestyle, travel, dining and entertainment benefits for their affluent and mass-affluent customers. Revenue is two-pronged: **corporate revenue** (platform-as-a-service and technology fees from ~50+ multi-year client contracts — £29.2m in H1 2026) and **supplier/transaction revenue** (member travel, dining and event spend, £4.5m in H1 2026). Operations span Europe, the Americas and AMEA from 20+ offices, ~1,188 employees.

The model is a digital-first reinvention of white-glove concierge: Active Members grew 23% to 436k (H1 2026 vs 354k), with proprietary platforms (Ten Digital Platform, Talia AI assistant, Ten PX personalisation, Ten Box Office ticketing) pushing higher engagement and operating leverage (Net Revenue per FTE +11%, opex per request −9%). The FY2025 story: record net revenue £65.7m (+4.5%), record adj. EBITDA £14.6m (22.2% margin), profit before tax up >5× to £2.9m, net cash £9.7m.

## Trait Assessment

Winning strategy — **Rule Breakers** (S×C 0.39), a genuine near-tie with wonderful-and-fair (0.35). Management is growing a high-touch service business into a mass-affluent digital scale play.

**Right Place and Time — 0.55 / 0.6.** Banks and premium brands sit in a durable drive to retain affluent customers through loyalty experiences; digital concierge is an emerging, expanding niche. But it is not a structurally huge or obviously-winning industry — competitive, service-heavy, no dominant economics. Real but narrow runway.

**Top Dog — 0.65 / 0.6.** Ten is arguably a category leader in B2B concierge-as-a-loyalty-platform (27 years of data, 50+ client banks, first B-Corp on AIM), but competes with in-house bank perks teams and travel/events aggregators. Credible leader in its niche; not a winner-take-most market.

**Sustainable Advantage — 0.6 / 0.55.** Moat is a blend of switching costs (multi-year embedded client contracts), proprietary data/IP (Talia, Ten PX, MAID), and 27 years of supplier relationships. Real but thin — defensibility is moderate and unproven at scale against better-capitalised entrants. Confidence low (0.55): needs the annual report's competitive-position narrative.

**Durable Growth — 0.55 / 0.6.** Revenue growth has been lumpy and modest (FY23 +37% off a COVID base, FY24 +0.9%, FY25 +3.5%; H1 2026 net revenue +9% cc). Active Members +23% and margin expansion (adj. EBITDA margin 22.2% FY25, 20.7% H1) show genuine operating progress, but the underlying growth rate is single-digit — not a compounding engine. Score sits where it is only because the digital/AI transformation carries a credible re-acceleration story.

**Organic Growth — 0.85 / 0.9.** Growth is organically built, not bought: £0 of acquisition spend, goodwill flat-to-falling as a share of assets, contracts won organically (Emirates NBD, new UK digital platform, AMEA/Europe wins, AEG/Box Office). No M&A fingerprint.

**Management Backing — 0.7 / 0.6.** Founder-CEO Alex Cheatle, long-tenured, communicates a clear digital roadmap. Backed by Singer (nominated adviser/broker). Founder conviction evident. Confidence 0.6 — thin on cultural/Glassdoor evidence.

**Incentive Alignment — 0.7 / 0.6.** Insider ownership ~29% (large personal capital at risk) and modest SBC (~£0.2m) point to alignment. But compensation-risk governance score 7/10 and board-risk 8/10 from Yahoo flag softness; confidence 0.6 pending the remuneration report.

**Customer Devotion — 0.6 / 0.55.** Strong B2B signal: 62% of members (H1) / 54% (FY25) say Ten is a strong-or-decisive factor in staying with the sponsoring brand — a genuine loyalty dividend. But stickiness is partly contractual (captive via client contracts), and there's no audited net-revenue-retention figure. Confidence 0.55.

**Why the other strategies lost.** *Wonderful-and-fair* (0.35): quality traits are real but modest — returns on capital average ≈0% over 4 years (latest ROIC 11.9%), thin reinvestment runway, and no long unbroken earnings record. *Cash-cow* (0.34): free-cash-flow is thin (FCF margin ~4–8%, capex eats ~70% of OCF into tech investment) and there's no dividend/buyback to return the cash — the opposite of a mature cow. *Freeroll* (0.23): no meaningful valuation floor (net tangible assets just £9.9m vs £91m market cap) and no time-bound re-rating catalyst — the stock already re-rated +65% in a year, so there's no cheapness left.

## Overall

Ten has real strengths: a defensible niche, genuine digital/AI re-acceleration (Active Members +23%, margin expansion, operating leverage), organic-only growth, no debt, and a founder-led aligned team. The detractors: the growth rate is still single-digit, free-cash-flow generation is thin (heavy tech reinvestment), returns on capital are low, and the stock has already re-rated sharply — so much of the digital-transformation upside is priced in. A credible small-cap turnaround-to-scale story, but not yet demonstrating durable compounding.

- **Dilution: +1.5%/yr** — reflects upcoming option/exercise creep and historical share issuance (FY25 issued stock; count rose over the window).

Result: **annualized ROI ≈ 3.0%** over 5 years. Attribution: business growth +8%, dilution −1.5pp, multiple re-rating −3.2pp, income 0 (no dividend). **The whole gain is growth; there is no margin of safety and no re-rating upside left.** The bottom-up cash return is unappealing at this price.

Agent score: **0.40.** A well-run, improving niche business — but at 94p the risk/reward is balanced-to-unfavourable: execution must convert digital engagement into durable revenue growth just to justify the multiple, and there is no floor if it stumbles. This is a quality story I respect but a price I would not chase.

## Thesis Connections

Ten fits a broader "loyalty/platform-as-a-service for affluent customer retention" theme — a structural shift by banks/insurers to keep high-value customers through experience, not just rates. It may be relevant to a thesis on financial-services customer-engagement vendors, but it is a single-company niche with no cross-listed peers in this book.

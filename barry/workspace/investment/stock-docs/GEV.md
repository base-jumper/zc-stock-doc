# GEV — GE Vernova Inc.

## Business Overview

GE Vernova is a global electric-power equipment and services company spun out of General Electric. It sells generation, grid, electrification, controls, and services products used to generate, transfer, convert, orchestrate, and store electricity. The company reports three segments:

- **Power:** gas, nuclear, hydro, steam, and related services. FY2025 revenue was **$19.8B**, adjusted EBITDA **$2.9B**, margin **14.7%**, and backlog **$94.4B**.
- **Wind:** onshore/offshore wind turbines, blades, and services. FY2025 revenue was **$9.1B**, adjusted EBITDA **-$598M**, and backlog **$21.6B**.
- **Electrification:** grid equipment, power conversion/storage, and software. FY2025 revenue was **$9.6B**, adjusted EBITDA **$1.4B**, margin **14.9%**, and backlog **$34.7B**.

The investment case is not “clean energy purity”; it is **electricity demand + grid bottlenecks + gas turbine scarcity + services monetisation**. GE Vernova says its technology helps generate about **25% of global electricity**, with roughly **7,000 gas turbines** and **59,000 wind turbines** installed. Q2 2026 strengthened the case: orders were **$24.2B** (+88% organic), revenue **$11.1B** (+12% organic), adjusted EBITDA **$1.25B**, free cash flow **$5.1B**, and backlog reached **$176B**. Gas Power backlog plus slot reservations rose to **116 GW**, while year-to-date data-centre Electrification orders exceeded **$5B**.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.90 / confidence 0.90

**Facts:** The power market is entering a structurally stronger phase driven by AI/data centres, industrialisation, electrification, energy security, and grid modernisation. GE Vernova’s 2025 annual report says electricity is about **20%** of final energy today and many project that share to rise **50% by 2050**. Q2 orders grew **88% organically**, backlog rose another **$13B sequentially** to **$176B**, and year-to-date data-centre-related Electrification orders exceeded **$5B**, more than double all of 2025. A June 2026 Chevron/Microsoft West Texas data-centre power project adds external evidence: the planned **2.67 GW** co-located facility expects a majority of generation from large GE Vernova turbines and associated electrical infrastructure, with first power targeted for 2028 subject to FID.

**Interpretation:** This is GEV’s strongest Rule Breaker trait. The setup is unusually good for a scaled industrial: long-cycle demand, constrained generation/grid supply, national-security relevance, and improving pricing. The caveat is that this remains capital equipment, so the cycle can eventually overbuild.

### Top Dog — 0.78 / confidence 0.80

**Facts:** GE Vernova has a large installed base, a global service footprint, and meaningful leadership in gas power and grid-adjacent electrification. The company says it helps generate roughly **25% of the world’s electricity**. Q2 2026 Gas Power equipment backlog plus slot reservations reached **116 GW**, and management raised its year-end expectation to at least **125 GW**. It is increasing annual gas-turbine output from 20 GW in Q3 2026 toward 24 GW in 2028 and 30 GW in 2030. Competitors remain formidable: Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Power, Hitachi Energy, Schneider Electric, Vestas, Siemens Gamesa, Nordex, Goldwind, Westinghouse, Framatome, and others.

**Interpretation:** GEV is clearly one of the top dogs in global power infrastructure, especially gas turbines and grid/electrification equipment. It is not a winner-takes-most platform; it is an oligopoly hardware/services business where several capable players can win.

**Firm-up:** Use future industry-share data or competitor order/backlog disclosures to test whether GEV is gaining, holding, or losing share in gas turbines and grid equipment.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.72 / confidence 0.75

**Facts:** Moat sources include installed base, long-term service agreements, engineering know-how, manufacturing capacity, utility relationships, data from deployed equipment, and an **$87B services RPO** in Q1 2026. The December 2025 investor update pointed to backlog margin improvement, better equipment pricing, and services revenue that should become more profitable in the 2030s.

**Interpretation:** The moat is real but industrial rather than magical. Installed base and service relationships are hard to replicate, and current turbine/grid scarcity gives pricing power. Confidence stays below 0.8 because we still need more direct disclosure on segment-level price/cost retention and incremental returns through the next capacity cycle.

**Firm-up:** Review future investor-day materials and 10-K segment notes for backlog margin, service margin, and pricing versus cost inflation.

### Evidence of Durable, Accelerating Growth — 0.86 / confidence 0.90

**Facts:** FY2025 revenue grew **9%**, adjusted EBITDA rose **57%** to **$3.2B**, and free cash flow more than doubled to **$3.7B**. Q2 2026 revenue grew **22% reported / 12% organic**, adjusted EBITDA rose to **$1.25B** from $770M, and FCF was **$5.1B**; first-half FCF reached **$9.9B**. Management raised 2026 guidance to **$45.5–46.5B revenue** and **$11.5–12.5B FCF**, while maintaining a **12–14% adjusted EBITDA margin** guide. Power organic-revenue guidance rose to **18–20%**. The 2028 outlook remains **$52B revenue**, **20% adjusted EBITDA margin**, and at least **$22B cumulative FCF from 2025–2028**, though that cumulative cash target now looks stale and should be revisited at the next investor update.

**Interpretation:** Growth quality is strong because orders/backlog are leading indicators and cash conversion is already visible. This is not software hypergrowth, but it is a credible industrial compounder with volume, price, margin, and service mix all working.

### Organic Growth over Acquisitive Growth — 0.78 / confidence 0.80

**Facts:** The current growth story is primarily backlog conversion, organic equipment demand, pricing, margin expansion, and services growth. Q2 2026 organic order growth was **88%**, and revenue grew **12% organically** despite Prolec GE increasing reported growth. The main acquisition item is Prolec GE, where GEV bought the remaining 50% stake in a core grid-equipment JV; 2026 Electrification guidance includes about **$3.1B** of Prolec revenue.

**Interpretation:** Growth is mostly organic and operating-led, with Prolec GE a strategic tuck-in/consolidation rather than a roll-up thesis. The score is not higher because the acquisition is material and mechanically boosts backlog/revenue, so future updates should keep separating organic growth from acquired contribution.

**Firm-up:** Track post-Prolec organic growth disclosures and any purchase-accounting/integration effects in 2026–2027 filings.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.68 / confidence 0.75

**Facts:** CEO Scott Strazik is not a founder, but management has executed the post-spin setup well: margin expansion, repeated guidance raises, backlog growth, disciplined underwriting language, investment-grade balance-sheet emphasis, buybacks, dividend increases, and core M&A. By Q2, GEV had returned **$3.9B** to shareholders year-to-date while retaining **$13.1B** of cash. Major institutional holders reported by Yahoo Finance include BlackRock, FMR, Vanguard, State Street, JPMorgan, Geode, Morgan Stanley, Vanguard Portfolio Management, Capital World, and Northern Trust.

**Interpretation:** Strong operator-management, but not a classic founder-led Rule Breaker team. Communication is long-term and mission-oriented enough to score above average; lack of founder economics and limited public standalone track record keep it below the top band.

**Firm-up:** More standalone evidence: 2026–2028 execution versus guidance, especially Wind discipline and capital allocation through a less euphoric market.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.72 / confidence 0.80

**Facts:** The 2026 proxy says the annual incentive plan uses **FCF 40% / adjusted EBITDA 40% / organic revenue growth 20%**, with safety/sustainability and individual modifiers. Long-term incentives use **3-year cumulative adjusted EBITDA 50% / 3-year cumulative FCF 50%**, with a **3-year relative TSR modifier**. Payouts are capped at **200%**, there are stock ownership guidelines, anti-hedging/anti-pledging rules, and a clawback policy. CEO and NEOs were compliant or expected to become compliant within the five-year guideline period.

**Interpretation:** Better-than-average alignment: FCF, organic growth, multi-year metrics, ownership rules, and clawbacks point management toward value creation. Weaknesses: adjusted EBITDA can still be gameable, ownership is largely granted rather than founder-bought, and the post-spin history is short.

**Firm-up:** Recheck the next proxy for realised pay versus FCF/share, organic growth, TSR, and any changes to adjusted-EBITDA definitions or ownership compliance.

### Customer Devotion — 0.50 / confidence 0.65

**Facts:** Customers are utilities, hyperscalers, governments, and industrial buyers purchasing mission-critical infrastructure. Evidence supports trust, necessity, and switching costs: backlog, long-term service agreements, utility relationships, and named demand from data-centre/grid customers. The Chevron/Microsoft West Texas announcement is a useful named-customer datapoint for AI-load gas power, though Chevron has not disclosed GE Vernova order value or whether all related equipment is already booked. There is no strong NPS/retention/voluntary-expansion metric comparable to software NRR.

**Interpretation:** GEV is respected and needed more than loved. Customers value reliability, service depth, capacity availability, and engineering competence. That creates stickiness, but much of it is lock-in/criticality rather than devotion.

**Firm-up:** Look for renewal rates, customer satisfaction metrics, named case studies, and service-contract expansion disclosures; absent that, this remains partly irreducible for a B2B infrastructure supplier.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Freeroll:** GEV does not have a hard downside floor near the current price. At **$980.03**, it trades around **31x** the conservative normalised FCF/share anchor used here. The pullback and Q2 upgrade improve expected return, but backlog conversion, guidance delivery and margin expansion are visible catalysts rather than a downside-protected special situation.
- **Wonderful and Fair:** GEV has a credible runway, strong balance sheet, and real pricing power, but it lacks the long standalone record of consistent, predictable earning power and high through-cycle returns that this strategy demands. It is becoming a wonderful business; it has not yet proven it across a full cycle.

## Overall

GE Vernova remains a **good but imperfect Rule Breaker / power-infrastructure compounder**. The industry setup is excellent, the backlog is real, cash flow is accelerating, and the company is one of the few scaled suppliers that can deliver into the AI/data-centre/grid/gas-turbine bottleneck.

The drawbacks are equally real: this is still a capital-equipment industrial, not an exponential software/network-effect business; Wind remains a drag; the moat depends partly on supply scarcity that can eventually normalize; and a large part of 2026 cash flow is working-capital benefit rather than steady-state earnings. The Q2 operating upgrade plus the price pullback lifts the valuation-aware score to **6.8/10**. Expected return now clears the 10% hurdle under a deliberately normalised cash-flow anchor, but execution and multiple risk remain substantial.

## Thesis Connections

- **AI infrastructure / data-centre power:** direct connection via gas turbines, transformers, grid equipment, substations, and electrification orders. Q1 2026 Electrification booked **$2.4B** of data-centre equipment orders.
- **Energy transition / electrification:** direct connection, with nuance: gas power is a reliability bridge, not pure renewable exposure.
- **Grid modernisation:** strongest long-duration FIT connection; this may be cleaner than the wind angle.
- **Power bottleneck:** direct beneficiary of electricity scarcity, grid capex, transformer/substation demand, and gas turbine supply constraints.

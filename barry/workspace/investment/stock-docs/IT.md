# IT — Gartner, Inc.

## Business Overview

Gartner sells decision support to enterprise executives. Its core Insights segment packages proprietary, analyst-led research, tools and advice into subscriptions that are generally paid in advance. Insights supplied 77% of Q2 2026 revenue and carried a 77.5% contribution margin. Conferences and Consulting extend the research franchise but are smaller and less predictable; Q2 revenue grew 15.5% and fell 8.8%, respectively.

The economic engine is the combination of reusable intellectual property, a large expert base, a global enterprise salesforce and customer switching costs created by Gartner's role in recurring, mission-critical technology and business decisions. Contract value (CV) is the leading indicator because it annualizes active subscription contracts. At Q2 2026 CV was $5.28 billion, up 1.7% year on year after growing 7–16% in 2021–24. This is now a mature, slow-growing subscription franchise rather than an emerging growth business.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.78, confidence 0.90

Gartner has remained profitable throughout the 2014–26 record, including 2020. Reported net income is lumpy because of the 2017 CEB integration, asset sales, tax items and a $150 million 2025 Digital Markets impairment, but the underlying subscription economics are steadier: revenue rose from $2.44 billion in 2016 to $6.50 billion in 2025, while operating margin expanded from 12.5% to 15.8%. Cumulative 2022–25 FCF was 1.25 times cumulative net income, receivables fell from 28.4% to 25.9% of revenue, and LTM FCF conversion was 166% of GAAP net income at Q2 2026. Stock compensation is real—$177 million is guided for 2026—and is retained in the owner-earnings assessment.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.90, confidence 0.95

The business is exceptionally cash generative. LTM FCF was $1.289 billion, or 19.9% of revenue, with capex only 1.4% of revenue. Advance customer payments and low physical capital requirements create favourable working-capital economics. Management's 2026 guidance is at least $1.185 billion of FCF; the lower figure partly reflects the divestiture and normalization from prior tax/working-capital benefits rather than a collapse in core cash generation.

### Fundamental Stability — 0.72, confidence 0.80

Fundamentals are durable but currently soft. Q2 CV growth accelerated to 1.7% from 0.8% at FY2025, excluding U.S. federal government business it was 3.3%, and Insights margins expanded. Against that, global client enterprises declined from 13,374 to 12,775 year on year, wallet retention slipped below 100%, and quota-bearing headcount fell 3.2%. AI can increase Gartner's value by making its research easier to query, but it can also commoditize some information access. No secular decline is evident yet; persistent sub-100% wallet retention and client losses would change that verdict. Confidence is capped at 0.80 because the AI substitution question is irreducible for now; renewal, wallet-retention and AskGartner adoption evidence over the next several quarters will resolve it.

### Pricing Power — 0.75, confidence 0.85

Gartner's moat comes from trusted brand, proprietary research, breadth of expert coverage and the switching/risk cost of replacing a standard decision framework. Gross margin has held near 68–70% since 2021 and Insights contribution margin reached 77.5% in Q2 2026. CV per enterprise rose from $371,000 in Q1 2025 to $414,000 in Q2 2026 even as client count fell, demonstrating higher spend per remaining client. The moat is stable rather than widening: global wallet retention was only 98.2%, versus 103.0% a year earlier, so price and expansion are no longer fully offsetting client budget pressure.

### Capital Allocation — 0.62, confidence 0.90

Gartner has reduced diluted shares from 91.0 million in 2019 to 66.6 million in Q2 2026, and to 63.1 million outstanding by July 31. The 3.6 million Q2 shares were repurchased at an average $148.93, well below the current price and likely highly accretive. The blemish is material: Gartner spent $1.99 billion on repurchases in 2025, when the stock traded at substantially richer valuations, and partly replenished cash with debt. It also impaired Digital Markets by $150 million before selling the business in February 2026. The long-run share shrinkage is valuable, but historical purchase discipline was uneven.

### Returns on Capital — 0.82, confidence 0.85

ROIC is the appropriate primary measure because ROE is distorted by buybacks and negative tangible equity. Reported ROIC was 25–36% in 2022–25, comfortably above a reasonable high-single-digit cost of capital. High goodwill—83% of invested capital at FY2025—and the 2025 Digital Markets impairment muddy acquisition-adjusted returns, while the latest calculated incremental ROIC is negative because invested capital and reported NOPAT moved sharply around buybacks and impairments. The underlying evidence remains strong: low capex, 20% FCF margins and a 77.5% Insights contribution margin. Recent returns are high but should not be read from the inflated ROE.

### Conservative Debt — 0.78, confidence 0.95

At Q2 2026 Gartner held $1.49 billion of cash against $3.0 billion of fixed-rate debt. Gross debt/adjusted EBITDA was 1.8 times, net debt/adjusted EBITDA 0.9 times, and the credit agreement leverage ratio was 1.6 times versus a 4.0-times covenant. All bonds are fixed-rate, investment grade and staggered from 2028 to 2035; the undrawn revolver provides another $1.0 billion. Negative book equity reflects treasury stock, not operating insolvency. The balance sheet is resilient, though debt-funded repurchases prevent a top score.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.62, confidence 0.75

Gene Hall has been CEO since 2004 and remains both chairman and chief executive, creating succession risk after more than two decades of strategy and operating-model ownership. Gartner's research, sales and subscription systems are institutionalized and the executive bench is established, so his departure should not break the franchise. Confidence would improve with a disclosed CEO succession plan or an orderly leadership transition; neither is presently available in the 2026 proxy.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.78, confidence 0.85

Compensation is anchored to CV, revenue and EBITDA; long-term awards vest over four years, executives face ownership requirements (six times salary for the CEO, three times for other officers), and all named executives complied at FY2025. The framework rewards the leading subscription metric and profitable growth. It is not perfect: CV can reward growth without explicitly charging for capital, CEO compensation was $19.2 million in 2025, and the board combined chair/CEO role weakens oversight.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.62, confidence 0.75

Gartner can still reinvest in experts, AI-enabled delivery and sales capacity across additional corporate functions, and its capital-light model makes the financial hurdle low. However, the observed runway has narrowed: global CV growth is 1.7%, client enterprises are declining, quota-bearing headcount is shrinking, and repurchases have exceeded FCF. That behaviour is rational if internal opportunities cannot absorb the surplus at historical returns, but it makes Gartner a cash cow first and a compounder second. Confidence would improve with sustained mid-single-digit new-contract-value growth and evidence that new sales hiring again produces attractive incremental CV; the next four quarterly supplements provide that source.

### Strategy selection

This is a genuine dual fit. Cash-cow wins narrowly because Gartner's cash generation and capital returns are stronger than its present reinvestment evidence. Wonderful-and-fair remains valid because earnings quality, moat and returns on capital are strong, but its reinvestment runway scores only 0.62 as client count contracts and surplus cash is overwhelmingly returned through buybacks. Rule-breakers fails its durable-growth gate—CV growth is 1.7%, not high or accelerating enough for an emerging-growth strategy. Freeroll lacks a hard asset/income floor and a specific forced re-rating catalyst; low valuation, buybacks and a possible CV recovery are not enough by themselves.

## Overall

Gartner is a high-quality, capital-light franchise temporarily growing below its historical rate. The core subscription moat, cash conversion and opportunistic 2026 repurchases are compelling. The deductions are a shrinking client base, wallet retention below 100%, uncertain AI substitution risk, imperfect historical buyback timing and dependence on a long-tenured CEO. This is investable at the current valuation, but the thesis now requires stable retention and at least modest CV reacceleration rather than relying on the old double-digit growth record.

## Valuation

The exit-multiple model uses FCF because cash conversion and buybacks drive the case. The $19.58 current FCF/share anchor averages Q2 LTM FCF of $1.289 billion with 2026 guidance of at least $1.185 billion, divided by the 63.15 million shares outstanding at July 31. This normalizes the unusually rapid recent reduction in shares without treating either the trailing peak or guidance floor as fully representative.

The five-year path assumes aggregate FCF growth of 3%, 4%, 5%, 6% and 6% as CV gradually recovers, and share shrinkage fading from 6% to 3% as excess cash is deployed and the price normalizes. A 12-times terminal P/FCF is above the current 9.56-times implied multiple but below Gartner's historical quality-business valuation; this introduces some re-rating dependence, so the result should be treated as a base case rather than a floor. No dividend is assumed. The model produces a 14.47% annualized ROI and a 1.97-times five-year return: 4.79 points from business growth, 4.39 points from buybacks and 4.64 points from multiple normalization. The roughly one-third reliance on re-rating is the main valuation weakness. The agent score is 0.76: attractive visible return potential and franchise quality, tempered by the low-growth/retention evidence and capital-allocation history.

## Thesis Connections

No existing FIT connection was identified. The relevant prospective thesis is enterprise decision infrastructure: organizations facing faster technology change may place greater value on trusted synthesis even as AI makes raw information cheaper.

## Watch

- Global CV growth: require continued sequential improvement toward at least mid-single digits; a return to contraction would weaken the thesis.
- GTS and GBS wallet retention: recovery above 100% is the clearest proof that pricing and expansion again exceed downsell.
- Client enterprises and quota-bearing headcount: determine whether current declines are deliberate productivity gains or franchise shrinkage.
- AskGartner adoption and evidence that generative AI increases engagement rather than substitutes for subscriptions.
- Repurchase prices, total dollars and leverage: confirm future buybacks remain FCF-funded and valuation-sensitive.
- CEO succession disclosure or a leadership transition plan.

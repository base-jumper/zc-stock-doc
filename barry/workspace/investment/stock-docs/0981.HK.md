# 0981.HK — Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corporation (SMIC)

## Business Overview

SMIC is China's largest and most advanced semiconductor foundry, manufacturing integrated circuits on 8-inch and 12-inch wafers for fabless and IDM customers. It competes with TSMC, Samsung Foundry, GlobalFoundries, UMC and Hua Hong, differentiated as the mainland's only credible advanced-node (14nm and below) manufacturer, and is the strategic linchpin of China's chip-localization push. Revenue is wafer sales and related photomask/assembly-test services.

FY2025 (ended 31 Dec 2025) revenue was **US$9.33b**, up 16.2% (from US$8.03b in 2024), gross margin **21.0%** (from 18.0%), and profit attributable to owners **US$685m**, up 39%. Growth and profitability then accelerated sharply in 2026: Q2 (ended 30 Jun 2026) revenue was **US$3.01b** (+20% QoQ, +36% YoY — a record), gross margin **25.3%** (from 20.1% QoQ), operating profit **US$534m** (more than double QoQ), and profit attributable to owners **US$479m** (up 262% YoY, helped by a one-time gain from a subsidiary). Wafer shipments were 2.87m 8-inch-equivalent units (+20% YoY), average wafer price rose 5.7% QoQ, and utilization reached **93.7%**. Q3 guidance: revenue +2–4% QoQ and gross margin 26–28%. Capital expenditure was US$3.4b in 1H26 (US$1.84b in Q2). As of June 2026, TTM revenue was about US$10.4b (+17.5% YoY). China is now 90.2% of revenue (up from 84.1% a year ago); consumer electronics is the largest application at 44.2%.

Current Yahoo snapshot (20 Aug 2026): **HK$72.1**/share, market cap ~HK$617b (~US$79b), trailing P/E ~102, forward P/E ~41, P/B ~3.6, P/S ~10, EV/EBITDA ~81. 52-week range HK$49.3–93.5. The stock trades on HKD while financials report in USD.

## Trait Assessment

*Winning strategy: rule-breakers (score 0.693, confidence 0.723).*

### Right Place and Time — score 0.85, confidence 0.85

The industry setup is genuinely exceptional: AI infrastructure buildout is driving broad foundry demand, and China's domestic semiconductor localization push (import substitution, Big Fund / National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund backing) gives SMIC a structural, policy-driven demand wave independent of the global cycle. Semiconductors are the arena of the US–China tech competition, and SMIC is the only mainland foundry that can produce advanced-node chips — a strategically protected, underpenetrated, large market.

The mark-down: foundry is a brutally competitive, capital-intensive, lower-margin manufacturing business; cycle risk is real (2023 revenue fell 13%); and the geopolitical overhang (export controls, equipment restrictions) cuts both ways. Confidence is high — the demand and policy drivers are well documented and current.

### Top Dog — score 0.68, confidence 0.70

SMIC is the #1 foundry in mainland China by revenue and capacity and the #5 foundry globally (behind TSMC, Samsung, GlobalFoundries, UMC), but it is not the global leader and its advanced-node capability remains behind TSMC. Its leadership is within a protected national ecosystem: it is the foundry of choice for Chinese fabless (design houses), and it gains share as China's localization accelerates — Q2 revenue rose 36% YoY while China mix grew to 90.2%. Domestically it is the clear top dog; globally, a strong regional contender. The lead is widening within China, which is the relevant competitive arena for the thesis.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — score 0.62, confidence 0.70

The moat is real but partial. On the plus side: enormous capital intensity and scale (a foundry requires tens of billions in capex and a long qualification cycle; SMIC's 2025 capex ~US$7–8b and 1H26 ~US$3.4b), technical know-how and IP in advanced and mature nodes, customer switching costs from long design-in/qualification cycles, and government/strategic backing that makes replication by rivals hard. On the minus side: equipment and technology restrictions constrain leading-edge progress; the advantage is tied to a protected domestic market, not global technology leadership; and mature-node competition (UMC, Hua Hong, GlobalFoundries) is intense. Gross margin improving to 25.3% reflects real pricing power in tight capacity, but it is well below TSMC's ~50%+.

### Durable Growth — score 0.78, confidence 0.80

Growth is strong and accelerating, not decelerating. Revenue CAGR: +39% (2021), +34% (2022), −13% (2023), +27% (2024), +16% (2025), and the 2026 trajectory is re-accelerating: Q2 +36% YoY with TTM +17.5%. Utilization at 93.7% with pricing power (wafer ASP +5.7% QoQ) shows the growth is real demand, not just volume. The reinvestment carve-out is genuine: profit is deliberately suppressed by huge depreciation and capex, but gross margin is expanding (18% → 21% → 25.3%), utilization is high, and operating leverage is turning — the definition of disciplined reinvestment with improving economics. This is an Amazon-style suppress-profit-to-build-leadership pattern.

Mark-down: net profit is still thin (net margin ~7%, ROE ~3.5%) and growth is lumpy/cyclical; the 2023 dip shows it is not monotonic.

### Organic Growth — score 0.85, confidence 0.80

Growth is overwhelmingly organic: expanding fab capacity, ramping utilization, raising wafer prices on existing lines, and serving growing domestic demand — not acquisitions. SMIC builds its own capacity (monthly capacity ~1.1m 8-inch-equivalent wafers, adding ~8k/month in Q2). No reliance on M&A to manufacture growth. Confidence is high — the capacity-and-utilisation model is disclosed quarter by quarter.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — score 0.62, confidence 0.65

Management (co-CEOs Zhao Haijun and Gao Yonggang, CFO Wu Junfeng) has executed a difficult strategic mission — keeping SMIC operational and advancing nodes under US equipment sanctions — and communicates clearly (Q2 2026: explained pricing negotiations, capacity reallocation, AI-demand spillover). Backing is exceptional at the national level: the Big National Fund (Big Fund) is a major shareholder (it became SMIC's third-largest shareholder in 2026 after a record Star Market buyout), and the company is a pillar of the state semiconductor strategy.

Mark-down: management credibility is hard for outsiders to verify; insider ownership is modest (insiders ~18% via state structures, not founder equity), and execution quality is obscured by state coordination. Confidence is moderate — the track record is real but the decision-making is not fully observable.

### Incentive Alignment — score 0.45, confidence 0.55

Mixed. Management is not primarily wealth-incentivized toward per-share value: compensation is state-sector-style, aligned with national industrial goals (capacity, localization) rather than pure shareholder value; equity/insider ownership is small on a personal basis; and capital raising/dilution has been ongoing (Star Market listing, follow-ons) — Yahoo shows insiders held ~18% but this is largely state and institutional, not management skin-in-the-game. No egregious self-dealing is apparent, and executive pay is modest by global standards, but the owner-compact is weak: shareholders are along for a national mission, and returns on capital (ROIC ~3.9%, ROE ~3.2%) show value isn't being optimized for minority owners. Confidence is moderate — remuneration-report detail needs deeper review.

### Customer Devotion — score 0.55, confidence 0.60

B2B devotion is real but capacity-driven rather than preference-driven. Customers (Chinese design houses, IDMs) come to SMIC because it is the only advanced-node option domestically — that is captivity/capacity constraint as much as love. Positive signals: full utilization, pricing increases accepted by customers in Q3, long-term qualification relationships, and pre-booked orders ahead of schedule. But retention is largely forced by the lack of alternatives (the counterfactual "would they stay if switching were easy" fails), and customer concentration by nation (China 90%) adds risk. This scores "respected and necessary, not loved."

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair and cash-cow fail because SMIC has thin, volatile earnings quality (net margin ~7%, ROIC ~4%), deeply negative free cash flow (structural capex), and no high durable returns on capital or dependable cash generation. Freeroll fails because there is no valuation floor near the current price (P/B 3.6, no hard asset floor at 72x P/E) and no clean time-bound re-rating catalyst beyond sentiment — the mispricing is not a distinct, identifiable edge. Rule-breakers is the only coherent frame: a strategic top-dog in a massive, policy-accelerated industry wave, compounding through reinvestment.

## Overall

SMIC is a high-quality rule-breaker in business terms but an expensive one in price terms. The strengths are real: the only advanced foundry in China, a national-strategic imperative behind it, accelerating AI-driven demand (Q2 revenue +36% YoY, gross margin 25.3%, utilization 93.7%), and a disciplined reinvestment story (suppressed profit, expanding margins, massive capex buying the future). The detractors are equally real: geopolitical/export-control risk on equipment and advanced nodes, brutal industry economics (low margins at scale, cyclicality), weak minority-shareholder alignment (value captured by a state mission more than by owners), and a stock price that already reflects much of the success (trailing P/E ~102, P/S ~10, forward P/E ~41).

The quality score is solid (0.693), but the ROI is negative at today's price, which drives the overall score to zero on the research/allocations axes. This is a business to own cheap in a cyclical downturn, not to chase at 72x trailing earnings.

## Thesis Connections

China semiconductor localization / self-sufficiency (FIT). SMIC is the indispensable domestic foundry; the thesis is that China's AI and chip buildout is a multi-year, policy-backed supercycle that SMIC captures as the only advanced-node national champion. Exposure is real and strategically important, but for a minority shareholder the value capture is diluted by state ownership and capital-intensity, and the entry price matters enormously.

## Watch

- **Export controls / equipment restrictions** — any US/EU tightening on advanced-node manufacturing equipment (EUV, DUV, tools) directly caps SMIC's technology roadmap; any easing is a re-rating catalyst.
- **Quarterly trajectory** — Q3 2026 guidance (+2–4% QoQ, 26–28% GM) and whether AI-driven orders keep utilization >90% and pricing rising into 2027.
- **Balance-sheet check** — capex (1H26 US$3.4b) vs operating cash flow, total debt (US$12.6b at 2025 YE; ~US$14.0b TTM) and net cash trajectory; FCF stays deeply negative.
- **Advanced-node roadmap** — N+2/7nm-class yield and revenue progress, Huawei-style domestic customers, and any credible sign of leading-edge capability improvement (or stall) under sanctions.
- **Minority-shareholder treatment** — Big Fund ownership shifts, follow-on issuance (dilution), related-party transactions, and whether state priorities override minority value.
- **Valuation** — current P/E ~102 / forward ~41; any normalization toward the high-teens/high-twenties multiple, or a cyclical downturn that drops the price to a P/E where the business case clears (the entry opportunity).
- **Street's forward estimates** — consensus +1y earnings growth ~23% and revenue growth ~22% (as of Aug 2026); watch whether actuals confirm or diverge.

# SEB-A.ST — Skandinaviska Enskilda Banken AB

## Business Overview

SEB is a Swedish universal bank with a corporate-heavy franchise. It earns mainly from lending and deposits, payments and securities services, investment banking, wealth and asset management, and Baltic banking. In H1 2026 Investment Banking generated 39% of group operating profit, Business & Retail Banking 23%, Wealth & Asset Management 14%, and Baltic banking 18%. The model combines rate-sensitive net interest income with fee income, while credit losses and operating costs are the main profit offsets.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.72 (confidence 0.80)

SEB remained profitable across the available 2022–2025 history: net income was SEK 26.9bn, 38.1bn, 35.9bn and 31.1bn. The path is cyclical rather than smooth because rates and credit costs matter, but Q2 2026 net profit rose 16% sequentially to SEK 8.7bn and H1 profit was stable year-on-year at SEK 16.2bn. Conventional operating cash flow is not a useful cash-conversion test for a deposit-taking bank; capital generation, credit losses and distributions are more decision-useful. Confidence would improve with a ten-year profit and credit-loss series from annual reports.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.78 (confidence 0.65)

For a bank, customer deposits, loans and wholesale funding make reported FCF structurally misleading. The economic analogue is profit remaining after credit losses and required regulatory capital. SEB produced SEK 16.2bn H1 net profit, held a 17.2% CET1 ratio and a 250bp management buffer, while its policy is to distribute about 50% of annual EPS and return surplus capital mainly through buybacks. This supports strong distributable cash generation, but the rubric mismatch keeps confidence low. A through-cycle organic capital-generation bridge would firm it up.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.82 (confidence 0.80)

The franchise is diversified across Nordic corporate banking, Swedish households, wealth management and the Baltics. H1 2026 operating profit was broadly flat despite net interest income falling 3%; Q2 fee income and lending growth offset part of the pressure. Credit quality remained robust: Q2 expected credit losses were five basis points and H1 six basis points. Structural threats are mortgage competition, rate normalisation, regulation and cyclicality in large-corporate demand, not an evident demand collapse.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.68 (confidence 0.70)

SEB has scale, licences, institutional relationships and switching costs, particularly with Nordic corporates and private-wealth clients. Those advantages support fee franchises and low credit losses, but pricing power is not complete: Swedish mortgage margins remained at historically low levels amid intense competition, despite SEB holding a 13% mortgage share. The moat appears stable rather than widening. Segment margin and market-share histories would improve confidence.

### Capital Allocation — 0.78 (confidence 0.75)

The bank targets a dividend near 50% of EPS and uses buybacks for surplus capital. It paid SEK 23.0bn of dividends and repurchased SEK 10.4bn of shares in 2025; ordinary shares fell from 2.11bn at end-2022 to 1.96bn at end-2025. The 2026 dividend was SEK 11.00 per share. The strong capital buffer supports these returns without weakening solvency, although repurchases near the current 1.95x book valuation are less compelling than those made cheaply. A price-weighted buyback history would firm up the value-creation verdict.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.72 (confidence 0.85)

ROE/ROTE is the relevant measure because capital is a bank's operating input. Reported ROE was 13.2% in 2022, 17.2% in 2023, 15.5% in 2024 and 13.5% in 2025; Q2 2026 improved to 15.7%. These are generally solid and the latest result clears the rubric's roughly 15% strong-bank anchor, but returns are rate-sensitive and not consistently above it.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.82 (confidence 0.85)

Ordinary debt ratios are inappropriate for a bank. SEB's CET1 ratio was 17.2% at 30 June 2026, 250bp above its regulatory requirement, while the liquidity coverage ratio was 144%. Moody's upgraded the senior unsecured rating to Aa3 in the quarter. These indicators, alongside very low current credit losses, show resilience; commercial-property and large-corporate concentrations remain the main cycle risks.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.80 (confidence 0.65)

SEB's advantage is institutional—licences, balance sheet, relationships, systems and brand—rather than dependent on CEO Johan Torgeby. A regulated group structure and broad divisional leadership reduce single-person fragility. Confidence is limited because this review did not test the formal succession plan and executive-bench disclosures; the next annual governance report would firm it up.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.68 (confidence 0.55)

Share-count contraction and management's explicit ROE, capital-buffer and payout ambitions point toward per-share discipline. However, this pass did not fully audit the 2025 remuneration report's scorecard, deferral, clawback, ownership requirements and executive transactions. The direction looks sensible but the evidence is incomplete; those named disclosures are the firm-up source.

Cash Cow wins because SEB is mature, profitable, well-capitalised and explicitly returns surplus capital. Wonderful-and-Fair loses on limited high-return reinvestment runway; Rule Breakers loses on mature industry growth; Freeroll lacks a depressed valuation and time-bound catalyst at a 52-week-high price.

## Overall

SEB is a high-quality Nordic bank with strong capital, low current credit losses, a useful corporate franchise and disciplined distributions. The detractors are rate and credit cyclicality, weak mortgage pricing power, an elevated 1.95x book valuation, and incomplete confidence on incentives and through-cycle distributable capital. It fits as an income-and-buyback cash cow, but the current price leaves little room for operational disappointment.

## Valuation

At SEK 215.30 on 18 July 2026, the shares trade at 13.2x the SEK 16.37 current-year earnings estimate and 1.95x book. The exit-multiple model uses earnings because bank cash flow is not economically comparable with industrial FCF: five years, 3% aggregate earnings growth, 2% annual net buybacks, a 12x exit P/E and a 5.11% dividend yield (SEK 11.00 / price). It produces an 8.76% annualised ROI: approximately 3.00% from business growth, 2.04% from buybacks and 5.56% from income, offset by 1.82% annualised multiple compression. Agent score: 0.62—good business and capital return, only fair value at today's near-high price.

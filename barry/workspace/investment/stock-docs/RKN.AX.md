# RKN.AX — Reckon Limited

## Business Overview

Reckon is an ASX-listed small-cap software company serving two markets. The Business Group sells SME accounting/payroll and related tools including Reckon Accounts, Reckon Accounts Hosted, Reckon One and Cashflow Manager. The Legal Group, trading as Zebraworks / nQ Zebraworks, sells cost recovery, cost management, scan and cloud workflow tools mainly to legal firms.

The current thesis is a cheap software special situation, not a clean long-duration compounder. FY2025 revenue was A$62.4m, split A$48.6m Business Group and A$13.8m Legal Group. Business Group is the cash engine; Legal Group is the option if Billing Workflows scales in US/UK law firms. Management is explicitly trying to migrate legacy Intuit-code-based customers to Reckon One through 2026-2027 while continuing to invest in cloud products.

## Trait Assessment

### Downside Support / Valuation Floor

Score: 0.55 | Confidence: 0.72.

The floor is valuation/yield support rather than asset value. At A$0.44/share on 20 Jul 2026, Reckon’s market cap was ~A$49.8m and EV ~A$58.1m. FY2025 operating cash flow was A$23.8m, but this overstates economics because Reckon capitalised A$15.1m of development spend; management itself reports operating cash flow after development spend of A$8.7m. A conservative floor is ~A$0.31/share: roughly 5x A$8.7m economic FCF less ~A$8.4m net debt, divided by ~113.3m shares. That is ~30% below the current price.

The latest A$0.025 fully franked dividend also supports the price: at A$0.44 it yields ~5.7%, with Yahoo Finance showing a ~38% earnings payout. The floor is real but not hard: it depends on the dividend staying covered and on capitalised development creating durable product value. The low current ratio and net debt make this weaker than a cash/NAV-backed floor.

### Fundamental Stability / No Value Trap

Score: 0.68 | Confidence: 0.78.

FY2025 showed stabilisation rather than deterioration. Revenue rose 15% to A$62.4m, or 4% excluding Cashflow Manager; NPAT rose to A$7.0m; management reported operating cash flow after development spend of A$8.7m versus A$3.4m in FY2024. Business Group cloud subscription revenue rose 8% to A$27.6m, Reckon One revenue rose 27% to A$8.9m, and Reckon One ARPU rose 29% while user numbers were stable.

The value-trap risk is still material. The core issue is structural migration from legacy products to Reckon One, not a simple cyclical trough. The transition has evidence of progress, but user-count disclosure, churn and legacy mix remain thin.

### Mispriced for an Identifiable Reason

Score: 0.70 | Confidence: 0.72.

The market appears to be pricing Reckon as a small, illiquid, ex-growth legacy software vendor with questionable cash conversion because development spend is capitalised; no material operating update was found through 20 Jul 2026 to change that interpretation. That concern is legitimate, but the stock remains cheap enough to create possible mispricing: trailing PE ~7.3x, EV/EBITDA ~5.5x, PS ~0.80x, and dividend yield ~5.7% using 20 Jul 2026 Yahoo Finance data.

The differentiated view is that headline FCF is overstated, but economic FCF is still meaningful and the market may be giving little credit for the Reckon One migration, Cashflow Manager integration, Billing Workflows growth, and a covered dividend. This is a neglected microcap setup rather than a clean informational edge.

### Identifiable, Time-Bound Catalyst

Score: 0.58 | Confidence: 0.65.

The catalyst is plausible but not forceful: evidence through FY2026-FY2027 that legacy Business Group customers are migrating to Reckon One without revenue leakage, while Legal Group’s Billing Workflows keeps scaling. Management says the Business Group focus is accelerating legacy migration through 2026 into 2027; Legal Group expects Billing Workflows to drive further growth, with contracted installations expected to lift attorneys using Billing Workflows to ~5,000 versus 1,600 at end-FY2024.

Other potential re-rating triggers are continued A$0.025 dividends, net debt reduction after the Cashflow Manager acquisition, cleaner disclosure, and any corporate transaction. The problem: none are hard catalysts. This is a two-year evidence catalyst, not an announced transaction.

### No Thesis-Breaking Disqualifiers

Score: 0.78 | Confidence: 0.72.

No thesis-breaking disqualifier identified as of 2026-07-20. The main concerns are not ignored: current ratio ~0.30, net debt, thin liquidity, high capitalised development spend, and legacy-product transition risk. These are serious monitoring items, but interest cover is high, StockAnalysis shows debt/EBITDA below 1x, the dividend appears covered on earnings, and management reported unused bank facilities of A$18.0m at FY2025 balance date.

## Overall

Reckon remains an interesting but imperfect freeroll. The stock is still cheap on ordinary multiples and after correcting for capitalised development, but it has risen since the last review and the downside floor is not hard enough to make this a slam dunk. The setup works if Reckon One migration and Billing Workflows growth prove that FY2025 was an inflection rather than a one-off acquisition-assisted year.

Current view: keep on watch / potentially small-size only. The judgement score falls because the price has moved farther from the floor without new operating evidence: the dividend helps and the software revenue is sticky, but the capital-only expected return is now modest, the catalyst is soft, and the floor could erode if legacy migration disappoints.

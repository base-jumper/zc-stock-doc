# SN — SharkNinja, Inc.

## Business Overview

SharkNinja is a global small-household-appliance company built around two core brands: **Shark** for cleaning/home environment and **Ninja** for kitchen, food prep, beverage and adjacent lifestyle categories. The company describes itself as a product-design and technology business rather than a traditional appliance roll-up: consumer insight, rapid engineering cycles, IP, retail/channel execution and value-for-money positioning are the operating model.

Q2 2026 materially strengthened the operating case. Net sales rose **22.2%** to **$1.766B**, the fastest growth since 2024, with Domestic up **15.5%** and International up **36.6%**. Adjusted EBITDA rose **18.6%** to **$264.9M** and adjusted EPS rose **29.9%** to **$1.26**, although GAAP net income fell 7.0% because share-based compensation and other adjustments widened. Management raised FY2026 guidance to **16–17%** net sales growth, **$6.45–6.55** adjusted EPS, and **$1.357–1.369B** adjusted EBITDA; about $0.15 of the EPS increase reflects expected net tariff-refund benefit.

The balance sheet remains sound at **$779.8M** cash versus **$718.9M** debt. First-half operating cash flow improved to **$275.5M** from a **$63.9M outflow**, and the company repurchased 1.01M shares for $119.7M. The important caveats are that gross margin slipped 30 bps to 48.7%, unresolved material weaknesses still make disclosure controls ineffective, and the stock has rerated to **$185.52**.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.74 / confidence 0.80

**Facts:** Small domestic appliances are a large, still-growing global market. External market estimates put the 2026 market above **$200B** with mid-single-digit long-term growth, while SharkNinja is compounding materially faster than category growth. International net sales accelerated to **36.6%** growth in Q2 2026 and reached 35.3% of sales, versus 31.6% a year earlier.

**Interpretation:** This is a good, not perfect, Rule Breaker setup. The opportunity is large and global, and SharkNinja’s innovation cadence is taking share across multiple everyday-use categories. The limitation is that small appliances are not a new platform market with network effects; it is a competitive consumer-products arena where winners must keep launching hits. Confidence would firm up with a current third-party category-share dataset by region and product category; add this to the Watch list.

### Top Dog — 0.80 / confidence 0.80

**Facts:** SharkNinja reports two billion-dollar global brands and says it has leadership positions/market-share gains across cleaning, cooking and beverage, food prep, beauty and home environment. In the 2026 proxy, management says SharkNinja reached **#1 U.S. market share in small household appliances** and **#1 U.K. market share in vacuum sales**. FY2025 revenue of **$6.399B** makes it a scaled global player, not a niche challenger.

**Interpretation:** SharkNinja looks like a genuine top dog in several subcategories and a fast share-gainer across the broader category. It is not a monopoly: competitors include Dyson, iRobot/Ecovacs, Bissell, Tineco, Instant Brands, Breville, De'Longhi, Philips, Panasonic, Samsung, LG, Whirlpool and retailer private labels. The score is high because the company has scale plus brand heat, but capped because leadership is category-by-category rather than winner-takes-most. Confidence would improve with independent market-share trend data rather than mainly company/proxy statements.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.64 / confidence 0.76

**Facts:** Moat sources include brand trust, retailer relationships, product-engineering speed, consumer-insight loops, patents/IP, supply-chain capability and a portfolio that lets successful design language migrate across categories. Q2 growth was broad—Cleaning +4.1%, Cooking/Beverage +36.5%, Food Preparation +13.3% and Beauty/Home Environment +65.3%—but gross margin slipped 30 bps to **48.7%** under tariff, FX and retailer-activation pressure.

**Interpretation:** The advantage is real but execution-dependent. SharkNinja’s edge is repeatable innovation and brand/channel muscle, not a hard lock-in moat. Consumers can switch, retailers can promote alternatives, and copycat products can narrow gaps. Sustaining the advantage requires consistently launching better products faster than competitors. Confidence is capped until we have cleaner evidence of pricing power and repeat purchase/brand-loyalty durability across categories.

### Evidence of Durable, Accelerating Growth — 0.88 / confidence 0.91

**Facts:** Q1 2026 was SharkNinja’s **12th consecutive quarter of double-digit organic net sales growth**, and Q2 accelerated further: net sales +22.2%, adjusted EPS +29.9% and adjusted EBITDA +18.6%. Management raised FY2026 guidance to 16–17% sales growth and $6.45–6.55 adjusted EPS, including about $0.15 of net tariff-refund benefit. First-half operating cash flow improved to $275.5M from a $63.9M outflow, while inventory grew 14.1% from year-end—below first-half sales growth of 19.1%.

**Interpretation:** Growth quality is the strongest part of the thesis. The company is growing revenue faster than the market, expanding internationally, and converting growth into higher adjusted earnings and cash generation. The main caveat is durability: product-hit cycles can fade, and consumer discretionary demand can slow.

### Organic Growth over Acquisitive Growth — 0.90 / confidence 0.86

**Facts:** The FY2025 10-K says SharkNinja has historically grown through **organic market share gains, geographic expansion, technological innovation, new products and increased demand**. Q2's 22.2% increase came from existing and new product categories across every reporting category and geography, with no material acquisition contribution disclosed.

**Interpretation:** This is a strong positive. SharkNinja’s growth engine appears built around product launches, geographic expansion and share gains rather than buying revenue. The score is not 1.0 because future acquisitions remain part of management’s toolkit and the public-company history is still short.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.75 / confidence 0.82

**Facts:** Mark Barrocas has served as President since **2008**, CEO/director since **2023**, was co-owner from 2008–2017, and was recognized by the company as a co-refounder. The 2026 proxy credits Barrocas with leading the company from a modest appliance business into a **$6.4B** revenue enterprise across **38 international markets** and **38 product categories**. Chair CJ Xuning Wang remains a major backer.

**Interpretation:** This is unusually founder/refounder-like for a public consumer-products company. The leadership team has a long operating record with the business and major insider-aligned backing. The score is not higher because control is concentrated, the public-company track record is relatively short post-listing, and external employee-review/culture signals are not yet strong enough to call it exceptional. Confidence would improve with more public-company earnings-call history through a downturn.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.64 / confidence 0.82

**Facts:** The 2026 proxy says fiscal 2025 incentive metrics included **adjusted net income, adjusted net sales and adjusted net operating cash inflows**. Executive pay includes annual cash bonuses plus time- and performance-based RSUs; the company says it generally grants executive RSUs on a three-year cycle. On Jan. 2, 2026, it granted time/performance RSUs vesting in tranches through 2029 and market-based RSUs for Barrocas and Shah tied to market-cap hurdles over five years. The company adopted share-ownership guidelines on Feb. 24, 2026: **7× salary for the CEO** and **3× salary** for other covered executives by the later of Jan. 1, 2031 or five years after entering a covered role. It also has a clawback policy. Beneficial ownership as of Mar. 16, 2026: Barrocas **2.45M shares / 1.7%**, CJ Wang **54.79M / 38.6%**, and all current executive officers/directors **40.8%**.

**Interpretation:** Alignment is mixed-to-good. Ownership, long-horizon equity and operating-cash-flow metrics are positives, and the new buyback reduced period-end shares below year-end. The soft spots are large cash bonuses, adjusted metrics, concentrated control through CJ Wang and Q2 share-based compensation of about $47M versus $11M a year earlier. The system points broadly toward growth and value creation, but not as cleanly as a long-term per-share-value/ROIC plan.

### Customer Devotion — 0.72 / confidence 0.72

**Facts:** The company’s brands have mass-market familiarity and broad retail distribution. Management repeatedly emphasizes “5-star consumer experience,” value/performance, and product-led category expansion. The company’s ability to create repeated category wins — vacuums, air fryers/cooking, beverage, beauty/home environment — is indirect evidence of consumer pull. A July 2026 Shark Beauty FlexStyle IonCurl launch is a small but fresh example of the product-cadence engine, not yet evidence of financial traction.

**Interpretation:** Consumer appeal is good: Shark and Ninja products are understandable, visible, frequently reviewed, and purchased on clear performance/value propositions. I’m not scoring this exceptional because public customer-service/review signals are noisy, and appliance affection is generally weaker than true lifestyle/platform brands. Confidence would improve with a durable third-party NPS/brand-loyalty dataset or repeat-purchase data by category.

### Why the other strategies lost

**Freeroll:** SN does not fit a classic asymmetric-payoff setup. There is no obvious near-term catalyst, asset-backed downside floor, or large mispricing gap; the thesis is compounding and execution, not a binary re-rate from depressed value.

**Wonderful and Fair:** SN has some Wonderful-and-Fair traits — profits, cash generation, brand strength and growth — but the unresolved material weaknesses in internal controls and execution-heavy consumer-products moat make it less clean as a mature high-certainty compounder. Rule Breakers remains the better framing because the upside depends on category expansion, international whitespace and repeated product innovation.

## Overall

SharkNinja remains a strong Rule Breaker candidate. Q2 provided unusually broad confirmation: faster sales growth, international acceleration, adjusted-EPS growth, cash-flow recovery and raised guidance. The unresolved material weaknesses in internal controls, gross-margin pressure, discretionary consumer exposure, concentrated control and execution-heavy moat keep it below the top tier.

Current view: **excellent execution, poor margin of safety at this price**. At $185.52 the market is capitalizing much of the operating upside; forward returns depend on sustained low-teens growth, continued category wins and premium-multiple support while control remediation remains unfinished.

## Thesis Connections

Potential fit for a growth-compounder / “brands that keep launching winners” thesis. It is less a pure technology platform and more a consumer-products execution machine, so it belongs in a basket where position size reflects hit-cycle, governance and controls risk.

## Watch

- FY2026 guidance: separate the underlying operating beat from the roughly $0.15 adjusted-EPS benefit tied to tariff refunds.
- Remediation of material weaknesses in IT general controls and financial reporting; disclosure controls remained ineffective at June 30, 2026.
- Gross margin recovery from 48.7% and whether tariff, FX, retailer activation and reinvestment keep adjusted EBITDA margin below sales growth.
- Product-hit cadence: new categories should add growth without bloating inventory, retailer activations or marketing spend.
- Independent market-share/brand-loyalty data to firm up Top Dog, Right Place and Time, and Customer Devotion.
- Insider/controlling-holder activity: CEO Mark Barrocas sold 100k shares on Jun. 18 at $140 and another 100k on Jun. 25 at $145 under Rule 10b5-1, retaining about 2.25M shares; watch whether planned sales stay orderly or broaden.

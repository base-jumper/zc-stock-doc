# CGS.AX — Cogstate Limited

## Business Overview

Cogstate is an ASX-listed neuroscience technology company focused on digital cognitive assessment and clinical-trial endpoint data quality. Its core business is **Clinical Trials**, where pharmaceutical and biotech sponsors use Cogstate technology/services to measure cognition and endpoint quality in CNS studies. It also has a smaller **Healthcare** business built around digital cognitive assessment tools such as Cognigram/CogMate, including an amended Eisai licensing arrangement.

FY25 was a record year: revenue **US$53.1M**, up **22%**; EBITDA **US$16.0M**, up **72%**; EBITDA margin **30.1%**; clinical-trials new contract sales **US$41.3M**, up **53%**; operating cash flow **US$11.5M**; cash **US$35.6M**. Clinical Trials generated **US$50.6M** revenue, while Healthcare declined to **US$2.5M**.

FY26 momentum accelerated through the year and the audited result (18 Aug 2026) confirmed a record year: group revenue **US$60.9M**, up **15%**; EBITDA **US$18.3M**, up **15%** at a **30%** margin; EBIT **US$15.2M** (25% margin); profit before tax **US$16.1M**; profit after tax **US$11.9M** (EPS **US$0.0696**); operating cash in-flow **US$8.5M**; year-end cash **US$34.8M**, no debt. FY26 net sales contracts were a record **US$89.0M**, up **116%** versus FY25’s **US$41.3M** and **230%** versus FY24’s **US$27.0M**. Active trials grew from **110** at 1 Jul 2025 to **171** at 30 Jun 2026 across psychiatric/mood (25% of FY26 new business), rare disease (27%), Alzheimer’s (23%), neurological/other (17%), and sleep (8%). Cogstate enters FY27 with **US$118.5M** contracted future revenue, up **32%**, of which **US$48.3M** is expected to be recognised in FY27, up **54%**. The board declared a second fully franked dividend of **A$0.04/share** (up from A$0.02).

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.80 / confidence 0.75

**Facts:** Cogstate sits at the intersection of CNS drug development, Alzheimer’s disease, psychiatry/mood disorders, sleep, rare neurological diseases, digital assessments, eCOA, and clinical-trial data quality. Management says sales are broadening across rare diseases, sleep, psychiatric, and neurodegenerative disorders. In 1H26, 45% of contract value came from mood, sleep, and other neurological conditions, almost a sixfold increase versus the prior corresponding period.

**Interpretation:** This is a strong setup. CNS trials are hard, expensive, and endpoint-sensitive; better cognitive measurement has real value. Alzheimer’s disease is a major catalyst, but the more important Rule Breaker angle is that Cogstate may be expanding from a narrow Alzheimer’s specialist into a broader CNS endpoint platform. Confidence is not higher because we do not yet have robust independent TAM/share data in this note.

### Top Dog — 0.70 / confidence 0.65

**Facts:** Cogstate calls itself a global leader in clinical-trial endpoint data quality and digital cognitive assessments. It has over 20 years of operating history, supports biopharma and academic research customers, and reported **171 active trials** at 30 Jun 2026 (up from 110 a year earlier). Competitors/alternatives in the broader market include Cambridge Cognition, MedAvante-ProPhase, Signant Health, VeraSci/NeuroCog, Pearson, CRO/eCOA bundles, and others.

**Interpretation:** Cogstate appears to be a leading specialist in a niche that matters. But “top dog” is not fully proven because the market is fragmented, private competitors limit visibility, and Cogstate is still tiny in absolute terms. Good candidate, not yet obviously dominant.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.66 / confidence 0.65

**Facts:** Moat sources include validated cognitive tests, long clinical-trial operating history, pharma relationships, scientific/endpoint expertise, trial data workflows, quality-assurance services, and channel leverage through Medidata. The Eisai arrangement also provides minimum cash royalties over a 10-year agreement and keeps Cogstate tied to real-world cognitive assessment use cases.

**Interpretation:** This is a credible specialist moat: switching endpoint methods mid-development is painful, sponsors care about validated tools, and reputation matters. But it is not impregnable. Larger eCOA/CRO/platform players can bundle services, and Cogstate still needs to prove AI products and Medidata can widen the moat rather than just provide distribution.

### Evidence of Durable, Accelerating Growth — 0.88 / confidence 0.90

**Facts:** FY25 revenue grew **22%**, EBITDA grew **72%**, EBITDA margin expanded to **30.1%**, and Clinical Trials contract sales grew **53%**. FY26 net sales contracts accelerated to **US$89.0M**, up **116%** from **US$41.3M** in FY25 and **230%** from **US$27.0M** in FY24. Quarterly FY26 net sales were consistently strong: **US$21.4M**, **US$20.3M**, **US$25.4M**, and **US$21.9M**. The audited FY26 result confirmed: revenue **US$60.9M** (+15%), EBITDA **US$18.3M** at **30%** margin, EBIT **US$15.2M** (25% margin), profit after tax **US$11.9M** (EPS US$0.0696), and active trials up to **171** from 110. Cogstate enters FY27 with **US$118.5M** contracted future revenue, including **US$48.3M** expected to be recognised in FY27, up **54%**.

**Interpretation:** This is the best Rule Breaker trait and the 4Q/FY26 update strengthens it. Revenue still lags contract wins because clinical-trial work is recognised over milestones and can take months to 4–5 years, but the backlog/revenue-visibility bridge is now much cleaner: FY26 bookings were not a one-quarter spike, and FY27 contracted revenue already covers a large base before new-year wins. The business is already profitable and cash generative, which is rare for a small-cap growth candidate.

### Organic Growth over Acquisitive Growth — 0.88 / confidence 0.75

**Facts:** FY25 growth appears overwhelmingly organic: revenue grew **22%**, Clinical Trials revenue grew **28%**, and new contract sales grew **53%**, driven by clinical-trial demand, indication broadening, software licensing, operational efficiency, Medidata pipeline development, and internal AI/data-product investment. The FY25 Annual Report does not show a material business acquisition or roll-up strategy. Intangible investment is mainly internally generated software/data assets; the notable Eisai change was a licensing amendment/reacquisition of rights, not buying external revenue.

**Interpretation:** This is one of the cleaner quality-of-growth traits. Cogstate is not manufacturing growth through M&A; the growth engine is contract wins, product capability, channel reach, and operating leverage. I am not scoring it 0.95–1.00 because the company does not provide a formal organic-growth bridge and some revenue mechanics around Eisai/reclassified activity muddy comparability. To firm this up: keep checking annual reports for business combinations, goodwill build, and whether future growth is still contract-led rather than acquisition-led.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.75 / confidence 0.70

**Facts:** CEO Brad O’Connor has overseen strong FY25 execution, improved margins, cash generation, capital returns, and renewed growth momentum. Yahoo Finance holder data shows insiders own about **40.3%** of shares, with institutions around **20.5%**. FY25 capital allocation included a **US$4.8M** share buyback and a maiden fully franked annual dividend of **A$0.02/share**.

**Interpretation:** High insider ownership is a major positive. Management appears disciplined and shareholder-aligned. The slight knock: this is not obviously founder-led, and more work is needed on individual insider ownership, recent director buying/selling, and long-term track record through prior weak cycles.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.70 / confidence 0.75

**Facts:** FY25 remuneration uses fixed pay plus STI/LTI. CEO target mix is **25% fixed / 37.5% STI / 37.5% LTI**; other executive KMP target mix is **55% fixed / 22.5% STI / 22.5% LTI**. STI is 50% company performance and 50% individual/strategic/operational targets for most executives; CEO company metrics include **50% profit before tax**, **20% divisional financial measures**, and **30% non-financial measures**. LTI is up to 150% of STI and tied to long-term objectives around **revenue and EPS growth**; FY25 also shows options cancelled where profit targets were not achieved. Director holdings are meaningful: chair Martyn Myer held **23.7M shares**, and CEO Brad O’Connor held **5.4M shares**, **920k performance rights**, and **2.25M options** at 30 June 2025.

**Interpretation:** Alignment is good, not perfect. The positives are large insider/director ownership, real performance gating, profit/EPS elements, and evidence that failed hurdles actually cancel awards. The soft spots are cash STI significance, revenue in the LTI mix, only partial visibility on target rigor, and the usual risk that options/rights reward share-price movement more than per-share intrinsic value. This deserves a solid score, but not a clean one. To firm it up: review the FY26 remuneration report/proxy detail for exact EPS/revenue hurdles, vesting periods, holding locks, recent insider sales, and dilution from equity plans.

### Customer Devotion — 0.48 / confidence 0.55

**Facts:** Cogstate is mainly a B2B clinical-trials company. Its products are used by sponsors, physicians, sites, and patients, but public customer-love/NPS-style data is limited. The Healthcare product set targets brief, accurate, self-administered cognitive measures for clinicians and consumers, but Healthcare revenue declined **37%** in FY25 to **US$2.5M**.

**Interpretation:** The customer appeal here is professional utility, not emotional love. Stickiness may be strong once a tool is embedded in a trial, but there is not enough evidence of passionate end-user pull. This trait is structurally awkward but not fatal.

### No Disqualifying Red Flags — 0.74 / confidence 0.75

**Reviewed:** business, management, financial, and structural red flags.

**Flags found:** No fraud/liquidity/accounting veto identified as of 2026-07-15. Cogstate had **US$35.6M** cash at FY25 and is profitable/cash-generative. The real flags are: small-cap customer/order lumpiness; clinical-trial revenue recognition over multi-year milestones; dependence on sponsor trial enrolment/timing; Healthcare revenue decline; possible concentration around large pharma/channel relationships; competitive pressure from broader eCOA/CRO platforms; and execution risk around AI/Medidata expansion.

**Interpretation:** No disqualifier, but enough small-cap/lumpiness risk to keep this below a clean score.

## Overall

Cogstate is a **natural Rule Breaker candidate**: small, specialist, founder-ish/insider-aligned, profitable, exposed to a large emerging need, and showing accelerating leading indicators. The attractive version of the thesis is simple: CNS clinical trials get more complex and numerous, cognitive endpoints matter more, and Cogstate becomes one of the default specialist platforms for measuring cognition and endpoint quality.

The audited 4Q/FY26 result (18 Aug 2026) resolves the key open question and moves the thesis in the right direction: record revenue, EBITDA margin held at **30%** despite growth investment, gross margin improved from 53% (1H26) to 62% (2H26), active trials up to 171, record bookings, and a doubled dividend. FY27 opens with materially better revenue visibility (US$48.3M contracted revenue expected to be recognised, +54%). The main reason this is not an obvious slam dunk is still evidence depth — market share, customer concentration, win rates, retention, and whether Medidata/AI materially widen the moat — plus the re-rated entry price. Still, as a small-cap compounder candidate, this is increasingly interesting.

## Thesis Connections

- **AI in healthcare / data quality:** Cogstate is launching AI-powered tools for automation, scalability, and trial signal detection.
- **Ageing / Alzheimer’s disease:** direct exposure through cognitive assessment, trials, and Eisai-linked healthcare tools.
- **Specialist vertical software/services:** attractive if Cogstate can become embedded infrastructure for CNS trials rather than a project-by-project service vendor.
- **Single Person Companies / automation-adjacent:** weak indirect link only; AI tools may improve operating leverage, but this is not the core thesis.

## Watch

- FY27 revenue conversion from **US$89.0M** FY26 net sales contracts and **US$48.3M** contracted FY27 revenue expected to be recognised (up 54%), noting management's expectation of a second-half bias.
- Whether FY27 EBITDA margins hold near the FY26 **30%** level given the planned two-year (FY27–FY28) AI/workflow-orchestration build-out; management guides margins maintained but this is the key execution watch.
- Evidence that Medidata contributes material revenue rather than just pipeline language (co-selling arrangements called out in FY26 result).
- Customer concentration and repeat-contract data.
- Independent market-share evidence versus Cambridge Cognition, Signant, MedAvante-ProPhase, VeraSci/NeuroCog, and CRO/eCOA bundles.
- Healthcare segment: FY26 was flat at **US$2.5M** with stable gross contribution; watch for stabilisation/uptick from the Eisai Global License.
- AI product adoption (Rater Training, Central Monitoring) and margin impact; gross margin path (1H26 53% → 2H26 62%).
- Director/insider trading and whether high insider ownership remains aligned.

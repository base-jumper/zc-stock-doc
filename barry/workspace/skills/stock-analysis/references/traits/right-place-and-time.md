# Trait: Right Place and Time

**What we're looking for:**
The company should operate in an industry where the underlying conditions create a long runway
for exceptional growth. This may be a genuinely new or emerging industry, or an established
industry entering a new growth phase because of fresh demand drivers, technology shifts,
regulatory change, infrastructure buildout, demographic change, cost breakthroughs, or changing
customer behaviour.

This trait scores the **industry setup**, not the company's dominance within it. A mediocre
company in a powerful wave can still do well for a while; a great company in a stagnant,
low-margin, over-competitive industry has to fight gravity the whole way.

**Key questions:**

* Is this industry new, emerging, or entering a structurally stronger growth phase?
* What has changed that should make the next decade better than the last?
* Is the addressable market large, expanding, and still underpenetrated?
* Are margins attractive, or is the industry structurally low-margin and brutally competitive?
* Are the growth drivers durable, or are they temporary/cyclical?
* Is this industry reshaping how people live, work, buy, build, compute, move, heal, learn, or communicate?

**Scoring guidance:**

* **0.70–1.00** Large and expanding opportunity with durable growth drivers; industry economics are attractive or improving; competitive intensity has not yet crushed returns
* **0.40–0.69** Real growth drivers exist, but the opportunity may be narrower, more cyclical, lower-margin, or already crowded
* **0.00–0.39** Mature, saturated, highly competitive, low-margin industry with no credible new growth driver

**Examples of attractive setups:** early e-commerce, streaming, AI compute, sleep-apnea treatment,
grid modernisation, data-centre power infrastructure.

**Notes:**

* "Emerging" is not mandatory. An established industry can score well if a new force changes its
  growth trajectory — for example, electrification increasing demand for grid equipment.
* Avoid confusing excitement with economics. A fashionable industry can still be structurally bad
  if competition is intense, customers have power, margins are thin, or differentiation is weak.
* The industry must be important enough to support very large outcomes, not merely interesting.

**Documentation:**

* The industry / sub-industry, and the specific shift creating the runway (new technology, regulation, demand driver)
* TAM today and its growth rate / penetration — with source and as-of date
* Industry economics: typical margins and competitive intensity
* Whether the growth drivers are structural or cyclical, and the expected duration
* The main risks to the industry thesis

# 9501.T — Tokyo Electric Power Company Holdings, Incorporated

## Business Overview

Tokyo Electric Power Company Holdings is Japan's largest electric-utility group and the incumbent utility for Tokyo/Kanto. Its main businesses are fuel and thermal generation, regulated power grid, retail/energy partner, renewable power, and holding-company functions tied to Fukushima Daiichi decommissioning and compensation obligations. The stock is not a normal dividend utility: it is a highly levered, policy-sensitive recovery/special situation whose equity value is dominated by nuclear-restart optionality, Fukushima liabilities, fuel-cost timing, regulatory decisions and balance-sheet trust.

Kashiwazaki-Kariwa Unit 6 resumed commercial operation on 16 April 2026, the first TEPCO reactor to return to commercial service since Fukushima. That materially de-risks the operating story, but the first quarter did not yet show a clean earnings inflection. FY2027 Q1 revenue rose 3.9% to JPY 1,481.2bn, while TEPCO swung to a JPY 34.3bn operating loss and ordinary profit fell 88.7% to JPY 11.4bn. The attributable loss was JPY 9.8bn. Full-year guidance remains undecided because fuel-price visibility is poor, and the company still forecasts no dividend.

## Trait Assessment

### Downside Support / Valuation Floor — 0.52 / confidence 0.80

The visible floor is asset-based, not income-based. Yahoo shows price JPY 520 versus book value per share around JPY 2,115, so the stock trades at only ~0.25x book. That is a large accounting discount even after the post-Fukushima liability overhang. The 52-week low is JPY 442.8, but the current price now sits 17% above it and 30% above my JPY 400 practical floor, so the margin of safety is weaker than at the prior review.

The caveat is important: book value is not a clean liquidation floor. At 30 June, cash had fallen JPY 336.3bn from March to JPY 601.0bn, while bonds, long-term borrowings and short-term borrowings totalled about JPY 6.71tn. TEPCO also carried JPY 1.25tn of disaster-loss provisions, JPY 517.3bn of nuclear-damage compensation provisions and JPY 430.1bn of reactor-core-removal provisions. These claims and political constraints can absorb value before common shareholders see it, and there is no dividend floor. I retain JPY 400/share as a conservative practical floor, but its binding mechanism remains weak. Confidence would improve with the reviewed Q1 filing and the next audited liability bridge.

### Fundamental Stability / No Value Trap — 0.55 / confidence 0.82

The electricity grid/retail demand base is durable: Tokyo/Kanto power demand is not structurally disappearing, and electrification plus data-centre/industrial load are medium-term supports. Unit 6 commercial operation improves the medium-term fuel-cost mix, and Q1 net assets edged higher to JPY 3.42tn despite the attributable loss.

This still scores only mid-range because the equity is exposed to unusual earnings and policy risks. Q1 operating profit reversed from positive JPY 64.7bn to negative JPY 34.3bn; Power Grid and Energy Partner recorded ordinary losses of JPY 31.2bn and JPY 50.9bn respectively. The business is essential, but the common equity can still be impaired by Fukushima obligations, fuel-cost/tariff timing, political pressure and refinancing needs. This is durable demand wrapped in unstable shareholder economics, not a cleanly stable utility.

### Mispriced for an Identifiable Reason — 0.52 / confidence 0.72

The market is clearly discounting TEPCO for identifiable reasons: Fukushima liabilities, no dividend, very high leverage, absent FY2027 guidance, political/regulatory intervention risk and weak near-term earnings. The variant view is that the current price may over-penalise those risks if Unit 6's commercial operation produces a visible earnings uplift and the Fifth Comprehensive Special Business Plan improves equity visibility.

The edge is not especially clean. Unit 6 is already operating, but Q1 still delivered an operating loss and the company could not issue guidance. That makes the low valuation more understandable and reduces the evidence that the market is wrong. The mispricing score remains moderate: there is a plausible gap between price and normalised value, but earnings proof is still incomplete. A quantified Unit 6 fuel-cost benefit and credible full-year guidance would firm this up.

### Identifiable, Time-Bound Catalyst — 0.48 / confidence 0.78

The original Unit 6 restart catalyst has fired: commercial operation resumed on 16 April 2026. The remaining catalyst is proof that Unit 6 materially lifts consolidated earnings, followed by a publishable FY2027 outlook and credible progress toward Unit 7 restart. Those developments could reset the market's view of TEPCO's ability to earn through its Fukushima obligations.

The remaining trigger is less concrete than before: Q1 did not show the expected consolidated inflection, full-year guidance is still absent, and no new Unit 7 commercial-operation timetable is visible. I retain a two-year window but lower the catalyst probability to 40%. This is now a marginal freeroll rather than a clean one.

### Why the other strategies lost

Cash-cow fails because there is no dividend, earnings are not dependable, debt is stretched and genuine distributable cash is questionable. Wonderful-and-fair fails harder: ROIC is low/negative across the available period, leverage is high, and the investment case depends on a turnaround/re-rating rather than compounding. Rule-breakers is not the right frame either. TEPCO is a dominant incumbent in an important power market, but it is mature, regulated, capital-intensive and not showing durable high growth.

## Overall

TEPCO is a speculative asset-backed utility turnaround, not a quality utility. The positive case is simple: the equity is priced at ~0.25x book, the core service is essential, and Unit 6 is now commercially operating. The negative case is equally real: Q1 failed to translate that milestone into consolidated operating profit, while Fukushima costs, heavy debt, no dividend and political constraints can keep the discount rational for a long time.

My view: keep it on the watchlist, but the setup is weaker at JPY 520 than it was near JPY 480. The realised Unit 6 catalyst is a genuine positive; the missing earnings proof and lack of a firm next catalyst keep this below allocation quality.

## Valuation

I used the asymmetric-payoff method because the thesis remains a catalyst re-rating from an asset-backed depressed price. Inputs: price JPY 520; floor JPY 400; catalyst fair value JPY 800; probability 40%; and a two-year window. JPY 800 is roughly 0.38x book and about 6.6x the thin next-year consensus EPS estimate of JPY 122, leaving a large haircut for Fukushima liabilities, leverage and no dividend. The probability is lower because Unit 6 is already operating but Q1 did not yet validate the consolidated earnings uplift.

The script-stamped model gives an expected annualized ROI of about 3.8%. The payoff is only moderately asymmetric: 23.1% downside to the floor versus 53.8% upside to fair value, or 2.33:1 up/down. The return is fragile because it depends on earnings proof and another policy-mediated nuclear milestone; if those stall, the stock can stay cheap indefinitely.

## Thesis Connections

Possible FIT connection: Japanese power-demand recovery, grid scarcity, nuclear baseload restart and data-centre/electrification load. Unit 6 commercial operation is the concrete expression of that theme, but TEPCO still carries unusually poor balance-sheet/liability quality.

## Watch

- Unit 6 availability, output and disclosed fuel-cost savings through Q2-Q4; this is the key earnings-proof signpost.
- Unit 7: local consent, inspection milestones and a credible restart/commercial-operation timetable.
- FY2027 guidance remains undecided because of fuel-price uncertainty; update the valuation when management publishes a credible earnings base.
- Fukushima Daiichi decommissioning, compensation and provisions; any material increase can erode the book-value floor.
- Cash, short-term borrowings and refinancing: Q1 cash fell to JPY 601.0bn while short-term borrowings rose to JPY 2.98tn.
- Power Grid and Energy Partner profitability after Q1 ordinary losses of JPY 31.2bn and JPY 50.9bn.
- Dividend remains zero for FY2027; any credible reinstatement path would be a material catalyst.

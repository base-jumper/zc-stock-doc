# MELI — MercadoLibre, Inc.

## Business Overview

MercadoLibre is Latin America's leading e-commerce and fintech platform, operating in 18 countries (Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Colombia and the broader region) plus Portugal. Revenue comes from two intertwined engines:

1. **Commerce** — a first- and third-party marketplace monetised through commissions, seller services, shipping, advertising, and first-party sales.
2. **Fintech (Mercado Pago)** — payments/acquiring, instalment credit, credit cards, digital wallet, and asset management.

The two reinforce each other — users active in both generate roughly 70% more GMV per user than marketplace-only users and ~90% more TPV than fintech-only users (Q2 2026 release) — which is why management keeps investing simultaneously in shipping, loyalty, credit cards and financial services.

Q2 2026 (quarter ended 30 June 2026, reported 5 Aug 2026): net revenue and financial income of **$10.169b**, **+50% YoY in USD** (+43% FX-neutral) — the fastest pace in four years and the first quarter ever above $10b. GMV ~**$22b** (+44% USD / +36% FX-neutral); items sold **795m** (+45%); unique active buyers **89m** (+26%); items per buyer +14%. Mercado Pago TPV passed **$100b** in a quarter for the first time (+56%); monthly active users **88m** (+30%). Income from operations was **$683m** (6.7% margin, down ~550bps YoY) and net income **$466m** (4.6% margin, down 11%) as management deliberately invested in free shipping, credit cards, first-party selection, cross-border trade and MELI+ over near-term margin. Credit book +75% to >$16b, credit-card book +91% to $7.7b; 15–90-day NPLs of 4.6% (cards) / 7.0% (total portfolio) — near historical lows. Advertising revenue +73% USD; MELI's share of LatAm digital advertising >10%.

FY2025: revenue **$28.9b** (+39%), gross profit **$12.86b** (+34%), operating income **$3.2b** (+18%), net income **$2.0b** (+4.5%); operating cash flow **$12.1b**, FCF **$10.8b** (working-capital tail from payables/consumer float). Balance sheet: ~$5.7–5.9b cash/marketable vs ~$11.4b total debt — leverage tied to the credit book and securitisation, not a distressed structure.

Leadership: founder Marcos Galperín (CEO 1999–2025) became **Executive Chairman effective 1 Jan 2026**; Ariel Szarfsztejn (ex-President of Mercado Pago, with the company since 2017) is now CEO — the first leadership transition in the company's history. Institutions ~81%, insiders ~7%.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.95 / confidence 0.85

**Industry.** LatAm e-commerce is structurally under-penetrated (online share of retail in the mid-teens % vs ~20%+ in developed markets) and digital fintech is even earlier — a large share of the region's adults are still first-time users of digital payments, credit and wallets. Digital advertising is early and MELI already takes >10% of LatAm digital ad spend (Q2 2026 release). These are durable, multi-decade adoption trends rather than cyclical.

**The shift.** Logistics (77% of fast shipments delivered within 48h), cross-border trade (+60% FX-neutral GMV, China-fulfillment volume +170% QoQ), digital payments replacing cash, AI-assisted conversion, and credit for the underbanked all widen the addressable market. The supporting market doc (`southeast-asia-taiwan-brazil-ecommerce-marketplaces`) sizes the combined opportunity at ~$42b today → **$125b at 2036** — a ~3x structural runway.

**Industry economics.** Gross margins ~45% are attractive for commerce; the current operating-margin compression (6.7% vs 12.6% FY24) is investment choice, not structural margin collapse.

**Main risks to the thesis:** Brazil macro/currency, Mexico's tax-reform headwind, and intensifying competition (Shopee, Amazon, TikTok Shop) in the most valuable market.

### Top Dog — 0.85 / confidence 0.80

**Position.** MELI is the #1 e-commerce marketplace in Latin America by GMV and buyers across the three biggest markets (Brazil, Mexico, Argentina), and Mercado Pago is a top payments/licensing platform. It defined the category — marketplace + wallet + logistics + ads + credit in one ecosystem — and holds the mindshare.

**Edge.** The full ecosystem combination is unmatched in the region: 89m buyers, fulfilment density and brand trust. By most market metrics MELI is the regional leader, with a widening engagement gap (ecosystem users more valuable per head).

**Threats.** Shopee and Amazon are investing heavily in Brazil; TikTok Shop is growing fast; fintech challengers (Nubank and peers) pressure the credit/wallet side. The lead is real but being probed in the most valuable market → top dog, not an unassailable monopoly.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.80 / confidence 0.80

**Moat sources, with evidence:**
- **Two-sided marketplace network effects** — buyers attract sellers, selection attracts buyers; MELI is the deepest liquidity pool in the region.
- **Logistics density** — own fulfilment across the region; ~77% of fast shipments in 48h; unit costs fall at scale.
- **Fintech flywheel** — payments data and credit behaviour feed a data moat that improves underwriting, funds commerce, and raises switching costs.
- **Brand/trust** — the default "buy online + pay" name in Brazil/Mexico/Argentina.
- **Scale economies** — fixed cost (logistics, tech, ads infrastructure) spread over a rapidly growing base.

**Pricing power** shows in advertising monetisation (+73% USD) and fee take over time, with gross margin ~45% high and stable even as mix shifts.

**Weakness.** Multi-homing keeps marketplace switching costs moderate; cross-border sellers and 1P scale let Amazon/Shopee/etc. meet selection; credit risk could dent the ecosystem economics. Advantage is durable but not impregnable → 0.80.

### Evidence of Durable, Accelerating Growth — 0.85 / confidence 0.85

**Stage & metric.** Maturing but still compounding; revenue is the right metric and grew **+50% YoY in Q2 2026** — the fastest in four years and accelerating vs FY25's +39% and FY24's +37%. Growth is broad-based: commerce +50%, fintech +49%, ads +73%, TPV +56%.

**Second derivative.** Q2 +50% follows 30 consecutive quarters above 30% — the rate is *accelerating*, not quietly decelerating; items sold +45%, active buyers +26%, items/buyer +14% all rising together.

**FCF tracking.** FCF (~$10.7b FY25) runs far above net income (~$2.0b) — reported profit converts strongly to cash (working-capital tail from payables/consumer float). No profit-without-cash red flag.

**Deliberate reinvestment validated.** Gross margin declined 50.2% → 44.5% (2022→2025) — the free-shipping threshold, first-party, credit and MELI+ investments — but unit economics (items/buyer, retention, credit NPLs) are improving with it. This is the disciplined-reinvestment carve-out, not cash burn.

### Organic Growth — 0.85 / confidence 0.80

**Natural engine.** Growth is overwhelmingly organic — the same marketplace, payments, advertising and logistics continuing to compound. No serial acquisitions: cumulative M&A is tiny (mostly tuck-ins), **goodwill is minimal** and stable, and the **diluted share count is essentially flat** (~50.7m for five years).

**M&A fingerprint clean.** No roll-up, no goodwill build, no dilution-funded growth, no paid-for growth. The engine is native ecosystem growth. Confidence 0.80 — M&A history is clean but not audited in filings; the read is solid.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.75 / confidence 0.70

**People.** Founder Marcos Galperín built MELI over 26 years and remains Executive Chairman; the CEO transition to internal 9-year-veteran Ariel Szarfsztejn (ex-President of Mercado Pago) is an orderly, founder-shepherded succession — the company's first — not a departure risk. Track record: start-up (1999) to LatAm's most valuable company.

**Communication.** Earnings releases are unusually transparent — management explicitly frames the margin pressure as the price of reinvestment (free shipping, cards, first-party, cross-border, MELI+) and states the counter-scenario (credit quality). Long-horizon, honest self-assessment.

**Backers.** ~81% institutional ownership — patient, respected long-term holders; no controlling family/VC overhang. Insiders (incl. founder) ~7%.

**Firm-up.** The new CEO's first-year strategy markers and the board's succession mechanics are still unproven — hence confidence 0.70; proxy/CEO-transition filings in 2026 will firm it up (also in Watch).

### Incentive Alignment — 0.65 / confidence 0.70

**Scoring basis.** SBC is modest (SBC ≈ 1% of revenue / 0.3% dilution per year in the models), the share count has been stable for years, and there are no outsized mega-grants. Founder + insiders hold ~7% — genuine skin in the game alongside long-term institutions.

**The alignment system looks sensible** — balanced long-term metrics (growth, operating margin, credit quality) rather than pure size — but I have not yet re-read the 2026 proxy's pay design, successor package detail, and equity grants line by line. That is the gap holding confidence at 0.70 rather than higher. No red flags observed (no self-dealing, no repriceable-option schemes, no cliff-heavy annual bonuses).

### Customer Devotion — 0.80 / confidence 0.75

**Commerce.** 89m unique buyers (+26%), items per buyer +14% — customers buying more per head, the classic devotion signal; 48h fast shipping and MELI+ (subscription) strengthen the marketplace habit. In the region, MELI is the default "shop online" property.

**Fintech.** 88m MAU (+30%); Brazil MAU +38%, Mexico +45%; users in both ecosystem legs grew +37% (faster than either alone). AUM $23b (+68%) — consumers park savings/investments in the ecosystem.

**Preference vs lock-in.** Marketplace switching is easy (multi-homing), so the sustained buyer growth reflects *preference* — the strongest devotion evidence. Slight discount for no explicit NPS/loyalty disclosure → 0.80.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Wonderful-and-Fair** (0.716 / 0.713): the genuine runner-up — MELI has wonderful quality, cash generation and a durable moat. But the current phase is reinvestment-carried, growth-first (margin deliberately suppressed), which is exactly the Rule Breakers frame; W&F would under-weight the growth engine and the leaderboard reflects that.
- **Cash Cow** (0.740 / 0.731): scores high on the quality/cash metrics but the strategy's philosophy is to *harvest*; MELI deliberately reinvests everything into a re-accelerating opportunity. Frame mismatch — a cow misprices the reinvestment into growth.
- **Freeroll** (0.467 / 0.715): no meaningful downside floor near the current price and no time-bound re-rating catalyst; this is a compounding-growth thesis, not a measured-floor trade.

## Overall

MELI is the dominant LatAm commerce×fintech ecosystem compounding at an accelerating 30–50% while deliberately trading margin for long-term position — a textbook rule-breaker on the two make-or-break traits (right place and time 0.95, durable growth 0.85). The runway is large (market doc: $42b → $125b), the moat is multi-faceted (logistics, payments, brand, data), and cash generation is strong (FCF ≈ 5x NI).

**Main detractors** from an exceptional score: (1) near-term earnings power is margin-suppressed (~$2.0b NI on $35b revenue TTM); (2) the credit book is scaling at +75% loans/+91% credit cards — NPLs are near historical lows but that trajectory is a late-cycle risk to watch; (3) competition in Brazil (Amazon, Shopee, TikTok Shop) is intensifying; (4) the founder-CEO transition is unproven; (5) the stock is not cheap under either the exit-multiple entry (~34x) or especially the tam-capture lens.

**Net:** excellent business, high-integrity execution, real but manageable execution/credit risks — a high-quality compounder priced for continued growth.

**Blend** (0.7 exit / 0.3 tam): **+9.96%/yr** weighted ROI.

The **wide spread** between the lenses (≈+14.9% vs ≈-1.5%) is the honest signal — bottom-up says current profitability growth justifies the price; top-down says the terminal market-implied revenue does too only if the capture thesis fully delivers. Weighting the exit lens more (0.7) because it is grounded in the actual, now-profitable earnings stream; the spread is why the strategy confidence sits at ~0.79 rather than higher.

**Agent score 0.65** (qv 0.523): the franchise and compounding are excellent and I expect the tam-capture build understates MELI's own revenue power (the marketplace-only boundary misses the fintech arm), but the credit-cycle risk, competition and margin-timing unknowns, plus the round-trip valuation case, keep it a solid but not elite rating.

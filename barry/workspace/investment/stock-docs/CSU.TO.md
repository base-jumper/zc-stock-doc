# CSU.TO — Constellation Software Inc.

## Business Overview

Constellation Software acquires, manages, and builds vertical-market software businesses. The model is deliberately decentralized: it buys mostly B2B/proprietary software companies, usually with recurring revenue, low attrition, and specific vertical or geographic niches, then operates them indefinitely with local autonomy. Revenue growth is a blend of acquired growth and modest organic growth; cash generated by the portfolio is recycled into more acquisitions.

As of the latest sources, the business remains a large, profitable serial acquirer rather than a simple organic software grower. FY2025 revenue was US$11.623B, up 15%, with 4% organic growth and US$1.579B of completed acquisition consideration. Q1 2026 revenue was US$3.181B, up 20%, with 6% organic growth; Q1 completed acquisition consideration was US$809M, with another US$786M of completed/open post-quarter commitments. Yahoo shows TTM revenue of about US$12.15B, TTM EBITDA of US$2.42B, TTM FCF of about US$2.73B, and market cap of about C$56.5B at C$2,667.24/share.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.78 / confidence 0.78

Cash earnings are much stronger and more stable than IFRS net income. FY2022-2025 revenue rose from US$6.622B to US$11.623B and FCF rose from US$1.256B to US$2.664B, while reported diluted EPS moved around more sharply (US$24.18, US$26.67, US$34.48, then US$24.15). The FY2025 EPS decline was partly distorted by Asseco-related liability/accounting effects and other non-operating items; Q1 2026 rebounded to US$17.32/share versus US$6.41 in Q1 2025. Interpretation: the owner-earnings stream is real and growing, but accounting profit is noisy because acquired intangibles, minority interests, and investment/liability revaluations matter. Confidence would firm up with a full MD&A bridge from IFRS income to recurring owner earnings by operating group.

### Returns on Capital — 0.70 / confidence 0.70

The quantitative read is good but not pristine. Yahoo-derived ROIC was roughly 16.7% in 2022, 4.9% in 2023, 13.7% in 2024, and 8.3% in 2025; ROE stayed high but is affected by leverage and intangibles. The better qualitative evidence is that Constellation's compensation system is built around ROIC and revenue growth, and operating-group managers receive no bonus if ROIC does not exceed the board-set risk-free hurdle in most groups. Interpretation: returns appear good enough to support compounding, but accounting invested capital is messy for an acquisition compounder. Confidence would improve with Constellation's own long-run ROIC / invested-capital disclosure by cohort.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.82 / confidence 0.68

The moat is portfolio-level: many small vertical-market software products embedded in customer workflows, where replacement cost and operational risk are high relative to the subscription price. The company's acquisition criteria explicitly prefer proprietary B2B software, recurring revenue, leading or increasing market share, diversified customers, and low attrition. Gross margin sits around 35-38% and FCF margins around 19-23% across FY2022-2025, consistent with sticky software economics. Interpretation: strong evidence of switching-cost moats across the portfolio, but customer retention, pricing, and churn are not disclosed in enough detail to score this with high confidence.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.90 / confidence 0.82

This is the heart of the case. Constellation continues to deploy large sums: US$1.847B acquisition spend in 2023, US$1.683B in 2024, US$1.513B cash spend / US$1.579B total consideration in 2025, plus US$809M completed in Q1 2026 and US$786M completed/open after quarter-end. The acquisition mandate spans 100+ countries and many verticals, and the company has expanded from very small businesses to larger technology businesses. Interpretation: even at much larger scale, the opportunity set still appears deep; the risk is not lack of targets, but preserving hurdle-rate discipline as capital available rises.

### Capital Allocation — 0.93 / confidence 0.82

Constellation is unusually clear about capital allocation: FCFA2S is intended to be invested in acquisitions meeting the hurdle rate, not paid out or used for routine buybacks. Share count has stayed essentially flat at 21.19153M through FY2022-2025, so growth has not been funded by dilution. The incentive system ties bonuses to ROIC and net revenue growth, not just size. Interpretation: this is one of the highest-quality allocation cultures in listed software, with the caveat that future returns may compress as the capital base scales.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.68 / confidence 0.75

The balance sheet is serviceable but not fortress-like. Yahoo shows about US$5.56B of total debt and US$3.01B of cash, versus TTM EBITDA of about US$2.42B and TTM FCF of about US$2.75B. FY2025 net debt was US$2.275B, and current liabilities exceed current assets partly because deferred revenue is large in software. Interpretation: debt does not look like a near-term thesis breaker, especially against recurring revenue and cash generation, but acquisition intensity plus negative tangible book means there is less hard-asset downside support than a headline quality investor might assume.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.70 / confidence 0.62

Founder/culture risk is real. Mark Leonard resigned as President on 25 September 2025, with Mark Miller appointed President; the board notes Leonard is not independent because of his executive role. The decentralized operating-group structure, long-tenured acquisition playbook, and incentive system reduce dependence on one person, but Constellation's culture is a major asset and is harder to underwrite after founder succession. Confidence would firm up over several years of post-transition acquisition performance and capital discipline.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.88 / confidence 0.82

The incentive design is strongly aligned with per-share value creation. Executive bonuses are based on ROIC and net revenue growth; in most operating groups, if ROIC does not clear a board-set risk-free rate, the manager receives no bonus. Executives generally must invest 75% of after-tax incentive bonuses into Constellation shares held in escrow for a minimum average four-year period. Mark Leonard waived salary and bonus in 2023-2025, and Mark Miller has waived salary and bonus from 2026. This is unusually shareholder-aligned, though not a substitute for watching actual acquisition returns.

**Why other strategies lost:** Cash Cow scores well on cash generation, but it is the wrong frame because Constellation is still actively redeploying cash into acquisitions rather than primarily harvesting and returning it. Rule Breakers is too early-stage/hypergrowth oriented for a proven, profitable compounder. Freeroll is not the right lens because there is no obvious asset floor or discrete mispricing catalyst; the case rests on compounding quality at a now more reasonable price.

## Overall

Constellation is a high-quality acquisition compounder with a rare capital-allocation culture, durable vertical-software economics, and a still-active reinvestment engine. The current share price is materially below the 52-week high and now screens more reasonable on owner earnings than the headline trailing P/E suggests.

The main detractors are scale, accounting complexity, leverage/intangible dependence, and succession/culture risk. Organic growth is positive but not spectacular; the long-term return still depends on continuing to find and integrate enough small software businesses at acceptable returns.

The resulting expected annualized ROI is about **12.1%**. Attribution is mostly business growth (~10.0%), with a small contribution from re-rating (~1.7%) and income (~0.2%). My agent score is 0.74: this is a very good business at a fair-to-attractive price, but not a slam dunk because the thesis still relies on sustained acquisition reinvestment at scale and the post-Leonard leadership transition proving itself.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a quality-compounder / long-duration capital allocation thesis: decentralized vertical-market software, recurring revenue, disciplined acquisitions, and shareholder-aligned incentives. It is less suited to a hard-catalyst or deep-value thesis because downside support is mostly business quality rather than tangible assets.

## Watch

- Acquisition spend and disclosed hurdle-rate discipline: watch for evidence that scale is forcing lower-return deals.
- Organic growth ex-FX: FY2025 was 4% reported organic / 3% FX-adjusted; Q1 2026 was 6% / 2% FX-adjusted.
- Q2 2026 results and conference call on 12 August 2026: refresh organic growth, acquisition deployment, cash conversion, debt, and management commentary.
- Post-succession execution under Mark Miller, especially acquisition pace and ROIC discipline.
- Debt and current liabilities relative to recurring cash flow if acquisition spending accelerates.
- Any disclosure on churn, retention, pricing, or cohort returns that can firm up the moat and ROIC scores.

# TRMB — Trimble Inc.

## Business Overview

Trimble connects the physical and digital worlds for critical industries — primarily construction,
geospatial, agriculture, and transportation. Revenue splits across three reportable segments:
AECO (architecture/engineering/construction software; Q2-26 $388M, 40% of revenue), Field Systems
(precision positioning hardware for agriculture/construction/geospatial; Q2-26 $442M, 46%), and
Transportation & Logistics (T&L; Q2-26 $141M, 14%). ~66% of revenue (~$1.27B in H1-26) is
subscription-and-services, with ARR reaching a record $2.51B (Q2-26), up 14% y/y (12% organic).
Trimble is transitioning to a recurring software narrative ("Connect & Scale") and embedding AI into
connected field workflows. Revenue is modestly growing (~10% organic), with GAAP operating margin
~13–14% but non-GAAP operating margin ~27% (Q2-26 non-GAAP Op Inc $260.6M, 26.8% of revenue). No
dividend; focused on buybacks ($1B authorization approved Q2-26).

## Trait Assessment

Scored under **rule-breakers** (primary; see below for how other strategies lost).

### Right Place and Time — 0.70 / 0.75
Construction, engineering, and precision-agriculture digitization is a real and under-penetrated
growth wave: infrastructure spending, labor shortages pushing productivity software, and
AI-enabled workflow adoption across field industries. Margins are attractive (70%+ gross). Industry
setup is genuinely favorable, though the construction end-market is cyclical and more mature than a
greenfield technology wave. *Firm-up:* a named TAM/sizing for construction tech adoption; sources
for AI-workflow penetration.

### Top Dog — 0.65 / 0.70
Trimble is a leader in precision ag, geospatial, and construction field tech, with deep product
and ecosystem reach (Trimble's field hardware + AECO software connecting to Autodesk and others).
But it is not a clear winner-takes-most player — it competes with Hexagon, Topcon, Caterpillar
(in-field), and Autodesk/Bentley (design software). It is a strong, defensible leader in niches
rather than a category-defining monopolist. *Firm-up:* market-share trend data per sub-segment.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.60 / 0.70
Real moat sources: high switching costs from deeply integrated field-to-office workflows,
proprietary positioning/data, and brand trust in construction/ag; gross margin ~70% supports some
pricing power. But the moat is narrower than a pure software compounder — hardware is
semi-commoditised and competition is credible. Advantage is stable-to-modestly-widening with the
subscription/data transition. *Firm-up:* NRR / net revenue retention disclosure, competitor share
trends.

### Durable Growth — 0.68 / 0.75
Growth is real and re-accelerating: FY25 revenue $3.59B; H1-26 $1.91B (+11% y/y, 10% organic);
Q2-26 organic +10% with ARR +14% (12% organic). This is a low-to-mid-teen recurring growth franchise
with improving mix, not high-octane hypergrowth. FCF tracks profit (FY25 FCF $361M vs GAAP NI $424M;
H1-26 FCF $502M). *Firm-up:* second-derivative read across 3+ years and gross-margin trend from
filings.

### Organic Growth — 0.58 / 0.70
Trimble historically grew acquisitively — goodwill stands at $4.8B (56% of total assets) from years
of M&A, and it wrote off $562M of T&L goodwill in Q2-26. The Go Forward plan pivoted to organic
growth, divestitures, and buybacks. Recent organic growth (10–12%) is real and self-driven, and it
has divested non-core lines, so the fingerprint is improving — but the goodwill stock proves growth
was largely bought in the past. *Firm-up:* filings detail on recent M&A spend ($230M in H1-26) vs.

### Management Backing — 0.62 / 0.70
CEO Rob Painter is a competent, long-tenured professional operator with a clear strategic
narrative ("Connect & Scale"), credible execution (raised guidance), and strong institutional
backing and governance. Not founder-led, and no exceptional founder conviction; solid professional
stewardship rather than visionary leadership. *Firm-up:* proxy-statement leadership detail and
communication signals.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.50 / 0.70
Mixed: significant stock-based compensation (~$146M/yr, ~4% of share count), which quietly dilutes
owners and is a real leakage the non-GAAP story adds back. Offsetting: substantial net buybacks
(FY25 $863M repurchased; $1B new authorization), paying down to a smaller share count. Several
events were one-time/impairment-driven rather than clean compounding. *Firm-up:* proxy pay design,
hold/horizon structure, and dressing of the net-dilution picture.

### Customer Devotion — 0.62 / 0.70
B2B field software with meaningful subscription/recurring element; high switching costs from
workflow integration (though much of that is lock-in, not devotion). ARR growth (+14%) suggests
retention and expansion, but no formal NRR/NPS is disclosed. *Firm-up:* NRR disclosure, G2/Analyst
peer-review standing, named reference work.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Wonderful-and-fair:** quality bar not met — earnings quality is compromised (reliance on
  non-GAAP, serial amortization and a $562M goodwill impairment; lumpy GAAP EPS), and returns on
  capital are modest (GAAP ROIC ~7% in FY25, inflated to 22% in FY24 by a one-off gain; goodwill
  drags the base). Earnings-quality and returns-on-capital both carry low floors, so this strategy
  scores clearly lower.
- **Cash cow:** a genuine near-tie (0.45 S×C), acknowledged in Overall. I still frame under
  rule-breakers because Trimble retains meaningful reinvestment runway (subscription transition,
  AI, large under-penetrated markets) and is growing ~10%+, so the growth narrative dominates;
  but the cash-generative, buyback-heavy, semi-mature segments are a legitimate cow echo and the
  primary-frame call is close.
- **Freeroll:** no deep-value floor-and-catalyst setup; the stock is a growth/quality re-rating
  play, not a cheap asset-floor bet.

## Overall

Strengths: a genuinely strong franchise in attractive, under-penetrated field-industry software;
high gross margins; re-accelerating recurring growth (ARR $2.5B, +14%); a cleaner balance sheet
with a $1B buyback and improving organic focus. Detractors: growth is moderate (not rule-breaker
explosive); returns on capital are depressed by heavy acquisition goodwill; earnings are heavily
non-GAAP-adjusted with real stock-comp dilution; and T&L strategic review/impairment signals past
M&A overreach. Primary read is rule-breakers, but the leaderboard flags a genuine dual-fit with
cash-cow (both land near 0.45 S×C) — a reasonable tension given Trimble's hybrid profile: a
growing digitization franchise echoing a compounder, yet with cash-generative, buyback-heavy,
semi-mature segments echoing a cow. I frame it under rule-breakers because the growth and
reinvestment narrative is the still-dominant driver. Agent_score 0.55 reflects a solid but not
exceptional mid-teen compounder at a reasonable, not cheap, price.

## Thesis Connections

Trimble is a fit for a "digitization of physical-work industries / AI-enabled workflow software"
thesis — a re-rating and compounding story as construction/ag/geospatial adopt connected,
AI-assisted software. Also relevant to broad cross-border/cyclical exposure: a meaningful
construction/end-market cycle and a mature, hard-asset industry tilt argue for patience on multiple.

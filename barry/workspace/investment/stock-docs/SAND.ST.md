# SAND.ST — Sandvik AB

## Business Overview

Sandvik supplies high-productivity equipment, tools, parts, software and services to mining, infrastructure and manufacturing customers. FY2025 external revenue exposure was 52% mining, 39% manufacturing and 9% infrastructure. The installed equipment base creates recurring aftermarket demand, while cutting tools and manufacturing software are tied more closely to industrial production. Q2 2026 revenue was SEK 36.8bn: Mining SEK 13.7bn, Rock Processing SEK 3.0bn, Machining SEK 15.9bn and Intelligent Manufacturing SEK 4.2bn. This mix combines cyclical equipment sales with a more resilient consumables, service and software stream.[1]

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.72, confidence 0.78

Sandvik has remained profitable, but industrial cyclicality prevents a top-band predictability score. Across 2022–25 net income ranged from SEK 11.2bn to SEK 15.3bn, with no loss year; cumulative FCF was 0.95× cumulative net income and receivables stayed near 15–17% of revenue.[2] Q2 2026 adjusted EPS rose 55%, though recurring restructuring adjustments warrant some caution. Confidence would improve with a ten-year profit-and-cash-conversion series from the FY2025 annual report.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.68, confidence 0.82

TTM FCF was SEK 18.7bn, a 15.3% margin. FY2023–25 FCF margins were 10.6–12.8%, while capex consumed only 20–29% of operating cash flow.[2] The caveat is physical-asset maintenance: capex was only 51% of D&A in 2025, so some apparent cash richness may reflect acquisition-intangible amortisation or temporarily low investment rather than permanently low maintenance needs.

### Fundamental Stability — 0.82, confidence 0.85

The franchise is not a melting ice cube. FY2025 revenue and EPS were only about 4% below their four-year peaks, while FCF was near peak. Q2 2026 then delivered 17% organic order growth, 23% organic revenue growth and a 103% book-to-bill ratio.[1][2] Mining aftermarket, automation and an ageing equipment fleet support durability; automotive and general-industry demand remain cyclical risks.

### Pricing Power — 0.76, confidence 0.83

Sandvik benefits from installed-base switching costs, mission-critical tooling, proprietary materials expertise and scale. Gross margin stayed around 40–41% through 2022–25 and reached an adjusted 42.7% in Q2 2026. Management attributed the quarter's 22.6% adjusted EBITA margin partly to price realization, alongside volume, savings and temporary tungsten timing benefits.[1][2] The moat appears stable, but raw-material pass-through and industrial cyclicality limit pure pricing power.

### Capital Allocation — 0.68, confidence 0.75

From 2022–25 Sandvik paid SEK 26.3bn of dividends, spent SEK 23.5bn on acquisitions and reduced net debt after the acquisition-heavy 2022 year. The dividend consumed 49% of 2025 earnings and was covered 2.1× by FCF; shares outstanding were effectively flat, so buybacks were immaterial.[2] Bolt-ons deepen attractive niches, but goodwill equals 37% of invested capital and ongoing acquisitiveness makes purchase discipline important. Confidence would improve with the FY2025 business-combinations note and post-deal return disclosures.

### Returns on Capital — 0.55, confidence 0.80

ROIC averaged about 12% in 2022–25 (10.6–13.6%), while ROCE averaged about 15%. These are value-creating but below the roughly 20% high-quality benchmark; the small ROE–ROIC gap indicates leverage is not flattering returns. Goodwill is material at 37% of invested capital, and the mechanical latest incremental-ROIC reading is distorted by falling invested capital, so it is not relied upon.[2] Returns are decent and stable rather than exceptional. A clean ex-goodwill ROIC reconciliation from the annual report would firm this up.

### Conservative Debt — 0.82, confidence 0.85

FY2025 net debt/EBITDA was 0.92×, down from 1.40× in 2022; EBIT covered net interest 17.1× and cash plus one year of FCF covered current debt 4.0×. The Q2 2026 report stated financial net debt/EBITDA of 1.0× after the Diemme acquisition.[1][2] This is conservative for a cyclical industrial company. The annual report maturity ladder remains worth checking, but no solvency or liquidity disqualifier is evident.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.78, confidence 0.65

Sandvik's value resides mainly in brands, installed bases, engineering systems and decentralized business areas rather than one individual. CEO Stefan Widing is important but the franchise is institutional. Confidence would improve with the FY2025 governance report's executive succession and bench disclosures.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.72, confidence 0.65

Stable diluted shares and Yahoo's low compensation/governance risk indicators are supportive, and no evidence of value-leaking control or material dilution emerged.[2] Evidence on the exact short- and long-term incentive metrics, vesting periods and executive holding requirements is incomplete. Confidence would improve by reviewing the FY2025 remuneration report.

### Why other strategies lost

Wonderful-and-fair is a genuine secondary fit, but only middling ROIC and a meaningful acquisition component make cash-cow the slightly stronger primary classification. Rule Breakers loses on mature, cyclical organic growth rather than business quality. Freeroll lacks a specific time-bound re-rating catalyst and a separately underwritten downside floor.

## Overall

Sandvik is a durable, conservatively financed industrial franchise with a valuable installed base, improving mix and strong current demand. It fits cash-cow best because cash conversion and dividends are dependable while organic growth and returns on capital are good rather than exceptional. The main detractors are cyclicality, acquisition reliance, material goodwill and a valuation that already prices in substantial quality.

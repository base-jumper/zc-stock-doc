# CSL.AX — CSL Limited

## Business Overview

CSL is a global biopharma company headquartered in Australia. It operates through **CSL Behring** (plasma-derived and recombinant therapies for immunoglobulin, albumin, haemophilia, hereditary angioedema and perioperative bleeding), **CSL Seqirus** (influenza vaccines and pandemic preparedness), and **CSL Vifor** (iron deficiency and nephrology). The company reports in USD but trades on the ASX in AUD.

FY2026 (year ended 30 June 2026) was the promised “reset year”: reported revenue rose 2% to **US$15.8b** (down 1% constant currency), underlying NPATA fell 2% constant currency to **US$3.1b**, and a **reported net loss of US$2.6b** reflected **US$0.8b** one-off pre-tax restructuring costs and **US$7.1b** impairment and related charges (US$5.4b net of tax; $4.8b attributable to CSL shareholders). Operating cash flow held at **US$3.5b** and the total dividend was **maintained at US$2.92/share** (final US$1.62, unfranked; record date 2 October 2026). Management had cut FY26 guidance on 11 May 2026 (interim CEO Gordon Naylor’s 90-day review), flagging ~US$5b of additional impairments; the actual FY26 result was consistent with that update.

The impairment is concentrated in **CSL Vifor** (US$4.9b including full goodwill write-off of US$1.7b and Ferinject/Venofer/Mircera/Velphoro IP) plus **Lengnau PPE (US$0.9b)**, sa-mRNA IP (US$0.6b), Seqirus PPE and other assets. Management guided to only **~US$200m of further FY27 impairments**, suggesting the bulk of the write-down is behind the company. CSL took decisive further action on the portfolio: Behring grew **mid-single digit** in 2H26 (Ig 2H +7% on PCP) while Vifor faces **~25% FY27 revenue decline** (iron generics, VELPHORO TDAPA expiry on 31 December 2026, TAVNEOS EU/EEA withdrawal after revocation), and Seqirus grew seasonal influenza 4% (the only global company to grow in the category).

FY2027 guidance (constant currency, FY26 FX rates): **revenue in line with FY26**, **underlying NPAT growth ~5%**, FX headwind of ~US$50m if current rates hold, capex ~**US$1b**, net debt/EBITDA **1.8x** (within 1.5–2.0x target), and a further **A$1.1b buy-back**. The proposed Seqirus demerger remains **deferred and underway** — no shareholder vote or timetable; CSL is instead executing an internal operational separation and simplification.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.62 / confidence 0.88

CSL has a real and long earnings record, but FY2026 is the messiest statutory year in its modern history. Underlying performance held: underlying NPATA of **US$3.1b** (down 2% constant currency) and underlying NPAT of **US$2.8b** (down 3%), which is what the market prices. The statutory line collapsed to a **US$2.6b net loss (basic loss per share US$5.35)** entirely from one-off restructuring (US$0.8b pre-tax) and **US$7.1b pre-tax impairments**, of which US$5.5b hit the second half. Underlying NPATA per share was **US$6.43** (down 3%), and FY27 guidance of ~5% underlying NPAT growth implies the underlying line stabilises rather than grows strongly.

The caveats are exactly what the trait warns about: recurring “one-off” charges (FY26 restructuring plus a full reassessment of acquired-asset carrying values, including the Vifor goodwill write-off), an elevated underlying tax rate (20.8% vs 15.9%), and cumulative FCF/net income of only **0.63x** over FY2022–FY2026 (FY2025 ~0.85x; FY2026 about 0.3x, flattered by the statutory loss denominator). Management has now taken most of the write-down, and the FY27 guidance is cleaner — but this is a demonstrated, not dependable, earnings record right now. Score 0.62 (was 0.68), confidence up to 0.88 (the FY26 accounts spell out every impairment line).

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.52 / confidence 0.80

Returns are decent, not exceptional, and FY26 reveals how much of the Vifor carrying value was not real. The local returns script shows ROIC of **10.4%, 9.8%, 10.9% and 11.9%** across FY2022–FY2025, with ROE around 14–16%; FY26 statutory returns are meaningless on a loss year, but underlying returns are similar. The full FY26 write-down of **Vifor goodwill (US$1.7b)** plus **US$3.9b of Vifor product IP (Ferinject, Venofer, Mircera, Velphoro, Tavneos)** confirms that the acquisition-era capital base was overstated.

For a healthcare operating company this remains value-creating but not elite: ROIC sits below the 20%+ “wonderful” anchor, and the reset lowers the base against which future returns are measured — which, if the underlying business holds, mechanically improves future ROIC. Score 0.52 (was 0.55), confidence up to 0.80 (the impairment note quantifies the asset-quality problem).

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.72 / confidence 0.72

CSL has meaningful moat characteristics: specialised biologics manufacturing, plasma collection scale, regulatory and quality gatekeepers, clinical trust, and leadership in immunoglobulin. FY26 group gross margin was **53.5%** (54.3% FY25); Behring margin **49.6%** and Seqirus **56.7%**. Behring’s Ig franchise is the anchor: **US$6.25b revenue, 2H26 +7% on PCP** on robust underlying demand, with Albumin down 17% (China cost-containment) and Perioperative Bleeding down 11% (KCENTRA US price pressure).

The mark-down is unchanged: policy and generic competition bite even in specialist medicine. China albumin, US Medicare Part D, Vifor iron (Ferinject/Venofer down ~19% each as generics enter EU/US), TAVNEOS revocation, and KCENTRA price pressure all show the moat is real but not impervious. The balance of evidence is a stable franchise under reimbursement pressure — 0.72 (was 0.75), confidence 0.72.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.55 / confidence 0.75

CSL still has a runway, but it is a mature-business runway rather than a hyper-compounder one. Ig demand (mid-to-high single digit), ANDEMBRY launch momentum (19 markets; HAE +14%), HEMGENIX (+25%), Seqirus influenza differentiation (only global company to grow seasonal revenue), FILSPARI/KAPRUVIA nephrology launches, Horizon 1/2 yield programs, Kankakee and Tullamarine capacity all give CSL places to reinvest — CSL is spending **US$1.5b** expanding Kankakee and guiding **~US$1b FY27 capex**.

The issue is that FY27 group revenue is guided to be **flat** at constant currency and Vifor is guided down **~25%**: the reinvestment is partly defensive (fixing Vifor, replacing generic-eroded iron revenue) rather than pure high-return expansion. FY26 underlying NPAT was down 3% and FY27 grows only ~5%. Runway exists but the payoff keeps moving right — 0.55 (was 0.58), confidence 0.75.

### Capital Allocation — 0.42 / confidence 0.85

This remains the weak spot, but the FY26 numbers show both the failure and the beginning of repair. The **Vifor acquisition destroyed value**: US$1.7b goodwill fully written off plus **US$3.9b of Vifor IP written down** (Ferinject US$1.55b, Venofer US$0.74b, Mircera US$0.30b, Velphoro US$0.18b, Tavneos US$0.17b), with **Lengnau PPE (US$0.93b)** and sa-mRNA IP (US$0.59b) adding to the impairment. The organic-growth script still shows **US$10.5b** cumulative acquisition spend in FY2023 and goodwill jumping from ~US$1.2b to ~US$8.1b.

The repair is real: **US$176m FY26 cost savings** (above target) building to ~US$400m FY27 and ~US$550m FY28, ~1% net share count reduction through the completed **A$1b buy-back (6.4m shares at A$166.16 average)**, a further **A$1.1b (announced A$1.15b) buy-back approved for FY27**, net debt/EBITDA down to **1.8x**, and management guiding FY27 impairments to ~US$200m. Buy-backs executed in a depressed-price range are rational. On balance the stewardship record is still poor (0.42, was 0.35) but confidence rose to 0.85: the audited report now quantifies both the damage and the capital-return discipline.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.75 / confidence 0.80

The balance sheet is in better shape than a year ago and is the strongest part of the reset story. Net debt/EBITDA ended FY26 at **1.8x** (FY25 1.8x, FY23 2.7x; 1H26 2.0x), within the 1.5–2.0x target; net debt down from ~US$9.5b to **~US$8.9b**; interest-bearing liabilities and borrowings of **US$10.9b** against cash of **US$1.5b**; and management reiterated “strong balance sheet and cash flows” while raising the buy-back to A$1.1b for FY27. Debt maturities are long-dated (weighted average ~10 years) and mostly fixed-rate.

Coverage metrics are fine (EBIT interest cover ~9–10x) and there is a US$1.75b undrawn revolver — plus the impairment reduced the asset base, which trims leverage mechanics but not cash obligations. Score 0.75 (was 0.72), confidence 0.80 — a stable, self-funding balance sheet with an announced buy-back.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.80 / confidence 0.65

CSL’s advantage is institutional rather than dependent on one person: plasma collection infrastructure, regulated manufacturing, quality systems, clinical relationships, product portfolios and global scale. The interim-CEO structure (Gordon Naylor since February 2026, following Dr Paul McKenzie’s retirement) and CFO turnover (Ken Lim appointed October 2025) underscore that leadership is in transition, but the asset base carries the franchise.

The confidence is moderate (0.65) because the transformation, Vifor/Behring integration and a permanent-CEO search are all execution-dependent, and a new CEO will reset priorities. Still, the business would not break if one individual left — score unchanged at 0.80.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.45 / confidence 0.60

The FY26 remuneration report finally answers the open questions, and the answers are mixed-to-improving. CSL has now received **two consecutive “strikes”** (2024 and 2025 AGMs) on its remuneration report — a real governance flag. In response, the Board overhauled the framework for FY27: the STI drops individual performance weight (95% company financial measures: NPAT replacing NPATA, revenue, cash flow), and **the LTI replaces ROIC/EPS-growth hurdles with relative TSR** against two peer groups — moves that respond directly to shareholder criticism. Minimum security-holding requirements were tightened from 1 July 2026.

Offsetting positives remain: the A$1b buy-back at depressed prices and the cost program. 42% of shareholders voted against FY25’s report; two strikes show the history is genuinely poor, but the FY27 redesign is responsive. Score stays 0.45, confidence up to 0.60 (was 0.45) — we have read the actual plan now. Watch: whether the new CEO’s package and the FY27 LTI grants embed the same rTSR discipline.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.60 / confidence 0.78

FY26 operating cash flow of **US$3.5b** (down 1%) against underlying NPAT of ~US$2.8b is roughly **1.25x underlying earnings** before capex — and capex of **US$766m (~4.8% of revenue)** is low for the class — the FY27 capex guide of ~US$1b keeps intensity ~6%. The statutory FCF picture is distorted (FCF ~US$1.9b against a US$2.6b reported loss); measured against underlying earnings, FY26 FCF conversion runs about **0.7x** once the ~US$339m restructuring cash costs and working capital are counted.

The local script's history (FCF/net income **0.52x** FY2024, **0.85x** FY2025, **~0.63x** cumulative FY2022–FY2026) remains the usual-state read: real cash generation with working-capital drag and periodic impairment-driven restatements. A cash cow needs FCF that reliably shows up — CSL shows up, but at a conversion that is merely decent. Score 0.60, confidence 0.78 (annual report figures now in hand).

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.66 / confidence 0.85

The core franchise is not a melting ice cube: Ig demand is mid-to-high single digit and growing, HEMGENIX and ANDEMBRY show new-product momentum, and China albumin — the big policy hit — is “stabilising” (price declining at a slower rate, 2H down 5% vs 1H down 27%). Seqirus' US immunisation-rate decline is slowing and the business grew seasonal influenza revenue while the market shrank.

The structural erosion is concentrated in Vifor: iron generics (EU and US), VELPHORO's TDAPA expiry (31 Dec 2026), and TAVNEOS' revoked EU authorisation are durable, not cyclical, and FY27 Vifor revenue is guided down ~25%. That is a real, quantified structural decline in one of three segments — but it is small enough (Vifor ~15% of revenue) that group earning power holds roughly flat and then regrows ~5%. Score 0.66 (was 0.62) — stabilisation is more visible than before, at higher confidence 0.85.

**Why other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair is close on franchise quality, but CSL’s current ROIC, Vifor capital-allocation failure and weak near-term growth make it less clean than a true high-return compounder. Cash-cow fits better: mature, profitable, dividend-paying, cash-generative, and cheap enough that the return case leans on normalized earnings, income and modest multiple recovery rather than exceptional reinvestment. Freeroll has a catalyst package — restructuring, buyback and Seqirus demerger — but downside support is not hard enough: book value and yield do not create a true floor. Rule-breakers fails on growth dynamism.

## Overall

CSL is a high-quality but seriously wounded healthcare franchise that has now taken most of its medicine. The strong parts are durable plasma/rare-disease demand, scale/regulatory barriers, good margins, a de-levered balance sheet (1.8x), and a valuation that has compressed dramatically (11x forward earnings, ~50% off its 2024 high). The weak parts are out on the table: the Vifor capital-allocation failure is quantified (US$4.9b written off), near-term growth is low (flat FY27 revenue, Vifor -25%), and inventory, policy and generics continue to bite in specialist medicine.

At **A$168.30/share** the market has already begun to re-rate the reset: the stock is up ~45% from its June low and ~55% above the 12-month low, but still down ~24% over 12 months and at a forward P/E that prices in continued caution. The base case is now roughly 5% underlying earnings growth (FY27 guidance ~5%, steadier into FY28 as cost savings compound), dividend of US$2.92 (2.8% yield at current price/A$), and a further A$1.1b buy-back. The case is no longer heroic, but it leans on delivery: FY27 must actually deliver that ~5% growth, and the Vifor decline must not leak much beyond guidance.

## Thesis Connections

CSL connects to durable healthcare demand: ageing populations, chronic immune deficiency treatment, rare-disease biologics, plasma supply infrastructure, and vaccine/pandemic preparedness. It is less a frontier biotech bet than a global healthcare infrastructure compounder trying to regain operational sharpness.

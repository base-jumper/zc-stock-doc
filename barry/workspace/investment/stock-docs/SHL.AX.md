# SHL.AX — Sonic Healthcare Limited

## Business Overview

Sonic Healthcare is an Australian-headquartered global medical diagnostics group. It provides pathology/clinical laboratory services, radiology and clinical services to doctors, hospitals, health systems and patients across Australia, New Zealand, the US, Germany, Switzerland, Belgium and the UK. The investment case is not hyper-growth; it is a mature diagnostics network with entrenched local lab positions, recurring medical demand, a long dividend record and a bolt-on acquisition model.

FY2026 revenue was A$10,867m (+13%), including 5% organic growth, with underlying EBITDA of A$1,933m (+11%), underlying net profit of A$621m (+17%) and underlying EPS of 125.6c (+14%); statutory EPS was 123.0c (+15%). Sonic operates across nine countries and 11 markets, serving over 140 million patients a year. FY2026 guidance was achieved. Management has guided FY2027 underlying EBITDA of A$1.95-2.03b constant currency (excluding A$30m of digital/AI transformation costs), with depreciation A$810-825m, amortisation A$90-95m, interest up 6% and a ~27% effective tax rate.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.60 / confidence 0.80

Sonic has remained profitable through the available Yahoo history: net income A$1.46b in FY2022, A$685m in FY2023, A$511m in FY2024, A$514m in FY2025 and A$608m statutory (A$621m underlying) in FY2026. Cash backing is better than reported profit, with cumulative FCF/net income of about 1.31x over FY2022-25 and operating cash flow up 8% in FY2026. Predictability is improving: FY2026 marked the third straight year of earnings growth off the COVID-normalised base, and underlying EPS of 125.6c (+14%) confirms the recovery path. The FY2022 COVID peak still distorts the five-year record, but a smooth uptrend has now been established. Confidence would firm up with a 10-year pre/post-COVID EPS and FCF bridge from annual reports.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.62 / confidence 0.78

The business throws off real cash, but not at elite cash-cow margins. FY2025 FCF was A$834m, FCF margin 8.7%; FY2024 was A$492m, 5.5%; FY2023 was A$974m, 12.0%; FY2022 was A$1.85b, 19.8%. Cash from operations rose 8% to A$1.405b in FY2026, with capex of A$631m (including A$280m of strategic property investments). FY2026 normalised FCF is roughly A$775m (about A$1.61/share on ~481m diluted shares), plus the non-recurring Brisbane lab sale gain this year; FY2025 FCF was A$834m (~A$1.73/share). That is distributable cash, but the capex and lab footprint keep it from being a capital-light annuity. The Brisbane hub lab sale-leaseback adds a capital-management tailwind. Confidence would improve with management's growth/maintenance capex split and a longer FCF history.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.72 / confidence 0.80

The core demand driver is durable: ageing populations, chronic disease management, specialist medicine and health-system outsourcing all support ongoing diagnostic volume. FY2026 organic growth was 5%, matching FY2025, and management expects organic growth to remain strong in FY2027. The post-COVID margin drag has now been worked through: FY2026 EBITDA was a record, revenue hit A$10.87b, and margins are recovering via operating leverage and synergies. The remaining soft spots are regional: Swiss fee cuts (~CHF 20m headwind for FY2027), delayed UK NHS contract integration, and an ongoing US operating review after prior payor-contract and Change Healthcare disruptions. Confidence would firm up with segment-level volume/price/mix disclosure and evidence that US restructuring restores margin.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.64 / confidence 0.72

Sonic has genuine local moats: scale lab networks, hospital and GP relationships, accreditation, clinical reputation and logistics density. Management describes leading market positions in seven countries and the company is one of the world's largest diagnostics operators. FY2026 showed strong growth in advanced diagnostics, a higher-margin segment, which supports quality of revenue. But pricing power is moderated by public/private payors and regulation: Swiss fee cuts, potential US PAMA cuts (excluded from FY2027 guidance), competitive tendering and reimbursement changes constrain how freely diagnostics companies can lift prices. Operating margin at ~17.8% underlying EBITDA margin remains well below the FY2022 peak. Confidence would improve with country-by-country market share and reimbursement exposure.

### Capital Allocation — 0.60 / confidence 0.68

Sonic has a long progressive dividend record, with FY2026 dividends of A$1.08/share, up 1 cent and franked to 60%, and FY2027 guidance-consistent earnings now provide better coverage than FY2025's ~102% payout. The concern is M&A and transformation spend. LADR (Germany, €423m) completed in July 2025 is integrating on schedule with synergies realising; Switzerland synergies continue to land. Capex of A$631m includes A$280m of strategic property investment, partly offset by the Brisbane lab sale-leaseback. Management also commenced a ~A$30m/yr, three-year digital and AI transformation program, which will weigh on near-term earnings before benefits. These moves look strategic, but goodwill is high and returns need proof. Confidence would firm up with post-deal synergy delivery and acquisition multiples.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.38 / confidence 0.77

This is the main quality weakness. Yahoo-derived ROIC was 15.1% in FY2022, then 7.0%, 4.8% and 5.3% in FY2025; Sonic's annual report gives after-tax ROIC of 5.9% in FY2025 and 6.5% in FY2024. FY2026 statutory profit up 18% on a broadly stable capital base should lift ROIC modestly. Goodwill is a large share of capital, which is exactly the penalty for an acquisition-led model. The business creates value operationally, but not at the high-return compounder level. Confidence would improve by computing returns on tangible operating capital and incremental returns from LADR and other recent deals.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.68 / confidence 0.80

The balance sheet is manageable, not pristine. Sonic's covenant-defined FY2025 net debt/EBITDA was 2.1x versus a <3.5x covenant limit; interest cover was 10.1x versus a >3.25x covenant limit; gearing was 24.7% versus a <55% note covenant limit. The Yahoo script shows net debt/EBITDA of 1.67x on FY2025 annual numbers excluding some lease/snapshot differences, with EBIT interest cover about 9.0x. FY2026 interest expense rose 19% to A$188m and management guides interest up ~6% in FY2027, while debt has been managed down with total debt at A$5.17b versus A$7.21b at FY2024. Debt is appropriate for a stable healthcare services group, but acquisitions and rising interest expense limit flexibility. Confidence would firm up with the exact post-LADR debt and maturity profile.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.78 / confidence 0.76

The thesis is not dependent on one charismatic founder. Sonic has a federated operating model, medical leadership culture, local subsidiary autonomy and experienced regional management teams. Dr Jim Newcombe, CEO since late 2025, has now delivered a on-guidance FY2026 in his first nine months and reaffirmed the investment proposition (scale/diversification, Medical Leadership, operational excellence). CEO execution matters, especially for acquisitions and US restructuring, but the network, relationships, systems and clinical reputation should survive leadership transition. Confidence would firm up with a deeper succession/bench review from the annual report governance section.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.62 / confidence 0.60

The alignment read is incomplete. Insider ownership is material at roughly 8% per Yahoo, governance-risk flags are generally low except audit risk, and sellers of LADR accepted almost 14m Sonic shares, which helps align that acquired team. But without a full remuneration-plan read, the pay metrics, hurdle rigour and dilution discipline are not fully assessed. Confidence would firm up by reviewing the FY2026 remuneration report and recent insider transaction history.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair loses on low ROIC and only moderate reinvestment runway; this is not a high-return compounder. Rule-breakers loses because diagnostics is a mature, regulated healthcare service category with modest growth, not an emerging winner-takes-most wave. Freeroll has a reasonable yield/book-value floor but lacks a concrete ≤4-year catalyst; the re-rate mostly depends on earnings recovery and sentiment.

## Overall

Sonic is a decent cash-cow candidate, not a great one. The positives are durable diagnostics demand, global scale, real FCF, a long dividend habit, a delivered record FY2026 (guidance achieved, underlying EPS +14%) and a still-undemanding price around 16x forward EPS / 12.4x current FCF. The negatives remain: ROIC is low after years of acquisitions, goodwill is large, debt is moderate with rising interest expense, productivity growth is modest, and the US business is in review with potential PAMA cuts ahead. FY2027 guidance (+4-7% underlying EBITDA at mid-point) is steady, not exciting. This remains investable if Nick wants healthcare-income/defensive exposure, but it is still not an obviously superior compounding engine.

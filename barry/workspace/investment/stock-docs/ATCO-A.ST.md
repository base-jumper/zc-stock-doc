# ATCO-A.ST — Atlas Copco AB

## Business Overview

Atlas Copco sells compressors, vacuum equipment, industrial assembly and vision systems, and portable power equipment, plus a large installed-base service offering. Compressor Technique is the core profit engine: FY2025 revenue was SEK 77.1bn with a 24.7% operating margin, versus SEK 36.7bn/18.4% for Vacuum, SEK 26.4bn/17.8% for Industrial, and SEK 29.0bn/16.7% for Power. The decentralized model combines global product platforms with local sales and service in about 180 countries. Recurring aftermarket demand, niche leadership and customer productivity savings support unusually durable industrial economics.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.84 / 0.92 confidence

FY2022–25 revenue ranged from SEK 141.3bn to SEK 176.8bn and operating margins held near 20–22%; annual FCF was positive throughout and reached SEK 26.4bn in 2025. FY2025 revenue and EPS fell 4.8% and 11.1% amid currency and softer demand, but Q2 2026 organic orders rose 26%, organic revenue rose 8%, and the operating margin remained 20.6%. Cash conversion is strong despite normal industrial working-capital swings. Interpretation: cyclical, but highly profitable and cash-generative through the cycle.

### Returns on Capital — 0.88 / 0.93 confidence

Reported ROCE was 24% in FY2025 and Q2 2026 LTM against an 8% internal WACC benchmark. Yahoo-derived ROIC was about 20–24% in FY2022–25; Compressor Technique's FY2025 ROCE was 78%. Returns remain comfortably above the cost of capital despite acquisition goodwill and the 2025 slowdown, supporting a durable-moat reading.

### Pricing Power — 0.84 / 0.82 confidence

The group sustained a 20.6% Q2 2026 operating margin while currency was a 3% revenue headwind and acquisitions diluted margin; management attributed the adjusted-margin improvement to volume, price and mix. The dense installed base, mission-critical equipment, service reach and energy-efficiency savings reduce total ownership cost and support price realization. Direct price-versus-volume disclosure is limited; confidence would improve with the FY2026 results' full organic sales bridge.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.82 / 0.82 confidence

Semiconductor vacuum demand, energy-efficient compressors, automation/vision, electrified portable equipment and service penetration provide multiple long-lived growth vectors. Q2 2026 orders grew across most industries and regions, led by semiconductor equipment, while the group continues internal R&D and disciplined bolt-on M&A. The runway is broad, although mature compressor markets and cyclicality cap the score. Confidence would improve with a current capital-markets-day breakdown of addressable markets and organic growth by platform.

### Capital Allocation — 0.76 / 0.80 confidence

Atlas Copco balances organic investment, bolt-on acquisitions and dividends. FY2025 FCF was SEK 26.4bn; cash acquisitions were SEK 11.6bn and dividends paid SEK 14.6bn. The proposed FY2025 distribution was SEK 5.00 per share, including SEK 2.00 additional distribution. Net share repurchases largely hedge incentive plans rather than shrink the share count. Leverage remains modest, but serial M&A and SEK 51.2bn of goodwill make deal discipline the main risk. Confidence would improve by reviewing the FY2025 business-combinations note deal by deal and subsequent impairment record.

### Conservative Debt — 0.90 / 0.90 confidence

At June 2026 net debt/EBITDA was 0.5x, net debt/equity 21%, and interest-bearing liabilities had a 4.1-year average maturity. Q2 interest expense was covered many times by operating profit, and the A+/A1 ratings provide ample refinancing capacity. Lease and pension claims are visible and immaterial relative to cash generation. This is conservative financing for a cyclical industrial company.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.88 / 0.80 confidence

The advantage is institutional: a century-old portfolio, decentralized operating companies, global service infrastructure and repeatable acquisition processes. CEO Vagner Rego matters, but the business has survived many leadership transitions and does not depend on one inventor or customer relationship. Investor AB holds 22.4% of votes, providing stable oversight without individual-founder dependence. Confidence would improve with the latest governance report's succession and executive-bench discussion.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.72 / 0.72 confidence

Long-term share-related incentives and Investor AB's 17.1% capital stake support long-horizon ownership, while share transactions primarily hedge plan delivery and diluted share count has been broadly stable. The score is restrained because public summary data do not establish the exact performance metrics, hurdle rigor, executive holding requirements or dilution cost. Confidence would improve through a full review of the FY2025 remuneration report and AGM incentive-plan resolution; this is a Watch item.

Wonderful-and-fair wins: proven earnings, high returns, moat and reinvestment runway fit a compounder. Cash-cow's two additional gate readings are good (FCF generation 0.74; fundamental stability 0.82), but substantial high-return reinvestment opportunities make the income-harvest framing inferior. Freeroll is ruled out by weak downside support (0.12), no discrete catalyst (0.10), and no persuasive mispricing (0.18). Rule-breakers is ruled out by mature rather than exceptional durable growth (0.42), notwithstanding a top-dog position (0.88) and strong advantage (0.85); organic growth is diluted by frequent acquisitions and direct customer-devotion evidence is thin.

## Overall

Atlas Copco is an exceptional industrial franchise: high returns, resilient margins, conservative leverage and several secular growth vectors. The principal detractors are a premium valuation near a 52-week high, industrial/semiconductor cyclicality, and execution risk from frequent acquisitions. Quality is investable; the current price offers only a middling expected return.

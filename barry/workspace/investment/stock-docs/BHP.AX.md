# BHP.AX — BHP Group Limited

## Business Overview

BHP is a global diversified miner whose earnings are now dominated by copper and Western Australian iron ore, supplemented by steelmaking coal, energy coal and the developing Jansen potash business. It sells largely index-priced commodities, so profit is driven by realised prices, ore grade, production volume, unit cost, royalties and capital intensity rather than conventional brand pricing.

The latest complete financial year is FY2025: US$51.3 billion revenue, US$26.0 billion underlying EBITDA at a 53% margin, US$10.2 billion underlying attributable profit, US$18.7 billion operating cash flow, company-defined free cash flow of US$5.3 billion and 20.6% underlying ROCE. In H1 FY2026, stronger copper prices lifted underlying EBITDA 25% to US$15.5 billion; copper generated 51% of Group EBITDA, net operating cash flow was US$9.4 billion, free cash flow US$2.9 billion, net debt US$14.7 billion and the interim dividend US$0.73 per share.

FY2026 operations finished strongly. BHP produced 1.953 Mt copper, record iron ore of 264.7 Mt on its reporting basis, 18.6 Mt steelmaking coal and 16.4 Mt energy coal. WAIO remained the lowest-cost major iron ore producer and all major assets were expected to finish within unit-cost guidance. The growth pivot is copper and potash: Jansen Stage 1 was 84% complete and targets first potash in mid-CY2027, while BHP is developing options at Escondida, Copper South Australia, Vicuña, Resolution and Cerro Colorado. Full FY2026 financial results are due on 18 August 2026 and were not available for this analysis.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.60 / confidence 0.90

BHP has demonstrated real earning power, but not predictable earnings. Yahoo's comparable series shows net income of US$30.9 billion in FY2022, US$12.9 billion in FY2023, US$7.9 billion in FY2024 and US$9.0 billion in FY2025; H1 FY2026 then re-accelerated with higher copper prices. No year in that window was loss-making, and BHP says its EBITDA margin has averaged above 50% for 20 years, which supports asset quality. The amplitude of the cycle, however, means a shareholder could not have forecast earnings three years ahead with normal compounder precision.

Cash backing is strong. Yahoo-derived cumulative FCF was about 0.98 times cumulative net income across FY2022–FY2025, while receivables remained around 8–9% of revenue. Statutory and underlying figures diverge because of recurring commodity-company exceptional items: Samarco, WA Nickel, asset disposals and, for FY2026, the expected US$2.3 billion Jansen impairment. These are disclosed rather than hidden, but they make headline profit less clean. The score therefore recognises genuine cash earnings while penalising cyclicality and adjustment dependence.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.76 / confidence 0.91

ROIC is the appropriate primary metric for this capital-heavy operator. Yahoo-derived ROIC was 40.7% in FY2022, 27.6% in FY2023, 21.1% in FY2024 and 18.1% in FY2025—an excellent average but a declining path from a commodity-price peak. BHP's own underlying ROCE was 20.6% in FY2025 and 23.6% in H1 FY2026; in that half, WAIO earned 43% and Escondida 48% ROCE. Goodwill was only about 2% of invested capital, and the latest ROE–ROIC gap was small, so leverage and acquisition accounting are not manufacturing the operating return.

The caveat is incremental return. Capital employed has risen while profits remain below FY2022, producing a weak mechanical incremental-ROIC read; Jansen, Vicuña and copper projects are consuming capital before contributing. The evidence supports high returns on mature Tier 1 assets, but not yet equally high returns on every new dollar.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.56 / confidence 0.86

BHP has little direct pricing power: most copper, iron ore and coal sales are linked to market indices or spot prices, with quality and location differentials. FY2026 realised copper prices rose 35% and WAIO iron ore prices 3% because markets moved, not because BHP independently increased prices. That prevents a high score.

The moat is instead a cost-and-asset advantage. WAIO has been the lowest-cost major iron ore producer for six years; H1 FY2026 C1 cost was US$17.66/t, and planned infrastructure targets sustainable production above 305 Mtpa with medium-term unit cost below US$17.50/t. Escondida, Copper SA and Antamina add scale, long lives and by-product credits. The moat appears stable, supported by logistics networks, resource depth and the BHP Operating System, but it is exposed to grade decline, cost inflation, higher royalties, Simandou supply and execution risk. BHP can out-earn higher-cost rivals through the cycle; it cannot dictate the commodity price.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.64 / confidence 0.84

There is substantial room to reinvest: capital and exploration expenditure is guided to about US$11 billion in FY2026 and FY2027 and US$10 billion annually on average in FY2028–FY2030, with about 70% of medium-term capital aimed at copper and potash. Management targets 3–4% attributable copper-equivalent production CAGR from FY2027 to FY2035. The routes are tangible—Escondida debottlenecking/new concentrator, Copper SA mine and smelter expansions, Vicuña, Resolution, Jansen, WAIO Ministers North and port/rail productivity.

Returns are mixed rather than uniformly compounder-grade. The Escondida new concentrator indicates 13–16% IRR, while WAIO Ministers North and the sixth car dumper indicate returns above 30%. Against that, Jansen Stage 1 expenditure rose from an original US$5.7 billion to US$8.4 billion and triggered an expected US$2.3 billion impairment; Vicuña remains an early, complex binational project. BHP has a long runway, but much of it is capital intensive, permitting-dependent and only moderately above the cost of capital.

### Capital Allocation — 0.60 / confidence 0.90

The Capital Allocation Framework imposes useful discipline: maintain the balance sheet, invest in maintenance and attractive projects, pay at least 50% of underlying attributable profit as dividends, then allocate excess cash among growth, additional dividends, buybacks and portfolio actions. BHP has returned more than US$110 billion since the framework began in 2016. FY2025's US$1.10 dividend represented a 55% full-year payout; the H1 FY2026 dividend was a 60% payout. Across FY2022–FY2025, Yahoo cash-flow data shows dividends consumed about 76% of cumulative FCF and the share count rose only 0.2%.

Recent portfolio choices are mixed. Sequencing Escondida and Copper SA projects, selling non-core assets and using the WAIO-power and Antamina-silver transactions to unlock more than US$6 billion show active capital recycling. However, Jansen's overrun and impairment are serious blemishes, the 2023 OZ Minerals acquisition was large, and Vicuña adds execution risk. Management has generally created per-share value and kept funding conservative, but the current project slate must deliver before the score can move higher.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.88 / confidence 0.94

Debt is conservative for a cyclical miner. The latest Yahoo snapshot showed net debt/EBITDA of about 0.6x, EBIT interest coverage of 16.2x, a 1.65 current ratio and a 1.23 quick ratio. BHP reported A1/stable and A/stable credit ratings at H1 FY2026. The FY2026 operational review indicated June 2026 net debt of about US$9 billion, below the US$10–20 billion target range after asset/streaming proceeds.

Material liabilities have been considered rather than ignored. At June 2025, closure and rehabilitation provisions were US$10.5 billion and the Samarco provision US$5.8 billion; the latter remains estimation-sensitive and BHP is a secondary obligor for its share where Samarco cannot fund payments. These claims are large, but provisioned and currently manageable against BHP's cash generation, liquidity and low leverage. They do not create a solvency disqualifier.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.88 / confidence 0.85

BHP's competitive advantage is institutional: ore bodies, mine/rail/port systems, technical centres, the BHP Operating System, commercial relationships and a deep operating bench. It has no founder control or super-voting structure. Mike Henry handed the CEO role to former President Americas Brandon Craig on 1 July 2026, while the broader executive team largely remained intact. That visible internal succession supports the score. Craig is untested as Group CEO, but losing any one executive should be a disruption rather than a thesis break.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.82 / confidence 0.95

The 2025 Remuneration Report is strongly aligned on structure. The annual Cash and Deferred Plan was weighted 50% to ROCE, 25% to safety/sustainability and 25% to group/personal measures; two-thirds of the award is deferred into equity for two and five years. The LTIP has a five-year performance period and relative-TSR hurdles against metals/mining peers and the MSCI World Index. Malus/clawback applies, the CEO must hold five times salary in shares and other executives three times, and hedging of required/unvested holdings is prohibited.

Soft spots remain. Insider ownership is low in percentage terms, most equity exposure is granted rather than bought, and the FY2025 CEO CDP still paid 110% of target despite the Jansen cost update—although the report states Jansen reduced relevant outcomes and the five-year LTIP vested only 33% after 85% BHP TSR. Overall, doing what the plan rewards should usually improve long-term per-share value.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.60 / confidence 0.88

For the near-tied cash-cow lens, FCF is defined as operating cash flow less capital expenditure. Yahoo reports US$26.3 billion in FY2022, US$12.0 billion in FY2023, US$11.8 billion in FY2024 and US$9.3 billion in FY2025, with margins of 40%, 22%, 21% and 18%. The four-year average margin was 25.5% and cumulative FCF conversion was about 98% of net income, but the direction was down. The latest TTM statement through December 2025 showed US$10.3 billion FCF and a 19.1% margin.

The cash is genuine but not capital-light. FY2025 capex consumed 50% of operating cash flow and equalled 1.7x D&A, so BHP is investing rather than harvesting assets. Management separates roughly US$3 billion annual baseline sustaining capital, about US$1 billion deferred stripping, lumpy non-recurring sustaining projects and growth capital; company-defined FY2025 FCF was a stricter US$5.3 billion after those cash-flow adjustments. BHP generates large distributable cash, but high forward capital intensity keeps it out of the strongest cash-cow band.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.66 / confidence 0.88

The latest financial trajectory is volatile, not a simple secular decline. FY2025 revenue, EPS and FCF remained well below the FY2022 commodity peak, yet FY2026 delivered record iron ore, about 2 Mt copper, stronger realised prices and low unit costs. The weakness is mostly cyclical price normalisation plus reinvestment rather than asset obsolescence.

Long-term demand for copper is supported by electrification, grids and data centres; potash by food security; and steel inputs by urbanisation and infrastructure. The threats are material: Chinese steel production is plateauing, Simandou raises iron-ore supply, coal faces policy/royalty pressure, copper grades decline, and mining carries permitting, community and rehabilitation risk. The low-cost assets and portfolio shift toward copper/potash make the earning base durable, but not stable enough for the top band.

**Strategy verdict:** Wonderful-and-fair wins mechanically at about 0.69 quality / 0.89 confidence, but cash-cow is a genuine near-tie at about 0.68 / 0.89. The primary framing is therefore a capital-heavy, cyclical compounder with a substantial distribution stream—not a pure cash cow. Rule-breakers loses because BHP is a mature commodity incumbent: `right-place-and-time` is only 0.45 and `durable-growth` 0.43 despite a 0.88 top-dog position. `management-backing` confidence is 0.80 and irreducible for now because Brandon Craig has no Group-CEO record; revisit after FY2027 results. Freeroll loses because A$61.35 offers no hard floor (`downside-support` 0.18) and no differentiated mispricing case or forced re-rating catalyst (`catalyst` 0.35). Catalyst confidence is 0.78 and the specific cheap source that would firm it up is the 18 August 2026 FY2026 result; if it reveals materially higher sustainable FCF, reassess the setup.

## Overall

BHP is an unusually strong miner: Tier 1, long-life assets; world-leading copper scale; the lowest-cost major iron-ore position; high mature-asset returns; disciplined leverage; and a credible portfolio shift toward copper and potash. The universal disqualifier screen is clear: audited accounts are usable, solvency is strong, provisions are disclosed, there is no abusive controlling shareholder and no imminent business-model prohibition.

The stock is not a classic wonderful business. It remains a price taker, earnings are difficult to forecast, sustaining and growth capital are high, and Jansen shows that even BHP can destroy project value. Most importantly, the share price has risen about 48% over 12 months and sits close to the 52-week high immediately before FY2026 results. The visible quality is more than reflected in the valuation. Current stance: **watch, do not chase at A$61.35**.

## Thesis Connections

BHP connects directly to electrification, grid build-out and data-centre copper demand, and indirectly to global infrastructure through iron ore and food security through potash. It is the world's largest copper producer and has one of the deepest brownfield/greenfield copper option sets. The connection is macro- and commodity-dependent: BHP captures upside if supply remains constrained, but it does not own the end-customer relationship or set copper prices.

## Watch

- **FY2026 results on 18 August 2026:** replace preliminary financials, normalised FCF/share, dividend and valuation immediately.
- **Jansen Stage 1:** hold the revised US$8.4 billion budget, avoid further impairment and deliver first potash in mid-CY2027; test ramp economics and unit cost.
- **Copper execution:** FY2027 Escondida guidance falls to 1.0–1.1 Mt as grade declines; monitor recovery initiatives, Spence ore complexity, Copper SA projects and Vicuña FID.
- **Capital allocation under Brandon Craig:** confirm the new CEO preserves return thresholds and does not pursue expensive large-scale M&A.
- **WAIO durability:** keep unit cost near guidance while lifting sustainable capacity above 305 Mtpa; monitor Simandou and Chinese steel demand.
- **Samarco and closure liabilities:** track cash payments, provision revisions and UK/Brazil legal developments against the balance-sheet thesis.
- **Entry valuation:** reconsider below roughly 17x normalised P/FCF or after a durable FCF step-up—not merely after a higher spot commodity price.

# EDP.LS — EDP, S.A.

## Business Overview

EDP is a Portugal-headquartered integrated utility built around three pillars: EDP Renewables, electricity networks, and flexible generation/client solutions. Its portfolio is global, but the earnings base is still anchored in Iberia, North America and South America. The 2025 annual report shows **€5.0b recurring EBITDA**, split roughly **39% EDPR, 31% electricity networks and 30% Flex Gen & Clients**, with **€1.279b recurring net profit**, **€15.4b net debt**, **20.9% FFO/net debt**, and a proposed **€0.205/share** dividend.

Operationally, this is a mature utility/infrastructure compounder rather than a classic high-return capital-light business. EDP reports **32.7 GW** installed capacity, **87% renewable installed capacity**, roughly **90% renewable generation**, **391k km** of distribution/transmission networks, and **12.0m** connected network customers. The 2026–28 plan targets about **€12b** gross investment, including **€7.5b** in EDPR and **€3.6b** in electricity networks, while using asset rotation and disposals to help fund growth.

The investable question is whether a regulated/contracted, capital-heavy clean-power utility can compound at acceptable low-risk returns from electrification, renewables and grid investment. It is not a clean cash cow today because GAAP free cash flow is negative after growth capex, and it is not a classic wonderful business because returns on capital are modest. The best-fit frame is therefore **wonderful-and-fair, but low-conviction and infrastructure-adjusted**.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.55 / confidence 0.75

EDP has demonstrated profitability, but reported profits are not perfectly smooth. Yahoo annual data shows common net income of roughly **€0.68b, €0.95b, €0.80b, €1.15b** for 2022–2025, with diluted EPS rising from **€0.17** to **€0.28**. The 2025 annual report reports **€1.279b recurring net profit** and **€1.150b reported net profit**. No loss years appear in the recent Yahoo window.

The weakness is cash backing. Yahoo’s cash-flow data shows FCF of about **€0.28b, -€3.22b, -€1.31b, -€1.00b** from 2022–2025 because capex is heavy. That is not necessarily accounting-quality failure — EDP is investing into regulated networks and renewables — but it means earnings are not readily distributable owner earnings. Confidence would improve with a longer 7–10 year through-cycle reconciliation of recurring net profit to maintenance-only free cash flow.

### Returns on Capital — 0.42 / confidence 0.80

This is the main reason the score is capped. Yahoo-derived ROIC/ROCE are steady but modest: ROIC around **6.8%, 6.8%, 4.6%, 6.8%** for 2022–2025, with latest ROCE about **7.1%**. For a regulated utility that may still clear its cost of capital, but it is not the high-return profile normally wanted from a wonderful-and-fair compounder.

The positive read is durability: returns appear relatively stable, and EDP’s capital base is backed by regulated networks, contracted renewable assets and long-duration infrastructure demand. The negative read is arithmetic: heavy reinvestment at mid-single-digit returns produces slow compounding unless leverage, regulation and asset rotation are handled well.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.62 / confidence 0.65

EDP has infrastructure-style moats rather than consumer-style pricing power. Electricity networks are regulated monopolies; renewable and hydro/flex generation assets rely on site control, interconnection, PPAs, scale and operating capability; client solutions have less structural moat. The 2025 annual report notes updated regulatory frameworks for Portugal **2026–2029** and Spain **2026–2031**, plus continued network investment needs, which supports durability.

The company cannot simply raise prices at will: regulated returns, wholesale power prices, auctions, PPAs and political/regulatory constraints set much of the economics. I score the moat as real but bounded — strong enough to support durable infrastructure earnings, not strong enough to create exceptional pricing power.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.60 / confidence 0.65

The runway is real. EDP’s 2026–28 business plan targets **~€12b** gross investment, including **~€7.5b** in EDPR and **€3.6b** in electricity networks, explicitly tied to electrification, data-centre power demand, renewables, BESS, grid modernization and Iberian network capex. Management targets EBITDA rising from **~€4.9b** in 2025 to **~€5.2b** by 2028 and net income to **~€1.3b** by 2028.

The caveat is the rate of return. This is a long runway, but it is capital-intensive and regulated/contracted; the incremental dollar is unlikely to earn capital-light software-like returns. Asset rotation can improve equity returns, but it also introduces execution risk and dependence on transaction markets.

### Capital Allocation — 0.50 / confidence 0.70

EDP is trying to balance growth, leverage and shareholder returns. The annual report says 2025 gross investment was **€3.9b**, net debt fell to **€15.4b**, and asset rotation/disposals contributed **€1.8b** of proceeds. The 2026–28 plan targets **~€5b** of asset rotation proceeds and **~€1b** of disposals while keeping net debt around **€16b** in 2025–2026 and reducing it toward **€15b** by 2028.

That is rational on paper: recycle mature assets, fund core networks/renewables, preserve BBB credit quality, and pay a moderate dividend. The offset is that dividends are not covered by simple GAAP FCF during heavy capex years, and diluted shares rose roughly **5%** from 2022 to 2025 in Yahoo’s data. This is disciplined infrastructure capital recycling, not a pristine self-funded compounder.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.52 / confidence 0.80

EDP is leveraged, but not obviously distressed for a regulated/contracted utility. The annual report gives **€15.4b** net debt and **20.9% FFO/net debt** in 2025, with ratings maintained by the major agencies. Yahoo annual data gives net debt / EBITDA of about **3.4–3.8×** over 2022–2025, latest **3.57×**, and EBIT interest coverage around **3.4×**.

For this business type, that is stretched but manageable: stable infrastructure cash flows can carry more leverage than cyclicals, but coverage is not fat and simple FCF is negative after capex. The watch item is whether asset-rotation proceeds and FFO growth keep pace with capex, dividends and interest costs.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.75 / confidence 0.60

The thesis does not depend on one irreplaceable person. EDP is a large regulated utility/infrastructure platform with assets, licences, operating systems and institutional processes. CEO Miguel Stilwell d’Andrade matters for strategy and capital allocation, but the moat is embedded in assets and regulation rather than an individual founder.

Confidence is moderate because I did not do a deep succession/bench review. Still, the business should survive leadership changes without the investment case breaking.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 / confidence 0.45

I have limited remuneration detail in this pass. The broad evidence is mixed-positive: the 2026–28 plan emphasises FFO/net debt, EBITDA, net income, efficiency, dividend policy and credit quality — owner-relevant metrics for a utility — but I have not inspected the full remuneration scorecard, executive ownership, holding requirements or payout hurdles.

Until verified from the remuneration report, I score this as middling: likely institutionally reasonable, but not proven strongly owner-aligned. Confidence would improve by reviewing the compensation metrics and insider ownership directly.

### Why the other strategies lost

**Cash cow** loses because free cash flow generation is weak after capex. A mature utility with a 4%+ dividend looks cow-like, but Yahoo shows negative FCF in 2023–2025 and capex above operating cash flow, so the dividend is supported by recurring earnings, asset rotation and financing capacity rather than clean surplus FCF. **Freeroll** loses because there is no hard downside floor close to price and no specific ≤4-year re-rating catalyst; the shares trade around **1.65× book** and the dividend yield is not FCF-covered on a simple basis. **Rule breakers** loses because EDP is mature infrastructure, not an emerging hyper-growth category winner.

## Overall

EDP is a durable, strategically relevant utility exposed to real electrification and grid-investment tailwinds. The positives are regulated/network assets, a large renewables platform, a BBB-style balance-sheet discipline, a visible dividend, and a multi-year investment runway. The negatives are equally clear: low-to-mid single-digit ROIC, heavy capex, negative simple FCF, leverage, and dependence on asset rotation/regulatory outcomes.

This is not a screaming bargain or a high-quality compounding machine. It is a reasonable infrastructure watchlist name where expected return is mostly dividend plus modest earnings growth, with limited room for underwriting error.

## Thesis Connections

Fits an electrification/grid-modernisation thesis: renewables, batteries, flexible generation, distribution networks, and data-centre/load growth all sit inside the long-term power-demand stack. It is a conservative infrastructure expression of that theme rather than an explosive AI/data-centre power winner.

## Watch

- 2026–28 plan execution: EBITDA path toward **~€5.2b** by 2028 and net income toward **~€1.3b**.
- Net debt and FFO/net debt: management targets net debt down toward **~€15b** and FFO/net debt toward **~22%** by 2028.
- Asset rotation: confirm proceeds and gains are achieved without selling the crown jewels or relying on hot transaction markets.
- Simple FCF vs dividend: watch whether heavy capex keeps the dividend dependent on financing/asset sales.
- Regulatory frameworks in Portugal and Spain; any allowed-return pressure or adverse political intervention.
- U.S. renewables/data-centre demand: whether EDPR converts pipeline into contracted, return-accretive capacity.
- Remuneration/ownership detail: verify incentive alignment from the annual remuneration report.

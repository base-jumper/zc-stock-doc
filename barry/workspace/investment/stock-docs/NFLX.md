# NFLX — Netflix, Inc.

## Business Overview

Netflix is the global scaled streaming entertainment platform. The core business is paid memberships, supplemented by advertising, selective live events, games, video podcasts, licensing/partnerships, and price/mix expansion. It is no longer the old high-cash-burn streaming land-grab story: Netflix now has mature profitability, large free cash flow, a cleaner balance sheet, and an active buyback program.

FY2025 revenue was **$45.2b**, up **16%**, with operating margin expanding to **29.5%**. Q2 2026 revenue was **$12.56b**, up **13.4%**, operating income was **$4.19b**, operating margin was **33.4%**, and FCF was **$1.53b**. First-half revenue was **$24.81b**, operating margin was **32.8%**, and FCF was **$6.62b**. First-half net income includes the **$2.8b Warner Bros.-related termination fee**, so headline EPS is not a clean run-rate measure.

Management narrowed 2026 guidance to revenue of **$51.0b-$51.4b** (**13%-14%** growth), operating margin of **31.5%**, and FCF of about **$12.5b**, including the termination-fee benefit. It still expects ad revenue to roughly double to **about $3b**. At June 30, cash and equivalents were **$9.1b**, gross debt was **$14.3b**, and net debt was **$5.24b**; July's **$1b 5.25% notes due 2036** refinance the 2026 maturity.

No universal disqualifier found, and the Q2 10-Q reported no material change to disclosed risk factors. The main risks are content execution, competition for attention, ad-tier execution, live/content rights inflation, regulatory/content obligations, buybacks above intrinsic value, and governance continuity after Reed Hastings left the board.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.84 / confidence 0.82

Netflix has consistent, cash-backed earnings. Yahoo annual history shows net income rising from **$4.5b in 2022** to **$11.0b in 2025**, with no loss years in the available four-year window. Operating margin expanded from **17.8% in 2022** to **29.5% in 2025**, then reached **33.4% in Q2 2026**.

Cash conversion is good but needs interpretation because content accounting can obscure owner earnings. Cumulative FCF/net income over 2022-2025 was about **0.84x**, and management still expects cash content spend to amortization of about **1.1x** in 2026. The score remains below the top tier because content amortization requires judgement, title cycles create lumpiness, and the termination fee inflates 2026 EPS. Another full year of post-paid-sharing results would firm up predictability.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.82 / confidence 0.82

Netflix has crossed into strong FCF generation. FCF was **$9.46b in 2025**, a **20.9% FCF margin**, after **$6.92b in 2024** and **$6.93b in 2023**. The cash-flow script now shows **$11.15b TTM FCF** and a **23.1% TTM margin**. Q2 FCF fell to **$1.53b** from **$2.27b**, mainly because of higher cash taxes and content payments, but full-year guidance remains **about $12.5b**; roughly **$1.5b** is the after-tax termination-fee uplift.

The reason this is not a 0.90+ is content reinvestment. Economic capex is mostly content spend, not PP&E, and first-half additions to content assets rose to **$9.77b**. A clean pre-fee 2026 FCF result and the cash-spend/amortization ratio would firm up the recurring owner-earnings level.

### Fundamental Stability — 0.86 / confidence 0.82

The earnings power is not eroding. Revenue, margins, diluted EPS, and FCF are all at or near window highs. Q2 revenue growth slowed to **13.4%** from **16.2%** in Q1, but every region still grew at least 10%, operating margin held above 33%, and full-year guidance still implies **20%+ operating-income growth**. This looks like normalization from paid-sharing and pricing benefits, not structural decay.

The business is exposed to hit-rate volatility, but the platform has become broader and more resilient: global membership scale, paid sharing monetisation, advertising, pricing, regional/local content, live events, and product improvements all diversify the growth drivers. This is a stable compounder, not a melting ice cube.

### Returns on Capital — 0.86 / confidence 0.82

Returns are now excellent. The returns script showed ROIC improving from **14.4% in 2022** to **17.2% in 2023**, **23.1% in 2024**, and **28.4% in 2025**, with ROCE around **30.3%** in 2025 and estimated incremental ROIC above **100%** across the window. That is the quantitative fingerprint of a scaled, high-fixed-cost entertainment platform moving into operating leverage.

I am not taking the incremental ROIC literally as a steady-state number — recent margin expansion, paid sharing, pricing, and ad ramp make the denominator/period unusually favourable — but the direction is clearly strong. The main question is whether incremental content spend and live rights can keep earning high returns as the business broadens.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.84 / confidence 0.82

Netflix has meaningful pricing power because it is a global default streaming service with habit, brand, breadth, recommendations/data, and a differentiated slate. Management said first-half price changes in the US, Mexico, and Spain performed consistently with prior increases and expectations; it also reported healthy acquisition and retention. Q2 revenue still grew double digits in every region, while first-half viewing rose **2%** to more than **97b hours** despite the Olympics and World Cup.

The moat is strong but not unassailable. Netflix competes with all screen-time alternatives; content can be copied, talent is mobile, and rights costs can inflate. The moat is scale + brand + product + data, not a monopoly. Churn or retention data after the full price-change cohort matures would firm up the score further.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.79 / confidence 0.80

There is still runway. Netflix remains under **45% penetrated** against its broadband-household TAM and has about **5% global TV view share**. It remains on track for **about $3b of 2026 ad revenue**, while programmatic access, video podcasts, creators, local partnerships, cloud games, and AI-assisted discovery broaden the surface area. Live programming is already useful for acquisition—six of the top ten new-member sign-up days over five years—but is expected to consume just over **5% of 2026 content spend** for only about **1% of viewing hours**, so its returns still need proving.

The runway is no longer venture-style explosive. This is a large business approaching a billion-person audience; future growth should be healthy rather than hypergrowth, with more coming from monetisation and margin than pure membership volume. Ads profitability and live-content cohort economics are the next evidence needed.

### Capital Allocation — 0.72 / confidence 0.85

Netflix is self-funding content and returning excess cash, but the price paid now matters. It repurchased **$4.71b** in Q2—**52.9m shares**—at monthly average prices of **$97.47**, **$88.68**, and **$79.08**. Diluted shares fell **2.0% year over year** to **4.261b**, and **$27.1b** remains authorized. Over 2022-2025, buybacks equaled **86% of cumulative FCF** and diluted shares fell **3.7%**.

The Warner Bros. bidding process showed acquisition discipline, and Q1's **$587m** acquisition is immaterial to the thesis. The mark-down is buyback valuation: much of Q2's repurchase spend occurred above today's **$71.71** quote and above this model's conservative current earnings value. The program is shrinking the count, but it was not clearly counter-cyclical or deeply accretive.

### Conservative Debt — 0.86 / confidence 0.88

The balance sheet is strong. Annual-statement leverage was **0.18x net debt/EBITDA in 2025**, with EBIT interest coverage above **22x**. Q2 ended with **$9.1b** cash, **$14.3b** gross debt, **$5.24b** net debt, and an undrawn **$3b revolver**. In July, Netflix issued **$1b of 5.25% fixed-rate notes due 2036** to refinance its 2026 notes, removing the nearest maturity risk.

Content obligations rose to **$25.1b** from **$24.0b** at year-end, so contractual commitments—not bank debt—remain the better stress point. Conventional solvency risk is low.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.78 / confidence 0.75

Reed Hastings leaving the board in June 2026 marks the end of a founder-governance era, but Netflix has already transitioned operational leadership to co-CEOs Ted Sarandos and Greg Peters. The culture is important, but the business is institutionally deep and the product/content machine is not dependent on one person.

I leave this below 0.85 because culture has been a genuine Netflix advantage. Anne Sweeney also resigned from the board in July, explicitly without disagreement. The operating transition remains orderly, but the post-Hastings board and leadership bench should be watched.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.72 / confidence 0.80

The 2026 proxy supports adequate-to-good alignment. Executive ownership guidelines are **6x salary for the co-CEOs** and **3x for other executives**; hedging is prohibited; clawbacks apply; 2025 long-term awards were split evenly between RSUs and PSUs; and the PSUs use a **three-year relative-TSR** test. The annual bonus used FX-neutral revenue (**35%**) and operating margin (**65%**), which balances growth with profitability.

The mark-down is for high pay—about **$53m** for each co-CEO in 2025—the use of revenue and TSR rather than explicit per-share owner earnings/ROIC, and disclosed family production engagements involving Ted Sarandos, albeit under an Audit Committee review policy and at stated market terms. The 2027 proxy's ownership-compliance and realized-pay disclosures should firm up the read.

**Why Wonderful and Fair is the chosen frame:** the mechanical scorer gives Cash Cow a tiny edge because Netflix now throws off serious FCF and returns a lot of it through buybacks. I still choose Wonderful and Fair because the live thesis is not just harvesting cash: Netflix still has reinvestment runway in ads, pricing, global penetration, live/video formats, games, podcasts, and product/AI improvements. Rule Breakers is too late-stage: the disruption already happened; the current thesis is quality compounding.

## Overall

Netflix is a very good business. The old objections — cash burn, debt, questionable streaming economics — are mostly obsolete. Today the question is cleaner: can a global entertainment platform with high engagement, pricing power, growing ads, and improving margins keep compounding at a high enough rate to justify a mid-20s normalized multiple?

My answer is **yes, with a modest margin of safety**. At **$71.71/share** post 10-for-1 split, the base case is anchored near a **22.4x** normalized entry multiple. The Q2 sell-off improved the return without changing the operating thesis, although the valuation still assumes sustained double-digit earnings growth.

**Current rating: 7.8/10.** High-quality compounder at a more attractive price. It is not a deep-value setup, and buyback discipline plus proof of ads/live economics remain the key reservations.

## Thesis Connections

- **Global attention platform:** Netflix monetises scarce consumer attention across subscriptions, ads, live events, and cultural franchises.
- **AI/product leverage:** better recommendations, discovery, mobile experiences, creator tooling, localisation, and production workflows can improve engagement and cost efficiency.
- **Advertising shift:** Netflix is still early in building an ad stack against a very large global TV/video ad market.
- **Growing wealth divide / value-tier entertainment:** streaming remains relatively cheap entertainment, and ad-supported plans help monetise more price-sensitive households.

## Watch

- Q3 2026 versus guidance: **$12.86b revenue**, **11.7% growth**, **33.2% operating margin**, and **$0.82 EPS**; watch whether the full-year guide moves.
- Ads: delivery against **about $3b of 2026 revenue**, upfront commitments, programmatic access, advertiser breadth, and ad-tier economics.
- Pricing and membership: retention after first-half price increases and what the renewed free-trial tests reveal about acquisition efficiency.
- Engagement: title quality and total viewing after Netflix moves What We Watched to annual publication beginning in 2027.
- Content economics: cash content spend/amortization near **1.1x**, the **$25.1b** obligation balance, and whether second-half amortization growth slows as guided.
- Live/events: acquisition and retention economics versus the **>5% of content spend / ~1% of viewing hours** profile; avoid a rights-cost arms race.
- Buybacks: quarterly average price versus intrinsic value and net share-count shrinkage; **$27.1b** authorization is not itself value creation.
- Governance: board refresh after Reed Hastings' departure and Anne Sweeney's resignation; monitor any further leadership or culture slippage.

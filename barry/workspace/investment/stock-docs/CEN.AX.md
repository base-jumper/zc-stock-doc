# CEN.AX — Contact Energy Limited

## Business Overview

Contact Energy is a New Zealand electricity generator and retailer listed on NZX and ASX. It owns geothermal, hydro and thermal generation, sells electricity, gas and telecommunications services, and supplies commercial and industrial users. The July 2025 Manawa acquisition materially enlarged the business by adding hydro assets and contracted renewable output.

FY26 included Manawa for almost the full year. Reported EBITDAF was NZ$1,011m, profit NZ$423m, operating free cash flow NZ$648m and average ROIC 5.9%. Renewable output rose 37% and represented 98% of generation, with Manawa assets and PPAs contributing 2.4TWh. Contact achieved the full NZ$28m targeted Manawa cost synergies on a run-rate basis, with NZ$22m recognised in FY26.

The investment case is durable utility demand, renewable portfolio growth and a rising dividend, offset by capital intensity and only moderate returns on invested capital. Contact continues to fund large projects: FY26 stay-in-business capex was NZ$145m and growth capex NZ$375m; FY27 guidance rises to NZ$178–188m and NZ$460–470m respectively.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings quality — 0.62 / confidence 0.90

Contact has a consistent record of profitability, but earnings remain exposed to hydrology, wholesale prices, gas availability and commissioning schedules. FY26 profit was NZ$423m or 41.5 cents per share. The comparison with FY25 is flattered by Manawa's inclusion and by using FY25 underlying profit of NZ$261m, which excluded the prior Ahuroa Gas Storage provision release.

Cash backing improved: operating free cash flow rose to NZ$648m from NZ$434m. However, NZ$55m of the year-on-year improvement came from the working-capital movement, and operating free cash flow excludes growth capex. The earnings are real and recurring for a utility, but not smooth or fully distributable.

### Free cash flow generation — 0.38 / confidence 0.92

Contact's reported operating free cash flow was NZ$648m after NZ$145m of stay-in-business capex. After also deducting NZ$375m of growth capex, only about NZ$273m remained before acquisitions, strategic investments and dividends; this rough post-total-capex figure is around 27 cents per FY26 share. The company paid NZ$387m of dividends during the year.

FY27 cash demands rise: stay-in-business capex guidance is NZ$178–188m and growth capex NZ$460–470m. The business produces substantial operating cash, but most is committed to an infrastructure build-out. That prevents a cash-cow score despite the stronger FY26 result.

### Fundamental stability — 0.82 / confidence 0.92

Electricity demand and Contact's diversified renewable generation base are structurally durable. Retail connections increased by 50,000 to 692,000, including growth in energy, telecommunications and multi-product customers. The enlarged geothermal and hydro portfolio reduces reliance on thermal generation, although year-to-year results still move with hydrology and wholesale conditions.

The main threats are execution, market design, fuel security and project-cost inflation rather than secular demand loss. FY26's national hydro inflows were 118% of mean, so the strong year should not be treated as a new through-cycle baseline without adjustment.

### Pricing power — 0.60 / confidence 0.78

Contact has asset-backed pricing power, but not brand-monopoly pricing. FY26 average electricity sale pricing fell 11% to NZ$140/MWh as hydro conditions normalised, while retail input costs rose. The portfolio's geothermal baseload, hydro flexibility, batteries and contracted supply improve its ability to manage scarcity and shape load.

Confidence would improve with a through-cycle comparison of retail gross margins, churn and realised prices against Meridian, Mercury and Genesis.

### Capital allocation — 0.67 / confidence 0.86

The Manawa acquisition is tracking better than the prior evidence allowed: integration is complete, the full NZ$28m targeted run-rate synergies have been secured, and the acquired assets materially lifted renewable output. Contact also commissioned its first 100MW battery, began a second 200MW battery, progressed Te Mihi Stage 2 and moved Kōwhai Park solar into commissioning.

Management raised NZ$575m of equity in February 2026, reducing leverage and funding the development pipeline. This protects the balance sheet but dilutes per-share economics. The 40-cents-per-share FY26 dividend represented 65% of operating free cash flow, while the DRP issues new shares at a 2% discount. Allocation is coherent, but the acid test remains whether projects earn attractive returns per share.

### Returns on capital — 0.52 / confidence 0.92

Average ROIC improved by 100 basis points to 5.9%, with FY26 NOPAT of NZ$557m on NZ$7.4bn of average invested capital. That is acceptable for a utility but below the level expected of a classic wonderful business. Manawa integration and newly commissioned assets lifted returns, yet the large FY27 capex programme means incremental returns remain the central question.

### Conservative debt — 0.72 / confidence 0.92

The balance sheet improved after the equity raise. Point-in-time net debt/EBITDAF was 2.1x at FY26 year-end versus 2.3x in FY25 and below management's sustainably-below-3.0x target. Closing net debt was about NZ$2.2bn, average debt tenor was 7.2 years, and the maturity profile is spread across bank facilities, capital bonds and euro medium-term notes.

Net interest expense rose to NZ$140m because of Manawa funding and lower capitalised interest on commissioned assets. Leverage is conservative for a diversified utility, though continuing project commitments mean balance-sheet headroom should not be treated as surplus.

### Key-person risk — 0.78 / confidence 0.78

Contact is an institutional, asset-heavy utility with board oversight and a broad executive team; the thesis does not depend on one founder or product visionary. Confidence is capped because the current review did not independently assess executive succession depth below the disclosed leadership team.

### Incentive alignment — 0.67 / confidence 0.90

FY26 disclosure materially improves the evidence. CEO variable pay included a cash STI of up to 50% of base salary, equity STI up to 30%, and equity LTI set at 40%. The cash STI was 50% financial, using EBITDAF and total expenditure, 20% safety and 30% strategy/operations. The three-year LTI was 70% relative TSR and 30% strategic delivery, and Manawa integration has a separate multi-year incentive.

Relative TSR and three-year testing are aligned. The softer points are EBITDAF and project-delivery metrics that can reward scale before per-share ROIC is proven, plus deferred shares whose only hurdle is continued employment.

### Reinvestment runway — 0.60 / confidence 0.85

Contact has a sizeable renewable pipeline and credible routes to deploy capital: Te Mihi Stage 2, two Glenbrook batteries, Kōwhai Park and Glorit solar, Tauhara 2 drilling and Southland Wind. It has also contracted industrial demand and is exploring data-centre load.

The runway is real, but its quality is not yet exceptional because group ROIC is only 5.9%. FY27's NZ$460–470m growth-capex guidance will provide a direct test of whether the next dollar earns more than the historic asset base.

### Why the other strategies lost

Cash-cow remains a close conceptual fit but loses because total capex absorbs too much operating cash. Freeroll loses because the current price is not protected by a hard asset or income floor and there is no clear near-term re-rating catalyst. Rule-breaker is structurally inappropriate for a mature utility.

## Overall

Contact is a sound renewable utility, not a standout compounder at the current price. FY26 reduced two important uncertainties: Manawa integration delivered the targeted synergies, and leverage returned comfortably below 3x. Earnings, operating cash flow, ROIC and the dividend all improved.

The remaining constraint is per-share return on capital. Contact is still spending heavily, reported ROIC is only 5.9%, and the DRP plus the February equity raise spread business growth across more shares. The current valuation gives little credit for execution risk: most expected shareholder return comes from dividends while multiple compression offsets business growth.

## Thesis Connections

Contact fits electrification and renewable-infrastructure themes, particularly geothermal baseload, hydro flexibility, grid batteries and industrial electrification in New Zealand. It is not a high-growth clean-energy developer; it is a mature utility funding a long-duration build programme.

## Watch

- FY27 delivery against normalised and expected EBITDAF of NZ$1,045m and the 42-cents-per-share dividend target.
- FY27 stay-in-business capex of NZ$178–188m and growth capex of NZ$460–470m; downgrade if project spending rises without a corresponding ROIC lift.
- Average ROIC: require progress beyond FY26's 5.9% as Manawa, batteries and new renewable assets mature.
- Net debt/EBITDAF: keep sustainably below 3.0x while funding the Contact31+ pipeline.
- Te Mihi Stage 2 schedule for Q3 CY2027, Glenbrook Battery 2 for Q1 CY2028, and Kōwhai Park commissioning.
- Per-share economics: monitor new shares issued through the DRP and any further equity funding.
- Hydrology normalisation: FY26 inflows were 118% of mean, so compare FY27 against a mean-hydrology baseline.

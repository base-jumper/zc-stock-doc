# ADYEN.AS — Adyen N.V.

## Business Overview

Adyen is a Dutch global payments and financial-technology platform. Its core product is a single integrated payments stack for large merchants and platforms: online acquiring, in-person point-of-sale, unified commerce, risk/fraud tooling, local payment methods, issuing, accounts, capital, payouts, and newer money-movement products. The business monetizes by taking a small net revenue spread on processed payment volume plus related financial-products income.

The important commercial pillars are:

- **Digital:** online-first enterprise merchants. H1 2025 net revenue was €638.9m (+10% YoY); H2 was €696.1m (+7% reported, +10% constant currency). Growth was muted by a single large-volume customer and tariff exposure in online retail.
- **Unified Commerce:** merchants using Adyen across online and physical channels. H1 2025 net revenue was €334.1m (+31% YoY); H2 was €431.3m (+29% reported, +33% constant currency). This is currently the cleanest expression of Adyen’s moat: one customer/data layer across checkout, in-store payments, fraud, and routing.
- **Platforms:** software platforms embedding payments and financial services. H1 2025 net revenue was €120.5m (+55% YoY); H2 was €143.3m (+45% reported, +49% constant currency). This is still smaller, but high growth and strategically important.

2025 group net revenue was €2.364b (+18% reported, +21% constant currency), processed volume was €1.394t (+8%), EBITDA was €1.246b at a 53% margin, net income was €1.063b, and free cash flow was €1.081b. Regional net revenue mix was diversified: EMEA 58%, North America 27%, APAC 10%, LATAM 6%.

The company’s stated 2026 objectives are 20–22% constant-currency net revenue growth, EBITDA margin broadly in line with 2025, EBITDA margin above 55% by 2028, and capex up to 5% of net revenue.

## Trait Assessment

Primary strategy: **Wonderful and Fair**. Adyen is already highly profitable, cash-generative, conservatively financed, and still has a long reinvestment runway in unified commerce, platforms, financial products, and geographic expansion. It is not a classic cheap catalyst trade or mature payout stock.

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.88 / 0.90

Adyen’s earnings are real, recurring, and backed by cash. In 2025 it generated €2.364b of net revenue, €1.246b of EBITDA, €1.063b of net income, and €1.081b of free cash flow. EBITDA margin improved to 53% from 50% in 2024, while net income grew 15% despite lower finance-income tailwinds. The payment-processing model has volume sensitivity, but the merchant base, geographic spread, and expansion with existing customers make the earnings base more durable than a single-product software company.

The main blemish is customer/mix noise: processed volume grew only 8% in 2025 and Digital volume was affected by a single large-volume customer. That matters because it shows headline volume is not a perfectly clean proxy for economic growth. Net revenue and margin quality are better indicators.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.72 / 0.78

Reported ROE was about 22% and operating margins are excellent, but return-on-capital optics are complicated by Adyen’s bank-like balance sheet: customer funds and large cash balances distort simple capital metrics. The qualitative evidence is still strong: a 53% EBITDA margin, low capex intensity, organic growth, and high free-cash conversion point to a structurally high-return platform.

Confidence is not higher because a clean invested-capital bridge is needed to separate operating capital from banking/customer-funds balances. Next update should calculate a normalized ROIC using operating assets only.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.80 / 0.85

Adyen’s moat is not simple price hikes; payments is competitive and merchants are sophisticated. The stronger moat is performance-based: authorization uplift, lower fraud, unified online/offline data, local acquiring, regulatory licenses, and lower operational complexity for global merchants. 2025 take rate rose to 17.0 bps from 15.5 bps due to merchant mix. The 2025 annual report gives harder product-outcome evidence: Adyen Uplift routing optimization customers saw average scheme/interchange savings of 9.62%, a 42% reduction in false positives, and conversion improvements of up to 6% at the transaction point.

I lifted confidence because this is now grounded in quantified customer outcomes and reported churn, not just product narrative. This is a real moat, but not invulnerable. Stripe, Worldpay/FIS, Fiserv, PayPal/Braintree, Checkout.com, local acquirers, and in-house enterprise builds all keep pressure on take rates. Score is high but not elite-monopoly high.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.86 / 0.85

Runway is the central positive. Adyen is still scaling share of wallet with existing global merchants, expanding Unified Commerce, growing Platforms, and layering financial products on top of payments. The 2025 annual report called out issuing volume up 8x YoY, capital loan volumes doubling, payouts around 40% YoY growth, and a CashOut pilot already at several hundred million euros of annualized volume. Geographic investment in APAC/LATAM and local licenses adds more runway.

Management’s 2026 guide for 20–22% constant-currency net revenue growth and margin above 55% by 2028 supports the view that this is still a compounder rather than an ex-growth payments processor.

### Capital Allocation — 0.82 / 0.85

Capital allocation is disciplined and mostly organic: Adyen continues to invest in product, infrastructure, licenses, and data centers while holding capex to 5% of 2025 net revenue and targeting up to 5% going forward. The business has not needed leverage or large acquisitions to grow. It also does not pay a dividend or run buybacks; cash is being retained for resilience, banking operations, and reinvestment.

The 2025 annual report firms this up: capex was €116.8m, 5% of net revenue, mainly data-center capacity; 51,505 shares were issued for equity programs; and no shares were repurchased. That is rational for a business with this runway. The score stops short of excellent because shareholders are not yet seeing explicit capital returns and dilution, while modest, is still real.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.95 / 0.95

Balance-sheet risk is very low. Yahoo Finance data showed roughly €10.8b cash and €252m debt, with debt/equity around 4.8%. Enterprise value is far below market cap because of the net cash/customer-funds-heavy balance sheet. This financial strength gives Adyen room to keep investing through cycles and absorb regulatory, operational, or macro shocks.

The caveat is that payments and banking licenses bring operational and regulatory risk, but this is not a leverage problem.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.80 / 0.88

Adyen still has founder DNA: Pieter van der Does is co-founder and Co-CEO, and the culture is a major part of the edge. The business has also institutionalized more than a single-founder model: Ingo Uytdehaage joined in 2011, served as CFO, and became Co-CEO in 2023; the Management Board includes leaders across commercial, risk/compliance, HR, finance, and technology.

Important update: on May 27, 2026, Adyen announced that CFO Ethan Tandowsky will step down effective August 31, 2026 to pursue an external opportunity outside fintech. The Supervisory Board has started a successor search. This does not break the thesis — Adyen has 4,771 FTEs, 29 offices, own licensing stack, formal risk/compliance functions, Supervisory Board oversight, and a documented risk-control framework — but it is a small negative for management-bench continuity, especially because the company itself said the timing was surprising.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.84 / 0.90

The remuneration review materially improved confidence. Adyen awarded no variable remuneration to Management Board members in 2025, so management is not being pointed at short-term revenue, EBITDA, or EPS targets. Pay is base salary plus, for some board members, share-based compensation subject to a five-year holding period; the co-CEOs and CCO continue to receive only cash base salary, while existing large shareholdings/founder ownership carry the long-term upside. The remuneration report says most Management Board pay remains significantly below benchmark, and the CEO pay ratio was 8:1, low by large-cap standards.

This is a cleaner incentive system than the usual bonus-scorecard mess: fewer gameable annual targets, meaningful long-term ownership, modest dilution, and no obvious self-dealing or outsized perks. I am not taking the score to excellent because the co-CEOs' current pay is not explicitly tied to per-share return metrics, and part of the alignment rests on legacy/founder holdings rather than a universally formal ownership requirement.

### Additional low-confidence trait review

I also firmed up several lower-confidence non-primary traits because they affect the secondary Rule Breakers / Freeroll reads:

- **Catalyst — 0.42 / 0.75:** score lifted only slightly from 0.35 / 0.50. There *are* concrete signposts now: the August 13, 2026 earnings date, the 2026 constant-currency net revenue guide of 20–22%, the 2028 EBITDA-margin target above 55%, and an easy-to-monitor reversal of the 2025 APAC/tariff and large-online-retailer drag. But this still is not a hard catalyst. The re-rating depends on execution and sentiment recovery, not a forced event like a buyback, spin, dividend, tender, or activist/corporate action.
- **Mispricing — 0.62 / 0.78:** lifted from 0.55 / 0.55. The market concern is now clearer: 2025 growth was tempered by tariff changes and APAC-headquartered online retail exposure, while FX and trade-policy uncertainty hit sentiment; the share price is near the 52-week low and far below the 52-week high. Our variant view is that this is a temporary volume/mix drag on a still-excellent compounder, not a broken moat: 2026 consensus revenue growth is ~19.8%, next-year revenue growth ~20.7%, next-year EPS growth ~21.8%, and Yahoo shows forward P/E around 17.5x / EV-EBITDA around 13.0x. I am not scoring this higher because the buy-side is not exactly asleep — 33 analyst opinions, strong-buy recommendation mix, and a mean target well above the share price mean the “mispricing” is more controversy/overreaction than hidden orphaned value.
- **Downside Support — 0.45 / 0.80:** confidence lifted from 0.65 but the score stays low. Adyen has quality support: high margins, strong free cash flow, low financial debt, no dividend dependence, and net cash-like optics. But the apparent cash pile is not a clean hard floor because payment companies carry merchant/customer funds and regulatory liquidity; there is no dividend yield, buyback authorization being actively used, asset NAV, or takeout floor close to the current price. The floor is “great business at a sane multiple,” not a Freeroll-style hard downside anchor.
- **Customer Devotion — 0.78 / 0.80:** lifted from 0.72 / 0.60. Adyen does not disclose NRR, which keeps this below elite confidence, but the 2025 annual report reported churn below 1% excluding one merchant, growth driven by expansion with existing customers, and quantified Uplift benefits: 9.62% average scheme/interchange savings, 42% fewer false positives, and up to 6% conversion improvement. This is stronger evidence of voluntary expansion and preference than I had in the first pass.
- **Management Backing — 0.78 / 0.88:** confidence lifted from 0.70, but the score is held at the prior 0.78 after the CFO transition. The management review confirms a founder/Co-CEO plus a seasoned Co-CEO who joined in 2011 and a broader bench across commercial, risk, HR, finance, and technology. The culture language is unusually consistent: speed, autonomy, direct collaboration, and long-term thinking. Offsetting that, CFO Ethan Tandowsky announced he will leave on August 31, 2026 for an opportunity outside fintech; Adyen called him a core leadership-team member and said the timing was surprising.
- **Top Dog — 0.72 / 0.78:** lifted from 0.70 / 0.70. Adyen is clearly a scaled global enterprise processor, but the annual report explicitly notes competitors with larger merchant portfolios, greater scale, or broader resources. That keeps the score in “strong contender / emerging leader” rather than “undisputed dominant platform.”

### Strategy fit / why the others lost

The scorer produced a genuine near-tie between **Cash Cow** and **Wonderful and Fair** because Adyen’s free cash flow and earnings quality are already excellent. I’m framing the doc under **Wonderful and Fair** and setting it as the chosen strategy because the economic logic is still reinvestment-led: no dividend, no buyback program, and a visible runway in Unified Commerce, Platforms, financial products, and geographic expansion. Cash Cow is a valid secondary read, but it understates the compounding runway.

- **Rule Breakers:** Adyen has some rule-breaker traits — large market, product innovation, founder culture, and durable growth — but it is no longer an early/profitless disruptor. It fits better as a proven compounder.
- **Freeroll:** valuation is much more reasonable after the drawdown, and the mispricing case is clearer after checking the tariff/APAC/FX overhang. But there is still no forced time-bound catalyst, and downside support is mostly business quality rather than a true asset/yield floor.

## Overall

Adyen is a high-quality compounder at a price that finally looks sane again. The strongest pieces are the single-platform architecture, financial strength, high cash conversion, long reinvestment runway, and management’s credible 20%+ constant-currency growth guide. The main risks are competitive pricing pressure, customer concentration/noisy volume metrics, regulatory/operational complexity from being a licensed financial institution, and the possibility that payments take rates compress faster than software/financial-products attach can offset.

My current view: **watch/accumulate bias rather than table-pounding cheap**. At €829, the stock is no longer priced like perfection. If Adyen keeps compounding EPS/free cash flow around the mid-teens-plus and exits around a low-20s earnings multiple, expected returns are attractive. The thesis breaks if net revenue growth falls sustainably below the high teens while the multiple still assumes a premium compounder.

# SAAB-B.ST — Saab AB

## Business Overview

Saab is a Swedish defence and security group spanning aeronautics (Gripen fighters), weapons (including Carl-Gustaf and missile systems), surveillance and command-and-control (Erieye/GlobalEye and Giraffe radars), and naval systems. Revenue is earned through long-duration government contracts, production deliveries and aftermarket support. At 30 June 2026, a multi-year backlog, expanding production capacity and unusually strong European defence demand were the central profit drivers.

No universal disqualifier was identified: the latest reports show positive earnings, liquidity and cash generation, with no disclosed accounting, solvency, control or business-model kill-shot. Defence-export permissions and programme execution remain ordinary but material risks.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.90 / 0.90

European rearmament is a structural demand shift rather than a normal replacement cycle. Saab's FY2025 order bookings rose 74% to SEK 168.5bn and backlog reached SEK 274.5bn; Q2 2026 added SEK 68.4bn of bookings, including Poland's SEK 47bn submarine order. NATO's July 2026 selection of GlobalEye for negotiations covering up to ten aircraft broadens the runway, although no order had yet been signed. Industry constraints are skilled labour, supply chains, sovereign procurement timing and the possibility that spending normalises after the current security cycle.

### Top Dog — 0.68 / 0.72

Saab is not the global prime-contractor leader across defence: Lockheed Martin, RTX, BAE Systems, Thales and others are larger and better capitalised. It is, however, a credible category leader in several niches. NATO selected GlobalEye as its future AEW&C solution; the company also won Gripen E/F orders from Colombia and Thailand, and delivered record volumes of Giraffe 1X radars and ground-combat products in 2025. The mixed portfolio and opaque niche-share data prevent a top-band score. Confidence would rise with independently measured market shares for AEW&C, ground-combat weapons and tactical radar.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.78 / 0.78

The moat is technical IP, sovereign trust, installed-base switching costs and certification/integration know-how. GlobalEye combines Saab's Erieye ER radar, sensors and command system; replacing such mission systems is costly and slow. The moat appears to be widening through NATO selection and broader export wins. Gross margin stayed near 21–22% in 2022–25 rather than expanding materially, so pricing power is not obvious in reported margins. Confidence would rise with programme-level win rates and contract margin disclosures, which are not currently public.

### Durable Growth — 0.88 / 0.92

Saab is a maturing profitable grower, so revenue, earnings and FCF are the appropriate metrics. Revenue rose from SEK 42.0bn in 2022 to SEK 79.1bn in 2025, a 23.5% CAGR, with annual growth steady at roughly 23–24%. FY2025 net income was SEK 6.3bn and FCF SEK 4.9bn; conversion was 77%, after a weaker 45% in 2024. Q2 2026 sales grew 29.8% organically, EBIT grew 41%, and EBIT margin improved to 11.0%. The 52-week share-price change was -7.6% before the 17 July result, a valuation/sentiment divergence rather than evidence that operations stalled.

### Organic Growth — 0.92 / 0.88

The growth fingerprint is overwhelmingly organic. Between 2022 and 2025 revenue compounded at 23.5%, cumulative acquisition spend was only SEK 0.34bn, goodwill fell from 7.4% to 4.2% of assets and diluted shares increased only 0.9%. Saab explicitly reported 25.6% organic growth in FY2025 and 29.8% in Q2 2026. No large acquisition is manufacturing the growth.

### Management Backing — 0.72 / 0.70

CEO Micael Johansson has led Saab since 2019 and the current execution record includes rapid capacity expansion, roughly 10,000 hires over four years, major export wins and rising margins. The 2025 letter is product- and customer-oriented, describing deliveries and next-generation systems rather than only financial targets. This is capable long-duration stewardship, but Saab is not founder-led and culture/backer evidence was not deeply tested. Confidence would rise from the 2026 AGM materials, employee-retention data and a full review of major long-term owners.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.60 / 0.58

Share dilution has been modest, which is consistent with per-share discipline, but Yahoo reports only 1.8% insider ownership and elevated governance risk scores for compensation, board and shareholder rights. The current analysis did not verify the 2025 remuneration report's exact bonus metrics, vesting periods, holding requirements or clawbacks. Confidence would rise by reviewing those specific tables in Saab's FY2025 remuneration report; this is a Watch item.

### Customer Devotion — 0.62 / 0.55

Defence customers do not publish NPS or conventional retention metrics. Repeat programmes, France's GlobalEye order, NATO's selection, Germany's SEK 8.7bn frigate-systems order and follow-on Gripen demand demonstrate trust, but long contracts and switching costs are captivity as much as devotion. The score therefore recognises reference-customer validation without double-counting lock-in. Confidence is irreducible for now; signed follow-on orders and competitive re-tender outcomes will provide the next evidence.

### Wonderful-and-Fair dual fit — 0.73 / 0.82

The runner-up is a genuine near-tie. Earnings quality is 0.78/0.88: FY2025 FCF was 77% of net income and cumulative cash generation is positive, though project working capital makes annual conversion uneven. Returns on capital are 0.55/0.90: ROIC rose from 6.9% in 2022 to 12.8% in 2025 and incremental ROIC was approximately 26%, but the average level remains below the strategy's high-return ideal. Pricing power is 0.66/0.75: differentiated systems and rising EBIT margin support it, while flat 21–22% gross margin and undisclosed programme economics limit proof; programme-level margin disclosure would firm this up. Reinvestment runway is 0.82/0.82: record backlog and capacity expansion provide years of internally funded growth, with recent incremental returns encouraging. Capital allocation is 0.76/0.78: heavy organic capacity and R&D investment has outpaced negligible M&A, but the payoff remains execution-dependent; segmental investment returns would firm this up. Conservative debt is 0.82/0.92: FY2025 net debt of SEK 6.1bn was modest against EBITDA of SEK 11.7bn, and Q2 2026 reported net liquidity of SEK 2.5bn. Key-person resilience is 0.86/0.80: 28,000 employees, sovereign programmes and institutional engineering knowledge reduce dependence on one executive; detailed succession disclosure would raise confidence.

Rule Breakers narrowly wins because the structural defence wave and durable organic growth are the strongest parts of the case. Wonderful-and-Fair is the legitimate secondary framing, but moderate through-cycle ROIC and a price far above a conventional fair multiple weaken it. Cash Cow loses because cash is being reinvested in capacity rather than distributed; Freeroll loses because the shares lack both a conservative valuation floor and a re-rating catalyst from an already depressed valuation.

## Overall

Saab combines a record backlog, broad product exposure, organic growth and improving margins with difficult-to-replicate defence IP. The key weakness is price: operational quality is strong, but the equity already discounts a long period of high growth. Programme delays, fixed-price cost overruns, export restrictions and defence-budget normalisation could hurt both earnings and the multiple.

## Valuation

The canonical Rule Breakers valuation uses only the applicable bottom-up lens. TAM-capture was omitted because Saab spans several sovereign defence markets and no single role model provides a boundary-consistent market share and margin; forcing a group-wide TAM would create false precision.

At the 18 July 2026 Yahoo quote of SEK 567, the model anchors on SEK 14.28 current-year EPS (the available 2026 consensus estimate), implying an entry P/E near 39.7x. It fades aggregate earnings growth from 20% to 12% over five years, assumes 0.5% annual dilution, a 30x exit P/E and the 0.46% indicated dividend yield. The growth path is below recent operating growth and roughly consistent with the 2026–27 consensus fade; the exit multiple still assumes Saab retains premium quality. The scripted result is a 9.71% annualized ROI (1.59x over five years): 15.97% business growth, a 0.50% dilution drag, a 5.45% annualized de-rating drag and 0.62% income contribution. It should be read as an expected return conditional on continued execution, not a margin-of-safety valuation. `overall.agent_score` is 0.58: an excellent operating setup at a demanding entry price.

Fits a European rearmament and sovereign-defence-capacity thesis: increased NATO spending, replenishment of depleted inventories, and demand for non-US strategic autonomy support Saab's sensors, weapons, aircraft and naval franchises.

# CEN.NZ — Contact Energy Limited

## Business Overview

Contact Energy is a New Zealand gentailer: it generates electricity, sells electricity/gas/broadband to retail customers, and contracts with commercial/industrial customers. The generation base is increasingly renewable, anchored in geothermal and hydro, with batteries and solar being added for flexibility. Contact completed the Manawa Energy acquisition on 11 July 2025, adding hydro schemes and contracted PPAs, and reported that 1H26 output was 97% renewable.

The business earns money from wholesale generation, contracted sales/CFDs, retail margins, and ancillary income. The profit drivers are hydro/geothermal availability, wholesale electricity prices, gas scarcity/flexibility value, retail customer growth, line-cost pass-through, and disciplined capital deployment into renewables.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.62 / confidence 0.75

Contact has been profitable in each Yahoo-covered year from FY22-FY25, with net profit of NZ$182m, NZ$127m, NZ$235m and NZ$331m respectively. FY25 underlying profit was NZ$261m, up 13% from FY24, while reported profit included a non-cash NZ$71m profit benefit from the Ahuroa Gas Storage provision release. The direction is positive, but earnings are exposed to hydro conditions, wholesale power prices and gas/fuel scarcity; FY23 shows the lumpiness.

Cash backing is mixed if using raw Yahoo FCF because growth capex is heavy: cumulative FCF/net income over FY22-FY25 was only ~0.09x. Contact’s own operating free cash flow measure is more useful for a utility build-out: FY25 operating free cash flow was NZ$434m, up 2%, with stay-in-business capex down to NZ$110m. Confidence would firm up with a longer 7-10 year history and a full reconciliation of maintenance versus growth capex through FY26 after Manawa.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.58 / confidence 0.75

This is cash-generative before growth projects, but not capital-light. FY25 operating free cash flow was NZ$434m, while Contact spent NZ$363m on growth capex and paid 39c/share in declared dividends. Stay-in-business capex was NZ$110m, down 29% from FY24, which suggests the existing asset base can throw off distributable cash once maintenance is separated from expansion.

Raw Yahoo FCF was thin because total capex includes the renewable build programme: NZ$95m FCF in FY25, NZ$74m in FY24 and negative NZ$146m in FY23. The score is therefore moderate: the mature assets behave like a utility cash cow, but the current company is also funding a large renewables pipeline. Confidence would improve with management’s FY26 operating-FCF bridge after Manawa and a recurring maintenance-capex split.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.78 / confidence 0.80

Demand for electricity in New Zealand is durable and likely supported by electrification. Contact cited 3-5TWh of new grid demand expected over the next five years, and its portfolio is moving toward a 98% renewable mean-year output mix after new geothermal and Manawa generation. FY25 revenue, operating margin, EPS and FCF were all at Yahoo-covered highs, and 1H26 EBITDAF rose to NZ$500m, or NZ$522m excluding Manawa transaction/integration costs.

The major instability is hydrology/gas-market cyclicality rather than obvious secular decline. Dry years, hydro inflows and gas availability can move earnings sharply, but the renewables/battery build and long-term gas contracts are meant to reduce that volatility rather than harvest a melting ice cube.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.65 / confidence 0.65

Contact has a real but regulated/commodity-adjacent moat: scale generation assets, geothermal baseload, hydro flexibility, retail customer relationships, and scarce firming capacity in a tightening gas market. FY25 highlighted that flexible gas/storage and geothermal output let Contact provide risk-management support to other participants during dry periods.

That said, electricity is not a pure brand-pricing-power business. Wholesale prices, regulation, lines charges and political scrutiny limit unilateral pricing. The moat is better described as asset scarcity and portfolio flexibility than customer love or brand premium. More confidence needs competitor cost-curve and market-share data.

### Capital Allocation — 0.72 / confidence 0.70

Management is returning cash while reinvesting heavily into renewable assets. FY25 dividends were 39c/share, and the FY25 final dividend took payout to 82% of FY25 operating free cash flow per Contact’s result release. At the same time, the company invested NZ$363m of growth capex in FY25 and announced a NZ$525m equity raise in February 2026 to fund Glenbrook battery 2.0, Glorit solar and Tauhara 2 drilling.

The Manawa acquisition appears strategically coherent: it adds hydro schemes, contracted PPAs and portfolio flexibility, and Contact reported more than 80% of cost synergies secured on a run-rate basis by 1H26. The main caveat is return discipline: average ROIC was only 4.9% in FY25, so reinvestment must lift future cash flows enough to justify dilution and debt.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.45 / confidence 0.80

Returns are stable but not high. Yahoo-derived ROIC rose from 5.3% in FY22 to 7.7% in FY25, and Contact reported four-year average ROIC of 4.9% in FY25, up from 3.7%. Incremental ROIC screens better, but this remains a capital-heavy utility earning modest returns, not a classic high-ROIC compounder.

For a utility, mid-single-digit ROIC may still create value if funding costs are controlled and assets are long-lived. But it does cap the quality score and is the main reason Contact is framed as a cash-cow/infrastructure-income stock rather than a wonderful-and-fair compounder.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.72 / confidence 0.80

Leverage looks appropriate for a stable utility. The latest annual net debt/EBITDA was 2.32x in FY25, with EBIT interest cover of 6.5x. Yahoo’s more current snapshot showed net debt/EBITDA around 2.79x and EBITDA interest cover around 9.1x. These sit comfortably inside the regulated/contracted infrastructure band, and the NZ$525m equity raise should help fund the growth pipeline without over-stretching the balance sheet.

The caveat is trend and capex: annual net debt rose from NZ$931m in FY22 to NZ$1.94b in FY25, before Manawa and the new investments. Refinancing risk needs the full maturity ladder, fixed/floating split and committed facilities from filings.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.85 / confidence 0.65

The thesis does not depend on one irreplaceable person. Contact’s value sits in long-lived generation assets, retail systems, contracts, project execution capability and regulatory/market position. CEO Mike Fuge matters, especially for the Contact31+ execution agenda and Manawa integration, but the asset base would not break if one executive left.

Confidence is moderate because I did not dig deeply into succession planning or second-line leadership disclosures.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 / confidence 0.45

The strategic direction is broadly shareholder-relevant: operating free cash flow, dividends, renewable project execution, synergies and ROIC are visible in investor communications. But I did not review the latest remuneration report in enough detail to assess executive scorecard metrics, ownership, hurdles, clawbacks or dilution from incentives.

This is scored as mixed/uncertain rather than bad. It should be firmed up from the FY25 integrated report remuneration section.

### Why the other strategies lost

Rule-breakers loses because Contact is a mature utility, not an emerging hyper-growth category winner. Wonderful-and-fair loses on high-return compounding: returns on capital are modest and the business is capital-intensive. Freeroll loses because there is no obvious hard valuation floor close to price and no time-bound re-rating catalyst; the thesis is income plus execution, not asymmetric deep value.

## Overall

Contact is a solid renewable utility/infrastructure income name with durable demand, a useful portfolio of geothermal/hydro/flexible assets, and a clear capital-return habit. The Manawa acquisition and Contact31+ pipeline improve strategic positioning as New Zealand electrifies.

The drawbacks are equally clear: returns on capital are modest, the company is in a heavy investment phase, headline FCF is suppressed by growth capex, and valuation is not obviously cheap at ~20.8x trailing earnings. It looks investable as a steady cash-cow style compound/income holding, but not an obvious bargain.

Contact connects to electrification, renewable generation build-out, grid flexibility/storage, and New Zealand energy security. It is more an infrastructure/utility cash-flow thesis than a disruptive growth thesis.

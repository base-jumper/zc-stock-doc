# FCX — Freeport-McMoRan Inc.

## Business Overview

Freeport-McMoRan is a large, vertically integrated copper producer with seven U.S. copper operations, Cerro Verde in Peru, El Abra in Chile, and the Grasberg copper-gold district in Indonesia. Copper supplied 75% of 2025 revenue, gold 15%, and molybdenum 8%. Morenci, Cerro Verde and Grasberg together produced 70% of 2025 copper. The economic engine is therefore copper volume multiplied by the market copper price, with Grasberg gold credits materially lowering consolidated unit costs.

The asset base is unusually deep: year-end 2025 consolidated recoverable proven and probable reserves were 112.3 billion pounds of copper, including 42.5 billion in the U.S., 45.6 billion in South America and 24.2 billion in Indonesia. These reserves were tested at $3.25/lb copper, far below the August 2026 spot environment. Growth is predominantly brownfield: leach-recovery technology, a potential 200–250 million lb/year Bagdad expansion, Safford/Lone Star studies, an El Abra project capable of more than 700 million lb/year, and further Grasberg underground development.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was $7.03 billion, attributable net income was $984 million ($0.68/share), operating cash flow was $2.05 billion and capex was $1.10 billion. Production remained constrained by the September 2025 Grasberg mud-rush incident: Q2 copper sales were 710 million lb versus 1.0 billion lb a year earlier. Management guides to 3.1 billion lb of 2026 copper sales, $1.90/lb consolidated unit net cash costs, $8.3 billion operating cash flow at $6.00/lb copper and $4.3 billion capex. The Grasberg recovery is the near-term swing factor.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.83 / confidence 0.90

The IEA projects copper demand to grow 30% by 2040 under stated policies, driven by grids, industrial electrification, construction and EVs. More importantly, announced mined supply falls about 30% short of the IEA's 2035 requirement; declining grades, rising project costs and slower discoveries make the gap difficult to close. This is a durable demand/supply setup, although copper remains cyclical and substitution, recycling, slower electrification or a global downturn can interrupt the path.

FCX is already operating at scale in this constrained market rather than depending on a greenfield discovery. Q2 consolidated net cash cost guidance of $1.90/lb leaves substantial operating leverage to copper prices, but commodity economics also amplify reversals.

### Top Dog — 0.74 / confidence 0.80

FCX describes itself as one of the world's largest publicly traded copper producers and owns interests in three globally significant districts: Morenci, Cerro Verde and Grasberg. Its 2026 sales guidance of 3.1 billion lb (about 1.4Mt) represents a meaningful share of global mine supply, while 112.3 billion lb of reserves and integrated U.S./Indonesian processing support longevity. The edge is scale, orebody quality, infrastructure and decades of operating knowledge—not winner-take-most network effects. It competes for capital and projects with BHP, Rio Tinto, Glencore, Southern Copper and state-owned producers.

Confidence would improve with a current, independently compiled mine-by-mine global production ranking on a consistent attributable basis; this is otherwise irreducible until 2026 industry production tables are complete.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.60 / confidence 0.80

The moat is asset-based. A well-funded entrant cannot recreate Grasberg, Morenci, Cerro Verde, their permits, infrastructure, skilled workforces and 112.3 billion lb reserve inventory quickly. FCX's leach technology also generated 101 million incremental lb in 1H26 and targets a 300 million lb annual run-rate by year-end, offering a capital-light operating edge if results persist.

The limitation is decisive: copper is exchange-priced. FCX has essentially no customer-level pricing power, and consolidated margins move with commodity prices, grades, by-product credits and disruptions. Gross margin fell from 33.8% in 2022 to 28.2% in 2025 despite the physical moat. Confidence would improve after independent validation of sustained leach recovery across multiple ore bodies; revisit after full-year 2026 operating data.

### Evidence of Durable Growth — 0.55 / confidence 0.90

FCX is a profitable, capital-intensive cyclical, so revenue, production, margin and FCF are the appropriate measures. Revenue rose from $13.9 billion in 2020 to about $25.9 billion in 2025, but the path was driven heavily by copper prices and was not smooth: long-run EDGAR data include a 24% revenue decline in 2019 and losses in 2016 and 2019. Recent revenue was $22.8 billion in 2022, $22.9 billion in 2023, $25.5 billion in 2024 and $25.9 billion in 2025; 2025 FCF fell to $1.12 billion from $2.35 billion as capex remained near $4.5 billion.

The growth pipeline is credible, but recent economics do not yet show a clean, stable compounding record. Q2 2026 sales were depressed by Grasberg's ramp-up, while unusually high copper prices boosted realized pricing. This is growth with considerable cycle and execution variance.

### Organic Growth — 0.85 / confidence 0.85

Current growth is overwhelmingly organic. The main routes—leach recovery, Grasberg underground development, Bagdad, Safford/Lone Star and El Abra—expand existing districts. Q2 leach initiatives added 47 million lb, and FCX targets 300 million lb annualized by end-2026. Bagdad could add 200–250 million lb/year, while El Abra could add more than 700 million lb/year. No material recent acquisition underwrites the forecast; 2025 cash-flow statements show no business purchase, and goodwill is only about $0.4 billion against $58.2 billion of assets.

This deserves a high score, tempered because mega-project cost escalation is real: Bagdad's current capital estimate is roughly 30% above the earlier $3.5 billion estimate.

### Great Management and Smart Backing — 0.72 / confidence 0.75

Kathleen Quirk became CEO in 2024 after a long FCX career. The relevant evidence is execution rather than founder charisma: balance-sheet repair, a restrained acquisition posture, leach-recovery progress, and a phased Grasberg restart after a severe operating incident. Q2 copper sales beat April guidance and unit cash costs were better than forecast. The 2025 mud rush nevertheless exposes operational-risk governance, and Indonesia negotiations remain a major external dependency.

Institutions hold about 89% of shares, while insiders hold only about 0.7%; recent disclosed officer transactions were sales rather than open-market purchases. Confidence would improve with independent findings on the Grasberg incident and a complete post-restart safety/production record; revisit after the investigation and full-rate ramp-up.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.72 / confidence 0.85

The 2026 proxy shows 91% of the CEO's target direct compensation was at risk and 72% depended on measurable performance. Performance stock units use three-year ROI and relative total-shareholder-return goals; executives face ownership requirements, clawbacks and holding rules, and all named executives exceeded their guidelines at year-end 2025. The CEO and chair must each hold stock worth at least six times salary.

The plan is sensibly designed around return and shareholder outcomes rather than just production growth. Alignment stops short of exceptional because most ownership comes through compensation, total insider ownership is low, and annual operating targets can still encourage cycle-sensitive behavior.

### Customer Devotion — 0.20 / confidence 0.75

This trait does not translate well to an exchange-priced commodity. FCX sells concentrate, cathode and rod under annual, multi-year and long-term contracts, but customers principally value specification, availability and delivery; there is no evidence of brand-like advocacy, NRR or voluntary product expansion. Stickiness comes from supply-chain integration and contracts rather than devotion. The 2025 10-K reports only one customer above 10% of consolidated revenue during 2023–2025 (Mitsubishi Materials in 2024), indicating manageable concentration rather than loyalty.

Confidence is capped because FCX does not disclose renewal or satisfaction metrics. The specific source that could firm this up is a company customer-retention/contract-renewal disclosure; absent that, this limitation is irreducible for a commodity producer.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair was second (0.566 / 0.852 confidence), held back by cyclical earnings, no pricing power and only mid-teen ROIC. Cash-cow lost because high development capex leaves thin and volatile free cash flow. Freeroll lost because the $69.62 share price is near its 52-week high, lacks a defensible near-price asset floor, and already prices in a strong copper/ramp-up outcome.

## Overall

FCX is a high-quality way to own structural copper scarcity, but not a classic compounding business. Its strongest features are irreplaceable long-life assets, a large organic pipeline, manageable leverage and exposure to a commodity with a credible long-run supply deficit. Its weak points are equally structural: no product pricing power, heavy capital requirements, volatile FCF, Indonesia tenure/political exposure and material underground operating risk.

The investment decision is therefore price-sensitive. At $69.62, 34.1× trailing earnings and roughly 17.0× forward earnings, the equity already discounts high copper prices and a successful Grasberg recovery. Quality is good; asymmetry is not.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a structural electrification / grid-modernisation thesis: copper intensity rises with grid investment, data centres, electric transport and renewable generation, while mine supply responds slowly. FCX adds U.S. supply-chain optionality and leverage to constrained primary mine supply. It is a poor fit for a predictable-income or low-volatility compounder thesis.

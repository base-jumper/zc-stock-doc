# 328130.KQ — Lunit Inc.

## Business Overview

Lunit is a South Korean medical-AI software company focused on cancer screening and precision oncology. Its **Lunit INSIGHT** products assist clinicians reading chest X-rays, mammography and digital breast tomosynthesis; its **Lunit SCOPE** platform analyzes pathology / tumor-microenvironment images for biomarker research and companion-diagnostic workflows.

The company is still early-stage financially but has real commercial traction. Lunit reported 2025 revenue of **KRW 83.1b**, up **53% YoY**, with 92% overseas sales, and H1 2026 **record revenue of KRW 45.8b**, up **23% YoY** (Q2 KRW 21.8b, +22.5%), with overseas sales at **95%** of total and North American revenue up **30%** on Lunit International upselling. The cancer-screening segment (Lunit INSIGHT) brought in KRW 42.8b in H1, up 20%; the SCOPE biomarker platform KRW 2.9b, up **114%**, driven by Americas/Europe pharma collaborations. INSIGHT adoption had reached **7,400+ medical institutions** by end-2025; the acquired Volpara / Lunit International breast-health network added **3,600+ institutions** and more direct U.S./ANZ infrastructure (over **10,000** medical institutions company-wide per market-doc). SCOPE collaborations include Agilent, CellCarta, Labcorp, Daiichi Sankyo, and a July 2026 eight-company AI drug-development consortium (CellCarta, Indica Labs, Mindpeak, Nucleai and others). New Aug-2026 U.S./UK contracts added GenesisCare UK, VA Tulsa (Dept. of Veterans Affairs) and DeTar Healthcare System, extending the DHA government work begun in 2024.

The catch is funding and dilution, though losses are narrowing. 2025 operating loss was about **KRW 83.1b** and 2025 FCF about **negative KRW 64.8b**; H1 2026 operating loss improved **31% to KRW 29.0b** and EBITDA loss fell **~50% to KRW 14.65b**, with management reiterating the FY2026 EBITDA break-even target. Q1 2026 still produced a **KRW 13.6b operating loss** and roughly **KRW 9.4b negative FCF**. The April 2026 rights offering raised **KRW 211.5b** for debt repayment and operations, materially improving near-term liquidity but at a substantial dilution cost.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.82 / 0.72

Medical imaging and oncology workflows are credible AI adoption zones: radiology capacity constraints, cancer-screening demand, richer pathology data, pharma biomarker needs and regulatory acceptance all create a structural growth setup. Lunit has FDA / CE / MFDS-cleared products across CXR, MMG and DBT, and its SCOPE product is positioned in AI-enabled biomarker / CDx development. A third-party market estimate cited the global AI-in-oncology market growing from **US$8.3b in 2026 to US$38.9b in 2033**.

Confidence is not higher because the exact TAM boundary for Lunit is fuzzy: cancer-screening AI, breast-risk software and AI biomarker/CDx workflows overlap but are not one clean market. Firm-up source: a current investor deck or industry report sizing Lunit's own target markets by segment.

### Top Dog — 0.58 / 0.60

Lunit is a serious contender, not yet an obvious category winner. Positives: INSIGHT has 7,400+ institution adoption and, with Lunit International, more than 10,000 medical institutions company-wide, partners with major imaging/PACS companies including GE Healthcare, Philips, Fujifilm, Hologic, Agfa and Sectra, and Lunit SCOPE has named pharma / diagnostics collaborations. In Japan, Fujifilm's CXR-AID using Lunit technology has reportedly reached **4,000+ institutions**. H1 2026 added U.S. federal and hospital wins (VA Tulsa, DeTar, GenesisCare UK) and North American revenue grew 30% YoY, showing the direct-sales model gaining traction.

The negative is that healthcare AI remains crowded, procurement-heavy and locally regulated. Lunit's installed base is impressive, but revenue is still only KRW 83b and the company has not shown winner-takes-most economics. Firm-up source: segment market share versus Aidoc, ScreenPoint, RadNet/DeepHealth, Paige, Tempus and large imaging OEM software stacks.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.55 / 0.60

The moat is plausible but still forming. Regulatory clearances, clinical publications, embedded OEM/PACS partnerships, a large installed base and Volpara's breast-health data/workflow footprint are useful barriers. The Q2 IR letter added a U.S. patent covering INSIGHT CXR's current-prior lesion-comparison technology, while Lunit and Leica launched SCOPE PD-L1 CAL10 inside Leica's Aperio workflow. SCOPE may gain advantage if these integrations become embedded in pharma biomarker and clinical-trial workflows.

But the moat is not yet proven in numbers: the company has negative ROIC, heavy cash burn, and no disclosed retention / NRR in the sources reviewed. Large imaging OEMs, hospital IT vendors and better-funded AI-health companies could compress pricing or bundle competing tools. Firm-up source: renewal rates, gross retention, net revenue retention and win/loss data.

### Durable Growth — 0.78 / 0.82

Growth is real, though decelerating from a small base. Revenue rose from **KRW 13.9b in 2022** to **KRW 83.1b in 2025** (~82% CAGR, 53% in 2025) and H1 2026 grew **23% to KRW 45.8b** (Q2 +22.5%). The SCOPE segment accelerated from +159% in 2025 to +114% in H1 2026 on pharma collaborations. Gross margin is reported at 100% in Yahoo's normalized data, consistent with software-like economics, and management reiterated its FY2026 EBITDA break-even target while H1 EBITDA loss narrowed ~50%.

The penalty is deceleration and cash burn, though the trend is improving: revenue growth slowed from 116% in 2024 to 53% in 2025 to ~23% in H1 2026, and both operating and EBITDA losses, while still negative, are narrowing materially. Firm-up source: H2 2026 filings confirming whether EBITDA break-even is genuinely on track rather than a one-half event.

### Organic Growth — 0.45 / 0.75

This is the weakest rule-breaker trait. Lunit's headline growth is not purely organic: the May 2024 Volpara acquisition created a large goodwill balance, added direct breast-health infrastructure and SaaS revenue, and Yahoo-derived data shows acquisition spend of roughly **KRW 255b** in 2024 plus share-count growth of about **30%** from 2022 to 2025.

There is still organic evidence — pre-acquisition revenue growth was strong and SCOPE grew 159% in 2025 — but the current company is materially acquisition-shaped. Score stays mixed until Lunit discloses organic growth excluding Volpara / Lunit International.

### Management Backing — 0.72 / 0.62

Lunit retains founder involvement: Seungwook Paek, co-founder and chairman, was reappointed as inside director in 2026 and is described by the company as leading key business strategy, U.S. market/customer strategy and governance enhancement. The company has executed credible global partnerships and acquired Volpara to deepen U.S. breast-health access.

Confidence is moderate because the sources reviewed show strategic activity but not enough evidence on execution quality through adversity or culture. Firm-up source: long-form shareholder letters / earnings-call transcripts and employee/customer references.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.60 / 0.58

There are some positive signals: the rights offering closed with a 104.7% existing-shareholder subscription rate; Chairman Seungwook Paek and CEO Beomseok Suh reinvested all proceeds from selling subscription rights, while Atinum Investment committed KRW 30b. Paek is the largest shareholder, but the reported largest-shareholder stake was only **6.76%** in the 2025 business report table; Yahoo reports insiders around **13.8%** overall. The AGM also approved stock options, and repeated equity issuance means dilution discipline still matters.

Confidence is low because I did not review the full Korean remuneration mechanics. Firm-up source: remuneration report with option terms, hurdles, vesting periods, executive ownership changes and dilution from grants.

### Customer Devotion — 0.62 / 0.60

Adoption and renewal evidence strengthened. 7,400+ INSIGHT institutions and 3,600+ Volpara/Lunit International institutions use the products (over 10,000 company-wide); Lunit International renewed RadNet, and the integrated breast-screening platform reached **330+ providers across the Americas**. New 2026 contracts with VA Tulsa, DeTar and GenesisCare UK, plus continued upselling of Patient Hub / Risk Pathways and Analytics / Scorecard into existing Lunit International customers, point to platform expansion and retention. H1 INSIGHT growth (20%) outpaced the broader screening base, suggesting renewing customers are adding adjacent products.

But this is not yet proof of devotion. The missing evidence is retention, expansion, NRR, logo churn, usage intensity, renewal pricing and customer advocacy independent of distribution partners. Firm-up source: SaaS retention metrics and enterprise case studies with renewal / expansion detail.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair and cash-cow fail mechanically because Lunit has no demonstrated consistent profits, negative FCF and negative returns on capital. Freeroll also fails because there is no hard valuation floor: tangible book and net cash floors are negative, and the current price is not backed by dividends or assets. Lunit is best framed as a rule-breaker: real growth in a large AI-healthcare opportunity, offset by funding, dilution and moat uncertainty.

## Overall

Lunit is a legitimate rule-breaker candidate, but a risky one. The attractive part is clear: clinically deployed AI cancer-screening software, a growing oncology-biomarker platform, global partners, U.S. government and hospital wins, and revenue scaling fast from a small base (H1 2026 record KRW 45.8b, +23%, with North America +30% and SCOPE +114%). H1 operating loss narrowed 31% and EBITDA loss ~50%, and management reiterates the FY2026 EBITDA break-even target; the completed rights offering removes the immediate funding overhang. The unattractive part remains losses, negative FCF, acquisition-led growth, substantial dilution and uncertain competitive durability, plus decelerating top-line growth and a now higher entry price.

I would keep it on watch rather than treat it as allocation-ready. The next 12–18 months matter: if revenue keeps compounding while EBITDA break-even arrives without repeated dilution, the score can rise sharply. If the rights issue only buys time and losses persist, the thesis weakens fast. The improved cost discipline and the broader INSIGHT/SCOPE trajectory are genuine positives that offset some of the risk from the higher entry price.

## Thesis Connections

Possible FIT: AI-enabled healthcare workflow automation and precision oncology tooling. The strongest connection is not generic "AI in healthcare"; it is software embedded into clinical and pharma workflows where regulatory clearance, workflow integration and data feedback loops can create durable switching costs.

# 3690.HK — Meituan

## Business Overview

Meituan is China's dominant local-commerce platform. Revenues come from three engines: **food delivery** (the largest local-commerce category), **in-store/hotel/travel** (IHT — commission and advertising on restaurant bookings, hotels, experiences), and **new initiatives** (grocery/instant retail via Xiaoxiang Supermarket, and overseas food delivery via Keeta). Delivery and IHT together make up **Core Local Commerce**, which was over RMB 333 billion of 2025 revenue; new initiatives contributed RMB ~32 billion. FY2025 total revenue was **RMB 364.9 billion (+8.1% YoY)**, but the company swung to a reported net loss of **RMB 23.4 billion** (vs +RMB 35.8 billion profit in 2024) — driven by the 2025 **food-delivery subsidy war** (JD.com and Alibaba's Ele.me entered with aggressive incentives) plus a **fair-value loss on investments**. The underlying adjusted (non-IFRS) loss was RMB 18.6 billion, confirming the damage was primarily competitive, not an accounting fluke. Q1 2026 showed early normalization: revenue RMB 91.0 billion (+5.6% YoY), adjusted net loss narrowing to RMB 5.0 billion, food-delivery unit economics approaching breakeven, and IHT margins holding ~25%. The company holds ~RMB 180 billion cash incl. short-term investments vs ~RMB 113 billion debt (net cash), and does not pay a dividend.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.60 / 0.60

Local-commerce (delivery + in-store services) is a massive, still-underpenetrated market. The global local-commerce delivery market doc places 2026 spend-share at ~11% of eligible restaurant/grocery/retail spend, rising to ~22% by 2036, with the market expanding USD 95bn → 270bn. China is the largest single market and Meituan is its structural leader. But the opportunity is now contested (JD, Ele.me, Douyin), and China macro/consumer sentiment is soft — so this is a good but not exceptional setup, partially crowded. Confidence 0.60: the market doc and company disclosures support the size; the competitive intensity is a live negative.

### Top Dog — 0.65 / 0.55

Meituan is the clear #1 in China food delivery (~60%+ blended GTV share; >70% on orders above RMB 30) and in local services. It was not the first mover in every category but consolidated the market. The lead is real but was tested in 2025: JD's entry and Ele.me subsidies took share temporarily, and Douyin keeps pressure on in-store. The franchise survived, with Q1 2026 showing share holding above 60% and competitor UE gap *widening* to Meituan's advantage. It's a strong contender, arguably dominant, but not yet a category-defining winner on the scale of an Amazon-in-ecommerce with an unassailable moat. Confidence 0.55 (self-reported market share; competitive read partly from sell-side).

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.55 / 0.50

The moat is mostly **scale and density economics** — the largest courier network and order density in China lower cost per delivery and make matching extremely capital-intensive — plus merchant relationships, habit (app usage), and logistics tech. These are genuine but not unassailable: the 2025 subsidy war showed players with comparable capital can force Meituan to spend. The moat is stable-to-widening (density compounds), but it is more "strong economics in a contestable space" than a bulletproof network effect like a payments rail. Confidence 0.50 — the moat strength is evidenced by share retention and UE but the durability is being actively tested by well-funded entrants.

### Durable Growth — 0.45 / 0.60

Revenue growth has decelerated sharply: +25.8% (2023) → +22.0% (2024) → +8.1% (2025) → +5.6% (Q1 2026), and delivery revenue actually fell ~7% YoY in Q1 2026 as subsidies normalized. The growth engine is no longer compounding at high rates; it's a large, mature-ish franchise growing at single digits in core delivery, with faster growth in new initiatives (Keeta overseas, grocery) that are still loss-making. FCF collapsed to −RMB 27bn in 2025 (subsidy-driven) from +RMB 46bn in 2024 — the reinvestment carve-out analogy doesn't fully apply because the spending was defensive competition, not clearly improving unit economics. This "no growth vetoes" floor-0 trait scores clearly below the bar for a rule-breaker. Confidence 0.60 (reported financials).

### Organic Growth — 0.90 / 0.80

Growth is overwhelmingly organic. Cumulative acquisition spend over 4 years is only RMB 0.47bn (0.1% of revenue); goodwill is ~8% of assets and flat; diluted share count fell ~1.3% over the period (net buybacks/dilution offset). No roll-up or serial acquirer fingerprint. Confidence 0.80 (cash-flow statement and share count; no major acquisitions disclosed).

### Management Backing — 0.60 / 0.50

Founder Wang Xing remains CEO after the 2024-25 market turbulence and made the strategic choice to defend share rather than protect margins. The backers are high quality (Tencent historically, large institutional holders ~39%). Management has a long-term orientation but the 2025 decision to fight the subsidy war — while defensible strategically — was expensive and the communication around recovery has been incremental. Confidence 0.50 (public sources only; no deep governance access).

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 / 0.50

Insider ownership is low (~1.8%). Compensation is oriented to long-term results, but stock-based comp ~RMB 6bn/yr is modest relative to revenue (~1.6%) and share count is roughly stable. No obvious self-dealing surfaced. The alignment is decent but not exceptional — founders hold less skin than typical Chinese founder-led platforms (Wang Xing's stake is meaningful but not super-voting controlling). Confidence 0.50.

### Customer Devotion — 0.65 / 0.50

The strongest loyalty signal is **behavioral**: over 60% GTV share and >70% share on high-value orders despite rival subsidies, plus strong merchant dependency on the platform. Meituan's membership (Shen/Kuai scheme) drives stickiness. But it's partly lock-in (merchant necessity, habit) rather than pure love — customers multi-home when incentives appear, as 2025 showed. No first-party NPS disclosed. Confidence 0.50.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Wonderful-and-Fair**: earnings-quality is mid at best (0.40 — FY25 net loss, 2 of last 4 years loss-making, adjusted-profit reliance), returns-on-capital weak (0.38 — FY25 ROIC −9%, FY24 +16%, volatile), pricing-power modest (0.45 — competition forced subsidy spending). Not a consistent compounder on the current record.
- **Cash-Cow**: free-cash-flow-generation 0.35 and earnings-quality 0.40 dog it — FY25 FCF was −RMB 27bn; the business is not a dependable cash machine outside the subsidized trough. Fails the defining floor traits.
- **Freeroll**: the floor (downside-support 0.55) is real (net cash ~RMB 67bn, ~10x forward earnings) but the catalyst (0.55) is a gradual recovery over 2-4 quarters, not a sharp re-rating trigger on a ≤2-year clock; mispricing 0.55 is a "recovery undervalued" view, not a differentiated variant perception. It's a decent freeroll but the leaderboard prefers rule-breakers (S×C 0.34 vs 0.28).

## Overall

Meituan has real strengths: dominant, density-compounding position in China's largest local-commerce market; an expanding global delivery opportunity (Keeta); fortress balance sheet; disciplined, organic growth; proven resilience through the 2025 price war. The detractors are significant: growth has decelerated to single digits in core; the 2025 loss and negative FCF show the moat is contestable; margins are depressed by competition (IHT ~25% OP margin is the bright spot, delivery is breakeven-ish); and China macro/consumer policy risk is ever-present. It scores as a "solid but not exceptional rule-breaker" — a top dog in a good industry whose growth engine has faded, weaker on the two veto traits (right-place-and-time is strong-but-crowded; durable-growth clearly decelerating).

## Watch

- **Q2 2026 results (due Aug 28, 2026)** — the single most important near-term item: food-delivery UE approaching/at breakeven, IHT margins holding ~25%, new-initiatives loss trend, and any guidance.
- **Food-delivery war relapse**: JD.com / Ele.me subsidy behavior, regulatory stance (SAMR anti-subsidy pressure), and whether Meituan keeps ~60% blended share above RMB 30 orders.
- **Adjusted vs IFRS profitability**: mix of fair-value investment swings vs operating economics in future prints; whether adjusted profit re-inflects to the RMB 33-40bn run-rate.
- **Keeta international (Saudi, Brazil, 55 cities)** — loss trajectory and path to breakeven; a sustained cash sink would cap the bull case.
- **Grocery/instant-retail (Xiaoxiang Supermarket)** unit economics vs JD/douyin — scale economics are brutal; watch AOV and density gains.
- **China macro/consumer policy** — any stimulus, regulatory action on platform commissions, or consumption-tax changes.
- **Dilution/share-count**: buyback cadence — modest net share shrinkage (−1.3% over 4y) could become a real buyback engine returning cash.

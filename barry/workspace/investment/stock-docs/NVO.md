# NVO — Novo Nordisk A/S

## Business Overview

Novo Nordisk is a Danish global pharmaceuticals company focused on diabetes, obesity, and rare disease. The profit engine is GLP-1-based diabetes and obesity medicines: **Ozempic**, **Wegovy**, **Rybelsus** and related pipeline assets. The company sells through healthcare systems, payers, pharmacies and prescribers globally, with the U.S. the most economically important market.

2025 sales were **DKK 309.1b**, up **6% reported / 10% CER**. Obesity care grew **26% reported / 31% CER** to **DKK 82.3b**; diabetes care was flat reported / up 4% CER to **DKK 207.1b**. The company remained highly profitable, with 2025 operating profit of **DKK 127.7b**, though reported operating profit fell 1% as gross margin compressed from **84.7% to 81.0%** on Catalent-site amortisation/depreciation, restructuring costs, capacity expansion and currency headwinds.

Q2 2026 showed a better underlying trajectory than the difficult full-year setup implied. Adjusted sales rose **7% CER** to **DKK 78.5b** and adjusted operating profit rose **11% CER** to **DKK 33.4b**. For H1, adjusted sales and operating profit both grew **2% CER**; obesity care grew **19% CER**, while GLP-1 diabetes sales fell **5% CER**. Novo raised 2026 adjusted sales and operating-profit guidance to **0% to -6% CER**, from **-4% to -12%**, because GLP-1 expectations improved. Reported H1 figures are not comparable without adjustment because they include a DKK 26.8b 340B rebate-provision reversal; Q2 operating profit also includes DKK 6.3b of non-cash pipeline impairments.

The story has shifted from scarcity-led hypergrowth to competitive execution. Novo is still a superb franchise, but Eli Lilly has turned GLP-1 into a two-horse race. Consensus on 8 August still expected 2026 revenue of about **DKK 295.9b** and EPS of **DKK 21.81**, both below 2025 despite the guidance upgrade. The ADR traded at **US$47.26**, implying about **13.1x** the normalised US$3.60 earnings anchor used below and a market cap around **US$209b**.

No universal disqualifier found. The balance sheet is not stressed, there is no evidence of fraud or hidden liabilities in the sources reviewed, and the business model is not broken. The risk is competitive erosion and valuation assumptions, not eligibility.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.80 / confidence 0.84

Novo has demonstrated real, repeatable earning power. Yahoo financials show net income rising from **DKK 55.5b in 2022** to **DKK 102.4b in 2025**, with no loss years in the available four-year series. Margins remain exceptional: 2025 gross margin was **81.0%**, and Yahoo's TTM snapshot shows profit margin around **37%** and operating margin around **62%**.

The caveat is cash conversion. Free cash flow was strong in 2022-24 but fell to **DKK 29.0b in 2025**, only **0.28x** net income, because capex surged to support GLP-1 capacity. H1 2026 FCF rebounded **44%** to **DKK 55.3b** as operating cash flow reached DKK 79.3b and PP&E spending fell 15% to DKK 24.0b. This supports the view that 2025's weakness was investment-led, although the H1 cash figure also benefits from timing and does not yet establish a new run rate.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.88 / confidence 0.85

The returns remain outstanding. The returns script shows ROIC of **33.4% in 2025**, averaging **48.8%** over 2022-25, with ROCE at **41.2%** in 2025. Even after the Catalent-related asset build and heavy capacity spending, these are well above any reasonable cost of capital.

The direction is the only reason this is not closer to perfect: ROIC has fallen from the extraordinary 2023-24 levels as capital employed rose sharply. Incremental ROIC is still strong at about **23.6%**, so the business is creating value on new capital rather than merely harvesting the old base.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.78 / confidence 0.82

Novo's moat is built on drug IP, clinical data, regulatory approvals, manufacturing scale, prescriber familiarity, payer relationships, and category leadership in diabetes/obesity GLP-1s. Gross margins above **80%** and decades of diabetes profitability are strong quantitative evidence that the moat has been real.

The moat is no longer unchallenged. Lilly's tirzepatide franchise has become a formidable competitor, and payer pressure/direct-to-consumer channels are making price and access more important. Q2 adjusted U.S. obesity-care sales grew only **4% CER** as volume gains were partly offset by lower realised prices; H1 U.S. adjusted sales fell **4% CER**. At the same time, Novo retained leadership in branded-obesity new-patient starts, and the Wegovy franchise reached about **575,000 weekly U.S. prescriptions** by 17 July. The evidence now supports a real but narrowing moat, hence the lower score and higher confidence.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.76 / confidence 0.84

The runway is still meaningful: obesity prevalence is large, GLP-1 penetration remains low relative to the potential treated population, and Novo is investing heavily in capacity, oral semaglutide, CagriSema and adjacent metabolic/cardiovascular indications. Novo's 2025 annual report says the branded GLP-1 obesity market grew **104% by volume** and that Novo held **59.6%** global branded volume share.

The constraint is that this is no longer a wide-open, winner-takes-most setup. Lilly is already capturing a large share of incremental economics, pricing is likely to keep normalising, and 2026 consensus still implies a pause or contraction. The commercial evidence nevertheless improved: Wegovy pill exceeded **265,000 weekly U.S. prescriptions** by 17 July and more than **5m cumulative prescriptions** since launch, while early UAE and UK uptake was encouraging. The **15 July EU approval of Wegovy pill and single-pen Wegovy 7.2 mg** opens further rollout in H2. Against that, the **31 July ZEUS phase 3 failure** narrowed the cardiovascular pipeline; HERMES and ARTEMIS continue toward H1 2027 readouts.

### Capital Allocation — 0.68 / confidence 0.80

Capital allocation is mixed but broadly rational. Novo returned substantial cash in 2022-24 through dividends and buybacks, then throttled buybacks sharply in 2025 while capex surged — a sensible response if manufacturing capacity is the bottleneck and the shares were no longer obviously cheap. The capital-allocation script shows cumulative dividends of **DKK 153.0b** and buybacks of **DKK 75.6b** over 2022-25, with the diluted share count down about **2.1%**. Novo has since authorised up to **DKK 15b** of repurchases over the 12 months from 4 February 2026; this is supportive at the current valuation but small relative to market value.

As of 3 August, Novo had repurchased **DKK 7.53b** of B shares since 4 February at an average DKK 278.35, while H1 FCF recovered to DKK 55.3b. The blemishes remain timing and complexity: the company spent heavily on acquisitions/capacity in 2024-25, 2025 dividend coverage by FCF was weak, and DKK 6.3b of Q2 pipeline impairments — including DKK 4.0b for monlunabant — show that some development capital will not earn a return. Confidence would improve after reviewing the full Catalent/Akero economics and buyback prices versus intrinsic value.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.66 / confidence 0.82

Novo remains strongly cash-generative, but current capacity investment keeps it below a pure cash-cow profile. Yahoo's TTM snapshot shows **DKK 47.8b** of FCF, a **14.5%** margin, after DKK 29.0b in 2025. The H1 filing is more encouraging: FCF rose **44%** to **DKK 55.3b**, while PP&E capex fell 15% to DKK 24.0b and consumed about 30% of operating cash flow. The 2022-25 average FCF margin was about 25%, but the trend fell as capex rose far above depreciation. The cash is genuine and not the product of deferred maintenance; the uncertainty is how much current spending is temporary growth capex versus the ongoing cost of defending GLP-1 scale.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.72 / confidence 0.82

Demand for diabetes and obesity treatment is secular, and the franchise is not a melting ice cube: 2025 revenue and EPS remained at record levels, Q2 adjusted sales returned to **7% CER** growth, and management raised guidance. The weak points are economic rather than demand-related. H1 U.S. adjusted sales fell 4% CER, realised prices declined, and full-year adjusted sales are still guided between flat and down 6%. Novo therefore looks fundamentally durable but less stable than its pre-2025 record; stability depends on oral Wegovy, access expansion and international volume offsetting price and share pressure in the U.S.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.88 / confidence 0.90

The balance sheet is conservative for a stable, high-margin pharma company. The debt script shows 2025 net debt / EBITDA of **0.61x**, EBIT interest coverage around **45.9x**, and near-term debt covered **4.7x** by cash plus annual FCF. Yahoo's TTM snapshot shows net debt / EBITDA still below **1x**.

Leverage has risen from near-zero levels because of acquisitions and capacity investment, and current ratios are optically low. But net debt improved from **DKK 95.4b at December 2025 to DKK 86.5b at June 2026**, while H1 FCF reached DKK 55.3b. The absolute leverage remains modest and operating cash flow is very large. This is a resilience strength, not a thesis risk.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / confidence 0.75

Novo is institutionally strong rather than founder-dependent. The company has more than a century of operating history, deep scientific/manufacturing capability, and advantages embedded in products, approvals, scale, and relationships. 2025 did include a CEO transition from Lars Fruergaard Jørgensen to Mike Doustdar and a board reset, which is a governance event worth watching, but the franchise does not depend on one individual.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.62 / confidence 0.65

The remuneration evidence is adequate but not pristine. The 2025 remuneration report says short-term incentive outcomes were materially below maximum and LTIP 2024/2025 were tracking below targets, which suggests some pay-for-performance discipline. The same report also shows large departure/severance economics for the former CEO, which is not ideal optics.

The Novo Nordisk Foundation control structure creates a long-term stewardship orientation, but minority shareholders ultimately rely on that steward acting rationally. Confidence would improve with a deeper review of exact STI/LTI metrics, return-on-capital/per-share value linkage, and executive shareholdings.

**Strategy fit:** Cash Cow is a genuine secondary fit (**0.758 score / 0.813 confidence**) because earnings, FCF, balance-sheet resilience and capital returns are strong. Wonderful and Fair remains primary (**0.784 / 0.816**) because Novo still has credible high-return reinvestment routes in oral GLP-1s, capacity and new indications; it is not merely harvesting a mature franchise. Rule Breakers has a large growth market but Novo is a mature, highly profitable incumbent, not an emerging disruptor. Freeroll is weak: there is no hard valuation floor near the current price and no clean, time-bound catalyst that forces fair value within four years.

## Overall

Novo is a high-quality compounder that has gone from market darling to contested incumbent. The business quality is still excellent: very high ROIC, enormous margins, low leverage, and leadership in a structurally important therapeutic category. Q2's underlying growth, oral-Wegovy uptake and raised guidance make the reset look more manageable, while the share price remains well below the GLP-1-euphoria valuation.

The reason the score is only moderate-to-good is that the next chapter is harder. Growth has decelerated, U.S. realised prices are falling, Lilly is taking share/mindshare, 2026 consensus still points to lower revenue and EPS, and ZEUS removes one cardiovascular expansion path. FCF recovery is encouraging but needs to persist. This remains a **Wonderful and Fair** candidate, not a slam-dunk: the thesis needs oral Wegovy and broader access to offset pressure on injectable franchises and convert capacity spending into renewed profitable growth.

## Valuation

I used the exit-multiple method on **earnings**, because Novo is consistently profitable and the market usually values large pharma on earnings rather than temporarily investment-depressed FCF. Inputs refreshed 9 August 2026: **US$47.26** ADR price (7 August close); **US$3.60** current normalised owner-earnings anchor, triangulated between the 2025 result, H1 adjusted EPS and current consensus; five-year aggregate growth path of **3%, 3%, 5%, 6%, 6%**; **15x** exit P/E; **-0.5%** annual dilution from net buybacks; and **3.9%** dividend yield. Consensus still expects a difficult 2026 — about **DKK 295.9b revenue and DKK 21.81 EPS** — followed in 2027 by only **2.5% revenue growth** and a **0.9% EPS decline**. The raised guidance improves confidence that the growth trough is manageable, but does not justify increasing the growth path.

The refreshed model produces an **11.79% annualised ROI** over five years (1.75x total return). Approximate annualised attribution is **4.59% business growth, 0.50% buyback lift, 2.70% multiple re-rating and 3.83% income**. This is more conservative than the prior 17x case and depends mainly on compounding and payout rather than a large re-rating. The main fragility is still whether a 15x quality-pharma multiple remains deserved if Novo loses obesity economics structurally. If oral Wegovy, CagriSema and capacity investments work, the growth path may be too low. My qualitative valuation-aware score is **0.62**: Q2 reduces near-term execution risk, but lower pricing and the ZEUS miss keep the thesis from scoring higher.

As a secondary lens, the TAM-capture model reads Novo's **$27.44b mobility-adjusted 2036 obesity revenue** directly from `global-obesity-pharmacotherapy`. It applies a **50% EBITDA margin**, **18x EV/EBITDA**, **-0.5% annual dilution** from net buybacks and zero terminal net debt. Lilly is the direct role model: its current EBITDA margin is about **52.4%** and EV/EBITDA about **27.4x**, so the inputs haircut both operating economics and the ten-year exit rating. At the **US$47.23** ADR price and **4.448b** diluted shares, the model produces a **2.14% annualised ROI** over ten years (1.24x; **US$58.38** terminal value), comprising about **1.63% annual value creation** and **0.50% annual buyback lift**.

This is intentionally not the selected ROI. The obesity market doc excludes Novo's diabetes-labelled and rare-disease revenue, so TAM-capture materially understates whole-company terminal value and is not a standalone expected-return estimate for this mature diversified incumbent. The five-year earnings exit-multiple remains the primary valuation and the input to overall ranking.

## Thesis Connections

Potential FIT connection: healthcare platforms that treat large chronic diseases with durable demand and high returns on capital. Novo is relevant to any obesity/metabolic-health thesis, but the key question is whether it remains a dominant economics capturer or becomes one of several price-competing suppliers.

## Watch

- Q3/FY2026 GLP-1 obesity and diabetes market share, especially U.S. Wegovy/Ozempic versus Lilly; separate volume from realised price.
- Net pricing / gross-to-net pressure in the U.S. and whether direct-to-consumer channels compress realised price.
- Conversion of 2024-25 capex and Catalent capacity into volume growth and FCF recovery.
- H2 2026 European launch timing, reimbursement, realised pricing and early uptake for the newly approved Wegovy pill and single-pen 7.2 mg; continue tracking CagriSema and amycretin milestones.
- Whether 2026 revenue/EPS contraction stabilises into renewed growth in 2027.
- Whether raised 2026 adjusted sales and operating-profit guidance of **0% to -6% CER** holds through Q3, and whether H1 FCF recovery persists without working-capital help.
- HERMES and ARTEMIS ziltivekimab readouts expected in **H1 2027**; assess whether ZEUS's null MACE result is population-specific or weakens the mechanism broadly, and quantify the Q3 non-cash impairment.
- Updated remuneration metrics and any further governance changes after the 2025 CEO/board reset.

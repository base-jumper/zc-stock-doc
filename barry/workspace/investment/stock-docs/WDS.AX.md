# WDS.AX — Woodside Energy Group Ltd

## Business Overview

Woodside is a large Australian-headquartered oil and gas producer with LNG, pipeline gas, crude/condensate, NGL and emerging ammonia exposure. Revenue is driven mainly by production volumes and realised commodity prices across Australian LNG/gas assets including Pluto, North West Shelf and Wheatstone, international oil/gas assets including Sangomar, Shenzi, Atlantis and Mad Dog, plus marketing/trading.

The 2025 result was operationally strong but financially cyclical: Woodside reported record production of 198.8 MMboe, US$12.984b revenue, US$2.718b NPAT, US$9.277b EBITDA excluding impairment, US$7.8/boe unit production cost, and a US112c/share full-year dividend at an 80% underlying NPAT payout. Q1 2026 production was 45.2 MMboe, down 8% vs Q1 2025, while operating revenue was US$3.261b, down 2% vs Q1 2025 and up 7% vs Q4 2025.

The forward story is project execution. Scarborough/Pluto Train 2 was 96% complete at Q1 2026 and remains on budget for first LNG in Q4 2026; Trion was 56% complete and targeting first oil in 2028; Louisiana LNG was 24% complete, Train 1 31% complete, targeting first LNG in 2029. Those projects can extend production and cash generation, but they also explain the heavy current capex and weak free cash flow.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.56 / confidence 0.78

Woodside is consistently profitable on the limited Yahoo/filing window, with no loss years from 2022–2025 and 2025 NPAT of US$2.718b. The caveat is cyclicality and accounting noise: net income fell from US$6.498b in the 2022 commodity-price peak to US$1.660b in 2023, recovered to US$3.573b in 2024, then fell 24% in 2025. Yahoo-derived net-income growth dispersion is high, and 2023 included US$1.917b of impairments while 2025 had a smaller US$143m impairment.

Cash backing is mixed. Cumulative 2022–2025 free cash flow was only about 46% of cumulative net income, mostly because growth capex is consuming operating cash. This is not a fraud/accrual red flag, but it means reported earnings are not currently dropping cleanly to owner cash. Confidence would improve with a longer through-cycle commodity-price history and maintenance-vs-growth capex split.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.34 / confidence 0.82

This is the main weakness for a cash-cow frame. Woodside generated US$7.192b operating cash flow in 2025, but capex of US$7.974b drove free cash flow to negative US$782m. FCF margins were strong in 2022 during peak prices, modest in 2023–2024, and negative in 2025; cumulative 2022–2025 FCF conversion was only 46% of net income. The dividend was not covered by FCF in 2025.

Interpretation: Woodside owns real cash-generating assets, but the company is currently in a heavy reinvestment/build phase rather than harvesting surplus cash. That makes it a low-scoring cash cow until Scarborough and later projects convert from capex consumers into cash contributors.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.58 / confidence 0.72

Demand for LNG and oil is not disappearing over the medium term, and Woodside has long-life assets plus major sanctioned projects. The company also secured long-term LNG sale agreements in 2025, and Q1 2026 guidance still called for 172–186 MMboe production for 2026. That supports durability.

The offset is commodity cyclicality and energy-transition uncertainty. Revenue, margins, EPS and FCF are all down materially from 2022 peaks; the business is price-taking rather than structurally stable. Scarborough, Trion and Louisiana LNG reduce depletion risk but add execution and capital-cycle risk. Confidence would firm up with updated reserve-life economics and post-startup cost/volume delivery for Scarborough.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.46 / confidence 0.66

Woodside has scale, operating capability, LNG marketing relationships, infrastructure positions and advantaged Australian gas/LNG assets. Those are real competitive advantages in project access and reliability: operated LNG reliability was about 98% in 2025 and key assets reported ≥99% reliability in Q1 2026.

But the product is still substantially commoditised. Woodside can optimise marketing exposure and contract structures, yet realised prices move with LNG, oil and gas benchmarks. This is a narrow moat, not a consumer/brand/software-style pricing-power moat. Confidence is moderate because a deeper contract-by-contract price-indexing review would sharpen the score.

### Capital Allocation — 0.50 / confidence 0.73

Woodside’s framework is sensible on paper: investment-grade credit rating, 10–20% target gearing, and a 50–80% underlying NPAT payout ratio. The 2025 dividend was at the top of that payout range, and gearing of 18.2% was inside target. Partnering at Louisiana LNG with Stonepeak and Williams also reduces Woodside’s capital exposure to below total project cost.

The concern is timing and cash coverage. Dividends exceeded free cash flow in 2023 and 2025, while the company is simultaneously funding Scarborough, Trion and Louisiana LNG. This may be rational if the projects deliver, but from a cash-cow lens it is not clean surplus-cash distribution. A sustained period of post-Scarborough FCF-covered dividends would raise the score.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.37 / confidence 0.80

Returns are below the bar for a high-quality compounder/cow. Yahoo-derived ROIC was 6.7% in 2025, 8.5% in 2024, 6.5% in 2023 and 15.5% in 2022, averaging about 9.3% and trending down. ROE was 7.6% in 2025. For a cyclical, capital-heavy energy producer, that is not disastrous, but it is not clearly above cost of capital across the cycle.

The major projects could improve capital productivity if delivered on budget into favourable prices, but that is an underwriting assumption rather than demonstrated durability.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.70 / confidence 0.82

The balance sheet is a relative strength. Woodside reported 18.2% gearing at 2025 year-end, inside its 10–20% through-cycle target, US$9.3b liquidity, investment-grade credit ratings, and net debt/EBITDA below 1x on Yahoo/script calculations. Current and quick ratios were also healthy.

The caveat is cycle and capex. Net debt rose with growth spending, 2025 FCF was negative, and 2026 capex guidance remained US$4.0–4.5b. Debt is not alarming today, but the balance-sheet score depends on disciplined project delivery and continued access to debt markets.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / confidence 0.65

Woodside is an institutional-scale operator with a deep technical and commercial bench, diversified producing assets and formal governance. Liz Westcott was appointed CEO/Managing Director in 2026, but the business does not appear dependent on a founder or single rainmaker. Confidence is only moderate because this was assessed from public governance/scale evidence rather than a detailed management-bench review.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.58 / confidence 0.58

Governance appears broadly acceptable for a large listed company, and Yahoo governance risk is low-to-moderate overall. The concern is that industry executive incentives often lean on production, project and adjusted earnings metrics that can reward size and growth as much as per-share value. Insider ownership is also low. Confidence is the lowest trait score because I did not do a full remuneration-table teardown; that should be checked in the next update if WDS becomes a serious candidate.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair fails on durable high returns and reinvestment quality; this is too cyclical and capital-heavy. Rule-breakers is not the right frame for a mature commodity producer. Freeroll has possible catalysts in Scarborough/Trion, but no clean downside floor plus mispricing case was established; price-to-book around 1.1x is not enough by itself.

## Overall

Woodside is a real, investable energy major, but not a clean high-quality cash cow right now. The positives are scale, project pipeline, LNG demand exposure, solid liquidity, management’s stated payout discipline, and near-term Scarborough startup. The detractors are more important for scoring: commodity-price dependence, mediocre through-cycle ROIC, negative 2025 free cash flow, dividends not currently covered by FCF, and large execution-heavy capex commitments.

My current view: worth watching for a post-Scarborough cash-flow inflection, but not a high-conviction allocation candidate at A$29.05 unless Nick specifically wants LNG/oil exposure or believes the market is underpricing the project pipeline.

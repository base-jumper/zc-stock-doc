# CPRT — Copart, Inc.

## Business Overview

Copart is a global online vehicle auction and remarketing platform, best known for salvage and total-loss vehicles. Sellers are mainly insurers, plus dealers, rental/fleet operators, charities, financial institutions, and other vehicle owners. Buyers are licensed dismantlers, rebuilders, used-car dealers, exporters, and consumers who bid through Copart's internet-only auction platform.

The engine is simple but powerful: Copart aggregates damaged/used vehicles, handles towing, storage, title processing, vehicle imaging, sale logistics, and auction liquidity, then charges seller and buyer fees. Its moat comes from physical yard density, insurer relationships, title/process capability, buyer liquidity, and a global online marketplace that can route local salvage supply to the highest-value buyer worldwide. In FY2025, Copart generated $4.65b of revenue, $1.70b of operating income, $1.55b of net income, and $1.23b of free cash flow. The 2025 10-K says 69.8% of U.S. vehicle units sold went to buyers outside the state where the vehicle was located, including 38.8% to international members by auction IP address — a useful proof point for marketplace liquidity.

Growth is now more mature. FY2022-2025 revenue grew at about 10% CAGR and margins stayed excellent, but current analyst estimates and Q3 FY2026 reporting point to a slower near-term patch: Q3 FY2026 revenue was reported at about $1.2b, +2.1% YoY, while consensus FY2026 revenue/EPS are roughly flat before FY2027 growth resumes.

## Trait Assessment

Primary strategy: **Wonderful and Fair**. Copart is a very high-quality, asset-backed marketplace with real cash earnings, negligible financial leverage, and some remaining reinvestment runway in land, international growth, non-insurance sellers, and higher-value services. The pushback is valuation and growth deceleration: this is wonderful, but only arguably fair after the share-price decline.

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.88 / 0.86

The earnings are real and cash-backed. FY2025 revenue was $4.65b, operating income $1.70b, net income $1.55b, and free cash flow $1.23b. FY2022-FY2025 net income rose from $1.09b to $1.55b, and free cash flow rose from $839m to $1.23b. Margins are unusually steady for an auction/asset-services business: gross margin stayed around 45%, operating margin stayed in the mid/high-30s, and net margin was above 31% across the latest four annual periods.

Quality is not perfect because salvage volumes, used-car prices, catastrophic weather, and insurer behavior can move results. The latest estimates also show near-term growth flattening. Still, this is not accounting fluff: operating cash flow in FY2025 was $1.80b versus net income including non-controlling interests of $1.55b.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.74 / 0.82

Reported returns are good, but no longer absurdly high. Yahoo-derived ROIC moved from about 24.2% in FY2022 to 15.1% in FY2025, while ROE moved from 23.6% to 16.9%. That decline partly reflects heavy reinvestment in land/facilities and a very cash-rich balance sheet, not a broken business. The underlying margin structure remains excellent: FY2025 operating margin was 36.5% and EBITDA margin was about 41%.

The score is high, not elite, because Copart is capital intensive in a way software marketplaces are not. Land, yards, towing capacity, and catastrophe response capacity are part of the moat, but they absorb cash and can lower incremental returns if growth slows.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.78 / 0.75

Copart has real moat evidence: decades of insurer relationships, national/international yard density, title-processing capability, and auction liquidity. The 2025 10-K's out-of-state and international buyer mix supports the idea that Copart's online marketplace improves vehicle realization by widening the bidder pool. That liquidity is hard for a small competitor to replicate.

This is not a pure price-hike story. Insurers are sophisticated customers, IAA/RB Global and other auction channels remain relevant, and fee structures are visible enough to invite pushback if Copart over-earns. Confidence is 0.75 because the next level of proof would be customer-level retention/share data and fee/yield trend detail beyond public filings.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.70 / 0.75

Copart still has places to invest: new U.S. and international facilities, catastrophe-response capacity, non-insurance sellers, global buyer expansion, and services that make salvage/title handling easier for sellers. The FY2025 10-K noted continued facility openings across the U.S., U.K., Spain, Canada, Brazil, and Germany in recent years.

The runway is not open-ended hypergrowth. FY2022-FY2025 revenue CAGR was roughly 10%, but consensus points to near-flat FY2026 revenue/EPS and only mid-single-digit FY2027 recovery. The score reflects a durable compounder whose growth rate is fading toward mature-market levels, not a fresh category creator. To firm this up, watch unit volume, revenue per vehicle, international unit mix, and yard expansion productivity.

### Capital Allocation — 0.76 / 0.82

Management has allocated capital conservatively and mostly organically. FY2025 capex was $569m, acquisitions were tiny at $8m, and the balance sheet ended with $4.79b of cash and short-term investments versus $104m of debt/leases. Goodwill is only about 5% of assets, so the growth has not been bought via serial acquisition.

The limitation is shareholder return. Copart pays no dividend and has not been using material buybacks; diluted shares drifted from 964.6m in FY2022 to 977.6m in FY2025. Retaining cash is defensible while land and capacity still compound value, but at slower growth rates the hurdle for cash hoarding rises.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.97 / 0.95

This is one of the cleanest balance sheets in the large-cap universe. FY2025 cash and short-term investments were $4.79b, total debt/lease obligations were only $104m, and stockholders' equity was $9.19b. Debt/equity was roughly 1.1%. The 2025 proxy letter also cited total liquidity of about $6b including undrawn credit capacity.

Financial risk is not the issue here. The risk is paying too high a multiple for a slower-growth business, not balance-sheet fragility.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.78 / 0.80

Founder influence remains meaningful: Willis Johnson is chairman, A. Jayson Adair is executive chairman, and the proxy describes both as company founders and among Copart's ten largest stockholders. That founder culture has likely helped preserve long-term discipline.

The business itself appears institutionalized: public since 1994, global operations, professional management, and a process/technology-heavy model. The score is not higher because founder involvement is still culturally important and the long-term strategy has been unusually consistent under their influence.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.76 / 0.78

Alignment is generally good. Founders remain large holders, the proxy emphasizes long-term stockholder value, directors receive option-heavy compensation tied to share-price appreciation, and CEO base salary was not increased in FY2025. CEO reported compensation also looks modest relative to Copart's scale.

Confidence is below 0.8 because I did not fully rebuild the beneficial ownership table and incentive-plan metrics line by line. Next update should extract exact insider ownership, performance metrics, and realized pay versus TSR from the latest proxy.

### Why the other strategies lost

**Cash Cow** scores well on earnings quality, FCF generation, stability, and balance sheet, but Copart does not yet behave like a cash-return vehicle: no dividend, no material buyback, and continuing land/facility reinvestment. **Freeroll** is a poor fit because there is no obvious hard catalyst or asset floor close to the share price. **Rule Breakers** is also a poor fit: Copart is already mature and highly profitable rather than an early-stage disruptive growth bet.

## Overall

Copart is a high-quality compounder with a real marketplace/operations moat, strong cash conversion, and a fortress balance sheet. The business is almost annoyingly good: excellent margins, global buyer liquidity, minimal acquisition dependence, and huge net cash.

The investment problem is expected return. At $29.27, the stock still trades around 21x current owner FCF, while the next year looks close to flat and medium-term growth is likely mid-single-digit unless Copart finds another leg of unit/revenue-per-vehicle expansion. That makes the stock worth watching, but not a screaming buy on this model. Quality is high; value is only middling.

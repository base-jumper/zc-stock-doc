# AMZN — Amazon.com, Inc.

## Business Overview

Amazon is now three businesses braided together:

1. **Retail marketplace and logistics** — first-party online stores, physical stores, Prime, fulfilment, and third-party seller services. This is huge, operationally hard, lower margin, and increasingly monetised through seller services and ads rather than just retail gross margin.
2. **AWS** — cloud infrastructure and platform services for developers, enterprises, start-ups, governments, and AI workloads. This remains Amazon’s profit engine.
3. **Advertising and subscriptions** — high-margin monetisation layered on top of consumer intent, Prime engagement, streaming, and the marketplace.

Q2 2026 net sales were **$200.6b**, up 20%, and operating income was **$27.5b**, up 43%. AWS accelerated to **37%** growth and **$42.2b** quarterly revenue, with **$16.6b** operating income and a 39.4% margin. Advertising grew 26%, third-party seller services 16%, and online stores 15%. TTM operating income reached **$93.7b**. Reported TTM net income of **$135.3b** is not a clean earnings anchor because Q2 alone included **$53.4b** of non-operating pre-tax income, primarily from Amazon's Anthropic investment.

The key current tension: Amazon’s earnings power is improving rapidly, but free cash flow is being crushed by AI/AWS and satellite capex. TTM operating cash flow rose 33% to **$161.4b**, while purchases of property and equipment net of proceeds rose 64% to **$169.0b**, leaving **-$7.6b** of headline free cash flow.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.73 / confidence 0.88

Amazon now has strong demonstrated earning power. Operating income improved from **$36.9b in 2023** to **$68.6b in 2024**, **$80.0b in 2025**, and **$93.7b TTM** at June 2026. The profit mix is also better than old Amazon: AWS, advertising, seller services, and subscriptions are structurally higher-quality than first-party retail. Q2's **$62.6b** net income is not representative because it included **$53.4b** of non-operating pre-tax investment income, primarily Anthropic.

The caveat is cash conversion. Reported free cash flow was **negative $7.6b TTM** because net property-and-equipment purchases rose to **$169.0b**, mostly around AWS/AI infrastructure. I am treating this as partly growth investment rather than pure maintenance capex, but the distinction matters. If AI capex becomes a permanently high toll rather than high-return reinvestment, earnings quality is lower than accounting profits suggest.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.68 / confidence 0.80

Current ROIC is good rather than elite: StockAnalysis showed TTM ROIC around **13.5%** and ROE around **24.3%**. That understates the quality of AWS and advertising, but it also reflects Amazon’s capital-heavy fulfilment and data-centre footprint. Returns have improved materially since the 2022 post-pandemic overbuild, but the AI capex cycle is again expanding the asset base quickly.

Interpretation: Amazon is a strong compounder, but not a capital-light compounder. The returns are attractive if new AWS/AI capacity earns cloud-like economics; they are merely okay if capex outruns demand.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.86 / confidence 0.86

Amazon has multiple reinforcing moats: Prime habit and convenience, enormous selection, fulfilment density, seller network effects, advertising tied to purchase intent, and AWS scale/developer mindshare. The company’s 10-K explicitly frames its competitive advantages around selection, price, convenience, speed and reliability, plus service/tool quality for sellers and enterprise customers.

Pricing power is clearest in AWS, advertising, seller services, and Prime. Q2's 37% AWS growth at a 39.4% segment margin and 26% advertising growth show that the highest-quality layers are scaling faster than consolidated revenue. Retail product pricing remains brutally competitive, but Amazon increasingly monetises the ecosystem around retail rather than relying on product margin alone.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.90 / confidence 0.90

The runway is still large despite Amazon’s size. AWS reported a **$169b annualised revenue run rate**; AWS's AI and chip businesses each passed **$25b annual revenue run rates** and were growing at triple-digit rates. Advertising continues to take share from less measurable channels; third-party marketplace services scale with merchant dependence; international retail can keep moving from low margin toward profitability; and logistics automation can improve retail unit economics.

The risk is not a lack of things to invest in — it is whether the next wave of AI infrastructure earns enough return. Amazon’s projected 2026 capex is massive, and investors need evidence that AWS demand, AI deals, and internal productivity absorb that capacity profitably.

### Capital Allocation — 0.68 / confidence 0.78

Jassy’s tenure has shown better cost discipline: North America and International profitability improved materially, and operating margins have expanded. Amazon has also kept leaning into high-return areas — AWS, ads, marketplace services, and Prime — rather than pretending retail product gross margin is the whole story.

The open question is AI/satellite capital intensity. TTM net property-and-equipment purchases rose **64% to $169.0b**, and Amazon issued **$67.0b** of long-term debt in the first half. AWS growth and margins currently argue that demand is absorbing capacity, but the cash commitment is enormous. The Leo satellite project and other bets add optionality, complexity and capital risk.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.68 / confidence 0.88

Amazon is financially strong but no longer net-cash pristine. At 30 June it held **$78.2b cash** and **$44.8b marketable securities**, while long-term debt had risen to **$128.9b** from **$65.6b** at year-end after **$67.0b** of first-half issuance. Liquidity and interest coverage remain ample relative to Amazon's earnings power, but the balance sheet is now actively funding the AI buildout.

The balance sheet can handle a cycle, but the debt and capex load mean valuation should not ignore capital intensity.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.85 / confidence 0.80

Amazon is no longer a founder-dependent operating story. Bezos remains Executive Chair, but Andy Jassy has now run the company for years, AWS has its own leadership, and the operating machine is institutional. Culture matters, but the business does not break if one individual leaves.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.65 / confidence 0.60

Management and insiders retain meaningful ownership exposure, and Amazon’s culture still emphasises long-term thinking. That said, at this scale, incentives can drift toward empire-building, especially with AI infrastructure and satellite investments. Alignment is positive, but we should keep watching whether capital allocation produces per-share value rather than just larger revenue and assets.

### Why the other strategies lost

**Rule Breakers** is now a genuine dual fit — its mechanical score is slightly higher because AWS/AI, ads and automation are accelerating — but Amazon is no longer an emerging disruptor whose value lacks a current earnings anchor. The stored primary frame remains Wonderful-and-Fair because normalized positive earnings support an exit-multiple valuation and the blended company includes mature retail/logistics.

**Freeroll** is the wrong frame. There is no obvious downside floor near the current share price and no time-bound re-rating catalyst. This is a compounding/earnings-power thesis, not a buy-near-the-floor setup.

## Overall

Amazon is one of the cleaner mega-cap compounders because its lower-margin retail surface hides several excellent businesses: AWS, advertising, seller services, Prime/subscriptions, and logistics infrastructure. The company’s earnings trajectory since 2023 is impressive, and it still has major reinvestment runway.

The reason this is not a slam dunk is valuation plus capex. At **$271.58/share** on 31 July 2026, AMZN trades around **32x** the normalized earnings anchor used below. That is defensible for Amazon's quality and Q2 acceleration, but the equity return is sensitive to whether AI/AWS capex converts into durable earnings growth. Headline FCF is currently negative because investment is so heavy.

**Current rating: 7.1/10.** Q2 materially strengthened the AWS/AI and advertising growth case, but the higher entry price and capital intensity keep expected returns in the high-single digits.

## Valuation

Inputs used for the exit-multiple model:

- Price: **$271.58**
- Fundamental: **$8.50/share normalized earnings**. The anchor is based on Amazon's current operating-income run rate after excluding the large Anthropic mark-to-market gain; it is intentionally far below reported **$12.44 TTM EPS**. I am using earnings rather than FCF because reported FCF is distorted by growth capex; this is imperfect, so the ROI should be read as earnings-power return, not owner-cash return.
- Growth: **14% p.a.** for five years, supported by 37% AWS growth, 26% advertising growth and continued retail margin expansion, while still assuming a substantial fade from Q2's 43% operating-income growth.
- Dilution: **1% p.a.**
- Exit multiple: **25x earnings**, below the current **32.0x** normalized entry multiple, to avoid relying on multiple expansion.
- Dividend yield: **0%**

The refreshed model gives an expected annualized ROI of about **7.5% p.a.** over five years. Aggregate earnings growth contributes **14%**, 1% dilution costs about **1%**, and multiple compression from about 32.0x to 25x costs about **4.8%** annually. To clear a 10% hurdle at today's price, Amazon needs some combination of faster normalized earnings growth, a higher justified exit multiple, buybacks, or a lower entry price. My **agent_score is 0.71**: the business momentum is excellent, but the return still depends on very large AI investment earning high incremental returns.

## Thesis Connections

Strong FIT links:

- **AI infrastructure:** AWS is one of the few platforms capable of financing and monetising frontier-scale AI compute.
- **Autonomous/automated operations:** warehouse automation, logistics optimisation, robotics, and AI-driven retail operations.
- **Growing wealth divide / platform economics:** Amazon monetises both consumers seeking value/convenience and businesses needing access to demand, infrastructure, and ad inventory.

## Watch

- Q3 2026 guidance: sales of **$197–202b** and operating income of **$22.5–26.5b**; watch whether AWS momentum offsets the Prime Day comparison effect.
- AWS growth and margin: especially whether AI workloads lift revenue without structurally compressing returns.
- Capex: 2026 spend versus actual AWS/AI revenue conversion; watch OCF less capex and capital leases, not just EPS.
- Advertising growth: whether high-20s growth persists or decelerates toward retail growth.
- North America and International margins: continued operating leverage versus renewed price/logistics pressure.
- Leo / Globalstar: whether satellite spending remains controlled or becomes a capital sink.
- Regulatory pressure: marketplace antitrust, labour, data, advertising, and cloud competition scrutiny.
- Share count: stock-based comp/dilution versus any future repurchase discipline.

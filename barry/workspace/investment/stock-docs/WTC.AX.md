# WTC.AX — WiseTech Global Limited

## Business Overview

WiseTech is a global logistics software company. Its core product, CargoWise, is an execution platform used by freight forwarders and logistics service providers to manage complex, cross-border workflows: customs, forwarding, transport, warehousing, compliance, documents, data and integrations. The business model is mostly recurring software revenue, with usage, customer rollouts, pricing and new modules driving growth.

FY25 revenue was US$778.7m, up 14%, with 98% recurring revenue. CargoWise revenue was US$682.2m, up 18% reported / 17% organic, and CargoWise customer attrition stayed below 1% for the thirteenth year. WiseTech generated US$287.0m of free cash flow in FY25, up 31%, with a 34% FCF margin. In 1H26, after the e2open acquisition, revenue rose 76% to US$672.0m; CargoWise revenue rose 12% reported / 9% organic; EBITDA rose 31% to US$252.1m; and FCF rose 24% to US$153.6m.

The strategic question is no longer “is this a good software business?” — it is. The question is whether WiseTech can absorb e2open, reduce leverage, preserve CargoWise’s moat, and turn AI/product restructuring into faster per-share compounding rather than self-inflicted execution risk. Governance also remains central: on 7 July 2026 Raelene Murphy became Independent Chair, while Richard White stayed on the board as executive director and Chief Innovation Officer.

## Trait Assessment

The scorer classifies WiseTech as a **Rule Breakers / Wonderful-and-Fair near-tie**, with Rule Breakers narrowly leading on the mechanical score. I’m framing the doc around Rule Breakers because CargoWise is still a dominant platform compounding inside a large digitising logistics market, while noting that the investment case now looks more like a proven quality compounder than an early-stage disruptor.

### Right Place and Time — 0.76 / 0.78

Global logistics remains complex, regulated, fragmented and under-digitised. That is fertile ground for a system-of-record software platform. WiseTech also has an AI angle that could matter because logistics workflows are rule-heavy and document-heavy — exactly where automation can create customer ROI. The score is not higher because this is not a brand-new explosive market; WiseTech is operating in a maturing but still expanding software category.

### Top Dog — 0.86 / 0.78

CargoWise looks like one of the clear category leaders for global freight forwarding/logistics execution. The evidence is strong: top global forwarder adoption, top 300 customers contributing more than 70% of CargoWise revenue, expansion across cohorts, and <1% annual customer attrition for 13 years. The main confidence gap is lack of a clean independent market-share dataset.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.86 / 0.84

The moat is workflow depth, switching cost, regulatory content, global integrations, and customer data/process embedding. Customers do not casually rip out the operating system that runs freight forwarding, customs and compliance. The <1% attrition record is the most important evidence here. E2open could widen the network/product footprint, but integration risk stops this from being a perfect score.

### Evidence of Durable, Accelerating Growth — 0.74 / 0.80

FY25 revenue grew 14%, CargoWise grew 18% reported / 17% organic, and FY26 guidance implies a much larger business after e2open. But organic CargoWise growth slowed to 9% in 1H26, so the growth engine is good rather than obviously accelerating. The thesis needs CargoWise growth to re-accelerate from new commercial model, CTO rollout, LGFF rollouts and AI/product gains.

### Organic Growth over Acquisitive Growth — 0.48 / 0.83

This is the biggest rule-breaker deduction. WiseTech’s core CargoWise business still grows organically, but the reported FY26 step-change is acquisition-driven by e2open. The company also has a long acquisition history. That does not invalidate the thesis, but it changes the risk: investors now need to underwrite integration and capital allocation, not just organic product-led compounding.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.60 / 0.82

Product execution under Richard White has been excellent, but founder/key-person and governance risk are material. Zubin Appoo’s CEO appointment is constructive, the early e2open cost synergy claim is promising, and Raelene Murphy’s July 2026 appointment as Independent Chair is a real governance improvement. The combination of founder influence, CEO transition, AI restructuring and large integration program still makes management quality more mixed than the product quality.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.65 / 0.76

Founder ownership is enormous: Richard White held about 120.4m shares at 30 June 2025. KMP equity ownership guidelines are also in place. That is real alignment. The offset is governance complexity and concentrated founder influence, which can be a strength when product judgement is good and a liability when oversight matters.

### Customer Devotion — 0.90 / 0.84

For enterprise software, <1% attrition for 13 straight years is about as close as we get to observable customer devotion. CargoWise recurring revenue was 99% in 1H26, and customers continue expanding usage despite price increases. This is mission-critical retention, not consumer fandom, but it is extremely strong evidence.

### Wonderful-and-Fair near-tie traits

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.85 / 0.86

WiseTech has high-quality recurring revenue and strong cash conversion. FY25 recurring revenue was 98% of total revenue; 1H26 recurring revenue was 95% of reported revenue and CargoWise recurring revenue was 99%. FY25 EBITDA was US$381.6m on US$778.7m revenue, and FY25 FCF was US$287.0m. 1H26 FCF was US$153.6m, up 24% despite e2open integration and M&A costs.

The main deduction is accounting complexity: large capitalized development spend, acquired amortization, M&A costs and e2open integration costs make statutory earnings less clean than the underlying cash engine. Still, the recurring, cash-generative software base is real.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.74 / 0.68

The business appears structurally high-return: gross margin was 86% in FY25, EBITDA margin was high, customer attrition is exceptionally low, and the core product scales globally. The complication is capital measurement. WiseTech capitalizes a large share of R&D — 55% in FY25 and 54% excluding e2open in 1H26 — and has accumulated meaningful acquired intangibles/goodwill. That makes simple ROIC less clean.

Confidence is only moderate because a clean post-e2open ROIC bridge would firm this up. For now, the qualitative evidence supports high returns, but the acquisition-heavy balance sheet and capitalized development base prevent a perfect score.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.88 / 0.82

This is the best part of the thesis. CargoWise has mission-critical workflow depth in a regulated, fragmented, complex industry; <1% annual customer attrition for 13 years; and continued price increases alongside usage expansion. FY25 CargoWise revenue grew across customer cohorts, and WiseTech says top 300 customers contributed more than 70% of CargoWise revenue. That concentration is not ideal, but for global freight forwarders it also reflects deep enterprise adoption.

Interpretation: CargoWise looks like embedded infrastructure, not a nice-to-have app. Switching costs, regulatory complexity, workflow integration, and network/product depth form the moat.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.82 / 0.76

WiseTech is still reinvesting heavily. FY25 R&D investment was US$263.8m, 34% of revenue, and the company delivered 1,226 CargoWise enhancements. In 1H26, product investment was US$175.3m and more than 6,300 CargoWise enhancements had been delivered over five years. The runway is broader than freight forwarding alone: customs, landside logistics, compliance, trade data, e2open supply-chain assets and AI-enabled automation.

The risk is not lack of runway; it is execution. E2open is large, debt-funded and integration-heavy. The announced AI/restructuring program may improve productivity, but it also increases product and customer-service execution risk.

### Capital Allocation — 0.58 / 0.67

WiseTech has historically reinvested aggressively into product and acquired domain capabilities. That has created value in CargoWise, but the record is now harder to underwrite because e2open is a large US$2.1b acquisition funded through a US$3.0b debt facility. Management says it achieved US$50m annualized e2open cost synergy run-rate savings by January 2026, far earlier than planned, which is encouraging.

Still, this is not clean “wonderful business, low-drama capital allocation”. The company is taking integration risk, leverage risk and restructuring risk at the same time. Score is middling until e2open proves it can improve per-share economics, not just revenue scale.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.45 / 0.82

Before e2open, WiseTech had modest leverage: US$167.4m cash and US$65.0m borrowings at 30 June 2025. After e2open, borrowings rose to US$2.36b at 31 December 2025 and net leverage was 3.2x. Management expects deleveraging to ~3.0x by FY26 year-end, ~2.5x by FY27 year-end, and below 2.0x by August 2028.

For a capital-light recurring software business, 3.2x net leverage is not fatal, but it is not conservative. The cash generation gives them a path out; the current balance sheet still lowers the quality score.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.50 / 0.84

Richard White remains a huge shareholder and central product figure as Chief Innovation Officer and executive director, but he is no longer Chair after Raelene Murphy’s appointment as Independent Chair on 7 July 2026. Founder control and product vision have clearly been assets, but dependence on a single founder still creates governance and succession risk. Zubin Appoo’s CEO role and the refreshed board help; successful execution under the new structure would improve this score further.

This remains one of the main reasons the stock does not get a higher rating despite the excellent product economics, although the independent-chair change lowers the risk a notch.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.65 / 0.76

Alignment is mixed-positive. Richard White held about 120.4m shares at 30 June 2025, so founder ownership is enormous. Executive KMP are subject to equity ownership guidelines, and the disclosed KMP equity holdings are meaningful. That said, large founder influence plus recent governance/key-person concerns are not the same as clean institutional alignment. The score is supportive, not top-tier.

### Why the other strategies ranked where they did

**Wonderful-and-Fair:** Near-tie runner-up. WiseTech is absolutely a high-quality compounder, but leverage, e2open integration, capital allocation uncertainty and key-person risk drag the quality score below the pure product/moat score.

**Freeroll:** The share-price collapse creates valuation interest, but WiseTech is not a classic asset-backed/catalyst freeroll. There is no hard downside floor, and the thesis does not rest on a short, specific re-rating event. It is a platform compounder bought at a newly more reasonable price.

## Overall

WiseTech is high-quality and still much more investable than it was before the share-price reset, though the July rebound has removed some of the easy valuation upside. CargoWise has the kind of moat I actually like: mission-critical, deeply embedded, low churn, usage/pricing growth, and a long product runway. The business quality is real.

The bear case is also real: e2open increased leverage and complexity; organic CargoWise growth slowed to 9% in 1H26; founder/key-person/governance risk is improved but not gone; and the AI/headcount restructuring needs to improve productivity without damaging support, product quality or customer trust.

My current view: **watch / staged-buy quality compounder**, not an automatic back-up-the-truck. At ~A$37.37, the governance reset has helped but the valuation is no longer as obviously washed-out as it was in June; the next 12–18 months are still execution-heavy. I would want position sizing to respect the debt + integration + governance risk.

## Thesis Connections

WiseTech loosely connects to FIT themes around AI-enabled productivity and “single person / smaller team” leverage, but the stronger thesis is **global trade infrastructure software**: complex, regulated workflows being centralized into software systems of record. AI could widen the moat if it lets CargoWise automate more of the logistics workflow inside a trusted platform customers already rely on.

## Watch

- **CargoWise organic growth:** needs to re-accelerate from 1H26’s 9% organic growth toward the FY26 guide of ~14–21% CargoWise growth.
- **E2open integration:** monitor realized synergies, customer retention, revenue attrition, and whether cross-sell actually appears.
- **Leverage:** net leverage should fall toward ~3.0x by FY26 year-end and ~2.5x by FY27 year-end. Failure here would lower the score.
- **AI/headcount restructuring:** watch for support degradation, product delays, customer dissatisfaction, or margin gains that are one-off rather than structural.
- **Founder/governance risk:** Raelene Murphy’s appointment as Independent Chair is positive; now watch whether Zubin Appoo’s CEO transition and the refreshed board prove operational independence and stability.
- **Capitalized development:** if capitalization stays high while growth slows, quality of earnings gets worse.
- **Next update:** refresh price, FY26 guidance progress, current FCF/share, leverage and any ASX updates on restructuring/e2open.

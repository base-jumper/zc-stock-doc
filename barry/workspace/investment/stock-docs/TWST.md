# TWST — Twist Bioscience Corporation

## Business Overview

Twist manufactures synthetic DNA and related life-science tools using a proprietary silicon-chip platform that miniaturizes more than one million oligonucleotide synthesis reactions per chip. Its integrated hardware, software, manufacturing and e-commerce stack sells genes, oligo pools, DNA libraries, proteins and antibody-discovery services under **DNA Synthesis and Protein Solutions (DSPS)**, plus target-enrichment and sample-preparation products under **NGS Applications**. Customers span therapeutics, diagnostics, industrial biotechnology, agriculture, academia and government.

Q3 FY2026 revenue was $118.4m, up 23% year on year: DSPS grew 39% to $56.6m and NGS Applications grew 12% to $61.8m. Management raised FY2026 revenue guidance to $456–457m (about 21% growth), guided Q4 to $123–124m and reiterated adjusted-EBITDA breakeven in Q4. GAAP economics remain well behind that milestone: Q3 operating loss was $36.3m and nine-month operating cash burn was $41.3m. After quarter-end, a 3.125m-share offering at $96 raised about $284.3m net, lifting the pro-forma share count to about 65.8m before the underwriter option and materially extending runway.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.80 / 0.78

Twist supplies picks-and-shovels to expanding synthetic-biology, genomics, diagnostics and AI-enabled drug-discovery workflows. Revenue has compounded at about 23% from FY2022 to FY2025 and the company now expects roughly 21% FY2026 growth, while its customer base spans several structurally growing end markets. The industry is attractive but not easy: buyers are price-sensitive, technologies turn quickly, and large incumbents can fund competing tools. Firm-up: the companion market doc's bottom-up 2026–2036 sizing is the best current boundary-matched evidence; refresh it when credible market-share data improve.

### Top Dog — 0.64 / 0.66

Twist is an emerging leader rather than the clear category winner. The company says it is the only provider synthesizing DNA on silicon at large commercial scale; it serves more than 3,800 customers annually and shipped about 369,000 genes in Q3, up 56%. Yet the field includes GenScript, IDT/Danaher, GeneArt/Thermo Fisher, Azenta, Eurofins, Agilent, Illumina and multiple emerging synthesis platforms. Public whole-market share data are sparse and Twist remains smaller than several diversified rivals. Firm-up: independent revenue-share data for outsourced gene synthesis and NGS target enrichment.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.74 / 0.72

The silicon platform is a credible cost-and-throughput moat: Twist reports 99.8% lower chemical use per gene than plate-based synthesis, more than one million oligos written per chip, and an integrated automated/e-commerce workflow. Fourteen consecutive quarters of sequential growth, improving gross margin and rising gene volumes show that the advantage is commercially relevant. The moat is not impregnable: competition is intense on price, quality and turnaround; rivals have greater resources; and alternative enzymatic or silicon-based approaches could narrow the gap. Firm-up: independently benchmark price, error rate and turnaround against IDT, GenScript, Elegen and Ansa.

### Durable Growth — 0.82 / 0.90

The stage-appropriate evidence is strong. Q3 marked a fourteenth consecutive sequential revenue increase; revenue rose 23% year on year, customers shipped-to rose roughly 7%, and gene volume rose 56%. Both DSPS and NGS grew, FY2026 guidance implies acceleration from FY2025's 20%, and gross margin has risen from 36.6% in FY2023 to 50.7% in FY2025 and 52.8% in Q3 FY2026. The caveat is that GAAP operating loss remains large and adjusted EBITDA excludes $18.2m of quarterly stock compensation.

### Organic Growth — 0.84 / 0.84

Recent growth is overwhelmingly organic. From FY2022 to FY2025, revenue rose from $203.6m to $376.6m while disclosed acquisition cash spend totaled only about $16m; Q3 growth came from greater customer demand and gene shipments. Twist has used small capability acquisitions and, in 2026, committed $33.8m in cash and stock to license and invest in Invenra's bispecific-antibody platform, but this is an adjacency rather than a roll-up engine. Diluted shares still rose roughly 11% from FY2022 to FY2025, mostly from equity compensation and financing rather than acquired revenue.

### Management Backing — 0.70 / 0.78

Co-founder Emily Leproust has led Twist since 2013 after directing applications and chemistry R&D at Agilent. Management built the silicon platform into a diversified $450m-plus revenue business, expanded gross margin sharply and disposed of the non-core DNA-data-storage operation. The record is tempered by a $1.43bn accumulated deficit, repeated equity funding and the 2022-era securities allegations that were settled in 2026. The board includes relevant life-science and operating expertise, and large holders include Artisan, ARK and BlackRock. Firm-up: delivery of GAAP operating leverage after the adjusted-EBITDA milestone.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 / 0.85

The structure is mixed. FY2025 performance RSUs use multi-year revenue, gross-margin and adjusted-EBITDA goals; the CEO's long-term incentives were 95% of target direct compensation; ownership guidelines, clawbacks and anti-hedging rules are in place. Against that, revenue remains a major payout driver, adjusted EBITDA excludes stock compensation, FY2025 SBC was $64.5m (17% of revenue), and the base share count rose about 16% from FY2022 through the August 2026 offering before outstanding RSUs/PRSUs. Leproust owned about 819,000 shares after an August sale, but adopted a plan allowing potential sales of up to roughly 624,000 shares through August 2027.

### Customer Devotion — 0.76 / 0.84

Customer evidence is strong: **99% of FY2025 revenue came from customers that purchased more than once during the year**, up from 98% in FY2024. Quarterly customers increased to about 2,664 from 2,484, annual customers exceed 3,800, no customer contributed 10% of FY2023–FY2025 revenue, and gene volumes rose 56%. Because laboratories can multi-source synthesis and workflow inputs, the repeat behavior reflects preference and utility more than captivity. The score stops below exceptional because Twist does not disclose year-to-year logo retention, cohort expansion, NPS or independent satisfaction data.

Other strategies lose mechanically. Wonderful-and-fair and cash-cow are vetoed by the absence of demonstrated earnings and positive free cash flow. Freeroll lacks a hard floor: the post-offering tangible book value is only about $9.65 per share versus a $115.67 market quote, while Q4 adjusted-EBITDA breakeven is a milestone rather than a valuation backstop.

No universal disqualifier is triggered. The company has ample pro-forma liquidity after the August offering, no debt-maturity threat, effective disclosure controls at June 2026, and no restatement. The settled securities and derivative actions deserve monitoring but the disclosed net liability is not equity-threatening; the historical allegations therefore do not rise to an accounting-integrity kill-shot on current evidence.

## Overall

Twist is a credible but not elite Rule Breaker. The core platform is differentiated, growth is broad and persistent, and gross-margin expansion shows real scale economics. The sharp rise in gene volumes and DSPS growth suggests the product advantage is translating into share gains.

The investment case is held back by the price and per-share economics. The stock trades near its 52-week high at roughly 17–18× trailing sales on the post-offering share count, GAAP losses remain large, stock compensation is material, and management raised another $300m gross despite guiding to adjusted-EBITDA breakeven. The valuation confirms that a strong company outcome is already embedded: the modeled survivor path still loses value per share. Overall agent score: **0.35**.

## Thesis Connections

Twist fits a synthetic-biology picks-and-shovels thesis: falling DNA-writing costs and faster design/build/test cycles can expand biological R&D, while the tool supplier captures revenue across many therapeutics, diagnostics and industrial winners rather than underwriting a single molecule. The critical question is whether silicon synthesis remains differentiated as enzymatic synthesis and well-funded incumbents improve.

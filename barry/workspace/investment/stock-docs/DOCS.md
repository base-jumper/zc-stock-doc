# DOCS — Doximity, Inc.

## Business Overview

Doximity is a U.S. healthcare professional network and software platform. It reports more than 3 million registered members, including more than 85% of U.S. physicians, two-thirds of U.S. nurse practitioners/physician assistants and about 90% of graduating U.S. medical students. The platform gives clinicians collaboration, news, referral, telehealth, scheduling, faxing, messaging and clinical AI tools; Doximity monetises that engagement mainly by selling subscription Marketing Solutions, Hiring Solutions and Workflow Solutions to pharmaceutical manufacturers, health systems and medical recruiters.

The economics remain cash-rich, but Q1 FY2027 exposed the cost of the AI investment cycle. Revenue rose 7% to $156.6m, while GAAP gross margin fell from 89% to 85%, net income fell 54% to $24.3m, and FCF fell 34% to $39.6m. Workflow active providers grew 32% and AI Search queries rose more than 25% sequentially. FY2027 revenue guidance increased to $671m-$681m, but adjusted EBITDA guidance fell to $309m-$329m.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.65 / confidence 0.90

Doximity remains genuinely GAAP-profitable, but Q1 quality weakened. Net income fell from $53.3m to $24.3m and SBC rose 68% to $36.8m; quarterly SBC was 93% of FCF. The operating cash decline also reflected a $33.3m receivables build from billing and collection timing. The history remains profitable and cash-backed, but owner earnings are less clean while AI investment and equity compensation rise.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.86 / confidence 0.90

Trailing FCF remains about $297m, or roughly a 46% margin, after Q1 FCF fell to $39.6m from $60.1m. Capital intensity is still very low, but the quarterly decline, receivables outflow and higher SBC warrant a modest score reduction. This is still the defining strength, not evidence of a broken cash engine.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.75 / confidence 0.90

Revenue growth accelerated to 7%, $500k+ customers rose to 127 from 119, and workflow active providers grew 32%. Against that, NRR fell to 107% from 118% and 83% of trailing revenue came from the $500k+ cohort. Updated guidance supports roughly 4%-6% FY2027 revenue growth, but retention and customer-budget timing prevent a stronger durability score.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.65 / confidence 0.80

Doximity's verified physician network, targeted prescriber access and workflow engagement still form a credible niche moat. However, GAAP gross margin fell four points to 85% because of AI hosting/software costs, while NRR slipped to 107%. Existing-customer Marketing Solutions ARPU rose about 3%, so pricing power remains present but narrower than the historic near-90% margin implied.

The Q2 FY2027 prepared remarks and subsequent 10-Q disclosure on pricing, product mix and customer-cohort retention would firm this score above the current confidence threshold.

### Capital Allocation — 0.65 / confidence 0.88

Doximity repurchased 4.44m shares for $91.6m in Q1 at an average near $20.64, below the current price, and diluted weighted-average shares fell 5% year over year. The remaining authorization was $400.9m at quarter end. This is shareholder-friendly, but SBC rose to $36.8m and buybacks again exceeded quarterly FCF, so the score remains mixed-good rather than strong.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.68 / confidence 0.88

Returns remain good over the multi-year history, but Q1 operating income fell 38% as AI hosting, R&D and go-to-market spending rose. The business remains capital-light with no leverage distortion; the lower score reflects temporarily weaker incremental returns, not balance-sheet risk.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.96 / confidence 0.92

The balance sheet is excellent. At March 2026 Doximity had about $748.6m of cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities against only about $10.2m of lease/debt obligations, or roughly $738m of net cash. Current ratio was about 6.1x and near-term debt coverage was hundreds of times. This gives the company room to buy back stock, invest through budget pauses and absorb product/regulatory disruption without solvency risk.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.55 / confidence 0.70

Founder/CEO Jeff Tangney is a major asset and a concentration risk. The physician-first product culture and network credibility appear strongly tied to founder execution, and the 2025 proxy shows Tangney with 76.3% voting power and 28.9% total ownership when including affiliated trusts and exercisable options. There is a wider executive team, and FY2026 brought a new CFO and President, but the control structure and founder imprint mean minority shareholders are heavily underwriting Tangney’s judgement. Confidence would improve by reviewing succession depth, product-leadership retention and board independence in the next proxy.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.50 / confidence 0.85

Alignment is mixed. Founder ownership is meaningful, but dual-class control gives Class A holders little practical influence. Q1 SBC rose 68%, equalled 23% of revenue and almost matched FCF, while adjusted EBITDA excludes it. The net share count is shrinking because buybacks are large, but the compensation burden lowers the score.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair is plausible but loses to cash-cow because the near-term reinvestment runway is not strong enough: FY2027 revenue guidance implies low-single-digit growth, so this is more cash extraction plus selective AI/workflow expansion than a clearly high-ROIC reinvestment story. Rule-breakers loses because Doximity is already profitable and dominant in a defined niche, but revenue growth has decelerated to a mature-software pace; the rule-breaker upside depends on Clinical AI creating a second growth curve that is not yet proven in revenue. Freeroll loses because the net-cash floor is helpful but not dominant at roughly a quarter of the share price, and there is no concrete near-term catalyst.

## Overall

Doximity remains a high-quality cash cow with a real niche moat, a fortress balance sheet and exceptional trailing FCF margins. Q1 strengthened the AI/engagement thesis but weakened current owner economics: retention, gross margin, earnings and FCF all fell, and SBC rose sharply. At $25.63, expected returns are weak unless AI investment reaccelerates revenue or buybacks shrink the count faster than assumed.

## Valuation

I used the exit-multiple method on FCF/share. Inputs are the 11 August price of $25.63; $1.45/share of normalized current FCF, below trailing FCF/share to reflect weaker Q1 conversion and SBC leakage; five years; 5% aggregate FCF growth; a 14x exit P/FCF; and 1% annual net buybacks.

The script produces 1.2% annualized ROI: +5.0% business growth and +1.0% buyback accretion are mostly offset by -4.6% annualized multiple compression from 17.7x to 14x. My agent score falls to 0.50: business quality remains above average, but the current price and margin investment leave little base-case return.

## Thesis Connections

Potential FIT: physician workflow productivity and healthcare AI adoption. Doximity has a strong distribution advantage into U.S. clinicians, and FY2026 commentary says more than 800,000 active prescribers used workflow tools in Q4, nearly half used clinical AI, and prompts per user nearly doubled from January to April. The thesis connection is real, but it must show up in paid Workflow Solutions growth rather than only engagement metrics.

## Watch

- FY2027 revenue versus $671m-$681m guidance and adjusted EBITDA versus the reduced $309m-$329m range.
- Clinical AI monetisation: paid workflow seats, ARPU, churn and whether AI usage converts into revenue.
- NRR after falling to 107%; $500k+ customer growth and customer concentration.
- Customer concentration: whether any customer remains above 10% of revenue.
- AI investment economics: gross margin, hosting/software cost and whether 32% workflow-user growth converts into paid revenue.
- SBC as a percentage of FCF and whether buybacks reduce diluted shares after offsetting grants.
- Competitive pressure from healthcare AI/search tools such as OpenEvidence, Abridge and general AI platforms.
- Founder/control governance and succession disclosures in the next proxy.

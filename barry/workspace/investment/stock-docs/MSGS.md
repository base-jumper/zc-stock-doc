# MSGS — Madison Square Garden Sports Corp.

## Business Overview

Madison Square Garden Sports Corp. owns and operates two flagship New York professional franchises:
the **New York Knicks** (NBA) and the **New York Rangers** (NHL), together with the Westchester Knicks
(G League), the MSG Training Center, and the Hartford Wolf Pack (AHL Rangers affiliate). The company
does not own Madison Square Garden itself — the arena is owned by MSG Entertainment (MSGE), a
separate Dolan-controlled public company — so the teams operate at the Garden under a long-term arena
license with MSGE and share economics around suites, sponsorships, and concessions.

Revenue is concentrated in the two franchises: ~80% of fiscal 2026 revenue ($1,153.8M, +11% YoY) comes
from the Knicks, which is by far the larger and more profitable of the two. Key revenue drivers are
in-arena (tickets, suites, sponsorship, food & beverage), national and local media rights, and
merchandise. Fiscal 2026 was highlighted by the **Knicks winning the 2025-26 NBA Championship** (first
title in 53 years) and an increase in national media-rights fees from the NBA's new national deals.

The operating business is thin: fiscal 2026 operating income was $28.9M and adjusted operating income
$58.7M on $1,153.8M of revenue; net income was negative (-$22.4M) due to interest and corporate costs.
The Knicks carry the profitability; the Rangers business lost money in each of the last three seasons
absent deep playoff runs. The economic value sits in the franchises themselves, which carry substantial
private-market values far above what the public operating business earns.

**Proposed corporate action:** MSGS is spinning off the Rangers into a separate public company
(**MSGR**) from the Knicks company (**MSGK**, the renamed MSGS parent). For every two MSGS shares,
shareholders receive one MSGR share. The Form 10 was publicly filed on August 14, 2026, and completion
is expected by the end of October 2026 (subject to board/league approval, tax opinion, and Form 10
effectiveness). Both companies remain under Dolan family control via a dual-class structure (Class A 1
vote; Class B 10 votes, held by the family).

## Trait Assessment

The strategy here is **freeroll** — a sum-of-the-parts bet on a controlled holding company trading at a
discount to the value of its franchises, with a concrete, time-bound spin-off catalyst.

### Downside Support / Valuation Floor — score 0.72, confidence 0.60

The floor is the franchise enterprise value. Sportico (May 2026) values the Knicks at $9.85B and the
Rangers at $3.65B — a combined ~$13.5B EV; Forbes values them at $9.75B and $4.0B (~$13.75B). MSGS's
current enterprise value is ~$10.9B (market cap ~$9.8B + net debt ~$1.0B), i.e. roughly a 20-25%
discount to private sum-of-the-parts. Franchise scarcity (124 teams across the big four leagues, ~2 new
franchises in 20 years) and record private sales (Lakers ~$10B, Celtics $6.1B, Padres $3.9B) make the
asset values durable and rising. Even applying a ~20-25% liquidity discount to private SOTP, the floor
sits in the low-$300s per share, not far below today's price.

Confidence is capped (~0.6) because Sportico/Forbes valuations are private estimates, not audited
marks, and the franchises carry debt, lease obligations, and corporate overhead that a naive SOTP
subtraction ignores. The floor is real but fuzzy. *Firm-up:* the form 10 Ranger financials are now
public; a rigorous SOTP net of each franchise's debt/obligations would tighten the floor. Not a
falling knife — the assets are meaningfully valuable and appreciating.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — score 0.62, confidence 0.62

The underlying franchises are structurally durable, not in secular decline. National media-rights
fees are rising (NBA's new national deals began this season), in-arena per-game revenue grew in every
category in fiscal 2026, and the Knicks' championship strengthens the brand. The weakness is
operational and cyclical, not secular: the Rangers (and to a lesser extent the whole company) swing
to losses depending on playoff depth and arena economics, and local RSN media fees are contracting
(the MSG Networks local deal falls from ~$39M to ~$35M and expires after the 2028-29 season). The
franchise asset value is stable and growing even though the annual operating profits are thin. Score:
a notch above middling — durable assets, but the operating profitability is genuinely thin and
media-rights dynamics are a partial offset.

### Mispriced for an Identifiable Reason — score 0.60, confidence 0.58

The differentiated view: MSGS historically traded at a "Dolan discount" — public sports teams trade
at a discount to private transaction values because there is no scarcity value in a listed stock and
the operating businesses are low-margin. The spin-off is designed to surface value by giving each
franchise a clean public quote (especially the Rangers, which has never had one) and enabling
minority-stake sales or eventual per-franchise transactions. The counterweight is that this mispricing
has substantially compressed: the stock is up ~89% in a year and ~16% on the February spin-off
announcement, so the gap between price and fair value is much narrower than it was. The view remains
that the spin surfaces further value, but the edge is thinner than a year ago. Confidence ~0.58 — the
discount is real but shrinking.

### Catalyst — score 0.90, confidence 0.85

A strong, concrete, time-bound catalyst: the **Rangers spin-off**. The Form 10 was publicly filed
August 14, 2026; completion is expected by the end of October 2026, so the catalyst window is ~1-2
months, well inside the 4-year limit. The mechanism is clear — after separation, $MSGK (Knicks) and
$MSGR (Rangers) will each trade on their own, giving the market a clean valuation of the franchises,
with strategic options (minority investment, financing, or a future sale). Probability is high (~85%):
the company has committed to the timeline, there is no shareholder vote required, and the remaining
conditions (board/league approval, tax opinion, effectiveness) are procedural. Confirming signposts:
stock-spinoffs.com and Sportico both note the filing is public and the clock is on. This is the make-or-
break trait, and it scores highly.

### Why other strategies lost

- **Rule Breakers / Wonderful and Fair / Cash Cow:** not a growth compounder (no reinvestment
  runway — the cash is the franchises), not a wonderful-and-fair business (thin/negative operating
  earnings, negative book equity, poor returns on capital at the operating level), not a cash cow
  (no meaningful free-cash distribution). MSGS is an asset-holding sum-of-the-parts story, which is
  the freeroll's home territory. These strategies would crater on their gate traits
  (earnings-quality, free-cash-flow-generation, returns-on-capital).

## Overall

**Strengths:** Two premier, appreciating franchises; the Knicks are a top-tier NBA asset (~$9.85B)
fresh off a championship; concrete, high-probability spin-off catalyst imminent; meaningful
(if narrowing) discount to private sum-of-the-parts.
**Detractors:** Thin and cyclical operating profitability; negative book equity; Dolan family control
via dual-class shares (governance); rising tax burden on the new companies; the stock has already
re-rated sharply, so much of the "easy" money is gone; local RSN media fees in decline.

## Valuation

Freeroll is valued with the **asymmetric-payoff** method — probability-weight the floor against fair
value over the catalyst window.

- **Price:** $407.26 (8/18/26 quote).
- **Floor:** ~$330/share — a conservative sum-of-the-parts value netting franchise values
  (Knicks ~$9.85B + Rangers ~$3.65B = ~$13.5B EV) against net debt (~$1.0B) and applying a liquidity
  haircut, on ~24M shares. Franchise values are durable and rising, so the floor is relatively firm.
- **Fair value:** ~$520/share — the value if the spin-off surfaces the full SOTP (the two public
  companies reflecting franchise EV net of debt). This is consistent with unlocking toward the private
  valuations while retaining a modest liquidity discount.
- **Catalyst probability:** 85% — the spin-off completing and re-rating value.
- **Window:** ~1.5 years, ample for the completion and a post-spin settlement.
- **Expected ROI:** p·upside − (1−p)·downside = 0.85×0.278 − 0.15×0.189 ≈ +0.208 (~21%) over the
  window → annualized ≈ **~13.4%**. Asymmetry ratio ≈ 1.5 (upside-to-downside).

Attribution: the return rests almost entirely on value-unlock catalyst plus modest franchise
appreciation; it weakens the higher the stock runs. **`overall.agent_score` = 0.62** — this is a
real, imminent, high-probability catalyst attached to durable appreciating assets, but the edge has
been substantially reduced by the ~89% run-up, the operating economics are thin, and Dolan control
caps the governance score. A solid but no longer exceptional freeroll.

## Watch

- **Spin-off completion (primary):** confirm board/league approval, tax opinion, and Form 10
  effectiveness; record and distribution dates; expected end of October 2026. If delayed past ~6
  months, reassess the probability and window.
- **Post-spin valuations of $MSGK and $MSGR:** where each trades relative to franchise SOTP tells us
  whether the discount closed or a new one opened.
- **Rangers post-spin profitability / playoff run:** the Rangers' economics swing hugely on playoff
  depth; a miss keeps the newly-independent MSGR thin.
- **Local RSN media fees:** MSG Networks deal expires after 2028-29; NHL national deals after 2027-28 —
  the media-rights reset is a multi-year overhang.
- **Dolan family control / any minority-stake or sale signal:** whether separation leads to a
  strategic transaction or just continued control.
- **Rising compensation tax** on the new companies (the 2017 provision expansion from tax year 2027).

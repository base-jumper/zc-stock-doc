# NG.L — National Grid plc

## Business Overview

National Grid is a UK-listed regulated electricity and gas network utility. Its core assets are UK electricity transmission and distribution networks plus US regulated electricity/gas networks in New York and New England. The business earns regulated returns on a growing asset base, not commodity-price upside.

FY2026 continuing statutory operating profit was **£5.431b**, underlying operating profit was **£5.680b**, statutory EPS was **65.5p**, and underlying EPS was **78.0p**. Capital investment reached a record **£11.6b**, lifting group asset growth **10.9%** and regulated asset growth **11.7%**. Management now frames the next five years around **at least £70b** of cumulative capex, roughly **10% asset-growth CAGR**, and **8–10% underlying EPS CAGR** from the FY2026 baseline.

No universal disqualifier found. The main risks are regulatory allowed returns, delivery execution, balance-sheet funding, interest rates, and political/customer-affordability pressure — not fraud, imminent insolvency, hidden liabilities, value-leaking control, or a business-model kill-shot.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.65 / confidence 0.75

National Grid has real, recurring regulated earning power. FY2026 statutory operating profit rose **10%** to **£5.431b** and underlying EPS rose **8% at constant currency** to **78.0p**. Yahoo statement data shows no loss year in the 2023–2026 window, with net income of **£2.29b–£3.24b** in 2024–2026 continuing results.

The caveat is cash conversion. Reported FCF is negative because the company is investing far above operating cash flow: FY2026 operating cash flow was **£7.829b** versus capex of **£10.575b**, producing **-£2.746b** FCF. For a regulated growth utility this is partly the model — capex grows the regulated asset base — but it still means accounting earnings are not currently distributable owner cash.

Confidence would improve with a longer 7–10 year reconciled continuing-operations series across regulatory cycles and a fuller split between maintenance and growth capex.

### Returns on Capital — 0.55 / confidence 0.80

Returns are adequate for a regulated utility but not exceptional. The FY2026 results state group regulated RoE of **9.8%**, while Yahoo-derived ROIC was about **5.3%** and ROCE about **5.9%**. Those are stable and acceptable for low-risk regulated infrastructure, but they are not the high-return economics of a classic compounder.

Interpretation: National Grid can create value if allowed returns stay above its cost of capital and it executes the capex plan efficiently. The spread is narrower than high-quality asset-light businesses, so the score stays mid-range.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.72 / confidence 0.75

The moat is structural rather than brand-based. National Grid owns monopoly-like critical energy network assets with regulated revenue frameworks in the UK and US. Reliability was over **99.9%** across regulated electricity networks, with UK electricity transmission reported at **99.99999%** reliability in FY2026.

Pricing power is regulated: the company cannot freely raise prices like a consumer brand, but allowed revenue, inflation linkage, RAV/rate-base growth, and approved rate cases provide a durable path to recover efficient investment and earn a return. This is a strong moat, with the main threat being political/regulatory affordability pressure rather than direct competition.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.78 / confidence 0.85

The runway is the clearest positive. Management extended the five-year framework to **at least £70b** of capex through FY2031, with around **two-thirds** covered by regulatory agreements and supply-chain/delivery mechanisms secured for around **three-quarters** of the plan. Drivers include grid modernisation, UK transmission projects, US network investment, demand connections, renewables integration, smart meters, and gas-network asset health.

This is exactly the kind of regulated runway where retained/raised capital can be redeployed for years into approved assets. The limitation is return level: the runway is large and visible, but incremental returns are utility-like, not 20%+ compounder returns.

### Capital Allocation — 0.62 / confidence 0.70

Allocation is mostly coherent: sell non-core assets, fund a massive regulated capex programme, maintain the dividend policy, and protect credit metrics. FY2026 included completed divestments of National Grid Renewables and Grain LNG, with proceeds offsetting some investment funding. Management targets credit metrics consistent with the current group rating and regulatory gearing moving back toward the high-60% range by FY2031.

The blemish is funding intensity. The FY2025 rights issue materially increased the share count, and FY2026 FCF remained negative after growth capex while dividends continued. That is not automatically value-destructive for a regulated growth utility, but it makes per-share outcomes heavily dependent on allowed returns exceeding financing costs.

### Conservative Debt — 0.55 / confidence 0.80

Debt is high but broadly matched to regulated assets. FY2026 net debt was **£44.2b** in the company results, regulatory gearing was **61%**, and FFO/net debt was **13.0%**. Yahoo-derived net debt/EBITDA was about **5.6x** and EBIT interest cover about **4.6x**.

For a regulated utility, that is stretched-but-ordinary rather than alarming. It is not conservatively financed in an absolute sense, and FCF is negative during the investment ramp, but the asset base and regulatory frameworks support more leverage than a normal cyclical operating company could carry. Watch refinancing costs and rating-agency credit metrics closely.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.85 / confidence 0.75

This is an institutional infrastructure business, not a founder/key-person thesis. CEO execution matters, especially during a large delivery programme, but the core assets, regulatory relationships, engineering processes, and operating teams are embedded across the organisation. Losing any one executive would be disruptive, not thesis-breaking.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.65 / confidence 0.45

The available evidence is mixed but not worrying. Yahoo governance fields show low overall governance risk and low shareholder-rights/audit risk, while compensation risk is moderate. I did not inspect the full remuneration report in this run, so confidence is low. The next update should check whether LTIP metrics emphasise regulated asset delivery, safety/reliability, returns, customer outcomes, and per-share value rather than pure asset growth.

### Why other strategies lost

**Cash Cow** lost because reported FCF is deeply negative during the capex super-cycle. A mature utility with a covered dividend can look cash-cow-like, but this phase is not surplus-cash harvesting.

**Freeroll** lost because there is no clear hard floor plus time-bound catalyst. The shares are around a normal utility valuation, not obviously near a liquidation/NAV/yield floor with a forced re-rating trigger.

**Rule Breakers** lost because National Grid is not an emerging high-growth disruptor. It is a regulated incumbent with a large infrastructure runway.

## Overall

National Grid is a solid regulated-infrastructure compounder with unusually visible reinvestment demand. The good: monopoly-like assets, regulatory visibility, high reliability, a huge capex runway, inflation-linked dividend policy, and credible 8–10% underlying EPS growth guidance. The bad: low absolute ROIC, high leverage, negative FCF during the buildout, dilution/funding needs, and regulatory/political sensitivity.

My view: attractive if Nick wants a lower-volatility infrastructure compounder, less attractive if ranking against high-ROIC businesses. Quality is real, but the return is mostly a regulated-utility return plus dividend, not a truly wonderful-business return.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a grid-modernisation / electrification thesis: rising power demand, renewable connections, transmission bottlenecks, EV/heat-pump/load growth, and reliability spending all require regulated network investment. It is more “toll road on electrification” than explosive technology winner.

National Grid Ventures is also pursuing unregulated AI/data-centre upside. In July 2026 it agreed to invest **$1.75bn for a 35% stake in Joulent**, a platform building integrated power for US large-load customers (co-located gas, storage, renewables, across-the-meter grid connections). Joulent's foundational Project Kilby is a **2.67GW co-located facility in West Texas** (50/50 with Chevron) supplying a Microsoft data-centre campus under a **20-year power purchase agreement**, targeting first power in **2028**, with a multi-GW pipeline behind it. The investment is incremental to the £70bn plan, funded from balance-sheet headroom, and is not expected to change the current financial framework; Joulent is expected to be free-cash-flow positive from the early 2030s. This adds optionality (contracted unregulated returns above regulated returns) but carries development/execution and merchant-stage risk.

## Watch

- RIIO-T3 execution: project delivery, cost allowances, and any under/outperformance versus allowed returns.
- US rate cases, especially affordability pushback in New York and Massachusetts.
- Credit metrics: FFO/net debt, RCF/net debt, rating outlook, and refinancing costs. The **€20bn** NG plc / NG Electricity Transmission EMT program prospectus was re-published 17 Aug 2026 — watch funding spreads.
- Whether the £70b capex plan continues earning acceptable incremental returns after financing costs.
- Per-share outcomes after dilution/scrip: EPS CAGR must actually land near 8–10%. The 2025/26 final-dividend scrip issued **50.86M shares** (22 Jul 2026), lifting measured FY2026 scrip dilution near ~1.1% — track whether scrip take-up stays elevated.
- Dividend coverage through the capex ramp; distinguish accounting payout from free-cash coverage.
- Joulent: final investment decision expected by end-2026; Kilby first power targeted 2028; whether the initial $1.75bn / 35% earns contracted returns above the regulated cost of capital without straining balance-sheet headroom.
- Any political/regulatory cap on allowed returns due to customer bill pressure.

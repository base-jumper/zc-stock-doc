# IBE.MC — Iberdrola, S.A.

## Business Overview

Iberdrola is a global regulated-network and renewable-power utility headquartered in Spain. The business is now being deliberately tilted toward electricity networks: the 2026–2028 Transformation Plan targets **€58bn of investment through 2028**, including **€37bn in networks**, with the UK and US receiving the largest country allocations. By 2028 management wants **75% of EBITDA not dependent on energy prices**, supported by a regulated asset base of about **€70bn**.

FY2025 was solid: reported net profit rose **12% to €6.285bn**, adjusted EBITDA was **€15.68bn**, operating cash flow rose to **€12.81bn**, and adjusted net debt fell to **€50.2bn**. Networks EBITDA rose **21%**, while Power was down **10%** on lower prices and Iberian system costs. Q1 2026 showed continued progress, with **€1.85bn adjusted profit**, **€4.1bn adjusted EBITDA**, **€2.69bn investment**, and **54% of investment allocated to networks**.

The company is not a high-growth disruptor; it is a large, proven utility compounder. The investment case is that grid capex in stable regulatory regimes can compound earnings at acceptable returns while dividends provide part of the total return.

No universal disqualifier found. I did not find evidence of accounting integrity failure, imminent solvency risk, hidden liabilities large enough to threaten equity, value-leaking control, or a business-model kill-shot. The main risks are valuation, execution, regulatory returns, leverage, and project-cost inflation.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.80 / confidence 0.75

Iberdrola has demonstrated consistent profitability. Yahoo financials show net income rising from **€4.34bn in 2022** to **€6.29bn in 2025**, with no loss years in the four-year dataset and low net-income growth dispersion. Company FY2025 materials add a longer qualitative record: the business describes 25 years of sustained growth, with market capitalisation and dividends expanding materially since 2001.

Cash backing is adequate rather than pristine. Free cash flow was positive each year from 2022–2025, but cumulative FCF/net income was about **0.70**, reflecting the heavy capex required by networks and renewables. That is normal for a growing utility, but it means earnings are not as owner-cash-light as a capital-light compounder.

Confidence would improve with a 10-year audited statement table, especially through the 2020–2022 energy-market shock period.

### Returns on Capital — 0.62 / confidence 0.80

ROIC is respectable for a regulated utility but not exceptional in absolute terms: Yahoo-derived ROIC was roughly **5.7%–7.4%** over 2022–2025, with ROE around **10%–12.6%**. The 2026–2028 plan says new network investment is targeted at an average **9.5% ROE**, which is attractive for regulated assets if achieved and financed conservatively.

This is good enough for utility-style compounding, but not the high-ROIC fingerprint of a capital-light wonderful business. The score is therefore mixed-positive rather than excellent.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.72 / confidence 0.75

Iberdrola’s moat comes mainly from regulated and contracted infrastructure, not consumer brand pricing power. The regulated asset base, network concessions, grid connection scarcity, scale, financing access, and long-duration PPAs create durable cash-flow visibility. FY2025 networks RAB grew **12% to €51bn**, and the plan targets **€70bn** by 2028.

The caveat is that regulated returns are politically negotiated. The business can earn allowed returns, but cannot simply raise prices like a software or luxury brand. Confidence would improve with a country-by-country allowed-return and regulatory-period table.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.82 / confidence 0.85

The runway is the core positive. Iberdrola plans **€58bn** of investment over 2025–2028, **85% in A-rated/stable frameworks**, including **€20bn in the UK** and **€16bn in the US**. Networks receive **€37bn**, with distribution and transmission investments aimed at closed or advanced regulatory frameworks.

This is a credible, large, multi-year reinvestment path, and it is tied to real grid needs from electrification and renewables integration. The risk is execution and whether allowed returns remain attractive after inflation and financing costs.

### Capital Allocation — 0.74 / confidence 0.75

Capital allocation is broadly coherent: invest heavily where returns are regulated/contracted, keep dividend growth tied to earnings, and use acquisitions to consolidate strategic minorities such as Avangrid and Neoenergia rather than diversify randomly. FY2025 investment was **€14.46bn**, with **62% in networks** and 60% focused on the US and UK.

Shareholder returns are meaningful, with a proposed **€0.68/share** dividend for FY2025 and €4.5bn of dividend payments. The issue is that buybacks/acquisitions and capex must be judged against intrinsic value and allowed returns; Yahoo cash-flow data shows large capital spending and some share issuance in 2025, so per-share discipline needs watching.

Confidence would improve with a detailed acquisition-return review for Avangrid/Neoenergia minority purchases and a buyback-price-vs-intrinsic-value history.

### Conservative Debt — 0.70 / confidence 0.80

For a diversified regulated utility, leverage looks acceptable but not low. Yahoo-derived 2025 net debt/EBITDA was about **3.0x** on annual data, while the current snapshot was closer to **3.9x**. EBIT interest coverage was **~6.2x** in 2025. Company FY2025 materials cite adjusted net debt of **€50.2bn**, FFO/net debt of **25.5%**, and liquidity above **€21bn**, covering **29 months** of financing needs.

That is resilient for stable regulated cash flows, but the equity remains levered to rates, allowed returns, and refinancing conditions. The missing piece is the full maturity ladder and fixed/floating split.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.68 / confidence 0.65

Ignacio Galán remains an important long-tenured executive chair, and the strategy is closely associated with his long-term electrification view. That creates some key-person concentration.

The offset is that Iberdrola is a very large, regulated, process-heavy utility with a CEO, CFO, country platforms, boards, and regulated subsidiaries. The assets and regulatory relationships should outlast any one executive. Confidence would improve with a formal succession-plan review from the governance report.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.72 / confidence 0.75

The 2026–2028 Transformational LTIP is mostly aligned with shareholder outcomes: **75% financial / 25% sustainability**, including 2028 net profit, relative TSR, maintaining long-term credit strength, and strategic sustainability objectives. The plan reserves up to **20m shares**, about **0.3% of share capital**, and Iberdrola states no new shares will be issued for that plan.

The structure is directionally good: multi-year, performance-linked, and includes credit strength rather than pure growth. Some targets are still management-framed, and shareholding-rights governance scores from Yahoo are not perfect, so this is solid rather than flawless.

### Why other strategies lost

**Cash cow** is a partial fit but not the best fit. Iberdrola pays a meaningful dividend and has stable cash generation, but free cash flow is heavily consumed by growth capex; this is not a mature asset handing back all surplus cash. **Rule Breakers** does not fit a mature regulated utility. **Freeroll** does not fit because there is no clear hard floor plus time-bound re-rating catalyst; the case is compounding, not special-situation asymmetry.

## Overall

Iberdrola is a high-quality utility compounder with a unusually clear reinvestment runway in regulated grids. The strongest parts of the case are the network growth plan, stable regulated asset base, proven profitability, and acceptable balance-sheet metrics. The main detractors are valuation, capital intensity, regulatory dependence, and the fact that returns on capital are utility-good rather than exceptional.

The stock looks more like a “fine compounder at a full-ish price” than a bargain. I would keep it watched, but the current price requires confidence that management can deliver the 2028 plan without material allowed-return compression or capex overruns.

## Thesis Connections

Iberdrola is indirectly tied to electrification, power-grid bottlenecks, renewable integration, and the broader “power-backed compute / energy infrastructure” theme. It is not a pure AI-power or compute-infrastructure play, but it benefits from the same structural pressure: electricity demand, grid reinforcement, and regulated infrastructure spend becoming more valuable.

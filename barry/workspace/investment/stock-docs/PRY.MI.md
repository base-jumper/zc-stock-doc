# PRY.MI — Prysmian S.p.A.

## Business Overview

Prysmian is an Italian-listed global leader in energy and digital cable systems. The business makes and installs cables, accessories, and connectivity systems across four reporting areas: Transmission, Power Grid, Electrification, and Digital Solutions. The high-value end is project/system work for power transmission, grid interconnects, offshore/onshore energy links, and data infrastructure; the larger-volume end includes building wire, industrial cable, and telecom connectivity.

The strategic setup is attractive: electrification, grid hardening, renewable interconnections, data centers, AI-driven power demand, and digital network upgrades all require more cable and higher-spec cable systems. Prysmian's 2025 annual report frames the company as moving from cable producer to global solutions provider; it operates at large scale, with almost 34,000 colleagues, 109 plants, and 30 research centers. The 2024 Encore Wire acquisition materially increased North American exposure and made Prysmian the overall US market leader in electrical cables, while Channell, Xtera, and ACSM add Digital Solutions and Transmission depth.

The company is already profitable and cash-generative. FY2025 revenue was €19.65bn, adjusted EBITDA €2.398bn, group net income €1.270bn, and free cash flow €1.171bn. Q1 2026 continued the trend: revenue €5.218bn, 5.0% organic growth, adjusted EBITDA €601m, and adjusted EBITDA margin of 14.2% at standard metal prices. Current valuation is not cheap: Yahoo showed €139.05/share, ~€40.6bn market cap, trailing P/E ~29.5x, forward P/E ~23.3x, and EV/EBITDA ~16.8x on 2026-07-09.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.80 / 0.78

The earning power is real and improving. Yahoo-derived statements show no loss years across 2022–2025, with net income rising from €504m in 2022 to €1.270bn in 2025. Free cash flow exceeded net income cumulatively over the same window, with cumulative FCF/net income of 1.28x and receivables/revenue broadly stable at ~12–14%. FY2025 was a record year for EBITDA, net income, and cash generation, and Q1 2026 showed another step-up in profit.

The caveat is cycle depth and metal/input-price noise. Yahoo only gave four annual years, not the ideal 7–10 year through-cycle history, and Prysmian is still an industrial business with project execution and commodity pass-through complexity. Confidence would firm up with a longer pre-2022 statement history and a deeper read of adjusted items/restructuring charges in the annual reports.

### Returns on Capital — 0.58 / 0.72

Returns are improving but not yet obviously elite. The returns script shows ROIC rising from 8.4% in 2022 to 13.0% in 2025, ROCE from 11.0% to 14.6%, ROE around 19.6% in 2025, and estimated incremental ROIC of 19.2%. That is value-creating and trending the right way, but average ROIC of ~10% is below the usual high-quality compounder bar and goodwill is meaningful at ~32% of capital after acquisitions.

This score gives credit for the improving incremental return and scale moat, but withholds a top-band score until Prysmian proves that post-Encore capital earns high returns after goodwill and across a full cycle.

### Pricing Power — 0.68 / 0.65

Prysmian has a real moat, but not unlimited pricing power. Evidence for a moat: global leadership, high technical requirements in high-voltage transmission systems, project backlog, scale, installation capability, and a broad portfolio that lets it sell solutions rather than commodity cable alone. Gross margin improved to ~38.2% in 2025 from ~33.9% in 2022, and adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 14.2% at standard metal prices in FY2025.

The limit is that a meaningful part of the business is still competitive industrial cable exposed to copper/aluminium costs, project timing, and procurement pressure. Transmission and Digital Solutions look more moat-like than basic electrification products. Confidence is capped because I did not find clean price-vs-volume pass-through data by segment.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.80 / 0.72

The runway is the strongest part of the thesis. Prysmian's own 2025 report points to energy transition, energy security, electrification, decarbonization, and digitalization as the demand drivers. Transmission had a backlog above €17bn at FY2025, including EGL4, with another ~€2bn awarded but not yet in backlog. Management's 2028 targets call for adjusted EBITDA of €2.95–3.15bn and free cash flow of €1.5–1.7bn.

This is a large, multi-year reinvestment opportunity: grid upgrades, interconnectors, offshore wind, data-center connectivity, and North American building/electrical demand. The risk is execution/capacity discipline — adding vessels, plants, and acquisitions only compounds value if incremental returns keep rising.

### Capital Allocation — 0.62 / 0.65

Capital allocation is broadly rational but acquisitive. Positives: Prysmian has used M&A to build global leadership, has kept paying a modest covered dividend, and after Encore pointed to accelerated deleveraging. FY2025 free cash flow of €1.171bn covered the €0.90/share dividend comfortably, and leverage fell from the post-acquisition high. The Encore deal looked strategically coherent: €3.9bn enterprise value, ~8.2x 2023 EBITDA or ~6.3x including expected synergies, ~€140m run-rate annual EBITDA synergies within four years, and expected EPS accretion.

The caution is acquisition dependence and goodwill. Encore, Channell, Xtera, and ACSM strengthen the business, but this is not a purely organic compounder. The score stays mid/high until post-deal synergy delivery and returns on capital prove the deals created per-share value rather than just size.

### Conservative Debt — 0.72 / 0.82

The balance sheet is acceptable for an industrial with stable infrastructure demand. The debt script shows TTM net debt/EBITDA of 1.54x, FY2025 net debt/EBITDA of 1.11x, EBIT interest coverage of 9.4x, and near-term debt cover of 15.4x. Peak leverage in the script window was 2.45x in 2024, reflecting the Encore acquisition, and the trend is already falling.

This is not net-cash conservatism, and the business still carries project and industrial cyclicality, but there is no solvency stress signal. Confidence is high on the quantitative snapshot; it would improve further with a full maturity ladder and fixed/floating debt split from the notes.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / 0.75

Prysmian is institutionally run, globally distributed, and not founder-dependent. CEO Massimo Battaini matters, but the advantage sits in scale, technical know-how, plants, vessels, customer relationships, engineering/process capability, and project execution rather than one irreplaceable person. The board and management bench appear mature for a large listed industrial.

The main person-risk angle is execution culture: complex transmission projects and integration work need strong operating discipline. Losing top operators would hurt, but it would not break the thesis.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.70 / 0.60

Alignment looks decent but not fully audited. The annual report says 50% of employees were shareholders at year-end 2025, a genuinely positive cultural signal. Governance risk indicators from Yahoo are low, and the 2026–2028 Sustainability & Innovation Scorecard is explicitly intended to be integrated into decision-making and incentive schemes.

Confidence is only moderate because I did not complete a remuneration-report level audit of executive pay metrics, vesting periods, hurdle rigor, and executive ownership. To firm this up: read Prysmian's latest remuneration report and directors' holdings table.

### Why the other strategies lost

Cash Cow is plausible but secondary: Prysmian generates real FCF, yet it is still reinvesting into a large electrification/grid/data runway, so the best thesis is compounding rather than harvesting. Rule Breakers scores reasonably on right-place/time and top-dog, but Prysmian is a proven industrial leader, not an early-stage disruptive hyper-growth bet. Freeroll loses because the stock is not near a hard valuation floor and there is no single time-bound re-rating catalyst; the return depends on execution and compounding.

## Overall

Prysmian is a good business in a structurally attractive lane, but at a price that already recognizes a lot of the improvement. The strongest positives are global leadership, transmission backlog, expanding margins, clean cash conversion, and a multi-year electrification/data-center/grid runway. The main detractors are only-decent historical ROIC, meaningful acquisition/goodwill exposure, and a current multiple high enough that future returns are sensitive to derating.

My valuation-aware judgement score is 0.54: worth watching and probably a good business to own at the right price, but not an obvious bargain at ~€139/share.

# NXE — NexGen Energy Ltd.

## Business Overview

NexGen is a pre-revenue Canadian uranium developer. Its value is dominated by the 100%-owned Rook I project in Saskatchewan's Athabasca Basin, which hosts the Arrow deposit. The 2025 annual information form reports 240 Mlb of probable U3O8 reserves at 2.37% grade; the approved project is designed for up to 30 Mlb of annual production. The final federal environmental approval and construction licence arrived on 5 March 2026, all pre-construction licence conditions were cleared on 22 May, and licensed construction began on 8 June. Until production, shareholder value depends on construction execution, uranium prices, financing and dilution rather than operating results.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.78 / 0.78 confidence

Nuclear restarts, new reactors and Western energy-security policy support uranium demand, while new mine supply is slow to permit and build. Rook I's planned 30 Mlb output would be material relative to current Western supply. The setup is attractive but commodity-cyclical: demand is structural, while realized uranium prices and contracting cycles remain volatile. Confidence would improve with a current independent uranium supply-demand forecast using a consistently defined market boundary.

### Top Dog — 0.68 / 0.84 confidence

Arrow is unusually large and high grade: 240 Mlb of probable reserves at 2.37% U3O8. If built to plan, Rook I would be one of the world's largest uranium mines, but NexGen is not yet an operator and Cameco/Kazatomprom remain established producers. The asset is top-tier; the company has not yet proven top-tier execution.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.62 / 0.74 confidence

The moat is geological and regulatory: high grade supports low modeled unit costs, the deposit is 100%-owned, and the project now holds its principal construction licence after a lengthy process. Those advantages are difficult to replicate, but they do not remove construction, financing or commodity-price risk. Confidence would improve after a current bankable cost update replaces the 2021 feasibility baseline and 2024 internal trend update.

### Durable Growth — 0.45 / 0.86 confidence

There is no revenue history: NexGen remains pre-production and used C$145m on exploration, construction assets and related advances in the first half of 2026. Growth is prospective rather than demonstrated, but licensed construction beginning on 8 June turns the project from a permitted plan into an executing build. The historical durability test is still not met. This is irreducible until construction milestones and first production create operating evidence; revisit quarterly through commissioning.

### Organic Growth — 0.82 / 0.90 confidence

The core value was discovered and advanced organically. Rook I and the nearby PCE discovery sit within NexGen's own Saskatchewan portfolio; the 2026 drilling expanded PCE's high-grade subdomain to 550 metres vertically. Acquisitions are not the growth engine.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.72 / 0.82 confidence

Management advanced Rook I from discovery through provincial approval, federal approval and a construction licence, cleared all pre-construction licence conditions ahead of schedule needs, and began licensed construction on 8 June while retaining benefit agreements with four local Indigenous Nations. Execution credibility improved, but this team has not yet built and operated Rook I. Confidence would improve with a fixed construction budget, committed project-finance package and disclosed schedule baseline.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 / 0.72 confidence

Insiders hold about 6.5%, giving material exposure, but share count rose from 482.5m at end-2022 to 660.0m at end-2025 and then 670.5m at 30 June 2026, mostly through option exercises plus interest and project-related issuances. First-half stock-based payments were C$16.4m, more than double the prior-year period, and several directors and the CEO sold shares in May 2026. Alignment is mixed rather than absent. Confidence would improve from the 2026 management circular's complete ownership and pay-performance tables.

### Customer Devotion — 0.35 / 0.45 confidence

NexGen has no operating customers or disclosed offtake book, so customer devotion cannot yet be demonstrated. Indigenous support and benefit agreements improve social licence but are not customer retention. This is irreducible until uranium contracting and delivery begin; revisit when binding offtakes or first customer relationships are disclosed.

Cash Cow and Wonderful-and-Fair lose because NexGen has no revenue, profits or positive free cash flow. Freeroll loses because the current equity value is not close to a hard balance-sheet or conservative project-value floor, despite clear construction catalysts.

## Overall

NXE owns a scarce, high-grade uranium project with credible scale and further exploration upside. Rook I is now fully permitted and in licensed construction, removing one binary risk. The weak point remains decisive: shareholders are paying today for a mine that still needs years of build execution, a refreshed budget, substantial financing and successful commissioning. At 30 June NexGen held C$970m of cash and short-term investments but also C$612m of convertible debentures, while management says significant additional financing is required. This is a conditional development bet, not a proven compounder.

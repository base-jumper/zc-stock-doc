# ELI.BR — Elia Group SA/NV

## Business Overview

Elia Group is a regulated electricity-transmission infrastructure owner and operator centred on Belgium and Germany. Its main operating platforms are Elia Transmission Belgium and 50Hertz in Germany, with smaller non-regulated / adjacent activities including Nemo Link, EGI, re.alto, WindGrid and energyRe Giga.

This is not a normal merchant utility. The investment case is driven by regulated grid expansion for electrification, renewables integration, offshore wind and system reliability. Elia reported 2025 net profit attributable to the group of **€556.6m**, bonus-adjusted EPS of **€5.51**, adjusted ROE of **7.3%**, S&P rating **BBB / stable**, regulated asset base **€22.6b**, and total 2025 investments of **€5.2b**. Management expects annual RAB growth of more than 20% over the next three years, supported by a **€21.6b** capex plan in Belgium and Germany.

The catch is financing. This is a regulated growth-infrastructure story with structurally heavy capex, not a free-cash-flow compounder. Yahoo-derived financials show 2025 operating cash flow of about **€2.09b** against capex of **€5.04b**, producing FCF of roughly **negative €2.95b**. Total debt was about **€17.8b**, net debt about **€14.0b**, and the 2025 equity package raised **€2.2b**. In Q1 2026 Elia also placed **€900m** of hybrid securities and reiterated 2026 net-profit guidance of **€690m–€740m**.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.50 / 0.78

Elia has stable positive accounting profits: Yahoo-derived net income was positive in each of 2022–2025, and annual-report net profit attributable to Elia Group was **€556.6m** in 2025. EPS rose to **€5.51** despite the 2025 rights issue, and Q1 2026 guidance implies a step-up to **€690m–€740m** of 2026 group-share net profit.

The penalty is cash conversion. Cumulative FCF conversion over the Yahoo period is deeply negative because growth capex far exceeds operating cash flow. That is partly expected for regulated asset-base expansion, but it means reported earnings are not currently distributable owner earnings.

### Returns on Capital — 0.35 / 0.82

Yahoo-derived ROIC is steady but low: about **3.3%–3.6%** from 2022–2025, with 2025 ROIC **3.5%**, ROCE **4.2%**, and ROE **7.2%**. That is not a high-return compounder in the Terry Smith / Buffett sense. The business earns regulated returns on a very large asset base; value creation depends on allowed returns exceeding the true cost of capital and on financing staying available.

The steady ROIC and regulated framework deserve some credit, but leverage flatters ROE and the absolute spread over cost of capital is not obviously wide.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.64 / 0.72

Elia's moat is regulatory rather than brand or product pricing power. Transmission grids are natural-monopoly infrastructure with cost-plus / regulated-return frameworks in Belgium and Germany. The Belgian draft tariff methodology for 2028–2031 keeps cost recovery, embedded debt cost recovery and CAPM-based fair remuneration, with incentives and a 40% regulatory equity gearing ratio. That is a real protective structure.

The offset is that regulators, not Elia, set the economics. This is protected revenue, not unconstrained pricing power. Political pressure, permitting delays, tariff resets and public affordability concerns can narrow the effective moat.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.58 / 0.75

The runway is large: grid expansion for electrification and renewable integration should require years of investment. Elia's 2025 report says RAB grew **22.5%** in 2025 and management expects **>20% annual RAB growth** over the next three years from a **€21.6b** capex plan. Q1 2026 reiterated **€6.8b** of 2026 capex, split roughly **€1.7b Belgium** and **€5.1b Germany**.

But this is not a high-return reinvestment runway. Incremental economics are regulated and capital-hungry, and the runway is partly dependent on external financing, public acceptance and regulatory timetables.

### Capital Allocation — 0.42 / 0.72

Management is allocating capital to core regulated infrastructure, which is strategically coherent and aligned with the energy transition. The dividend is modest, with Yahoo showing roughly **€2.05** per share and about **1.5%** yield, and the payout is not excessive relative to earnings.

The weak point is per-share funding discipline. Elia issued **35.6m** new shares in connection with the **€2.2b** 2025 capital raise; Yahoo-derived diluted shares rose about **35%** from 2022 to 2025. The dividend is not covered by FCF during the capex surge, and future funding may still lean on hybrid capital, debt and potentially equity.

### Conservative Debt — 0.32 / 0.82

Elia retains investment-grade access: the annual report shows **S&P BBB / stable**, and Q1 2026 financing included a **€5.25b** green RCF at Eurogrid, a **€2.0b** sustainability-linked RCF at ETB, and a **€900m** hybrid bond. Interest coverage remains acceptable, with Yahoo-derived 2025 EBIT interest coverage around **5.5x**.

Still, leverage is high and rising. Yahoo-derived 2025 net debt / EBITDA is about **7.6x** on annual financials, with net debt around **€14.0b** and negative FCF. For a regulated utility this is not automatically disqualifying, but it materially reduces resilience and leaves the thesis dependent on capital-market access.

### Fundamental Stability — 0.72 / 0.82

Demand for transmission infrastructure is structurally durable. Revenue, operating margin and EPS have improved over the Yahoo period, with 2025 revenue **€4.44b**, operating margin **23.6%**, and EPS at a period high. The business is tied to critical national infrastructure rather than discretionary demand.

The main stability risk is not demand collapse; it is execution and financing strain during a huge investment cycle.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.05 / 0.90

This trait fails on the numbers. Yahoo-derived FCF was negative in each of 2022–2025 and deeply negative in 2023–2025. 2025 FCF was about **negative €2.95b**, with capex about **2.4x** operating cash flow. This is the central reason Elia is not a cash-cow strategy candidate.

### Durable Growth — 0.62 / 0.82

Revenue CAGR over the Yahoo period was about **7.1%**, and 2025 revenue grew **17.9%**. RAB growth is much faster and is the better operational growth measure: Elia reported RAB rising from **€18.5b** in 2024 to **€22.6b** in 2025 and expects continued strong RAB growth. Q1 2026 guidance implies net-profit growth of roughly **24%–33%** versus 2025 group-share profit.

The penalty is that growth is heavily capital-funded; it does not self-finance through surplus cash.

### Organic Growth — 0.75 / 0.70

Recent growth is mainly organic regulated infrastructure build-out, not a roll-up. Yahoo-derived cumulative acquisition spend over 2022–2025 was only about **€284m**, small relative to capex and RAB. Goodwill as a share of assets is falling.

The major dilution came from funding capex, not acquisition-driven empire building.

### Right Place and Time — 0.62 / 0.75

Grid modernisation is in the right structural lane: electrification, offshore wind, interconnection and congestion management require large transmission investment. Germany's Network Development Plan process points to very large system investment needs through 2045, and Elia's projects such as Princess Elisabeth Island, Ventilus, Ostwind 3/4 and Gennaker sit directly in that build-out.

The score is not higher because regulated transmission is mature, political and low-return. The industry tailwind is real, but it does not automatically create venture-like economics.

### Top Dog — 0.46 / 0.70

Elia is structurally important in its regulated geographies, but the rule-breaker concept of "top dog" only partially fits. Transmission operators are licensed regional monopolies; they do not usually win a global market through customer choice or winner-takes-most dynamics. Elia is a leading Belgian/German grid operator, not a scalable category king.

### Sustainable Advantage — 0.48 / 0.70

The regulatory licence and natural-monopoly asset base are durable advantages, but value capture is capped by regulation. This protects the business but prevents the kind of expanding moat / high incremental economics that a rule-breaker or classic compounder needs.

### Management Backing — 0.55 / 0.50

Management has executed a large financing package, advanced major grid projects and kept guidance intact. The annual report emphasises strategic investment, regulatory engagement and financing discipline.

Confidence is only moderate because I did not find a founder/owner-style management signal, long-form capital-allocation letters, or a deep independent record of per-share value creation.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.45 / 0.45

This is under-researched. The annual report includes detailed governance and remuneration disclosure, but I did not complete a full incentive-plan read. The public / regulated nature of the shareholder base and the presence of Publi-T as an anchor are stabilising, but not the same as high insider ownership or explicit per-share-value incentives.

### Customer Devotion — 0.25 / 0.55

Not very applicable. Elia serves system users and public stakeholders in a regulated monopoly context; the relevant metric is reliability, public acceptance and regulatory trust, not customer love. Community acceptance matters because project delays can damage the investment plan.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.80 / 0.70

Low key-person risk. The assets, licences, regulation and engineering organisation matter far more than any single executive.

### Downside Support — 0.42 / 0.75

There is some asset support: price/book is about **1.96x** and the regulated asset base is tangible, critical infrastructure. However, price/tangible book is about **3.0x**, net cash is deeply negative, the dividend yield is only about **1.5%**, and FCF does not cover the dividend during the capex cycle. The floor is not strong enough for a freeroll.

### Mispricing — 0.40 / 0.45

There may be a plausible debate: the market could underappreciate the EPS/RAB growth from the grid capex cycle, or over-penalise dilution and funding risk. But I do not have a differentiated edge beyond public guidance, and the stock has already risen strongly over the last year.

### Catalyst — 0.35 / 0.55

Potential catalysts include delivery of 2026 guidance, completion of major permits/projects, clarity on the Belgian 2028–2031 tariff methodology, and evidence that the remaining funding plan avoids heavy further dilution. These are real but not clean, time-bound re-rating catalysts.

**Why wonderful-and-fair won mechanically:** The scorer now puts wonderful-and-fair narrowly ahead on confidence-adjusted score, with rule-breakers a genuine near-tie. Elia has enough structural grid-growth exposure to partly fit rule-breakers, but the company is a regulated infrastructure owner rather than a scalable category disruptor, and it scores weakly on customer devotion / top-dog dynamics. Wonderful-and-fair is also imperfect because ROIC, leverage and FCF are weak, but its protected infrastructure quality and durable earnings base fit the evidence slightly better. Cash-cow is vetoed by negative FCF, and freeroll lacks a hard floor and strong catalyst.

## Overall

Elia is a decent regulated-infrastructure growth story, not a high-quality compounder in the classic sense. The business is essential, protected and positioned in a genuine multi-decade grid investment cycle. Management guidance points to strong near-term profit growth, and the regulatory framework provides visibility that most businesses do not have.

The investment case lives or dies on financing and per-share economics. If RAB grows quickly, allowed returns hold, and dilution stays moderate after the 2025 equity raise and 2026 hybrid bond, shareholders can earn a respectable regulated-growth return. If funding needs keep forcing equity issuance or regulators squeeze returns, the RAB growth may not translate into attractive per-share compounding.

The mechanically selected strategy is now wonderful-and-fair, whose valuation method is exit-multiple. I also retained the rule-breaker weighted-average block at a 100% exit-multiple weight for comparability because Elia is already profitable and tam-capture would be a poor fit for a regulated transmission utility: the addressable market is not captured through software-like share gain, but through regulated RAB and allowed returns.

For the exit-multiple component, I used **Earnings**, not FCF, because current FCF is distorted by growth capex in a regulated RAB build-out and the market normally values regulated utilities on earnings / RAB rather than near-term FCF.

Inputs: price **€136.30**, current earning-power fundamental **€6.60/share**. That fundamental triangulates 2025 bonus-adjusted EPS of **€5.51** with 2026 management guidance of **€690m–€740m** group-share net profit after the recent capital structure changes. I modelled five-year aggregate earnings growth fading from **15%** to **6%**, annual dilution of **2%**, dividend yield **1.5%**, and a conservative **17x** exit P/E versus an entry multiple above 20x.

This is deliberately cautious: business growth and dividends need to do most of the work, while the multiple contracts. The result should be treated as medium confidence because future equity/hybrid funding and regulatory outcomes can move per-share earnings materially.

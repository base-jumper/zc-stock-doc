# VST — Vistra Corp.

## Business Overview

Vistra is a large integrated US power company. It owns generation across gas, nuclear, coal, solar, and batteries, and sells electricity and gas through retail brands including TXU Energy, Ambit, Dynegy, Homefield, Energy Harbor, and U.S. Gas & Electric. The 2025 10-K says Vistra serves about **5 million** residential, commercial, and industrial retail customers and owns roughly **44 GW** of generation capacity across major competitive US power markets.

The profit engine is the spread between what Vistra earns from wholesale power/capacity/hedging/retail supply and what it pays for fuel, operations, financing, maintenance, and growth capex. The business is not a regulated utility; it is a leveraged merchant/retail power platform with hedging and long-term contracting layered over it. That makes it unusually direct exposure to scarce firm power, AI/data-centre load growth, nuclear scarcity, capacity-market tightness, and dispatchable gas value — but it also leaves real commodity, weather, regulatory, and balance-sheet risk.

Recent facts that matter:

- FY2025 GAAP net income was **$944M**, cash from operations **$4.070B**, ongoing operations adjusted EBITDA **$5.912B**, and ongoing operations adjusted free cash flow before growth **$3.592B**.
- Q2 2026 GAAP net income was **$305M**, including a **$472M** unrealised hedge loss, while ongoing adjusted EBITDA rose **31%** year over year to **$1.767B**. First-half adjusted EBITDA was **$3.261B**, up from **$2.589B**.
- Management reaffirmed 2026 ongoing adjusted EBITDA guidance of **$6.8B–$7.6B**, adjusted FCF before growth of **$3.925B–$4.725B**, and the prior **$7.4B–$7.8B** 2027 EBITDA midpoint opportunity. The 2027 range excludes Cogentrix and Meta PPA benefits.
- A June 2026 credit-agreement amendment increased revolving credit commitments from **$3.44B** to **$5.50B** and removed certain collateral/guarantee requirements, improving liquidity but not reducing underlying leverage risk.
- Vistra cleared about **10,924 MW** in PJM's 2028/29 capacity auction at a weighted-average **$325/MW-day**, confirming that firm capacity remains scarce and valuable across its PJM fleet.
- A July amendment increased TXU Energy's committed receivables-purchase facility from **$1.10B to $1.25B** and extended it to July 2027. This adds liquidity but is financing capacity, not operating growth.
- Vistra has contracted major nuclear output with hyperscalers: AWS for up to **1,200 MW** from Comanche Peak, and Meta for **2,609 MW** from PJM nuclear assets including **433 MW** of uprates.
- The announced Cogentrix acquisition would add about **5,500 MW** of modern gas generation, expected to close in mid-to-late 2026, at about **$730/kW** net of expected tax benefits.
- FERC has approved the Cogentrix acquisition. Vistra also committed up to **$1.0B** to Helix Digital Infrastructure alongside KKR, KIA and NVIDIA and became Helix's preferred power provider; this expands the data-centre opportunity but introduces fresh deployment and execution risk.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.88 / confidence 0.90

**Facts:** US power demand is tightening around AI/data centres, electrification, reshoring, reliability needs, and long interconnection/permitting timelines. Vistra owns scarce dispatchable generation, nuclear capacity, retail load, and market access in the places where that tightness matters. The strongest evidence is not a management slogan: hyperscalers are signing long-duration nuclear contracts, capacity markets are repricing reliability, and Vistra is deploying capital into gas additions/uprates where the grid needs firm supply.

**Interpretation:** VST is in almost exactly the right place for the current power bottleneck. Unlike equipment suppliers, it owns the scarce output itself. The score is not perfect because merchant power cycles can overbuild and political/regulatory intervention can cap economics if power scarcity becomes socially painful.

### Top Dog — 0.78 / confidence 0.85

**Facts:** Vistra is one of the largest competitive retail and generation companies in the US, with about 5 million retail customers and roughly 44 GW of capacity before Cogentrix. It has major positions in ERCOT, PJM, ISO-NE, and other competitive markets; owns hard-to-replicate nuclear assets; and is large enough that hyperscalers can sign multi-decade contracts with it.

**Interpretation:** Vistra is a top-tier player in competitive US power, especially as an integrated retail/generation platform. It is not the only top dog — other independent generators, regulated utilities, and new-build developers all matter — but it is clearly one of the scaled winners.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.72 / confidence 0.80

**Facts:** Moat sources include existing interconnections, nuclear licences/assets, operational capability, retail scale, hedging sophistication, capacity-market positions, and the scarcity value of already-built dispatchable plants. Nuclear and gas plants with grid connection, permits, operating teams, and fuel/logistics are difficult and slow to replicate. Long-term PPAs with AWS and Meta also convert some merchant exposure into contracted high-quality revenue.

**Interpretation:** The advantage is real but not software-like. Vistra does not have a monopoly, and commodity power economics can mean-revert. The best moat is scarcity of existing firm power plus operational and market integration; the weak spot is that policy, new supply, fuel prices, outages, or bad hedging can still erode returns.

**Firm-up:** Future updates should look for disclosure on PPA pricing, capacity-market outcomes, nuclear uprate returns, and segment-level hedge capture to judge how much scarcity value Vistra retains versus passes to customers.

### Evidence of Durable, Accelerating Growth — 0.78 / confidence 0.90

**Facts:** FY2025 ongoing adjusted EBITDA rose to **$5.912B** and adjusted FCFbG to **$3.592B**. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA grew **31%** to **$1.767B**, lifting first-half EBITDA to **$3.261B** from **$2.589B**. Management reaffirmed 2026 guidance of **$6.8B–$7.6B** adjusted EBITDA and **$3.925B–$4.725B** adjusted FCFbG, plus the **$7.4B–$7.8B** 2027 midpoint opportunity before Cogentrix and Meta. Higher realised energy and capacity prices and the Lotus assets drove the quarter. The July 2026 PJM auction cleared about **10,924 MW** of Vistra capacity for 2028/29 at **$325/MW-day**.

**Interpretation:** Growth is credible and nearer-term than many AI-power stories. It is not clean recurring SaaS-style compounding; it is a capital-intensive power platform with commodity and hedge noise. Still, the forward guide, PPAs, and acquisitions create a visible 2026–2030 growth bridge.

### Organic Growth over Acquisitive Growth — 0.56 / confidence 0.80

**Facts:** Vistra’s growth is mixed. Organic pieces include retail customer growth, nuclear uprates, potential large-load PPAs, market-price/capacity uplift, plant availability, and brownfield gas development. But acquisitions are also central: Energy Harbor transformed the nuclear base, Lotus added about **2,600 MW** in 2025, and Cogentrix would add another **5,500 MW**.

**Interpretation:** This is the softest Rule Breaker trait. The company is not a pure organic compounder; management is actively buying and developing generation assets. The acquisitions look strategic rather than random empire-building, but the growth thesis depends partly on M&A execution and price discipline.

**Firm-up:** Revisit after Cogentrix closes to compare actual EBITDA/FCFbG accretion, integration costs, leverage, and whether acquired assets meet the promised return profile.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.79 / confidence 0.85

**Facts:** Management has executed a large strategic pivot: Energy Harbor/nuclear integration, Lotus purchase, Cogentrix agreement, hyperscaler PPAs, continued buybacks/dividends, refinancing, and investment-grade progress. FERC approval removed an important Cogentrix closing hurdle. By Aug. 3, Vistra had repurchased about **$6.5B** of shares since Nov. 2021, reduced share count roughly **30%**, and retained about **$1.2B** of authorization expected to be completed by year-end 2027. The new Helix commitment provides another data-centre route but must earn its keep.

**Interpretation:** Management looks commercially sharp and shareholder-oriented. The main reservation is that capital allocation is being judged in a hot market for power assets; the true test is whether acquisitions and PPAs still look disciplined after the cycle normalizes.

**Firm-up:** Watch realised returns on Cogentrix/Lotus, leverage after close, and whether repurchases remain price-sensitive rather than automatic.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.82 / confidence 0.85

**Facts:** The 2026 proxy ties annual incentives to ongoing operations adjusted EBITDA, total cost, and adjusted FCFbG per share. Long-term performance stock units use adjusted FCFbG per share, relative TSR, and, for relevant grants, cost/performance measures. The proxy also discloses stock ownership guidelines, anti-hedging/pledging rules, and clawback policy. Importantly, the compensation language repeatedly uses per-share FCF measures rather than only size metrics.

**Interpretation:** This is better than average. Adjusted EBITDA is still a management-adjusted metric and can be gamed, but FCFbG per share and relative TSR point in the right direction. The buyback record also suggests management thinks in per-share value terms.

### Customer Devotion — 0.46 / confidence 0.65

**Facts:** Retail power customers are sticky enough for TXU to grow customer counts for five straight years, and hyperscalers are willing to sign long-duration nuclear PPAs. But electricity is a low-emotion product, and wholesale/large-load customers care about reliability, price, creditworthiness, and carbon attributes more than brand love. There is limited public evidence of NPS, retention cohorts, or customer advocacy.

**Interpretation:** Customers need and trust Vistra more than they love it. For Rule Breakers this is a weak trait; the company’s edge comes from asset scarcity and execution, not devotion.

**Firm-up:** Look for retail churn/retention, customer-count detail by market, large-load renewal activity, or named hyperscaler expansions after the first AWS/Meta deals. Until then, confidence remains inherently limited.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Wonderful and Fair:** Vistra has strong capital allocation, a real reinvestment runway, and improving contracted revenues, but earnings quality and balance-sheet conservatism are not clean enough for a classic wonderful-and-fair compounder. GAAP earnings are noisy because of hedge marks, standard FCF is distorted by growth capex, and leverage is meaningful for a merchant power business.
- **Freeroll:** VST has catalysts — Cogentrix close, Meta/AWS ramp, nuclear uprates, 2026–2027 guidance delivery — but it does not have a hard downside floor near the current share price. At about **$141**, it is more interesting than it was, but still not a cheap, asset-backed, downside-protected special situation; it is a re-rated power-scarcity compounder.

## Overall

VST remains one of the better direct listed expressions of the power bottleneck theme. It owns the scarce thing — firm electricity output — rather than just selling equipment into the build-out. The updated procedure classifies it best as a **Rule Breaker-ish power-scarcity compounder**, not because it is a young disruptor, but because the stock’s appeal is driven by a large, accelerating structural opportunity and Vistra’s ability to compound per-share value into that opportunity.

The strengths are substantial: scarce nuclear/gas generation, retail integration, hyperscaler PPAs, strong 2026 guidance, disciplined buybacks, and a credible path to higher FCFbG through 2027–2030. The detractors are also real: merchant/hedging volatility, leverage, regulatory risk, coal/closure liabilities, acquisition execution risk, and a share price that already reflects a lot more optimism than it did a year ago.

Current stance: **still good and more attractive after the pullback**. Q2 delivered the operating confirmation the prior note was waiting for: adjusted EBITDA rose more than 30%, guidance held, liquidity remained ample, and the Cogentrix process advanced. Helix adds upside optionality but is not yet underwritten as value. The valuation-aware score moves to **7.3/10**; merchant, policy, acquisition and leverage risks still demand position-size discipline.

## Valuation

The local Yahoo Finance quote was **$140.59** on **2026-08-10** (latest available snapshot; US market not yet open), with market cap about **$47.4B** and a 52-week range of **$132.66–$219.82**. Q2 results reaffirmed the operating anchor rather than changing the guidance range.

I use **FCF** for the exit-multiple model, specifically management’s ongoing adjusted FCF before growth rather than GAAP net income. GAAP earnings are too noisy because hedge marks can swing reported profit: Q1 2026 net income included a **$723M** unrealised hedge gain, while FY2025 included an **$808M** unrealised hedge loss. FCFbG is not perfect — it is a non-GAAP measure and excludes growth capital — but it is the cleanest owner-earnings proxy for current capital allocation capacity.

Current fundamental anchor:

- 2026 ongoing adjusted FCFbG guidance midpoint: **$4.325B**.
- Shares outstanding: approximately **336M** as of Aug. 3.
- Current FCF/share anchor: **$12.87**.

Interpretation: the lower price and confirmed PJM capacity tightness move the expected return further above the 10% hurdle without requiring a richer exit multiple. It is still not a slam dunk: the thesis needs real FCF growth and continued buybacks, while merchant-power, leverage, acquisition and regulatory risks remain meaningful.

Rough entry framework:

- **Below ~$140–145:** materially more interesting; prospective return improves while thesis risk is unchanged.
- **Around current ~$141:** attractive active-watch/position territory if sized for volatility.
- **Above ~$180 without guidance upgrades:** much less compelling; too much rests on flawless execution and/or a premium exit multiple.

## Thesis Connections

- **Power Bottleneck / FIT:** direct beneficiary of scarce firm power, grid reliability needs, and slow new-build/interconnection timelines.
- **AI data-centre power demand:** direct through AWS/Meta nuclear PPAs and broader large-load opportunity.
- **Nuclear scarcity:** existing nuclear output and uprates are central to the thesis; long-duration carbon-free power is hard to replicate.
- **Growing wealth divide / inflation resilience:** indirect; power scarcity can pass through into higher electricity costs, but this is not the main reason to own it.

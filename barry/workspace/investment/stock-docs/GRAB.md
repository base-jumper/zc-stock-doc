# GRAB — Grab Holdings Limited

## Business Overview

Grab is a Southeast Asian superapp operating in more than 900 cities across Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam. Its platform connects consumers with drivers and merchants for mobility, food and grocery delivery, and financial services. Most revenue comes from commissions and service fees retained from transactions; advertising, subscriptions, lending and digital banking add higher-frequency monetisation around the core network.

In 2025, Deliveries, Mobility and Financial Services contributed 53.4%, 36.2% and 10.3% of revenue. The profit pool is less balanced: Mobility produced $690 million of segment Adjusted EBITDA, Deliveries $287 million, and Financial Services lost $110 million. Q2 2026 continued the shift toward a broader ecosystem: Deliveries revenue was $531 million, Mobility $331 million and Financial Services $134 million, while the gross loan portfolio reached $2.3 billion.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.76, confidence 0.82

Grab sits at the intersection of Southeast Asia's urbanisation, mobile-first consumption, local-commerce digitisation and underbanked financial-services demand. Its 2025 filing describes food delivery, mobility and digital payments as still underpenetrated; the company's own execution supports that claim, with 2025 on-demand GMV up 21% and Q2 2026 GMV up another 21%. The setup is attractive but not pristine: delivery and ride-hailing are locally fragmented, incentive-heavy and exposed to labour, fee-cap and lending regulation. The companion market document supplies the market-size and penetration evidence used in valuation.

### Top Dog — 0.78, confidence 0.75

Grab is the largest integrated on-demand platform across its eight-country footprint by the evidence reviewed: it served 47.2 million monthly transacting users in 2025 and a record 53.9 million in Q2 2026, with $22.1 billion of 2025 on-demand GMV. Its regional breadth, 900-city distribution and ability to cross-sell delivery, mobility and finance distinguish it from narrower rivals. Country-level competition remains formidable—principally GoTo/Gojek, ShopeeFood, Foodpanda, Maxim and inDrive—and market structure resets locally rather than producing one global winner. Confidence would rise with a current, independently harmonised country-by-country GMV share series covering both mobility and delivery.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.70, confidence 0.80

Grab's moat combines local driver/merchant density, brand and trust, regional scale, dispatch and risk data, and the breadth of a single app spanning mobility, delivery and finance. Density improves availability and fulfilment; more transactions improve matching, fraud and underwriting models; advertising and lending deepen merchant monetisation. The moat is real but not impregnable: consumers, merchants and drivers can multi-home, switching costs are modest, and Q2 incentives rose faster than GMV. Confidence would rise with country-level share retention, GrabUnlimited churn/renewal data and independent measures of consumer and partner multi-homing.

### Evidence of Durable, Accelerating Growth — 0.82, confidence 0.95

Grab is a maturing growth company, so revenue, GMV, users and unit economics are the right metrics. Revenue rose from $1.43 billion in 2022 to $2.36 billion in 2023, $2.80 billion in 2024 and $3.37 billion in 2025. After the 2023 step-up, annual growth stabilised near 20%; Q2 2026 revenue grew 22% and full-year guidance calls for 22–23%. On-demand GMV grew 16% in 2024, 21% in 2025 and 21% in Q2 2026, while Q2 users grew 17% and GMV per user 3%.

Economics are improving with scale. Gross margin rose from 36.5% in 2023 to 42.0% in 2024 and 43.2% in 2025. Adjusted EBITDA moved from a $22 million loss in 2023 to $313 million in 2024 and $500 million in 2025; Q2 2026 added 54% year on year. Deliveries segment Adjusted EBITDA margin reached 2.3% of GMV and Mobility 8.6%. Reported Q2 profit was flattered by a one-time $307 million Superbank remeasurement gain, so the operating and cash metrics carry more weight than headline EPS.

### Organic Growth over Acquisitive Growth — 0.72, confidence 0.85

The core on-demand engine is predominantly organic: 2025 delivery and mobility GMV grew 21% and 19%, respectively, and Q2 2026 growth came from more users, transactions and spend per user rather than a platform acquisition. Grab has nonetheless become more acquisitive. It bought Everrise and Infermove in 2025, consolidated Superbank in June 2026 and acquired Stash in July 2026; these deals add supermarket, banking, robotics and U.S. wealth-management exposure. Goodwill was 7.5% of assets at end-2025, and cumulative acquisition cash spend was $588 million over 2022–2025. The score recognises organic strength but marks down rising adjacency and integration risk.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.78, confidence 0.80

Co-founder Anthony Tan still leads Grab and has taken it from a ride-hailing startup through a bruising subsidy era to IFRS operating profit, improving unit economics and a net-cash balance sheet. Management communicates through operating drivers—users, transactions, partner supply, advertising penetration and segment margins—and Q2 2026 guidance was raised after eighteen consecutive quarters of Adjusted EBITDA growth. Strategic shareholders include Uber, SoftBank, Toyota and MUFG alongside large institutions. The main deduction is governance concentration rather than operating competence. Confidence would rise with independent employee and partner culture data and a longer clean record of post-profit capital allocation.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.38, confidence 0.95

Alignment is the weakest trait. Anthony Tan and permitted entities economically owned only 3.2% of ordinary shares as of January 2026 but controlled 59.9% of votes; shareholders subsequently approved 90 votes per Class B share, increasing the founder-control buffer. That structure entrenches control and leaves public Class A owners with little practical influence. Share-based compensation remained material at $241 million in 2025 and $140 million in the first half of 2026, while potential dilution includes employee awards and the 2030 convertibles. The $1.75 billion of cumulative buyback authorisations is a genuine offset, but recent executive sales and a compensation system dominated by granted equity do not amount to owner-like capital at risk.

### Customer Devotion — 0.75, confidence 0.85

The consumer app carried a 4.8/5 Google Play rating across 16.6 million reviews and more than 100 million downloads when checked on 8 August 2026. Engagement also expanded: 2025 users grew 14%, on-demand GMV per user rose 4%, and Q2 2026 users and GMV per user grew 17% and 3%. This is preference-based convenience rather than contractual lock-in. The deduction is that incentives remain an important behavioural driver: 2025 incentives were 10.2% of on-demand GMV, and Q2 2026 partner and consumer incentives grew 32% and 27%, faster than GMV. The evidence supports strong habitual use, but not pure, unsubsidised devotion.

### Why the other strategies lost

Wonderful-and-fair and cash-cow were ruled out by earnings quality: 2025 was Grab's first full profitable year, the four-year record still contains three loss years, and headline profit and free cash flow require material normalisation for lending flows, fair-value movements and stock compensation. Freeroll was ruled out because there is no hard asset, dividend or take-out floor near the current price and no specific ≤4-year catalyst that forces fair-value recognition. Grab is a growth-and-execution thesis, not an income or re-rating setup.

## Overall

Grab is a credible Rule Breaker rather than a speculative concept. It has regional scale, multiple reinforcing demand loops, low-20s growth and demonstrably improving margins. The investment case now depends on whether those loops produce durable per-share cash flow, not merely more GMV. The largest detractors are founder entrenchment, sustained incentive intensity, stock-based compensation, rising credit exposure and an expanding acquisition perimeter.

## Valuation

The canonical Rule Breakers valuation blends two applicable ten-year lenses and produces an expected annualised ROI of **3.22%**. I weight exit-multiple 60% because it starts from current group cash generation and includes Financial Services; TAM-capture receives 40% because its independent market anchor is valuable but its boundary covers only mobility and delivery. The 1.52 percentage-point component spread is modest.

**TAM-capture — 2.31% annualised.** The Southeast Asia on-demand mobility and delivery market model estimates a $14.0 billion market in 2036 and $6.488 billion of mobility-adjusted GRAB revenue, a 46.35% capture after competitive mobility. Applying Uber's current 13.5% EBITDA margin and 21.9× EV/EBITDA role-model economics gives $19.18 billion of terminal enterprise value. The bridge uses 4.168 billion current diluted shares, 1% annual dilution and $2.0 billion of terminal net cash, producing $4.60 per share. Value creation contributes 3.34% annually and dilution subtracts 0.99%. This is the core-platform survivor case; it gives no value to a separately successful Financial Services franchise, but it also does not assume today's full $4.5 billion net-cash position survives ten years of acquisitions and buybacks.

**Exit-multiple — 3.83% annualised.** The current owner-earnings anchor is Q2 2026 trailing-twelve-month adjusted FCF of $450 million, or $0.108 per diluted share. The model fades aggregate FCF growth from 20% to 4% over ten years (9.5% CAGR), applies 1% annual dilution and exits at 22× FCF versus 33.9× at entry. Business growth contributes 9.50% annually, dilution subtracts 0.99% and multiple compression subtracts 4.23%, producing a $5.33 terminal price. The growth path is deliberately constrained by the market model: the earlier high-teens FCF path would have implied a terminal cash margin inconsistent with the available revenue pool.

At $3.66, the stock therefore prices in much of the successful execution visible in the trait score. The 0.35 agent score reflects a strong company paired with a weak base-case return, plus founder entrenchment, incentive intensity and credit/acquisition risk. A materially better outcome requires Financial Services to become a valuable profit pool, market capture to exceed the mobility-adjusted base rate, or dilution and the exit multiple to be more favourable than assumed.

## Thesis Connections

Grab fits a Southeast Asia digitisation thesis: mobile-first consumers adopt on-demand local commerce and an underbanked population creates adjacent lending and digital-banking demand. It also offers a narrower AI application thesis through dispatch, pricing, recommendations, fraud detection and credit underwriting, but the investment case does not require treating Grab as an AI platform.

## Watch

- On-demand MTU growth, GMV per MTU and total incentive intensity; flag growth increasingly bought with subsidies.
- Deliveries Adjusted EBITDA/GMV and Mobility Adjusted EBITDA/GMV; confirm scaling survives higher driver support costs.
- Adjusted free cash flow after lending, deposits and treasury-liquidity normalisation, less the economic cost of stock compensation.
- Basic and fully diluted share count against the $750 million repurchase programme and the 2030 convertible notes.
- Gross loan growth, expected-credit-loss ratio and Financial Services segment EBITDA as Superbank and Stash consolidate.
- Country-level mobility and delivery share against GoTo/Gojek, ShopeeFood, Foodpanda, Maxim and inDrive.
- Integration and valuation discipline for Everrise, Superbank, Stash and autonomous-vehicle investments.
- Regulatory changes affecting driver classification/pay, platform fees, digital-bank capital and data localisation.

# Trait: Switching Costs

**Production role:** selected direct-ridge feature for both `s1` and `r`.

**What we're looking for:**
Once a customer is on a provider, leaving is costly — in money, effort, risk, or lost data — so
share, once won, stays won. This is the *stickiness* of the market.

**Expected direction:** stronger switching costs lower `s1` and raise `r`. High-lock-in markets
(cloud, enterprise software) tend toward oligopoly rather than monopoly because lock-in protects
every incumbent's installed base, not only the leader.

**Sources to check:** data gravity / re-platforming cost (cloud, ERP), contractual lock-in,
learned workflows and certifications, integration depth, loss of history or social graph.

**Scoring guidance (0–1):**

* **0.70–1.00** Leaving is expensive and risky; multi-year lock-in in practice
* **0.40–0.69** Some friction, but a motivated customer switches within a cycle
* **0.00–0.39** Frictionless — customers change providers on price or whim

**Documentation:** the concrete source of lock-in, and any evidence on realized retention/churn or
contract length. Distinguish stickiness that holds share (this trait) from a network effect that
compounds it ([[network-effects]]).

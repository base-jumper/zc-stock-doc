# CPX.TO — Capital Power Corporation

## Business Overview

Capital Power is a Canadian independent power producer with natural-gas, renewables and battery-storage assets across Canada and the U.S. It sells power through a mix of contracted generation, merchant exposure and commercial optimisation. The 2025 acquisition of Hummel and Rolling Hills added roughly **2.2 GW** of U.S. gas-fired capacity in PJM and made the U.S. portfolio roughly **60% of capacity and adjusted EBITDA** by management’s description.

This is a mature infrastructure utility/generator, not a high-growth compounder. The attractive part is scale, dispatchable capacity, contracted cash flows, a 12-year dividend-growth record and exposure to rising North American power demand, especially AI/data-centre load. The hard part is that the same strategy is capital-heavy: debt rose sharply after acquisitions, GAAP free cash flow is thin after capex, and shareholder returns are being funded from AFFO/financing capacity rather than clean surplus FCF.

2025 results were mixed on the surface: revenue and other income of **C$3.72b**, adjusted EBITDA of **C$1.58b**, AFFO of **C$1.066b / C$7.08 per share**, and operating cash flow of **C$962m**, but GAAP net income fell to **C$159m** and diluted EPS to **C$0.88** after much stronger 2023–2024 profits. Yahoo/YFinance shows current price around **C$74.11**, market cap around **C$11.6b**, enterprise value around **C$18.6b**, forward EPS **C$3.08**, dividend yield about **3.85%**, and total debt around **C$7.0b**.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.45 / confidence 0.75

Capital Power is profitable and cash-generative on management’s adjusted metrics, but reported earnings are too volatile for a high-quality cash-cow score. Yahoo annual data shows net income of **C$138m, C$744m, C$699m, C$160m** for 2022–2025, with diluted EPS moving from **C$0.84** to **C$6.04**, **C$5.15**, then **C$0.88**. The 2025 release likewise shows full-year net income of **C$159m** versus **C$701m** in 2024, while adjusted EBITDA and AFFO rose.

Interpretation: the underlying power assets can generate real cash, but GAAP earnings are affected by asset sales, derivative/market effects, financing, depreciation and acquisition timing. I give partial credit for positive earnings and strong AFFO, but not a high score because the cash-cow strategy wants dependable, auditable profits that do not need heavy adjustment. Confidence would improve by reconciling several years of AFFO to GAAP cash flow directly from the annual report tables.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.35 / confidence 0.75

This is the key weakness. 2025 operating cash flow was **C$962m**, but purchase of property, plant and equipment and other assets was **C$864m**, leaving only about **C$98m** of simple FCF. Yahoo’s annual data shows FCF of roughly **C$253m, C$99m, C$74m, C$98m** for 2022–2025, with capex absorbing **73–94%** of operating cash flow in each year. The dividend alone was **C$339m** in 2025, so simple FCF coverage was poor.

The counterpoint is that management reports **C$1.066b** of AFFO in 2025 and guides/communicates the business primarily through AFFO and adjusted EBITDA; infrastructure growth capex can depress simple FCF while still creating future contracted cash flows. For a cash-cow score, though, I do not want to wave away capex. This is a capital-intensive generator that returns cash before it is obviously surplus on a GAAP FCF basis. Confidence would improve with a clean sustaining-vs-growth capex split.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.65 / confidence 0.75

The demand backdrop is supportive. Capital Power’s 2025 release highlights stronger long-term contracted cash flows, a new Midland Cogeneration Venture contract through **2040**, Ontario battery projects contracted through **2047**, a binding MOU for a **250 MW**, 10+ year Alberta data-centre ESA expected from 2028, and management’s view that AI demand, electrification and population growth are reshaping power demand. Generation availability was **91%** in 2025.

The business is not structurally obsolete, and dispatchable gas plus storage likely becomes more valuable if grids need firm capacity. But stability is only partial: power prices, gas prices, carbon policy, asset availability, financing costs and regulatory settings still matter. Revenue was not smooth across 2022–2025, and reported margins fell materially in 2025. This is stable enough to watch, not stable enough to call a clean bond-like cash stream.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.55 / confidence 0.55

Capital Power has some moat-like characteristics: hard-to-replicate generating assets, grid interconnection scarcity, operating expertise, long-term contracts and exposure to capacity-constrained markets such as PJM. The Hummel/Rolling Hills acquisition increased scale in a liquid U.S. power market, while batteries and data-centre ESAs can convert scarcity into contract value.

But pricing power is not the same as brand/customer pricing power. Much of the business is either contracted, merchant commodity-exposed, or constrained by market design and regulation. Capital Power can benefit from scarcity and volatility; it cannot simply raise prices at will. Confidence is lower because a proper score needs asset-by-asset contract duration, capacity-market economics and merchant exposure, not just company-level summaries.

### Capital Allocation — 0.42 / confidence 0.70

The positives: Capital Power has raised the common dividend for **12 consecutive years**, increased the dividend **6%** in 2025, added contracted assets, and has a plausible strategic rationale for U.S. gas generation growth at a time of rising power demand. It also raised **C$667m** of equity and issued **C$2.3b** of senior notes to fund expansion and preserve balance-sheet capacity.

The negatives are material. 2025 acquisition spend was around **C$3.0b**, debt rose, diluted shares increased sharply, and dividends were not covered by simple FCF. Yahoo-derived capital allocation data shows cumulative 2022–2025 acquisitions of roughly **C$4.6b** and share count up about **55%**. That may still prove rational if the U.S. assets earn attractive contracted/merchant returns, but the burden of proof is high. This is more empire-building risk than classic cash-cow harvesting.

### Returns on Capital — 0.35 / confidence 0.75

Returns are modest and volatile. Yahoo-derived ROIC was roughly **4.6%** in 2022, **11.8%** in 2023, **9.6%** in 2024 and **4.0%** in 2025. ROE also fell from strong 2023–2024 levels to roughly **3.3%** in 2025. For an infrastructure utility this is not automatically fatal — regulated/contracted assets often earn lower returns than software or branded consumer firms — but the strategy still needs durable cash returns on a heavy capital base.

Interpretation: Capital Power may earn acceptable utility-like returns over a cycle, but current reported returns do not show a high-return moat. The score could improve if newly acquired PJM assets show strong incremental returns after integration and normalised commodity/contract conditions.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.25 / confidence 0.80

This is the second major weakness. Yahoo snapshot data shows total debt around **C$7.0b**, cash around **C$139m**, enterprise value around **C$18.6b**, current ratio **0.69**, quick ratio **0.38**, and debt/equity about **146%**. Annual data shows net debt/EBITDA rising from roughly **2.0×** in 2023 and **2.5×** in 2024 to about **6.3×** in 2025 on Yahoo-derived EBITDA, with EBIT interest coverage down to roughly **1.6×**.

Management can reasonably argue adjusted EBITDA/AFFO and long-lived contracted infrastructure support more debt than an ordinary industrial company. But for scoring, leverage is stretched and rising. No disqualifier is triggered from the evidence reviewed — the company still has access to debt/equity markets and investment-grade-style funding — but the balance sheet reduces resilience and limits how much of the dividend/growth story I would underwrite.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.70 / confidence 0.55

Capital Power is an asset-heavy operating company, not a founder-dependent product company. The portfolio, contracts, regulatory relationships and operating processes should survive management changes. The CFO transition announced for March 2026 is relevant but not thesis-breaking.

Confidence is only moderate because I did not review the full management bench, succession disclosures or operating incident history. A deeper governance review could move this up or down.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.50 / confidence 0.45

I have no evidence of abusive control or related-party value leakage. Governance risk metrics from Yahoo are generally low-to-moderate, though shareholder-rights risk screens worse than the other categories. The strategic tension is whether management is incentivised to grow per-share value or simply grow the asset base through acquisitions.

This is deliberately neutral with low confidence until the 2025 remuneration report is reviewed. Firm-up source: annual proxy/remuneration disclosure, especially metrics tied to AFFO/share, ROIC, leverage, safety, emissions and total shareholder return.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-Fair loses because returns on capital are not high, leverage is stretched, FCF after capex is weak, and the current thesis depends on capital-heavy acquisitions rather than self-funded high-return reinvestment. Rule Breakers loses because this is a mature utility/generator, not an emerging high-growth category winner with explosive organic growth or customer devotion. Freeroll is not the primary fit because there is no clear hard asset/NAV floor or time-bound re-rating catalyst; the case is ongoing cash generation and dividend income, albeit imperfectly.

## Overall

Capital Power is an interesting power-demand/infrastructure watchlist name, but it is a compromised cash cow. The bull case is credible: dispatchable power and storage should matter more in North America, CPX has expanded into PJM, 2025 adjusted EBITDA/AFFO were strong, and the dividend record is real. The bear case is equally concrete: debt is high, reported earnings are volatile, simple FCF does not cover the dividend, and recent growth required large acquisitions plus equity issuance.

My judgement is **watch, not buy aggressively**. I would want clearer evidence that the Hummel/Rolling Hills assets and Midland recontracting translate into per-share AFFO growth while leverage falls. Until then, the stock looks more like a leveraged utility income/growth hybrid than a clean cash cow.

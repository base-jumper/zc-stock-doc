# HUBB — Hubbell Incorporated

## Business Overview

Hubbell is a US electrical and utility infrastructure manufacturer. It sells the physical components that let utilities, contractors, industrial customers, datacentres, OEMs, and distributors conduct, protect, connect, monitor, and manage power safely.

Segments:

- **Utility Solutions** — front-of-the-meter and edge-of-grid products: transmission and distribution components, substation equipment, connectors, arresters, insulators, bushings, enclosures, cutouts, switches, smart meters, AMI, grid communications, protection, control systems, and related utility infrastructure.
- **Electrical Solutions** — behind-the-meter and non-residential products: wiring devices, grounding/connectors, rough-in electrical products, industrial controls, harsh/hazardous products, and electrical components sold mainly through electrical/industrial distribution.

The thesis is not “tiny disruptor becomes category king.” It is better framed as **Wonderful and Fair**: a durable industrial compounder with real exposure to grid modernization, utility T&D/substation spending, electrification, datacentre load growth, reliability requirements, and bolt-on M&A. The business quality is good; the current valuation is the limiting factor.

## Strategy Fit

### Wonderful and Fair — preferred strategy

HUBB best fits **Wonderful and Fair**. It has consistent profits, high-teens operating margins, mid-teens ROIC, strong FCF conversion, manageable leverage, recurring replacement/upgrade demand, and a credible multi-year reinvestment runway in utility grid infrastructure and electrification.

The caveat: “fair” is still stretched even after the share-price pullback. At **$472.55** on 2 August 2026, HUBB trades around **23.2x** the midpoint of raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance and **26.25x** the normalised FCF/share anchor used below. That is not absurd for a high-quality electrical infrastructure compounder, especially after adding NSI, but it leaves limited room for multiple compression.

### Rule Breakers — secondary / weaker fit

HUBB still benefits from the right wave — grid modernization, datacentres, electrification, utility reliability — but it is mature, B2B, non-founder-led, acquisitive, and not obviously the dominant winner in a winner-takes-most market. It is a good compounder, not a classic Rule Breaker.

### Freeroll — poor fit

HUBB is not cheap, lacks a clear hard valuation floor, and has no time-bound catalyst that should force a near-term re-rating. Quality is real, but the setup is not asymmetric-special-situation cheap.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent Profits — score 0.82, confidence 0.89

Hubbell has clean enough, recurring enough earnings for a high-quality industrial. Q2 2026 net sales rose **15%** to **$1.712B**, organic sales rose **10%**, and adjusted diluted EPS rose **12%** to **$5.52**. First-half operating cash flow was **$336.4M** and capex **$77.6M**, implying about **$258.8M** of FCF; Q2 FCF was **$213M** versus $221M a year ago. Management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to **$20.25–$20.55** and continues to expect FCF conversion of about **90% of adjusted net income**. Acquisition accounting and NSI integration costs widen the GAAP-adjusted gap, but the underlying earnings and cash generation remain sound.

The main blemishes are normal industrial cyclicality, working-capital swings, acquisition accounting/amortization, and the gap between GAAP EPS and adjusted EPS. None look thesis-breaking.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — score 0.78, confidence 0.84

Returns are comfortably above average. StockAnalysis shows current ROE around **25.8%**, ROA **10.2%**, ROIC **16.8%**, and ROCE **18.8%**. ROIC has been consistently in the mid-teens since FY2022, and operating margin has expanded from **12.7% in FY2021** to about **20.9% TTM**.

This clears the “wonderful” bar, though it is not an asset-light software-style return profile. Hubbell still needs plants, inventory, capex, acquisitions, and working capital.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — score 0.73, confidence 0.82

The moat is practical: reliability in critical infrastructure, utility qualification, installed-base familiarity, standards/regulatory know-how, distributor relationships, brand breadth, manufacturing capability, and scale in fragmented niches. Q2 adjusted operating margin was **23.9%**, down 50 bps year over year but still strong while raw-material costs, tariffs and inflation rose. Management said price and productivity offset part of those pressures, and Utility Solutions adjusted margin held at **25.6%** versus 25.5% a year ago. This is credible pricing/mix evidence, though Electrical Solutions adjusted margin fell 130 bps to **21.2%**.

Limits: Hubbell’s own 10-K says it faces substantial competition and some competitors have larger financial resources. Patents help — roughly **3,250 active US and foreign patents** — but management says the business is not dependent on patent protection.

Confidence note: a more precise confidence upgrade would require segment/category market-share data from Hubbell investor presentations or independent industry reports; absent that, moat strength is inferred from margins, returns, brand breadth, and business description.

### Reinvestment Runway — score 0.82, confidence 0.86

The runway is credible and now better evidenced. Utility T&D replacement, substation expansion, grid hardening, AMI/grid monitoring, electrification, and datacentre load growth all pull demand toward HUBB’s product categories. Q2 organic sales grew **10%**: Utility Solutions rose about **6%** organically, including Grid Infrastructure sales growth of about **12%**, while Electrical Solutions rose **18%** organically. Management cited visible strength across utility and electrical markets, said first-half orders support the second half, and raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to **$20.25–$20.55** from $19.30–$19.85.

NSI adds another leg to Electrical Solutions: about **$570M** of expected 2026 revenue, >15,000 branded electrical products, and exposure to light industrial, datacentre, network infrastructure, and distributor cross-sell. The runway is not unlimited: this is a mature industrial, and growth still depends on utility capex cycles, project timing, regulatory approvals, non-res construction, and rational M&A execution.

Confidence note: confidence would improve with Q3/Q4 confirmation that double-digit organic growth persists and with quantified NSI revenue, margin and synergy disclosure after a full quarter of ownership.

### Capital Allocation — score 0.69, confidence 0.82

Capital allocation looks strategically coherent but now more execution-sensitive. In FY2025 Hubbell generated record FCF, paid **$286.6M** in dividends, repurchased **$225M** of shares, spent **$155.1M** on capex, and invested **$958.3M** in acquisitions. The dividend has been raised for 18 consecutive years. DMC Power looked strategically sensible in Utility Solutions, and NSI is a logical Electrical Solutions bolt-on with complementary fittings/connectors/wire-management brands.

The concern is price and leverage discipline. NSI cost **$3.0B**, around **15.5x anticipated 2026 EBITDA**, financed with a $900M term loan, $1.9B of senior notes and commercial paper. Hubbell also spent **$203.5M** acquiring shares in H1 2026, including Q2 open-market repurchases at an average near **$504**, while levering for NSI. The repurchases support per-share value only if the shares were below intrinsic value; at the prevailing full multiple, retaining more cash for deleveraging would have been easier to defend.

Confidence note: confidence would improve after 4–8 quarters of DMC Power/NSI contribution disclosure versus purchase expectations and clearer post-deal deleveraging priorities.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — score 0.62, confidence 0.90

Debt is manageable but no longer conservative in the ordinary sense. At 30 June 2026, Hubbell reported **$568.7M short-term debt**, **$4.804B long-term debt**, and only **$378.6M cash**, or roughly **$5.0B net debt** after funding NSI. Long-term debt rose from $2.036B at year-end 2025. Strong margins, cash generation and capital-market access reduce refinancing risk, but the balance sheet now depends on sustained earnings and disciplined deleveraging.

The business has strong cash generation and investment-grade-style access to debt markets, but pro-forma leverage is materially higher until NSI EBITDA, synergies, and retained FCF come through. This is not fragile; it is no longer pristine.

### Key-Person Risk — score 0.80, confidence 0.80

Hubbell appears institutionally run rather than dependent on one founder/operator. CEO Gerben Bakker matters, but the business has deep brands, channels, plants, product lines, and customer relationships. CFO succession was orderly: Bill Sperry retired at end-2025 and internal executive Joseph Capozzoli became CFO on Jan. 1, 2026.

Confidence note: irreducible for now — true bench depth only becomes more visible through future executive transitions and post-transition execution.

### Incentive Alignment — score 0.68, confidence 0.72

The pay design is directionally shareholder-aligned. The 2026 proxy says short-term incentives are 80% financial metrics and 20% strategic initiatives. Long-term incentives are 75% performance-oriented, with performance shares tied to three equally weighted metrics including adjusted operating profit margin and relative TSR, while stock appreciation rights tie a portion of pay directly to share-price appreciation. The company also has stock ownership/retention requirements, and all NEOs were compliant as of Dec. 31, 2025. The clawback policy covers erroneously awarded incentive compensation after accounting restatements.

The weakness is ownership. Local Yahoo Finance holders data shows insiders own only about **0.36%** of shares. This is normal for a mature public industrial, but it is not owner-operator alignment.

Confidence note: confidence would improve by reviewing the detailed 2026 proxy compensation tables and all 2026 Form 4 transactions to separate routine compensation-related sales from discretionary selling.

### Right Place and Time — score 0.78, confidence 0.82

Hubbell is in a strong part of the industrial economy: electricity demand, grid modernization, utility reliability, transmission/substation buildout, electrification, and datacentre interconnection. These are real multi-year pressures, not a one-quarter fad.

The reason this is not higher is that Hubbell is still tied to capital-project timing, regulation, utility budgets, construction cycles, raw materials, and tariff dynamics.

### Top Dog — score 0.62, confidence 0.62

Hubbell has leading positions in multiple utility/electrical niches and a broad brand portfolio, but the public evidence does not prove it is the unambiguous top dog across the whole opportunity. The 10-K explicitly describes broad competition across product categories, including larger competitors in some categories.

Confidence note: confidence would improve with category-by-category market-share data from investor day materials or independent industry research. Without that, the safe conclusion is “important scaled leader in niches,” not “dominant category king.”

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — score 0.68, confidence 0.72

This overlaps with the pricing-power view: the moat is real but mundane. Hubbell’s advantage is reliability, qualification, channels, product breadth, and operational execution. It is durable enough for a quality industrial thesis, but not obviously widening into a winner-takes-most flywheel.

Confidence note: confidence would improve with win-rate, retention, or market-share disclosure by category; current evidence is margin/ROIC/channel inferred.

### Durable Growth — score 0.72, confidence 0.84

Growth is solid, profitable, and cash-backed. First-half 2026 sales rose **13.3%** to **$3.229B**, and Q2 accelerated to **15%** reported / **10% organic** growth. Hubbell now guides to **16–18%** total 2026 sales growth, including **9–11% organic**, with adjusted EPS of $20.25–$20.55. The result strengthens the case that grid modernization, load growth and datacentre investment are translating into current orders rather than only thematic promise.

Confidence note: confidence would improve after FY2026 results clarify how much of growth is organic versus acquired and whether Grid Automation has recovered.

### Organic Growth over Acquisitive Growth — score 0.62, confidence 0.86

Hubbell has more organic momentum than the prior score captured: Q2 organic sales rose **10%**, including **18%** in Electrical Solutions. Acquisitions added another **5%**, and NSI will make M&A a larger share of reported growth from Q3 onward. This is still a hybrid—strong core growth plus premium-priced bolt-ons—not a purely organic compounder.

Confidence note: confidence would improve with segment-level organic/M&A bridge disclosure through the rest of FY2026.

### Management & Smart Backing — score 0.62, confidence 0.70

Management appears competent and operationally disciplined, but this is not founder-led, owner-operated, or backed by unusual strategic capital. Q2 earns credit for 10% organic growth, effective price/productivity action against inflation and tariffs, and a timely guidance raise. The remaining test is capital allocation: NSI's premium purchase price, higher leverage and full-price buybacks still need to translate into superior per-share FCF.

Confidence note: confidence would improve by reviewing a longer history of management capital-allocation decisions, acquisition ROIC, and insider trading patterns.

### Customer Devotion — score 0.42, confidence 0.58

Hubbell sells critical B2B infrastructure components. Customers may trust the products and stick with qualified suppliers, but this is professional reliability, not consumer love or obvious product fandom.

Confidence note: confidence would improve with customer retention/NPS/win-rate data, but this may be irreducible unless Hubbell starts disclosing those metrics.

### Freeroll Traits — poor setup

- **Downside support — 0.15:** no hard floor at the current multiple. A quality floor exists, but not a valuation floor.
- **Fundamental stability — 0.78:** stable, profitable, cash-generative business.
- **Mispricing — 0.25:** the market seems aware of the grid/electrification story.
- **Catalyst — 0.10:** no clear 1–4 year catalyst that forces re-rating; ongoing execution is not a freeroll catalyst.

Mispricing/catalyst confidence notes: confidence would improve with sell-side estimate-revision history and investor-day catalyst disclosures, but present evidence points to “known quality story,” not hidden special situation.

## Valuation

Yahoo Finance showed **$472.55** on 2 August 2026, market cap about **$25.0B**, and 52.83M shares outstanding in the Q2 filing. The lower price and higher post-Q2 owner-earnings anchor improve the setup, but the stock still commands a quality multiple.

For the **Wonderful and Fair** strategy, the primary model uses an owner-earnings/FCF anchor rather than headline EPS:

- Current owner-earnings proxy: **$18.00 FCF/share**, normalized between TTM FCF through June of roughly **$17.71/share** and FY2026 guidance-implied FCF of roughly **$18.36/share** (90% of adjusted net income at the guidance midpoint).
- Holding period: **5 years**.
- Aggregate FCF growth: **11%, 9%, 8%, 7%, 5%** fade, reflecting raised organic guidance, core grid/electrification demand and NSI/DMC contribution, but not assuming heroic synergy.
- Net buybacks: **0.5%/yr** share-count reduction, reduced because NSI deleveraging should consume more near-term cash.
- Dividend yield: **1.13%**.
- Exit multiple: **25x P/FCF**, below the current implied **26.25x** P/FCF.

The refreshed model produces an **8.75% annualised ROI** over five years (1.52x total return). Approximate annualised attribution is **7.98% business growth, 0.50% buyback lift, -0.97% multiple compression and 1.28% income**. My qualitative valuation-aware score rises to **0.72**: the lower price, higher current FCF anchor and stronger organic outlook improve prospective returns, balanced by substantially higher net debt and NSI integration risk.

EPS cross-check: at the **$20.40** midpoint of raised adjusted EPS guidance, the current price is about **23.2x** FY2026 adjusted EPS. A 20–22x range implies roughly **$408–$449**, while 24x implies about **$490**.

Interpretation: HUBB remains a high-quality grid/electrification compounder, but the stock is still priced for quality. It belongs on watch, not in “back up the truck” territory. A pullback toward **$400–$440**, or a credible FY2027+ FCF step-up from NSI/core demand, would make the risk/reward more interesting.

## Overall

HUBB is better than the old Rule Breaker-only analysis suggested, because the updated framework gives it the right home: **Wonderful and Fair**. The company is a durable, profitable, cash-generative industrial compounder with exposure to one of the more attractive physical infrastructure themes: the power bottleneck.

The updated verdict is improved but still valuation-aware: **good business at a less demanding, not obviously cheap, price**. Q2 converted the grid/electrification thesis into double-digit organic growth and higher guidance. The main counterweight is the roughly $5.0B net-debt load and the need to prove NSI's premium purchase price through per-share FCF.

**Action:** keep watching; prefer buying on a pullback or after a fundamental step-change in FY2027+ earnings/FCF expectations.

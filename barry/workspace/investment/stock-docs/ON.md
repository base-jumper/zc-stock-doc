# ON — ON Semiconductor Corporation

## Business Overview

onsemi supplies intelligent power and sensing semiconductors, with the business framed around automotive, industrial, and AI data-center power end-markets. Q2 2026 revenue was $1.604bn: Power Solutions Group (PSG) $829.0m, Analog & Mixed-Signal Group (AMG) $545.7m, and Intelligent Sensing Group (ISG) $228.8m. PSG is the largest segment and the clearest strategic engine: silicon carbide, power discretes/modules, and related power-management content for EVs, energy infrastructure, and data centers.

The recovery strengthened in Q2: revenue rose 9% year on year, GAAP gross margin reached 38.4%, non-GAAP EPS rose 40% to $0.74, and FCF quadrupled to $425m. PSG grew 19%, while AMG remained 2% below the prior year. Management expects AI data-center revenue to more than double in 2026. The strategic profile is also changing: onsemi agreed to acquire Synaptics in an all-stock merger expected to close around mid-2027, with Synaptics holders owning about 12% of the combined company. That can broaden onsemi into connected compute, but creates material integration, dilution, and capital-allocation risk.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.58 / 0.85

The earnings stream is real but cyclical. Revenue fell 15% in 2025 and GAAP net income collapsed to $121m after large impairment and restructuring charges, while FCF remained $1.419bn. Q2 2026 provides credible recovery evidence: revenue increased 9%, GAAP operating income rose 34% to $259m, non-GAAP EPS increased 40%, and FCF rose from $106m to $425m. Last-twelve-month FCF is now about $1.50bn.

Interpretation: cash backing and operating leverage are stronger than the old Q1 view suggested, but this is not yet a predictable earnings stream. The first half still included $371m of restructuring/impairment charges, including factory rationalisation and a 650-person workforce reduction. The score rises modestly because the recovery is now visible in both revenue and cash, not because cyclicality has disappeared.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.62 / 0.82

Yahoo-derived metrics show strong peak returns, then a trough: ROIC was ~21.1% in 2022, 20.2% in 2023, 13.4% in 2024 and 1.8% in 2025. Gross margin similarly fell from ~49% in 2022 to ~33% in 2025 before recovering to 38.5% in Q1 2026. This pattern fits a cyclical analog/power semiconductor business with good economics in normal demand, but not a consistently high-return franchise across the full cycle.

Interpretation: onsemi can earn attractive returns when utilisation and pricing are normal, but the 2025 trough shows meaningful operating leverage in the wrong direction. Q2's higher utilisation and 80bp gross-margin improvement are positive recovery evidence; the Synaptics transaction makes future returns harder to judge until purchase accounting, synergies, and combined-company cash generation are visible.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.60 / 0.75

The moat is moderate. onsemi has differentiated power and sensing products, long automotive qualification cycles, SiC/EliteSiC design wins, 900V EV collaborations, and AI data-center power-tree adoption. Those are real switching-cost and technical-qualification advantages. Against that, semiconductors remain competitive and cyclical, and the gross-margin fall from mid/high-40s to low/mid-30s during the downturn shows pricing/utilisation pressure.

Interpretation: this is a narrow-to-moderate technical moat, not a dominant pricing-power franchise. Q2 supports a small score increase because growth and margin expansion occurred together, though one distributor represented 14% of quarterly revenue. Customer economics, SiC share, and sustained gross margin above 40% would firm the moat read.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.70 / 0.80

The runway is improving. Automotive electrification, energy infrastructure, industrial automation, robotics, and AI data-center power create long-duration semiconductor-content growth. Q2 PSG revenue grew 19%, management now expects AI data-center revenue to more than double during 2026, and the NVIDIA MGX, Great Wall, GaNEXUS, and Rivian wins widen the addressable power tree. Synaptics would add connected-compute capability and new cross-selling paths, but the enlarged opportunity comes with merger execution risk.

Interpretation: there is real reinvestment room in differentiated power and compute, but current evidence still mixes secular growth with a cyclical recovery. Confidence is now higher because Q2 disclosed segment and end-market conversion; sustained growth after the recovery year and post-merger product revenue would resolve the remaining uncertainty.

### Capital Allocation — 0.60 / 0.85

Capital allocation is now mixed. onsemi repurchased $683m of stock in 1H26, including $335m in Q2 at an average $105.77, and still has $5.3bn authorised. But the proposed Synaptics merger will issue enough stock for Synaptics holders to own about 12% of the combined company, may restrict buybacks while pending, and introduces integration and inherited-debt obligations. The deal is expected to add higher-margin connected compute, but the return case is not yet proven.

Interpretation: aggressively retiring stock and then announcing a large all-stock acquisition at a much lower share price is an uncomfortable sequence. The acquisition may be strategically sound, but it changes the thesis from disciplined self-help to integration-dependent growth and warrants a lower score until combined-company FCF and per-share accretion are demonstrated.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.78 / 0.88

The balance sheet remains sound but is less pristine. At 3 July 2026, cash and short-term investments were about $3.86bn against $4.50bn gross long-term debt, including roughly $805m of 0% notes classified current. The company also had $1.5bn available under its revolver. Liquidity is ample, but the debt balance increased after issuing $1.5bn of 2031 convertible notes, and Synaptics' debt treatment could consume cash or create further dilution.

Interpretation: there is no solvency concern, and the 10-Q maturity/liquidity disclosures raise confidence. The modest score reduction reflects merger-related complexity and a less conservative capital structure, not near-term funding stress.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.72 / 0.65

onsemi is not a founder-led single-person thesis. CEO Hassane El-Khoury and CFO Thad Trent matter to the portfolio shift and cost restructuring, but the products, fabs, customer qualifications, and sales relationships are institutional assets. The business would likely be disrupted by poor execution, not broken by one person leaving.

Interpretation: moderate-low key-person risk. Confidence is capped because I did not review succession depth or board materials in detail; the next proxy statement would firm this up.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.62 / 0.60

The evidence is mixed/partial. Yahoo shows low governance risk overall and compensation risk of 1, which is positive, but insider ownership is only ~0.34%. Heavy buybacks reduce share count, but stock-based compensation was $144.3m in 2025, so dilution/comp design still needs proxy-level review. There is no obvious value-leaking control issue.

Interpretation: likely acceptable public-company incentives, but not owner-operator alignment. Confidence is low because this needs the 2026 proxy's pay metrics, holding requirements, realised pay, and insider transaction review.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Rule Breakers loses because onsemi is a mature cyclical semiconductor company rather than an emerging hypergrowth category winner; the industry opportunity is real, but durable-growth and customer-devotion evidence are not rule-breaker calibre. Cash Cow loses because onsemi does not pay a dividend and still needs technology/product reinvestment; cash generation is strong but the business is not a stable harvest. Freeroll loses because there is no hard valuation floor near the current price and no single time-bound catalyst that closes a mispricing within 1–4 years.

## Overall

onsemi remains a mixed **Wonderful and Fair** candidate. Q2 validates the recovery: revenue, margins, EPS, FCF, and Q3 guidance all improved, led by PSG and AI data-center demand. The price has also reset materially. The trade-off is that the proposed Synaptics acquisition introduces 12% ownership dilution, integration risk, and uncertain per-share accretion just as the standalone business is recovering.

The current view is more attractive than in July, but still not high-conviction. The stock remains a cyclical recovery/compounder hybrid whose outcome increasingly depends on disciplined merger execution.

## Thesis Connections

Possible FIT connection: electrification and power efficiency. onsemi's intelligent power, SiC, EV architecture, industrial energy, and AI data-center power exposure line up with long-running demand for more efficient power conversion and sensing. The risk is that this thesis is industry-wide; onsemi must prove it captures the economics rather than merely participating in a crowded cycle.

## Watch

- Q3 2026 revenue and gross margin versus guidance of $1.65bn–$1.75bn revenue and 40%–42% non-GAAP gross margin.
- Synaptics merger: regulatory/stockholder approvals, mid-2027 closing, integration plan, inherited debt, synergies, and per-share FCF accretion.
- Whether AI data-center revenue actually more than doubles in 2026 and PSG sustains growth without AMG deterioration.
- SiC and 900V EV design-win conversion into revenue, especially Geely/NIO and other OEM ramps.
- Manufacturing realignment: remaining charges, factory disposals, and whether gross-margin gains persist after the 650-person workforce reduction.
- 2026 proxy: executive pay metrics, ownership requirements, insider buying/selling, and SBC dilution.
- Buyback discipline: repurchase price versus intrinsic value, and whether the $6bn authorization is used counter-cyclically.

# Single Person Companies

## Thesis

AI adoption is allowing a single motivated person to do more than ever before. Over coming years, we should see a growing trend of one-person or very-small-team companies backed by powerful AI agents. These companies can use AI to handle coding, design, marketing, customer support, accounting, legal drafting, sales outreach, research, operations, and workflow automation.

The investable opportunity is less likely to be the single-person companies themselves, because most will be private, small, or fragmented. The better angle is pick-and-shovel exposure: companies providing the software, payments, compliance, infrastructure, distribution, and automation rails these tiny but highly productive businesses rely on.

## Initial Probability

~80% over a 5–7 year horizon.

This is currently the highest-probability FIT. It has fewer regulatory and physical-world dependencies than autonomous vehicles, and early signs are already visible. The key uncertainty is not whether solo operators become more capable; it is which public companies capture the value.

## Why This Could Happen

- AI reduces the need to hire specialists for many early-stage functions.
- Software, content, customer support, analytics, and admin work can increasingly be automated or semi-automated.
- Distribution through online platforms allows tiny teams to reach global markets.
- Cloud tools and APIs make it easier to assemble a business from modular services.
- AI agents may lower the cognitive load of running a company: reminders, bookkeeping, tax prep, customer follow-up, documentation, procurement, reporting, and project management.
- High-agency individuals can compound their leverage dramatically.

## Key Conditions / Enablers

- AI tools become reliable enough for business-critical workflows.
- Agentic software can operate across multiple business systems with permission controls.
- Payments, invoicing, payroll, tax, and compliance tools become more automated.
- No-code/low-code and AI coding tools keep improving.
- Distribution remains accessible through marketplaces, search, social, app stores, and ecommerce channels.
- Trust, identity, cybersecurity, and fraud prevention adapt to tiny AI-enabled firms.

## Broad Winners

- Accounting, tax, payroll, and compliance software.
- Payments and merchant services.
- Ecommerce and marketplace infrastructure.
- CRM, sales automation, marketing automation, and customer-support tooling.
- AI platforms, model providers, and agent orchestration tools.
- Cloud infrastructure and developer tooling.
- Cybersecurity, identity, and fraud prevention.
- Legal/document automation.
- Banking and financial services tailored to micro-businesses.

## Specific Winners To Investigate

### Intuit

Market leader in accounting and tax software. Strong fit if more people run small businesses and need bookkeeping, tax, invoicing, payroll, and financial admin. Intuit also has a large data advantage and can embed AI into workflows.

Things to watch:
- QuickBooks customer growth.
- ARPU expansion from AI features.
- Adoption among freelancers, sole proprietors, and micro-businesses.
- Attach rates for payments, payroll, lending, and tax.
- Whether AI reduces churn or allows Intuit to automate more accounting work.

### Xero

Strong accounting software competitor, particularly relevant in Australia/NZ/UK and other international SMB markets. Could benefit from the same micro-business formation trend.

Things to watch:
- Subscriber growth.
- ARPU and margins.
- AI feature adoption.
- Payments/payroll ecosystem development.

### Reckon

Smaller accounting/payroll software provider. Potentially more leveraged if it can capture cost-conscious micro-businesses, though competitive pressure from Intuit/Xero/MYOB is significant. Also has exposure to software for large legal firms, which may be a separate AI/legal-tech angle.

Things to watch:
- SMB customer growth.
- Product modernisation and AI features.
- Churn versus larger competitors.
- Legal software segment growth.

### Shopify

A major picks-and-shovels beneficiary if AI enables more solo ecommerce brands. Shopify provides storefronts, payments, fulfilment integrations, marketing tools, and app ecosystem leverage.

Things to watch:
- Merchant count.
- GMV from small merchants.
- Shopify Payments penetration.
- AI tools for store creation, product descriptions, customer service, inventory, and marketing.

### Block / Square

Relevant for tiny merchants, creators, and services businesses that need payments, banking, payroll, invoices, and point-of-sale.

Things to watch:
- Seller GPV.
- Banking/payroll attach rates.
- Micro-merchant growth.
- AI-enabled business admin features.

### HubSpot / Salesforce / Atlassian / Microsoft

These are broader software platforms that may benefit if tiny companies adopt professional-grade CRM, support, productivity, and workflow tools earlier in their lifecycle.

Things to watch:
- SMB customer growth.
- AI-agent features.
- Seat expansion versus risk that one-person firms need fewer seats.
- Usage-based monetisation replacing seat-based SaaS where appropriate.

## What To Monitor

- Growth in non-employer businesses and sole proprietorships.
- Number of solo founders reaching meaningful revenue milestones, e.g. $1m+ ARR with tiny teams.
- Revenue per employee outliers.
- New AI-agent products targeted at freelancers, creators, consultants, and micro-businesses.
- Intuit, Xero, Shopify, Block, HubSpot, Microsoft, Atlassian SMB metrics.
- Small-business formation data.
- AI adoption rates among SMBs.
- Search/social chatter around “one-person company”, “AI solopreneur”, and “agentic business”.
- Changes in SaaS pricing models from seat-based to usage/outcome-based.
- Demand for accounting, tax, legal, insurance, and compliance automation.

## Other Investment Angles

- **Payments:** Stripe if public, Block, Adyen, PayPal.
- **Commerce rails:** Shopify, Amazon marketplace, MercadoLibre, Etsy.
- **Creator/business tooling:** Canva if public, Adobe, HubSpot, Notion if public.
- **Developer tooling:** Microsoft/GitHub, Atlassian, cloud providers.
- **Legal/compliance automation:** legal-tech platforms and document automation providers.
- **Cybersecurity/identity:** small businesses using AI agents will need simple security and permissioning.
- **Banking/lending:** lenders with data access into micro-business cash flows.

## Risks / Bear Case

- AI platforms capture most of the economics directly, leaving SMB software providers with limited incremental value.
- Many one-person companies remain too small or too fragmented to drive public-market revenue meaningfully.
- AI reduces the need for some SaaS categories rather than increasing demand.
- Seat-based SaaS suffers if companies use fewer employees and fewer seats.
- Customer acquisition for solo businesses remains brutally competitive despite AI productivity.
- Regulatory/tax/compliance complexity increases, offsetting some productivity gains.
- Platform dependency risk: small businesses may become more dependent on marketplaces and AI platforms that squeeze margins.

## Investment Framing

The cleanest way to think about this thesis is: AI increases the number and productivity of micro-businesses, but the investable winners are the infrastructure layers that every micro-business touches.

Most attractive winners should have:

- Strong existing SMB distribution.
- High trust around money, tax, compliance, or core operations.
- Ability to embed AI deeply into workflows.
- Pricing power or attach-rate expansion.
- Low churn and high switching costs.
- Exposure to usage/payment volume rather than only seat count.

## Reckon Deep Dive Notes — 2026-05-29

### Current View

Reckon is cheap and potentially interesting, but not yet a clean high-conviction compounder. It is better framed as a small-cap value/special situation with an AI-modernisation option attached.

The valuation is undemanding: around AUD 46m market cap, AUD 54m enterprise value, ~5.8x forward PE, ~0.7x sales, ~2.3x EV/FCF using current data. The headline cheapness is real, but the market is likely discounting several issues: legacy product dependence, heavy capitalised development, small scale against Intuit/Xero/MYOB, limited liquidity, and execution risk around migration to Reckon One.

### What Looks Good

- Business Group is profitable and cash generative.
- FY2025 revenue grew 15%; 4% excluding Cashflow Manager.
- FY2025 NPAT grew 94%; 48% excluding Cashflow Manager.
- Reckon One revenue grew 27%, suggesting the cloud migration is not dead.
- Subscription revenue is very high: about 95% of divisional revenue in both Business and Legal.
- Cashflow Manager acquisition appears immediately accretive: AUD 6.1m revenue, AUD 3.3m EBITDA, AUD 1.6m NPAT in FY2025.
- Legal Group has a potentially valuable wedge into large law firms: works with 12 of the top 20 global firms and 40% of AmLaw 200.
- Legal Billing Workflows revenue more than doubled, from a small base.
- Insider ownership is meaningful, around 11%.
- AGM support looks strong: director re-election and remuneration report both passed with ~99% support.

### Skeletons / Risks

- A significant proportion of Business Group revenue/EBITDA still comes from legacy Reckon Accounts and Reckon Accounts Hosted products.
- Those legacy products use older Intuit code-based products and may not integrate easily with future third-party services or regulatory requirements.
- The core strategic task is migrating customers to Reckon One without losing them to Xero, Intuit, or MYOB.
- Heavy capitalised development is a major accounting judgement. FY2025 capitalised development was ~AUD 15.4m, similar to the company’s reported amortisation charge. Development assets are ~AUD 29.7m, large relative to market cap and book equity.
- Reported FCF looks extremely high because operating cash flow is before capitalised development in some headline sources. Economic free cash flow should subtract capitalised development. FY2025 operating cash flow was AUD 23.8m, but after AUD 15.1m capitalised development spend, management says operating cash flow after development spend was AUD 8.7m.
- Working capital is negative and cash on hand is low, although debt appears manageable.
- Net debt increased after the Cashflow Manager acquisition.
- Legal Group is still loss-making before tax at segment level despite EBITDA improvement.
- Competition is fierce: Xero, Intuit, MYOB and others have much larger product, brand, marketing and AI budgets.
- Product perception may matter. Reckon’s cheap/value positioning is useful, but if customers view the product as dated, AI may not be enough.
- There was a 2026 ASX query regarding a late director interest notice. It does not look thesis-breaking, but it is a small governance yellow flag.
- Liquidity is thin; position sizing matters.

### AI Modernisation Angle

AI could genuinely help Reckon if management uses it to accelerate Reckon One development, migrate legacy customers, modernise code, improve support, automate compliance workflows, and add practical features for small businesses.

But AI is not automatically bullish. Larger competitors can also use AI, and likely have more data, engineering resources, and distribution. Reckon’s opportunity is to use AI asymmetrically: move faster with a small team, focus on Australian compliance/payroll/tax niches, and improve product quality without exploding R&D spend.

### What Would Increase Conviction

- Reckon One revenue growth remains >20% while legacy revenue decline is controlled.
- Total Business Group organic revenue growth accelerates beyond low-single digits.
- Capitalised development as a percentage of revenue falls over time while product velocity improves.
- Evidence that AI materially reduces development/support cost or improves customer acquisition/conversion.
- Clear disclosure of Reckon One users, ARPU, churn, and migration rates.
- Legal Billing Workflows keeps doubling or becomes material enough to offset Document Workflows maturity.
- Net debt falls while dividends are maintained.
- Management provides concrete AI product roadmap, not generic language.

### Initial Verdict

Worth deeper watchlist work, but not yet an obvious buy purely from the PE. The low PE is partly explained by legacy/product transition risk and accounting quality concerns around capitalised development. The upside case is credible if Reckon One migration works and AI improves development leverage, but conviction should remain moderate until we see more evidence.

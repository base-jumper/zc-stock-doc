# CSL.AX — CSL Limited

## Business Overview

CSL is a global biopharma company headquartered in Australia. It operates through **CSL Behring** (plasma-derived and recombinant therapies for immunoglobulin, albumin, haemophilia, hereditary angioedema and perioperative bleeding), **CSL Seqirus** (influenza vaccines and pandemic preparedness), and **CSL Vifor** (iron deficiency and nephrology). The company reports in USD but trades on the ASX in AUD.

FY2025 revenue was about **US$15.6b** and dividends were **US$2.92/share**. The FY2025 annual report frames CSL as one of the world’s largest human-plasma collectors, supplying medicines in **100+ countries** with **29,000+ employees**. The live story is not clean: CSL has a high-quality plasma/vaccines base, but the Vifor acquisition increased leverage and complexity, and management is now simplifying the portfolio, reducing R&D sites, integrating Behring/Vifor commercial functions, pursuing cost savings, and proposing a Seqirus demerger.

At 1H26, CSL reported **US$8.33b revenue (-4% constant currency)**, **US$1.95b NPATA (-7%)**, **US$401m reported NPAT (-81%)** after restructuring/impairments, and **US$1.30b operating cash flow (+3%)**. Behring revenue fell **7%** in the half, Seqirus fell **2%**, while Vifor grew **12%**. On 11 May, however, the interim CEO cut FY26 guidance to about **US$15.2b revenue** and **US$3.1b NPATA** at constant currency and flagged about **US$5b** of additional non-cash pre-tax impairments across FY26–FY27. The causes included US Ig channel-inventory normalisation (~US$300m revenue impact), weaker China albumin market value (~US$200m), and a combined ~US$150m from Middle East disruption, slower HEMGENIX growth and iron competition.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.68 / confidence 0.82

CSL has a real and long earnings record, but the current period is messy. Yahoo-derived annual statements show no loss years from FY2022–FY2025: net income moved from **US$2.25b** to **US$3.00b**, revenue rose from **US$10.49b** to **US$15.43b**, and diluted EPS reached **US$6.17** in FY2025. The local earnings-quality script shows average net-income growth of about **10.5%** over the available four-year window, with moderate dispersion.

The caveat is cash backing and adjustments. Cumulative FCF/net income over FY2022–FY2025 was only **0.63x**, although FY2025 improved to **0.85x**. 1H26 also had very large restructuring/impairment charges: underlying NPATA was **US$1.95b**, but reported NPAT was only **US$401m**. The May guidance cut and planned ~US$5b additional impairment show that both the near-term earnings path and the acquired-asset carrying values were less dependable than the prior view allowed. Underlying earning power remains real, but predictability and accounting cleanliness deserve a larger discount.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.55 / confidence 0.75

Returns are decent, not exceptional. The local returns script shows ROIC of **10.4%, 9.8%, 10.9% and 11.9%** from FY2022–FY2025, with ROE around **14–16%**. Incremental ROIC was better at roughly **17%**, but goodwill from the Vifor deal is material: goodwill was about **28–31% of invested capital** after the acquisition.

For a healthcare operating company, this is value-creating but not an elite compounder profile. It sits below the usual 20%+ “wonderful” anchor, and the acquisition-heavy capital base muddies the read. The score would improve if CSL demonstrates post-transformation margin expansion and high incremental returns without further large M&A.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.75 / confidence 0.70

CSL has meaningful moat characteristics: specialised biologics manufacturing, plasma collection scale, regulatory and quality barriers, clinical trust, and leadership in immunoglobulin. FY2025 gross margin was around **51.5%** in the Yahoo-derived metrics, and 1H26 underlying gross margin was roughly **55.6%** in the company presentation. Behring also describes strong Ig market fundamentals: significant unmet medical need, mid-to-high single-digit growth, robust core-indication growth, and balanced demand/supply.

The mark-down is pressure from policy and competition. 1H26 Behring was hit by Medicare Part D and China policy changes; albumin fell **27%**, and iron in Vifor fell **15%** due to generic competition. The moat is real, but not immune to reimbursement, government policy, generic substitution and product-cycle pressure. Confidence would improve with a deeper product-by-product market-share and price/volume bridge.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.58 / confidence 0.75

CSL still has a runway, but it is a mature-business runway rather than a hyper-compounder runway. Ig demand, albumin expansion in China, HAE launches, HEMGENIX uptake, Vifor nephrology launches, Rika/iNomi donor-centre technology, and manufacturing yield improvements all give CSL places to reinvest. Management is also reallocating plasma collections to more efficient centres and reinvesting into US and China commercialisation.

The issue is that recent growth has decelerated and some of the runway now depends on fixing prior complexity rather than simply funding high-return organic expansion. FY2025 revenue growth was **5%**, 1H26 revenue was down **4% constant currency**, and May guidance implies FY26 revenue below FY2025. Yahoo consensus on 28 July modelled about **-1.2% FY26 revenue**, **-5.2% EPS**, and only **~1.3%** revenue/EPS growth in FY27. This is a moderate runway whose financial payoff has again moved right, not a pristine long-duration compounding engine.

### Capital Allocation — 0.35 / confidence 0.82

This is the weak spot. CSL historically reinvested well in plasma capacity and R&D, but the **Vifor acquisition** left a heavy acquisition fingerprint: the organic-growth script shows **US$10.5b** cumulative acquisition spend in FY2023, goodwill jumping from about **US$1.2b** to **US$8.1b**, and diluted share count rising about **3.6%** over the available window. The May review then flagged about **US$5b** of additional pre-tax impairments, including Vifor intangibles/product portfolio and under-utilised property, plant and equipment. That materially strengthens the evidence that prior investment decisions destroyed value.

There are positives: debt is being managed, the dividend is covered, and buying back stock around depressed valuation levels is more rational than buying at CSL’s former premium multiple. But the Vifor deal and current restructuring/impairments are now a major stewardship failure, not merely a blemish. Next update should check actual buyback execution price, Seqirus demerger terms, and the final audited impairment allocation.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.72 / confidence 0.75

The balance sheet is acceptable for a stable healthcare company, but no longer pristine. The debt script shows FY2025 net debt/EBITDA of **1.5x**, peak available leverage of **2.3x** in FY2023, EBIT interest coverage of **11.6x**, and near-term debt covered **5.8x** by cash plus a year of FCF. Management’s 1H26 presentation cites leverage of **2.0x** net debt/EBITDA and “strong balance sheet and cash flows.”

That is resilient, not aggressive. The mark-down is acquisition-related leverage and the lack of detailed maturity/fixed-floating information in the quick data pull. There is no solvency disqualifier.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.80 / confidence 0.65

CSL’s advantage is institutional rather than dependent on one person: plasma collection infrastructure, regulated manufacturing, quality systems, clinical relationships, product portfolios and global scale. CEO turnover/interim leadership matters less than it would at a founder-led software company.

The confidence is moderate because CSL is in a strategy-reset period. Execution of the transformation, Seqirus demerger and Behring/Vifor integration depends on management depth, not just static assets. Still, the business would not break if one individual left.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.45 / confidence 0.45

I did not deep-dive CSL’s remuneration report in this run, so this is deliberately low confidence. Yahoo’s governance fields flag a high compensation-risk score, and the recent history — a large acquisition followed by restructuring/impairments — makes incentive quality worth testing rather than assuming. Offsetting that, the expanded buyback at lower prices and cost-simplification program are owner-friendly on the surface.

This needs a proper remuneration review: LTIP metrics, return-on-capital hurdles, per-share value measures, executive ownership, holding requirements, and whether targets reward revenue/size versus owner returns.

**Why other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair is close on franchise quality, but CSL’s current ROIC, Vifor capital-allocation failure and weak near-term growth make it less clean than a true high-return compounder. Cash-cow fits better: mature, profitable, dividend-paying, cash-generative, and cheap enough that the return case leans on normalized earnings, income and modest multiple recovery rather than exceptional reinvestment. Freeroll has a catalyst package — restructuring, buyback and Seqirus demerger — but downside support is not hard enough: book value and yield do not create a true floor. Rule-breakers fails on growth dynamism.

## Overall

CSL is a high-quality but seriously wounded healthcare franchise. The strong parts are durable demand in plasma/rare diseases, scale/regulatory barriers, good margins, manageable debt, and a valuation that has compressed dramatically. The weak parts are now clearer: a major Vifor capital-allocation failure, modest ROIC including goodwill, another guidance reset, low near-term growth, and evidence that inventory, policy and competition can bite even in specialist medicine.

At **A$119.52/share**, the stock no longer needs heroic assumptions to work, but it does require earnings to stabilise after repeated resets. My base case is roughly 4% aggregate earnings growth plus dividends and modest buybacks, bought at a depressed multiple. That is attractive enough to keep watching, but the expected-return case is less robust until FY26 results prove the rebased earnings and impairment estimates.

## Thesis Connections

CSL connects to durable healthcare demand: ageing populations, chronic immune deficiency treatment, rare-disease biologics, plasma supply infrastructure, and vaccine/pandemic preparedness. It is less a frontier biotech bet than a global healthcare infrastructure compounder trying to regain operational sharpness.

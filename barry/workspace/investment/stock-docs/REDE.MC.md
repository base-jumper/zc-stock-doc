# REDE.MC — Redeia Corporación, S.A.

## Business Overview

Redeia is the Spanish regulated electricity-transmission group behind Red Eléctrica, with additional telecom/satellite exposure historically in the group. Yahoo’s live market data uses **RED.MC** rather than the queued symbol **REDE.MC**; this doc keeps the queued filename/ticker while citing RED.MC data for prices and financials.

The core thesis is regulated grid infrastructure: high visibility, monopoly-like network assets, and a business tied to electrification and grid reinforcement rather than discretionary demand. The trade-off is equally clear: the company is capital intensive, levered like a utility, and currently reinvesting more cash than it generates after capex, so the dividend is an earnings-yield story rather than a clean free-cash-flow yield.

As of 2026-07-19, RED.MC traded at **€15.59**, market cap about **€8.42bn**, trailing P/E about **16.6x**, forward P/E about **16.2x**, price/book about **1.61x**, dividend yield about **5.13%**, and EV/EBITDA about **12.8x**. H1 results are due on 2026-07-29, making that the next material checkpoint.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.58 / confidence 0.84

Redeia has remained profitable in each Yahoo-covered annual period from 2022–2025, with net income of about €665m, €690m, €368m and €506m. The issue is not losses; it is predictability and cash backing. Net income fell sharply in 2024 before recovering in 2025, and cumulative free cash flow over the four-year window was negative versus cumulative reported profit, because grid investment consumed operating cash. Accounting earnings therefore look real enough for a regulated utility, but not cleanly distributable owner earnings during this capex cycle.

The FY2025 primary materials confirm €506m net profit, €1,013m operating cash flow and €1,626m investment. The remaining uncertainty is how much of capex is growth versus economically sustaining expenditure; that distinction matters for owner earnings.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.48 / confidence 0.84

ROIC/ROCE are utility-like, not compounder-like. Yahoo-derived ROIC was roughly **5.6% in 2025**, **5.5% in 2024**, and about **7.0% in 2022–2023**; ROCE sat around **6–8%**. Those returns can be acceptable for regulated infrastructure if matched to a low WACC and predictable allowed returns, but they are not high-return compounder economics. ROE near 10% is partly supported by leverage, so I give more weight to ROIC/ROCE.

The 2026–2031 framework now sets a **6.58% financial remuneration rate**, 100bp above the prior period, while management targets roughly **9% ROE**. That supports a small score lift, but confirms capped utility returns rather than compounder economics.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.78 / confidence 0.84

The moat is structural rather than brand-led: Redeia operates critical electricity transmission infrastructure under regulation. A rival cannot practically replicate the grid, and the assets are essential as Spain electrifies and adds renewables. Margins are high and stable in the Yahoo data: operating margin was about **45% in 2025**, **44% in 2024**, and **49% in 2023**.

The limitation is regulatory: Redeia does not have unconstrained pricing power. Returns depend on CNMC-regulated remuneration, not unilateral price increases. That makes the moat durable but capped.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.64 / confidence 0.88

The 2026–2029 plan makes the runway concrete: **€6.5bn** group investment, including **€6.0bn** for the Spanish TSO, with annual TSO investment averaging €1.5bn. Management expects transmission RAB to grow from **€8.9bn in 2025 to €11.4bn in 2029** (6.4% CAGR), with RAB plus work in progress reaching €14.4bn. More than 70% of supplies are said to be secured through 2029, reducing—but not eliminating—execution risk. The runway is large and unusually visible; the limitation remains the regulated return.

### Capital Allocation — 0.52 / confidence 0.82

Management is prioritising regulated grid investment and dividends. The plan funds €6.5bn of investment without equity issuance, using FFO, subsidies, hybrids and higher debt, while growing the dividend 2% annually from **€0.80 to €0.87/share by 2029**. That is coherent and avoids dilution, but distributions remain aggressive against post-capex cash: 2025 dividends were €461m while investment was €1,626m. The dividend therefore leans on regulated earnings, external funding and future RAB monetisation rather than current surplus FCF.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.50 / confidence 0.88

FY2025 net debt was **€5.47bn**, or **4.4x EBITDA**, with 92% fixed-rate debt and €3.3bn liquidity. The plan deliberately lifts net debt to roughly **€7.8bn by 2029**, while targeting net debt/EBITDA below **5.5x**, FFO/net debt above 14%, and an A- credit rating. The maturity profile is manageable through 2029 but becomes concentrated from 2030 (€3.18bn shown for 2030 and later), and management expects gross debt cost to rise from 2.24% to 3.3%. This is financeable regulated-utility leverage, but no longer deserves an above-midpoint conservatism score.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / confidence 0.74

The thesis is institutional and asset-based, not dependent on one charismatic founder or star operator. Regulated grid ownership, technical systems, and public-market governance matter more than any single executive. Leadership quality still matters for capital allocation and regulatory execution, but the business would not break if one person left.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 / confidence 0.48

I have not yet reviewed the latest remuneration report, so this is deliberately mid-confidence. The public-company governance structure and state-linked strategic importance reduce the odds of extreme founder-style self-dealing, but Yahoo governance flags shareholder-rights risk at the high end and compensation risk mid-high. This needs direct proxy/remuneration evidence before treating incentives as a strength.

### Why the other strategies lost

**Cash cow** partially fits the mature, regulated, dividend-paying profile, but the defining trait — genuinely free cash after sustaining the asset base — is weak during this investment cycle. Free cash flow was negative in each Yahoo-covered annual period after heavy capex.

**Freeroll** has some floor-like qualities from regulated assets, book value, and yield, but there is no strong time-bound catalyst. At €14.96 the stock is around **1.5x book** and the dividend floor is not covered by post-capex FCF, so the asymmetry setup is not clean.

**Rule-breakers** is not a fit: this is mature regulated infrastructure, not an emerging high-growth category leader with winner-takes-most economics.

## Overall

Redeia is a durable regulated-infrastructure business with a visible dividend and now a well-specified electrification investment runway. The 6.58% allowed return and 6.4% planned RAB CAGR improve visibility, but the economics remain capped: returns on capital are modest, leverage is set to rise materially, and current free cash flow is poor because capex is heavy. This looks more like a fair regulated utility than a high-quality compounder.

The stock is worth watching mainly for income plus modest growth, not explosive upside. The key question has shifted from whether the runway exists to whether Redeia can commission projects on schedule, convert work in progress into remunerated RAB, and keep leverage inside its own thresholds.

# GOOG — Alphabet Inc.

## Business Overview

Alphabet is the holding company behind Google. The economic engine is still Google Services: Search and other ads, YouTube ads/subscriptions, Android/Chrome/Google Play, devices, Gmail, Maps, Photos and Drive. Google Cloud is the second major segment and is now material; Other Bets contains longer-duration bets such as Waymo.

The core model is scale-driven digital advertising and subscriptions, with Cloud and AI infrastructure increasingly important. Q2 2026 revenue was **$119.8b**, up **24%**, comprising **$94.5b** of Google Services revenue and **$24.8b** of Google Cloud revenue. The latest Yahoo quote was **$318.99/share**, implying a market cap of about **$3.90t**. Reported earnings are not a clean operating anchor: Q2's **$9.11 diluted EPS** included **$6.26/share** from a **$99.0b equity-security gain**, primarily unrealized gains on SpaceX and another private company.

No universal disqualifier found in the sources reviewed. The live risks are antitrust/regulatory remedies, AI disruption to search behaviour, and very heavy AI/data-centre capex — not fraud, insolvency, hidden-liability or value-leaking-control evidence.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.86 / confidence 0.90

Alphabet has a long record of real profitability. Yahoo annual statements show revenue rising from **$282.8b in 2022** to **$402.8b in 2025**, while net income rose from **$60.0b** to **$132.2b**. The earnings-quality script shows no loss years in the four-year Yahoo history, average net-income growth around **30%**, and receivables staying broadly stable at **14–16% of revenue**.

Cash conversion is no longer pristine because AI infrastructure capex is enormous. Q2 operating cash flow rose to **$39.1b**, but capex doubled to **$44.9b**, producing **negative $5.9b** of quarterly FCF; TTM FCF fell to **$53.3b**. Management raised 2026 capex guidance again, to **$195–205b**, and still expects a significant increase in 2027. I still score earnings highly because operating profit is recurring and scaled, but reported earnings contain a huge investment gain and owner earnings are difficult to normalize during this buildout.

### Returns on Capital — 0.90 / confidence 0.85

Returns are exceptional for a company at this scale. The returns script shows ROIC of **22.4% / 25.1% / 29.9% / 28.7%** across 2022–2025, average ROIC around **26.5%**, rising ROIC trend, ROE around **32%** in 2025, and an estimated incremental ROIC of **37.6%**. Goodwill is modest relative to capital, so the return profile is not mainly acquired accounting optics.

The main caveat is that incremental returns on the current AI capex wave are not yet fully observable. If AI capex becomes defensive spend just to preserve Search, this score should fall; if it extends Search, Gemini, YouTube and Cloud advantages, it is justified.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.91 / confidence 0.90

Alphabet has multiple structural moat sources: global brand/default behaviour in Search, massive advertiser demand/liquidity, YouTube's creator/viewer network, Android/Chrome distribution, data and infrastructure scale, and switching costs in Cloud once customers commit workloads. In Q2, consolidated operating income increased **30%** to **$40.8b** and operating margin expanded two points to **34%**, supporting the view that the moat is not being competed away.

Q2 strengthened the evidence: Search revenue grew **17%** to **$63.3b**, YouTube ads grew **13%**, and management said popular AI features were driving query growth. Gemini models processed 22b API tokens per minute and the Gemini app reached 950m monthly active users. The threat remains real—AI answer engines could change search monetisation, regulators may attack default-placement economics, and Cloud competes against AWS/Microsoft—but current operating evidence says AI is expanding engagement rather than cannibalising Search.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.94 / confidence 0.90

The runway is still long despite Alphabet's maturity. Q2 Cloud revenue accelerated **82%** to **$24.8b**, operating income rose from **$2.8b to $8.8b** (about a **35.6% margin**), and Cloud backlog reached **$513.9b**, with just over half of total company backlog expected to convert within 24 months. The quarter included initial TPU-system revenue, with the significant majority of revenue from the signed arrangements expected in 2027. These signals materially strengthen the case that AI infrastructure demand is real, though supply-constrained.

This remains the central underwriting question. Management now expects **$195–205b** of 2026 capex and a further significant increase in 2027. Demand, backlog and Cloud margin expansion support the build, but the useful life and consolidated returns of this infrastructure remain unproven. Confidence would improve when consolidated FCF recovers after capacity catches up.

### Capital Allocation — 0.47 / confidence 0.95

Historic allocation was strong: cumulative 2022–2025 FCF was roughly **$276b**, buybacks **$229b**, and diluted shares fell about **7.1%**. That record has changed materially. Alphabet completed the **$29.5b Wiz** and **$5.9b Intersect** acquisitions in March, issued roughly **$35b** of common and mandatory-convertible preferred securities plus **$10b** of common to Berkshire Hathaway in June, and established a **$40b ATM** program to help fund the AI buildout. The preferred carries a **6.25%** dividend until mandatory conversion in 2029.

The raise preserves balance-sheet capacity and was done near a high valuation, but it reverses the net-buyback thesis and introduces issuance, preferred-dividend and execution risk. Q2 confirmed **zero buybacks**, common shares outstanding rose about **1.2%** from year-end, and no ATM shares had yet been sold at June 30. Alphabet then issued another **$25.0b** of senior notes on 10 August, across 2028–2066 maturities with fixed coupons of **4.50–6.50%** plus two floating-rate tranches. This extends the external-funding wave and adds roughly **$1.4b** of annual pre-tax interest at current floating rates. I therefore assume modest ongoing dilution rather than a buyback benefit until the funding plan and post-build FCF are clearer.

### Conservative Debt — 0.86 / confidence 0.95

Financial strength remains excellent but less pristine. At June 30 Alphabet held **$242.5b** of cash and marketable securities against **$98.2b** of long-term debt, after raising **$49.6b** of common/preferred equity and issuing **$20.3b** of senior notes in Q2. The 10 August issuance adds **$25.0b** of debt, taking pro-forma long-term debt to roughly **$123b** before uses of proceeds. Alphabet remains in a very large net-cash position and the maturities are well laddered, so debt does not threaten resilience; the lower score reflects the speed and cost of funding a capital-light business's unprecedented infrastructure build.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.80 / confidence 0.65

Alphabet is institutionally deep rather than dependent on one founder-operator. Sundar Pichai matters, and the founders' super-voting Class B control remains a governance feature, but the business is diversified across major products, engineering organisations and distribution surfaces. The company would not break if one executive left.

Confidence is moderate because this is a qualitative governance/organisation judgement; better evidence would come from succession planning disclosure and deeper management-bench review.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.65 / confidence 0.85

Alignment is mixed-positive. The 2026 proxy confirms Larry Page and Sergey Brin retained **52.7% of total voting power** at the April record date; all directors and named executives met ownership requirements. Executive awards combine time-based GSUs and performance units, but founder control sharply limits outside-shareholder influence, and 2025 stock-based compensation remained material at **$25.0b**. The large 2026 equity raise also weakens the recent per-share alignment from buybacks.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Cash Cow also scores well, but Alphabet still has a credible high-return reinvestment runway in AI, Cloud and Waymo rather than being mainly a mature distribution vehicle. Rule Breakers is conceptually too late-stage: Alphabet is a mega-cap compounder, not an emerging disruptor, even though it owns rule-breaker-like projects. Freeroll fails the setup: there is no hard floor near the current price and no time-bound re-rating catalyst.

## Overall

Alphabet is a high-quality compounder at a not-crazy but not-cheap price. The business scores strongly on earnings quality, returns on capital, moat, runway and balance-sheet resilience. The main detractors are valuation, regulatory/antitrust uncertainty, and the possibility that AI capex is partly defensive rather than a high-return growth investment.

At **~21.6x normalized current EPS** and a 5-year forward ROI around **12%** under my base case, this is attractive enough to watch and potentially own, but not a screaming bargain. Q2 materially improved the evidence for Cloud monetisation and Search resilience; the funding plan and negative quarterly FCF increased per-share and execution risk. The thesis needs AI/Search durability and eventual FCF recovery—if either breaks, the growth and multiple assumptions compress together.

- **Dilution:** **0.5%** annual share-count growth, reflecting the June issuance, preferred conversion risk and zero Q2 buybacks, while treating the capital raise as partly one-off
- **Dividend yield:** **0.28%**, based on the $0.88 annualized common dividend and current price
- **Exit multiple:** **23x earnings**, above the current normalized multiple but below the prior assumption; quality warrants a premium, while dilution and capital intensity cap the terminal multiple

The refreshed script result is recorded in front matter. Return is carried mainly by business growth, with modest multiple expansion and income partly offset by dilution. The new notes' roughly **$1.4b** annual pre-tax interest burden is only about **0.6%** of the normalized earnings base after tax, so it does not warrant changing the 11% growth input or the stored ROI; it does reduce the margin for error around the AI build. My valuation-aware agent score is **0.75**: Q2 substantially improved the operating evidence and the entry price fell, but unprecedented capex and repeated external funding make per-share returns less dependable.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a quality-compounder / AI-infrastructure thesis: durable consumer attention + advertiser demand + scaled cloud/AI infrastructure. It also partially connects to autonomous mobility via Waymo, but that remains option value rather than the core valuation anchor.

## Watch

- Search AI: query growth, ad load/pricing, and whether AI Overviews/AI Mode sustain or dilute monetisation.
- Antitrust: U.S./EU remedies affecting default search distribution, ad-tech structure, Play/Android economics, or data access.
- AI capex returns: 2026 capex against the **$195–205b** guide, 2027 guidance, FCF conversion, and evidence that backlog converts at attractive margins.
- Cloud: revenue growth, operating margin progression, and share versus AWS/Azure.
- Capital allocation: utilisation of the **$40b ATM**, preferred dividends/conversion dilution, and whether buybacks resume after the buildout.
- TPU system sales: 2027 revenue conversion, economics, customer concentration and whether hardware mix changes Cloud margins.

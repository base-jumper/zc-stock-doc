# TRI — Thomson Reuters Corporation

## Business Overview

Thomson Reuters is a Canada-headquartered professional information, software and news business serving legal, corporate, tax, accounting, audit, compliance, government and media customers. The economic core is the “Big 3” professional segments — Legal Professionals, Corporates and Tax, Audit & Accounting Professionals — which use proprietary legal/tax/regulatory/news content, workflow software and AI tools such as Westlaw, Practical Law and CoCounsel. Reuters and Global Print are smaller legacy/adjacent segments.

This is a mature, high-margin, recurring-revenue information franchise, now turning trusted professional content into an AI workflow advantage. Q2 2026 revenue rose 9% to $1.95b, organic revenue rose 8%, Big 3 organic revenue rose 10%, adjusted EBITDA rose 10% to $745m and FCF rose 29% to $727m. Management raised FY2026 total and organic revenue growth guidance to about 8% and Big 3 guidance to 9.5-10.0%, while retaining about $2.1b of FCF and roughly 100bp of adjusted-EBITDA-margin expansion.

The portfolio is becoming cleaner. Thomson Reuters agreed to sell 51% of Global Print to KKR for about $500m of gross proceeds while retaining 49%, its intellectual-property rights and editorial control, plus an exclusive licence/royalty arrangement. Global Print will move to discontinued operations with Q3 results. The company also completed its $600m buyback, the $605m return-of-capital/share-consolidation transaction and repayment of $500m of notes.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.78 / confidence 0.86

TRI has cleanly positive, recurring earning power. Yahoo shows no loss years across 2022-25: net income was $1.338b, $2.695b, $2.210b and $1.502b, while FCF was $1.320b, $1.797b, $1.850b and $2.017b. Cumulative FCF/net income was about 0.9x, and receivables/revenue was stable at roughly 15%. Q2 adjusted EPS rose 14% to $0.99 and first-half adjusted EPS rose 11% to $2.22, while first-half FCF rose 26% to $1.06b. The accounting profit line remains lumpy because disposals, tax items and gains/losses affect IFRS EPS, so adjusted EPS and FCF are the cleaner operating read. Confidence would firm further with a 10-year adjusted EPS/FCF bridge and a deeper check on recurring adjusted exclusions.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.88 / confidence 0.90

This remains the strongest cash-cow trait. FCF was $2.017b in FY2025; Q2 FCF rose 29% to $727m and first-half FCF rose 26% to $1.06b, while FY2026 guidance remains about $2.1b. Across 2022-25, FCF margins were roughly 20%, 26%, 25% and 27%, while capex/operating cash flow was about 24% in FY2025 and below 31% in every available year. Capex is below D&A, but for a software/content-heavy business that is less alarming than it would be for a physical-asset operator. The retained Global Print royalty should further reduce physical intensity after closing. Confidence would improve with a growth-vs-maintenance capex split and longer FCF history.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.86 / confidence 0.88

The business is fundamentally durable. Q2 organic revenue grew 8%, Big 3 organic revenue accelerated to 10%, and recurring revenue grew 9%. Legal and Corporates both delivered 10% organic growth, with Westlaw and CoCounsel important contributors. The structurally declining piece, Global Print, fell 3% organically and is being moved into a KKR joint venture, which improves the continuing mix while preserving a royalty and content control. The main threat is whether AI shifts value away from incumbent information vendors faster than TRI can package trusted content into workflow tools; current CoCounsel-led growth argues that erosion has not begun. Confidence would improve with product-level retention and AI attach-rate data.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.80 / confidence 0.82

The moat is real: authoritative legal, tax, accounting and news content; deep workflow integration; switching costs; professional trust; and scale in domain-specific data. Q2 Big 3 organic growth reached 10%, recurring revenue grew 9%, and Big 3 adjusted-EBITDA margin expanded 40bp to 42.7%. Westlaw and CoCounsel drove Legal growth, and CoCounsel also contributed in Corporates and Tax/Audit. The live threat is that generative AI lowers information-search friction and invites new competitors, but current growth supports TRI's fiduciary-grade-AI pitch because professional users need verified, auditable answers. Confidence would firm up with retention/renewal, price-vs-volume and product-level share data.

### Capital Allocation — 0.72 / confidence 0.82

Capital allocation is mostly sensible but not flawless. TRI has raised the dividend for 33 consecutive years and lifted the annualized dividend 10% to $2.62 in 2026; the payout is covered by FCF. It completed the $600m buyback announced in February, the $605m return-of-capital/share-consolidation transaction, and repaid $500m of notes in May. The Global Print joint venture sharpens the business mix, brings about $500m of gross proceeds and retains a royalty plus 49% ownership, IP rights and editorial control.

The cautions are acquisition discipline and the KKR support arrangement. Cash acquisition spend totalled about $2.9b across 2022-25, goodwill is high and rising, and buybacks have historically occurred at richer valuations than the prior quote. TRI also agreed to financial support that gives KKR a minimum return in specified circumstances; the amount and probability need monitoring. Deal multiples/outcomes and a buyback-price-versus-intrinsic-value review would firm the score further.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.56 / confidence 0.78

This is the main quality limiter. Yahoo-derived ROIC was roughly 11.7-12.9% across 2022-25, with 2025 ROIC 11.9%, ROCE 14.6% and incremental ROIC about 10.5%. Those are value-creating returns, but not elite compounder returns, and goodwill is high at about 57% of invested capital in 2025 after years of acquisitions. The business likely earns much better economics on its content/software assets than consolidated ROIC shows, but the acquisition goodwill is a real capital-allocation burden. Confidence would improve with returns on tangible operating capital and segment-level incremental returns.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.86 / confidence 0.88

The balance sheet is strong for a recurring professional information business. At June 30, current and long-term indebtedness totalled $2.94b against $577m of cash after repayment of $500m of notes, and first-half adjusted EBITDA was $1.63b. FY2026 net-interest guidance remains $180-190m against about $2.1b of guided FCF, while the Global Print transaction should add roughly $500m of gross proceeds. Confidence would firm up with the exact post-transaction maturity ladder, fixed/floating split and quantified KKR support obligation.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / confidence 0.70

This is an institutional franchise, not a single-person story. Steve Hasker’s execution matters, especially around AI product cadence and portfolio reshaping, but the core assets are content, brands, workflows, customer relationships and editorial/professional systems. Reuters and the professional software/content franchises should survive any one executive. Confidence would improve with a deeper board/succession review from the proxy/annual report.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.58 / confidence 0.55

Alignment is mixed and under-researched. Insider ownership is unusually high because Woodbridge/Thomson-family interests control a large stake, which creates long-term-owner orientation but also governance concentration. Yahoo governance risk flags board/shareholder-rights risk as elevated, and without the management information circular I have not verified pay metrics, hurdle rigor, dilution discipline or minority-shareholder protections. Confidence would firm up by reading the 2026 proxy/management information circular.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair is close, but loses on only decent consolidated ROIC and a runway that is credible rather than exceptional. Rule-breakers has the right AI theme and strong position, but TRI is a mature incumbent, not an early emerging hyper-growth name. Freeroll loses because there is no hard valuation floor and no concrete ≤4-year catalyst; the case is durable FCF plus fair valuation, not a special-situation re-rate.

## Overall

TRI is a good cash-cow candidate with some compounder characteristics. Q2 strengthened the case through 10% Big 3 organic growth, 29% FCF growth and raised revenue guidance, while the Global Print joint venture improves the continuing mix. The strengths are high recurring revenue, trusted proprietary content, high margins, excellent FCF conversion, modest leverage, a long dividend record and a credible AI upsell pathway inside professional workflows. The weaknesses are consolidated ROIC only around 12%, heavy goodwill/acquisition dependence, concentrated control/governance caveats, and a valuation that is fair rather than obviously cheap. At the latest $101.83 quote, the expected return remains acceptable but has less room for error than at the prior price.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a “trusted data + AI workflow” thesis: customers in law, tax, compliance and accounting need accurate, auditable output rather than generic AI answers, and TRI owns proprietary content/workflows that can make AI more valuable and safer. It is not a moonshot; it is an incumbent monetising AI inside already-critical professional workflows.

## Watch

- FY2026 organic revenue versus the raised ~8% guide, and Big 3 organic revenue versus the raised 9.5-10.0% guide.
- CoCounsel/Westlaw/Pagero/SafeSend/Noetica adoption: look for evidence of AI products lifting retention, ARPU or new sales rather than just narrative.
- Adjusted EBITDA margin expansion: management guided about +100bp versus FY2025.
- FCF against the ~$2.1b FY2026 guide and capex near 8% of revenue.
- Acquisition discipline: goodwill trend, purchase prices, and whether bolt-ons produce organic growth rather than just bought revenue.
- Global Print transaction: Q4 closing, discontinued-operations treatment, royalty economics and the size/probability of the minimum-return support for KKR.
- Governance and incentives: read the latest management information circular before increasing conviction.

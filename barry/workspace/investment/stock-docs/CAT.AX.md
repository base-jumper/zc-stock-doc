# CAT.AX — Catapult Sports Ltd

## Business Overview

Catapult sells sports-performance technology to professional and elite sports organisations. Its core products are athlete-monitoring wearables, video-analysis tools, scouting/intelligence software, and now gym-performance monitoring via Perch. The model is SaaS-heavy: FY26 statutory revenue was US$140.7m, subscription revenue was US$118.6m, ACV was US$133.8m, and ACV is the lead indicator for future SaaS revenue.

The company changed materially in FY26. It acquired Perch, IMPECT, and assets from IsoLynx, doubled headcount to about 940 staff, raised equity to fund the IMPECT deal, and ended March 2026 with US$53.5m cash. The expanded platform is strategically more interesting, but the thesis is now less purely organic and the financials include substantial acquisition accounting, share-based payment and integration noise.

As of 2026-07-04, the ASX Markit header showed CAT at A$3.04, market cap about A$951m, bid A$2.96, ask A$3.05, and 1.286m shares traded. The stock is no longer obviously cheap on current FCF after the FY26 annual report: reported FCF excluding acquisitions was only US$6.6m, though management expects higher FCF as the enlarged business scales.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.78 / 0.82

Catapult sits in the growing professional-sports technology stack: athlete monitoring, video analysis, tactical/scouting intelligence, and strength-training analytics. FY26 reinforced that teams are buying broader workflow systems rather than isolated devices. ACV grew 32.3% to US$133.8m, or 18% organically after normalising for acquired ACV, while multi-vertical customers rose 51.7% to 1,124.

The category remains attractive but specialised. Elite sports budgets are expanding for data, injury prevention, analytics and workflow automation, yet this is still a niche enterprise-sports market rather than a mass-market SaaS category. The broad “sports technology” TAM remains less useful than evidence of team penetration, ACV per team and cross-sell.

### Top Dog — 0.84 / 0.82

Catapult looks like a category leader in elite-team performance technology. The FY26 annual report says Catapult works with more than 5,500 teams across 40+ sports and 100+ countries, and that more than half of FIFA World Cup national teams are customers. ACV reached US$133.8m, with Performance & Health at US$84.5m and Tactics & Coaching at US$46.5m.

The top-dog case is now broader than wearables: Vector, ClearSky, Perch, MatchTracker, Focus, Hub, Thunder and IMPECT create a wider team-performance workflow. The remaining uncertainty is independent market-share evidence versus Hudl, Stats Perform, STATSports, Kinexon, Genius Sports/Second Spectrum and sport-specific incumbents.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.72 / 0.80

The moat is workflow embedment, customer references, specialised data, switching cost, and product breadth. FY26 retention metrics are supportive: ACV churn was 3.9%, retention remained above 96%, lifetime duration was 7.5 years, and multi-solution pro teams reached 1,328. That suggests Catapult is not just selling gadgets; it is becoming part of training, match preparation, return-to-play, scouting and tactical workflows.

The moat is still not bulletproof. Professional teams can be relationship-driven, product leadership can shift, and the company must integrate acquisitions without creating a clunky bundle. Gross margin stayed strong at 80.3%, but FCF conversion is not yet strong enough to prove durable pricing power after all product investment and share-based compensation.

### Evidence of Durable, Accelerating Growth — 0.82 / 0.88

Growth is strong. FY26 revenue grew 20.8% to US$140.7m; subscription revenue grew 23.2% to US$118.6m; ACV grew 32.3% to US$133.8m; Performance & Health ACV grew 23% constant currency; and Tactics & Coaching ACV grew 40% constant currency, helped by IMPECT. Management EBITDA rose 67.4% to US$24.7m, and management EBITDA margin improved to 17.6%.

The caveat is quality of growth. Statutory NPAT was still a US$24.0m loss, down from an US$8.8m loss, mainly because of D&A, share-based payments and acquisition-related costs. FCF excluding acquisitions fell to US$6.6m from US$8.6m. The business is scaling, but the market needs to see FY27 convert the larger ACV base into materially higher FCF.

### Organic Growth over Acquisitive Growth — 0.62 / 0.88

This score moves down. Catapult still delivered good organic growth — management says FY26 organic ACV growth was 18% after normalising for Perch and IMPECT — but FY26 was explicitly acquisition-heavy. Perch, IMPECT and IsoLynx changed the platform, headcount, balance sheet and incentive/dilution profile.

The acquisitions look strategically coherent: Perch extends athlete monitoring into the gym, IMPECT adds soccer scouting and tactical intelligence, and IsoLynx contributes tracking assets. But this is no longer a clean organic SaaS compounding story for the current year. Integration execution and acquired-ACV retention are now central watch items.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.72 / 0.78

Will Lopes and the board have executed a meaningful repositioning: SaaS metrics improved, Rule of 40 excluding acquired ACV reached 36%, the product suite broadened, and the company raised capital for IMPECT while preserving a US$53.5m cash balance. The investor communication is unusually clear about ACV, churn, multi-solution usage, management EBITDA and FCF.

The risk is acquisition digestion. FY26 added three assets, hundreds of staff, large goodwill/intangibles, deferred/contingent consideration and more share-based payments. That may be the right strategic move, but it raises execution complexity and makes per-share value creation harder to audit.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.66 / 0.78

The FY26 remuneration report is better than the prior evidence gap. Executive variable remuneration is heavily equity-based; CEO target remuneration was 64% variable, maximum 75% variable. The FY26 company scorecard used Rule of 40 progress, with ACV growth and management EBITDA margin as inputs; the target Rule of 40 was 35%, management EBITDA margin hurdle was 16%, and actual outcomes were 36% and 18%.

This is directionally aligned with shareholder value, especially because some EIP allocations use share-price premiums and multi-year service vesting. The negatives are board discretion, a focus on management EBITDA rather than per-share FCF, substantial SBC, and dilution from employee plans/acquisition consideration. Alignment is improved, not perfect.

### Customer Devotion — 0.82 / 0.85

For B2B software, retention and expansion are the best public proxies. Catapult reported ACV churn of 3.9%, retention above 96%, 7.5-year lifetime duration, 1,124 multi-vertical customers, and 1,328 multi-solution pro teams. Average pro-team ACV exceeded US$30k for the first time, growing 10% year-on-year.

That is strong evidence that customers find the product valuable and are expanding usage. The remaining nuance is that retention can reflect switching costs and workflow captivity as much as “love”. More third-party customer reviews and renewal cohort data would firm this up further.

### Why the other strategies lost

Wonderful-and-fair loses on earnings cleanliness and predictability. Catapult has attractive SaaS economics, but statutory earnings are still negative and FCF conversion is not yet mature.

Freeroll loses because there is no hard asset downside floor and no discrete time-bound catalyst. This is a long-duration growth/platform thesis.

## Overall

Catapult remains a credible Rule Breaker candidate, with stronger evidence on customer demand, category leadership and platform breadth after the FY26 annual report. The bull case is now: category leader, >96% retention, 18% organic ACV growth even after acquisitions, cross-sell acceleration, 80% gross margin, and a larger platform that could become the operating system for elite team performance departments.

The bear case is sharper too. FY26 growth was acquisition-assisted, statutory losses widened, FCF excluding acquisitions was only US$6.6m, share-based payments were material, and future earn-outs/deferred consideration can dilute shareholders. At A$3.04, the stock still prices in a lot of future FCF scaling.

Current view: quality improved, valuation worsened. I’d keep watching/owning only with a clear tolerance for acquisition integration risk and the need for FY27 FCF to step up materially.

## Watch

- FY27 FCF conversion: management expects higher FCF; downgrade if FCF does not lift materially from FY26’s US$6.6m ex-acquisitions base.
- Acquisition integration: track Perch, IMPECT and IsoLynx integration, acquired-ACV retention, cross-sell, goodwill/intangible growth, and earn-out/deferred consideration dilution.
- ACV quality: keep organic ACV growth near/high-teens and churn below ~5%; a slowdown after the acquisition boost would weaken the Rule Breaker case.
- Per-share economics: monitor share count, SBC, employee-share-trust issuance and acquisition shares; value creation must show up per share, not just in ACV.
- Margin bridge: confirm management EBITDA improvement converts into true FCF after capitalised development, lease/device capex, working capital and acquisition costs.
- Re-run valuation if price moves >15%, FY27 FCF guidance/results differ materially from the A$0.040/share normalised anchor, or dilution outlook changes.

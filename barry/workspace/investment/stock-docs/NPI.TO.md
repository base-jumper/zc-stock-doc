# NPI.TO — Northland Power Inc.

## Business Overview

Northland Power is a Canadian independent power producer focused on contracted renewable and energy infrastructure assets. Its portfolio spans offshore wind in Europe and Taiwan, onshore wind and solar, battery storage, natural gas/dispatchable power, and a regulated utility in Colombia. Revenue is mainly generated from power sales under long-term contracts, PPAs, regulated returns, and market-linked power where contracts roll off.

The business is best read as an infrastructure cash generator with a growth project pipeline, not a high-return asset-light compounder. The key cash-producing assets are operating offshore wind facilities in Germany and the Netherlands plus Americas renewables, gas, storage and utility assets. The two current swing factors are the large offshore construction projects: **Baltic Power** in Poland and **Hai Long** in Taiwan. Management says Baltic Power remains on track for commercial operations in H2 2026 and Hai Long for 2027; execution here should lift EBITDA, but project commissioning, financing and wind-resource risk remain central.

2025 results were mixed. Revenue from energy sales rose to C$2.435b from C$2.346b, Adjusted EBITDA was broadly flat at C$1.253b, and non-IFRS free cash flow was C$382m / C$1.46 per share. GAAP net loss was C$108m, driven mainly by a C$527m non-cash pre-tax impairment at Nordsee One. 2026 guidance is for C$1.45–1.65b of Adjusted EBITDA and C$1.05–1.25 of free cash flow per share.

## Trait Assessment

Primary strategy: **Cash Cow**. Northland is a mature, contracted power/infrastructure company with real distributable cash flow and a dividend, but the score is held down by low accounting earnings quality, heavy leverage, low reported returns on capital, and dependence on successful delivery of large offshore projects. It is not a classic Wonderful-and-Fair compounder because returns on capital are modest and debt is material; it is not a Freeroll because the asset/dividend floor is not hard enough at the current price; and it is not a Rule Breaker because this is infrastructure execution, not an emerging dominant platform.

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.45 / 0.82

The cash stream is more stable than GAAP profit, but reported earnings are not clean or predictable enough for a high score. Yahoo financials show net income of C$828m in 2022, losses in 2023 and 2025, and profit in 2024. The 2025 company release attributes the annual net loss mainly to a C$527m Nordsee One non-cash impairment. That supports the view that the loss was not purely operating collapse, but recurring impairments/fair-value noise are still part of the shareholder experience.

Cash backing is better: company non-IFRS FCF was C$382m in 2025 and C$394m in 2024; Yahoo cash-flow data shows positive FCF each year from 2022–2025. The reason the score is only mid-band is that the trait asks for consistent, predictable profits, not just adjusted cash flow. Confidence is reasonably high because the GAAP/FCF contrast is clear, though a 7–10 year filing review would firm up the cycle read.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.72 / 0.82

Northland does produce meaningful free cash, especially on management’s proportionate, debt-service-adjusted measure. In 2025 it generated C$382m of non-IFRS FCF, or C$1.46/share, after scheduled debt repayments and interest; 2026 guidance is lower at C$1.05–1.25/share. Yahoo’s consolidated cash-flow view is stronger but less directly comparable: C$1.426b operating cash flow and C$1.291b FCF in 2025 because it does not apply Northland’s proportionate/free-cash-flow adjustments.

The positive read: non-expansionary capex was tiny in management’s FCF bridge, cash dividends were covered in 2025, and contracted infrastructure can throw off cash once built. The caveat: the business is capital intensive and a large part of value creation depends on development capex, scheduled debt service and project execution, so this is not a capital-light cash machine.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.68 / 0.78

The underlying demand for contracted power, renewables, storage and dispatchable capacity is durable; this is not a secularly shrinking business. 2025 energy-sales revenue was slightly above 2024, operating fleet availability was 96%, and offshore wind output recovered strongly in Q4. The growth projects also give a path to higher EBITDA once Baltic Power and Hai Long enter commercial operation.

The mixed part is variability. 2025 Adjusted EBITDA was down slightly because low offshore wind resource in H1 offset Oneida and better Americas onshore wind; GAAP earnings were hit by impairment; and Hai Long commissioning delays could reduce pre-completion revenue by C$150–200m at Northland’s share. The fundamentals look durable, but not smooth.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.62 / 0.74

Northland’s moat is mostly contractual/regulatory rather than brand-like pricing power. Long-term PPAs, regulated utility economics, grid interconnection positions and operating expertise create some durability. The Nordsee One five-year bilateral PPA for roughly one-third of production is an example of recontracting capacity into visible cash flows.

This is not a wide-moat price-setter. Renewables development is competitive, capital costs matter, and returns are constrained by auction/PPA pricing and financing markets. The score is above average because contracted infrastructure has defensible cash-flow visibility, but below high because pricing power is externally bounded.

### Capital Allocation — 0.55 / 0.72

The capital-allocation record is mixed. Positives: management cut the annual dividend to C$0.72/share, which improves coverage and frees cash while large projects are being completed; it has articulated a five-year funding plan and higher 12%+ return targets; and debt has been reduced in recent cash-flow periods. The 2025 dividend was covered by company FCF, and the new dividend is materially lower than the old C$1.20/share run-rate.

Negatives: the company is undertaking very large offshore projects in a high-rate, high-execution-risk environment; share count has risen over the last several years; and the 2025 impairment underlines that asset values can be marked down when rates or project economics shift. The allocation question is not settled until Baltic Power and Hai Long are delivered on budget and begin contributing cash.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.35 / 0.82

Reported returns are weak for a quality strategy. The returns-on-capital script shows ROIC of 1.1% in 2025, 5.3% in 2024, 1.5% in 2023 and 10.6% in 2022, with a four-year average of about 4.6%. This is below a typical cost of equity and not enough to call the business a high-return compounder.

Utilities and contracted infrastructure often earn lower but steadier returns than asset-light businesses, so the low absolute ROIC is not a total rejection of the business. But it does cap the quality score and makes valuation discipline important.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.38 / 0.82

Debt is the main weakness. The conservative-debt script shows a current Yahoo snapshot of net debt / EBITDA around 3.8x, but the annual statement-based 2025 figure is much worse at 7.0x because 2025 EBITDA was depressed by impairment/fair-value effects. Interest coverage on EBIT was only 0.7x in 2025 and 3.4x in 2024; EBITDA coverage was better but still not generous in weak years.

This is infrastructure debt matched to long-lived contracted assets, so a higher leverage tolerance is appropriate than for a cyclical operating company. Still, leverage is not conservative. Liquidity is a partial offset: the company reported C$931m of available corporate liquidity at 31 Dec 2025, including C$892m of revolver capacity.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.75 / 0.70

Northland does not appear to be a single-founder/key-person bet. The assets, contracts, project partners and operating processes are institutional. Christine Healy is the current CEO and execution quality matters, but the thesis does not depend on one irreplaceable person in the way a founder-led software or biotech story might.

Confidence is moderate because this update did not do a deep board/bench/succession review.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.60 / 0.60

The available evidence supports a neutral-to-positive but not fully proven alignment read. Management’s public strategy emphasizes long-term shareholder value, funding discipline, cost efficiency and higher project return targets, all of which are owner-relevant. The dividend reset also suggests willingness to make unpopular but financially sensible decisions.

Confidence is only moderate because this run did not review the latest proxy compensation metrics, insider ownership, holding requirements or recent insider transactions in depth. Next update should pull the circular/proxy and score this more tightly.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Wonderful and Fair:** failed mainly on returns on capital and debt. It has durable assets, but the return profile is not high enough and leverage is too high for a classic compounder.
- **Freeroll:** Baltic Power/Hai Long CODs are real catalysts, but downside support is not hard enough. The stock trades around 1.4x book / 2.0x tangible book with only a 3.3% dividend yield, so the floor is not close.
- **Rule Breakers:** renewable power and storage are attractive themes, but Northland is not a dominant emerging platform with winner-takes-most economics; it is a capital-intensive operator/developer.

## Overall

Northland is a lower-quality but potentially useful infrastructure cash-flow idea. The attractive case is simple: contracted power assets generate cash, the dividend is now better covered after the reset, and Baltic Power/Hai Long should raise EBITDA if delivered on schedule. The problem is equally simple: leverage is high, GAAP earnings are noisy, returns on capital are modest, and the valuation does not leave a huge margin of safety.

I would keep it watched, but not prioritize it ahead of higher-quality ideas unless the price falls closer to book value, leverage improves, or the offshore projects materially de-risk.

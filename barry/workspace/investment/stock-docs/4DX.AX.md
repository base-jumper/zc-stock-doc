# 4DX.AX — 4DMedical Limited

## Business Overview

4DMedical is an Australian respiratory-imaging software company. Its core pitch is that it can turn standard CT imaging into quantitative lung-function information, especially via **CT:VQ™**, a non-contrast CT-based ventilation/perfusion product. The company also sells/owns a broader cardiothoracic imaging suite, helped by the 2023 Imbio acquisition and the 2026 contextflow acquisition.

The business model is software/SaaS and scan-volume driven, sold through direct relationships with academic medical centres, imaging providers, pharma customers, and distribution partners. FY26 operating revenue was **$7.2m**, up **23%**, with gross margins above **90%**. But this is still pre-scale: FY26 customer receipts were only **$5.7m** and net operating cash outflow was **$32.6m**.

The major recent inflection is CT:VQ. FDA 510(k) clearance arrived in September 2025, U.S. CMS reimbursement was confirmed around **US$650.50** on top of the chest CT payment, and the product now has regulatory clearance across the U.S., EU, UK, Canada, New Zealand and Australia. 4DMedical also launched CLEAR, a Massachusetts General Hospital-anchored clinical program aimed at the much larger acute pulmonary-embolism workflow; management says that pathway could lift CT:VQ's obtainable U.S. market to **US$3b**. A bipartisan U.S. House bill separately authorises **US$20m** for a VA functional-lung-imaging pilot, but that is not yet enacted funding or booked revenue.

Commercial evidence is improving but still early. At 30 June, 4DMedical served **540 sites**, up **39%**, and processed a record **105,970 scans in Q4**, up **43%**; FY26 scans rose **77% to 344,075**. All five named CT:VQ academic adopters — Stanford, Cleveland Clinic, UC San Diego Health, University of Chicago Medicine and University of Miami — were on full commercial terms. SimonMed signed a three-year per-scan commercial agreement covering its 170-plus-centre outpatient network with no evaluation phase; onboarding was complete ahead of an August 2026 launch. Paid Australian contracts and scans also began soon after TGA approval, and a one-year GSK pharmaceutical-imaging contract started in May.

Balance sheet risk was transformed in early 2026: 4DMedical finished FY26 with **$278.0m cash** and a **$10m** secured Pro Medicus facility due July 2027. That provides about 30 quarters of runway at Q4's cash-burn rate, before post-year-end cash commitments for contextflow and RevealDx. It also highlights the valuation mismatch: at **A$3.63** on 31 July, the roughly **A$2.18b** market cap prices in a very large commercial success while FY26 receipts were only **A$5.7m**.

No universal disqualifier found: I did not find fraud, imminent insolvency, hidden-liability, value-leaking-control, or business-model kill-shot evidence in the sources reviewed. The risk is valuation/execution, not immediate eligibility.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.88 / confidence 0.90

The industry setup is genuinely attractive. CT:VQ is trying to move VQ imaging from nuclear-medicine workflows into routine CT infrastructure, avoiding contrast agents, radiotracers and specialised nuclear medicine capacity. The addressable market is meaningful: management cites **>1m U.S. VQ scans annually**, **>US$1.1b U.S. opportunity**, **>US$2.6b global opportunity**, and around **14,500 U.S. CT scanners**.

The timing is strong because regulatory and reimbursement barriers have started to fall together: FDA clearance, CMS reimbursement, CE Mark, UKCA, Canada/New Zealand clearance and TGA approval all landed within a tight window. CLEAR now adds a clinical-evidence route into acute pulmonary embolism, where contrast-free assessment could address a larger workflow than nuclear VQ alone. This is the right kind of rule-breaker setup: an established clinical workflow potentially being replaced by a software layer on existing infrastructure.

Main uncertainty: TAM is company-framed and depends on real reimbursement adoption and clinical behaviour change, not just regulatory permission.

### Top Dog — 0.74 / confidence 0.73

4DMedical has credible early leadership. The company says CT:VQ is the first and only non-contrast CT-based ventilation/perfusion imaging technology with FDA 510(k) clearance. It also claims 9 FDA-cleared devices, 100+ patents, and has named reference deployments across top academic centres.

That said, top-dog status is not yet proven economically. Five leading U.S. academic centres are now commercial and SimonMed contracted on commercial terms without an evaluation phase, but customer receipts remain tiny relative to market cap and the competitive structure is still forming. Existing nuclear/CTPA workflows are incumbents, and large imaging/software players could respond if CT-based VQ becomes obviously lucrative.

Confidence would improve with published market-share data, clear revenue per site/scan, and evidence that Philips/SimonMed materially accelerates paid volume.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.72 / confidence 0.70

The moat candidates are real: proprietary algorithms/IP, regulatory clearances, clinical validation, reimbursement precedent, integration into radiology workflows, and KOL/reference-site momentum. The AJRCCM study reporting a 76% response rate for CT:VQ-guided lung-volume-reduction-surgery selection versus 46% under the current approach strengthens the evidence base. Gross margins above 90% suggest attractive software economics if volumes scale.

But the moat is not fully bankable yet. Regulatory clearance is a barrier, not a monopoly. The durability of the advantage depends on whether clinicians standardise around CT:VQ, whether distributors push it effectively, and whether a larger imaging platform can replicate or bundle a substitute.

### Durable Growth — 0.64 / confidence 0.85

Operational metrics are growing fast: FY26 ended with **540 sites**, up **39% YoY** and excluding SimonMed, while Q4 scan volume rose **43% to 105,970** and full-year scans rose **77% to 344,075**. SimonMed's 170-plus sites are due to start clinical deployment in August 2026. This is real usage momentum.

The financial translation is weaker. FY26 operating revenue rose **23% to $7.2m**, while customer receipts were **$5.7m**, including **$1.34m** in Q4. For a roughly **$2.18b** market cap, revenue growth needs to become dramatically larger and more monetised. The gap between 77% scan growth and 23% revenue growth is the central metric to resolve.

Score is therefore mixed: strong leading indicators, but cash revenue still very early.

### Organic Growth — 0.62 / confidence 0.78

The CT:VQ thesis is primarily organic: regulatory clearance, clinical validation, site expansion, scan volumes and distributor-led commercial rollout. However, the broader platform has meaningful M&A in it: Imbio in 2023, contextflow in 2026, a **10% RevealDx stake** and a distribution partnership with Azra AI. These are strategically coherent additions to lung-cancer and patient-navigation workflows, but they make platform expansion less purely organic and raise integration/capital-allocation risk.

### Great Management & Smart Backing — 0.76 / confidence 0.75

Founder/CEO Andreas Fouras remains central to the story, and management has executed several difficult milestones: FDA clearance, reimbursement, multiple international approvals, major institutional placements, and strategic relationships with Pro Medicus and Philips. The Pro Medicus strategic investment is a strong credibility signal given PME's quality in medical imaging software.

The Q4 filing adds credible commercial execution: all five U.S. academic adopters are paying, SimonMed skipped evaluation and moved straight to a three-year commercial agreement, and paid Australian scanning began soon after approval. The score is not higher because revenue remains only A$7.2m and integration of contextflow, RevealDx and Azra adds complexity.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.60 / confidence 0.60

Yahoo Finance shows insider ownership around **16.7%** and institutional ownership around **21.4%**, which is positive. Founder-led ownership and high-profile strategic/institutional support help.

The offset is dilution and capital intensity while loss-making. Shares outstanding have expanded materially; early-2026 placements were well-timed and balance-sheet enhancing, but existing shareholders are still funding a long path to scale. I have not yet gone deep enough into the latest remuneration report to score pay design with high confidence.

### Customer Devotion — 0.72 / confidence 0.72

Named reference customers are impressive: Stanford, Cleveland Clinic, UCSD, UChicago and University of Miami are all on full commercial terms. Another leading U.S. academic centre completed its 90-day evaluation and entered commercial discussions, while SimonMed contracted on per-scan commercial terms from day one. Paid Australian contracts and a GSK pharma engagement broaden the evidence beyond academic pilots.

But the clean B2B love metrics are missing: no reported NRR, logo churn, expansion ARR by cohort, or quantified renewal economics. Confidence stays low until 4DMedical reports retention/expansion data or site-level economics.

**Strategy fit:** 4DMedical is clearly a **Rule Breaker** rather than Wonderful-and-Fair, Cash Cow, or Freeroll. It is loss-making, cash-burning, and valued on future market capture, not current earnings. The bull case is that CT:VQ becomes a new standard layer in respiratory imaging; the bear case is that adoption never scales enough to justify the already-large valuation.

## Overall

4DMedical is a high-quality story at a demanding price. The company has done the hard regulatory work, has an elegant software-on-existing-CT-infrastructure product, strong gross margins, credible reference customers, and enough cash to execute. That is exactly the kind of setup that can become a large winner if adoption inflects.

The problem is valuation discipline. At **$3.63/share** and roughly **$2.18b market cap**, the market is capitalising a lot of future success. FY26 revenue and receipts remain tiny, and 77% scan growth translated into only 23% operating-revenue growth. SimonMed, paid Australian rollout and the GSK contract improve the commercial pathway, but the VA bill should not be treated as revenue until funding is enacted and contracts/deployments follow.

My read: **watchlist, not obvious buy at current price**. I like the business setup and would happily keep tracking it, but I would want either (a) hard evidence that CT:VQ revenue is inflecting through Philips/SimonMed/AMCs, or (b) a meaningfully better entry price. The company may be excellent; the stock already knows a lot of that.

These inputs produce terminal EBITDA of about **US$30.4m**, terminal equity value of **US$548m**, and a terminal price of **US$0.714** after dilution, about **A$1.02** at constant FX. The script calculates **-11.95% annualized ROI** even on this modeled survival path: value creation contributes about **-9.75%** annually and dilution about **-2.44%**.

The narrow market model does not value contextflow's cancer-screening tools, RevealDx, Azra AI or other adjacencies, so it may understate 4DMedical's eventual total revenue. It is still a more defensible base than treating management's reimbursed-procedure opportunity as software-provider revenue. My **agent_score is 0.40**: the commercial story improved materially, but today's valuation requires CT:VQ to capture far more provider revenue, adjacencies to add substantial value, or both.

## Watch

- **CT:VQ revenue conversion:** quarterly customer receipts, SaaS revenue growth and scan monetisation, not just site/scan counts.
- **VA appropriation pathway:** whether the House bill's proposed US$20m survives the Senate/conference process, is enacted, and translates into procurement timing and paid deployments.
- **Philips / VA / DoD traction:** actual contracts, deployments and receipts through the Philips government channel; do not count legislative direction as revenue.
- **SimonMed rollout:** August 2026 clinical launch, site activation pace, paid scans and whether the 170-plus-site network becomes a meaningful revenue contributor.
- **AMC expansion:** conversion of the unnamed completed evaluation into a contract; new named commercial adopters; expansion within Stanford/Cleveland/UCSD/UChicago/Miami.
- **Reimbursement durability:** CMS payment stability, private payer acceptance, and Australian MBS progress after TGA approval.
- **Cash burn:** Q4 FY26 net operating cash outflow was **$9.3m** and FY26 outflow was **$32.6m**; cash is ample, but burn discipline matters.
- **Dilution/acquisitions:** whether the strong cash balance is used to accelerate organic CT:VQ adoption or diluted into expensive M&A.
- **Revenue per scan/site:** this is the missing metric that would make the thesis much more underwritable.

# 6861.T — Keyence Corporation

## Business Overview

Keyence is a Japanese factory-automation business selling sensors, machine-vision systems, precision measurement instruments, laser markers, barcode readers, digital/3D microscopes and related automation products. Its edge is high-value product design, fast application support, and direct technical sales into factories. The company says it serves about 400,000 customers in roughly 110 countries, and no single customer exceeded 10% of FY2026 sales.

FY2026 net sales were ¥1.169tn (+10.4%), operating income ¥595.8bn (+8.4%), and net income ¥445.2bn (+11.7%). Gross margin was ~83%, operating margin ~51%, and Yahoo-derived FCF was ¥402.3bn, a 34.4% FCF margin. Regional sales were Japan ¥390.1bn and overseas ¥779.2bn, with the Americas and Asia strong and Europe still cautious but partly recovering. Management expects demand from automation, quality improvement and energy efficiency, and is investing in high-added-value products, direct sales capability, and overseas expansion.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.90 / confidence 0.85

The last four Yahoo-covered annual periods show no loss years: net income rose from ¥363.0bn in FY2023 to ¥445.2bn in FY2026, with YoY net-income growth of ~1.8%, 7.8% and 11.7%. Cash backing is strong: cumulative FCF/net income over the four years was ~0.91, with FY2026 FCF ¥402.3bn against net income ¥445.2bn. Receivables/revenue stayed around 30–33%, so there is no obvious receivables blow-out. Confidence is below perfect because Yahoo only gives a four-year window here; the annual-report chart indicates a much longer profitable history, but I have not rebuilt the full 10-year series line-by-line.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.94 / confidence 0.85

This is the defining strength. FY2026 FCF was ¥402.3bn on ¥1.169tn revenue, a 34.4% FCF margin; the four-year average FCF margin was ~34.7%. Capex intensity is tiny for an industrial supplier: FY2026 capex was ¥28.4bn, only ~6.6% of operating cash flow, and capex/D&A was 1.65x, so FY2026 FCF was not obviously flattered by under-maintenance. FCF conversion was 0.90x in FY2026 and 0.91x cumulatively over the four-year window. Confidence would improve with a longer history and a maintenance/growth capex split.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.90 / confidence 0.82

The fundamentals are not melting. Revenue, EPS and FCF were at four-year highs in FY2026; gross margin stayed around 82–84%, and operating margin stayed around 51–54%. The business is cyclical to factory capex, but the structural direction — automation, quality inspection, traceability, energy efficiency and manufacturing productivity — remains favourable. Keyence’s own FY2026 outlook flags geopolitical/macro risk, but also says the market environment should see growing demand from technological innovation and automation needs.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.90 / confidence 0.80

The evidence points to a strong moat: 83% gross margins, 51% operating margins, broad product breadth, direct technical sales/application support, and high-value solutions embedded in production quality and automation workflows. Keyence’s own description emphasizes consultative sales, on-site demonstrations/application testing, trial units, same-day shipping, and direct technical support. Those features create switching/search-cost and service advantages even when the physical product category is competitive. Exact market-share trend by product category would firm up confidence.

### Capital Allocation — 0.66 / confidence 0.75

Allocation is conservative and owner-safe, but arguably not maximally efficient. The company has no debt, huge liquidity, low capital intensity, no meaningful dilution, and a well-covered dividend. FY2026 dividends paid were ¥109.1bn versus FCF of ¥402.3bn; the dividend applicable to FY2026 was ¥550/share, up from ¥350. The downside is a large cash/securities pile that depresses returns and limited evidence of buybacks. The CADENAS Technologies acquisition looks small relative to Keyence and goodwill remains below 1% of assets, so there is no serial-M&A red flag.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.76 / confidence 0.72

Keyence is high-margin and capital-light, but conventional ROIC is dampened by its enormous cash/securities balance. Yahoo-derived ROIC was ~12–14% and ROCE ~17–20% from FY2023–FY2026; operating margins were ~51–54%. The balance sheet held cash, deposits and marketable securities of roughly ¥1.49tn at FY2026 and no debt, which drags reported return metrics while making the company extremely resilient. A cleaner excess-cash-adjusted ROIC would firm this up.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 1.00 / confidence 0.90

This is about as clean as it gets. Yahoo shows total debt of zero, cash and short-term investments of ~¥1.49tn, current ratio ~10.5x, quick ratio ~10.0x, and positive FCF. The annual report balance sheet likewise shows large cash, deposits and securities, and no conventional debt burden. A downturn would hurt demand, but debt will not force management’s hand.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.78 / confidence 0.65

Keyence was founded decades ago and now appears institutionally run rather than dependent on one founder-CEO. The advantage seems embedded in product development, direct-sales process, technical support, and customer reach rather than one irreplaceable person. Confidence is moderate because I did not locate a detailed succession-process discussion or management-depth disclosure beyond the board/director list.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 / confidence 0.45

This is the weakest-underwritten cash-cow trait. Yahoo reports insider ownership around 22.8%, which is directionally positive, but the annual report extract does not provide enough remuneration detail to judge whether incentives are tied to long-term per-share value, ROIC, FCF/share, or less useful short-term metrics. I score it mixed: meaningful insider ownership helps, limited pay-plan evidence hurts. Firm this up with the Japanese corporate governance report / remuneration disclosure.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-fair is also a valid lens, but the mechanical scorer slightly prefers cash-cow because the company’s current evidence is strongest on durable cash generation, stability, debt resilience and shareholder-safe conservatism. Rule-breakers loses because this is a mature proven business, not an early/emerging disruptor with explosive optionality. Freeroll fails mechanically: the price is ~5.4x book, net cash is only ~8% of price, the dividend yield is ~0.7%, and there is no concrete ≤4-year re-rating catalyst.

## Overall

Keyence is an elite cash-generative automation franchise: enormous margins, clean earnings, strong FCF conversion, no debt, and a business model that benefits from automation and quality-control demand across global manufacturing. The problem is price. At ¥76,970/share, the stock trades around 42x trailing earnings and ~46x current FCF/share; even an exceptional business has to compound well and retain a premium multiple to produce acceptable forward returns.

The growth assumption is deliberately lower than the earnings case because this is framed as a mature cash machine, not a rule-breaker; the 35x exit multiple remains premium but below today’s ~46x P/FCF.

The valuation is the weak link. This setup produces a very low expected annualized ROI, because business growth and the small dividend are mostly offset by multiple compression. My valuation-aware agent score is 0.45: superb business, but the stock is priced for a lot of that quality already.

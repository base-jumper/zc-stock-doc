# ASSA-B.ST — ASSA ABLOY AB

## Business Overview

ASSA ABLOY is the global leader in access solutions. It sells mechanical and electromechanical locks, entrance automation, identity/access-control products and related services across five operating divisions. Demand is diversified across commercial, institutional, residential and hospitality end-markets; the installed base, specification relationships, brands and local distribution support recurring replacement and upgrade demand. Acquisitions are structural to the model: the group has completed more than 400 since 1994 and targets 5% acquired growth over a business cycle.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.82 / 0.88 confidence

ASSA ABLOY has a long profitable record and clean recent cash backing. FY2022–25 net income was SEK 13.3bn, 13.6bn, 15.6bn and 14.7bn; cumulative FCF was about 1.20× cumulative net income. The FY2025 decline was modest rather than a loss, and receivables fell from 16.4% to 14.0% of revenue over the observed period. Acquisition adjustments remain a recurring source of noise, but reported profits convert to cash.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.84 / 0.90 confidence

FCF was SEK 12.4bn in 2022 and SEK 18.6–18.8bn in each of 2023–25, equal to 10.2–13.3% of revenue. Capex absorbed only about 12–14% of operating cash flow in 2024–25, while working-capital swings did not prevent strong cumulative conversion. Acquisitions are discretionary growth investment and excluded from FCF; the underlying operations are forward-capital-light.

### Fundamental Stability — 0.82 / 0.82 confidence

Access control is necessary, replacement-driven and spread across geographies and end-markets. Mechanical products mature slowly while electronic credentials, entrance automation and building-security upgrades extend demand. FY2022–25 revenue rose from SEK 120.8bn to SEK 152.4bn and operating margins remained around 15–16%. The main risk is not terminal demand but acquisition execution and cyclical construction exposure.

### Returns on Capital — 0.62 / 0.88 confidence

ROIC was 12.1% in 2022 and roughly 10.5–10.8% in 2023–25; ROCE was 14.1–16.0%. These are value-creating but not exceptional, and the drift after the large 2023 acquisition program matters. ROE near 15% is not materially inflated relative to ROIC, but goodwill is substantial, so acquisition economics deserve continued scrutiny.

### Pricing Power — 0.78 / 0.78 confidence

Global scale, trusted brands, specification positions, distribution and an installed base create switching and replacement advantages. Gross margin expanded from 39.7% in 2022 to 42.6% in 2025 despite uneven demand, consistent with mix and pricing power. Confidence would improve with explicit price/volume/mix disclosure from the FY2025 annual report and Q2 2026 presentation.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.76 / 0.78 confidence

Digitization of access, electronic adoption and fragmented local markets provide a long runway, while the repeatable bolt-on program supplies reinvestment opportunities. The constraint is incremental return: more than SEK 53bn of business purchases in 2023 pushed net debt and goodwill sharply higher, while ROIC settled near 10–11%. Confidence would improve with management's organic/acquired growth bridge and post-acquisition return targets.

### Capital Allocation — 0.68 / 0.76 confidence

Capital deployment is coherent but acquisition-heavy. FY2023–25 business purchases were approximately SEK 53.6bn, 12.1bn and 11.4bn. FCF remained strong and dividends consumed only about 29–35% of FCF, but share count was broadly flat and debt funded much of the 2023 step-up. The record is positive, though recent large-deal returns are not yet proven. Firm-up source: FY2025 business-combinations note and acquisition return disclosures.

### Conservative Debt — 0.63 / 0.84 confidence

FY2025 net debt/EBITDA was 1.96×, EBIT interest cover 7.2× and cash plus one year of FCF covered current debt 1.4×. This is serviceable for a stable industrial franchise but less conservative than the 1.17× leverage of 2022; current liquidity is only around 1.0×. There is no apparent solvency issue, but acquisition appetite limits flexibility.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.84 / 0.75 confidence

The decentralized divisional structure, global brands and three-decade acquisition system are institutional rather than dependent on one founder or executive. Confidence would improve with the FY2025 governance report's succession and delegation detail.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.66 / 0.65 confidence

The long operating record and acquisition discipline suggest reasonable alignment, but this review did not fully audit remuneration metrics, insider ownership or performance-share hurdles. Firm-up source: FY2025 remuneration report and AGM materials.

**Why other strategies lost:** Wonderful and Fair is a close secondary fit, but only moderate ROIC and reliance on acquired rather than purely organic reinvestment weaken the compounding engine. Freeroll lacks a hard near-price floor and discrete catalyst. Rule Breakers is inappropriate for a mature, moderately growing incumbent.

## Overall

ASSA ABLOY is a durable cash generator with dependable conversion, a defensible installed base and a repeatable consolidation engine. The main deductions are only moderate ROIC, acquisition dependence, leverage above the pre-2023 level and a price that already recognizes much of the quality. Cash Cow wins mechanically, though the low dividend yield means shareholders still depend materially on retained cash being deployed well.

## Valuation

At SEK 348.40, the stock trades at 24.5× trailing EPS and about 22.0× the FY2026 consensus EPS of SEK 15.87. The exit-multiple model uses that current-year earnings run-rate, 8% aggregate growth, no dilution, a 22× exit P/E, a 1.93% dividend yield and five years. Growth is below the next-year consensus EPS growth of 12.4% and broadly consistent with a mature compounder whose organic growth is supplemented by acquisitions. A 22× exit multiple assumes no re-rating from the current run-rate multiple. The resulting scripted ROI is recorded in front matter; the return should be carried by earnings growth and dividends, not multiple expansion. Agent score: 0.65, reflecting strong durability offset by a full entry valuation and moderate capital returns.

## Thesis Connections

Fits a long-duration compounder thesis built around access digitization, building-security demand and consolidation of a fragmented industry. It is not a high-growth technology exposure; the thesis depends on steady organic progress plus disciplined M&A.

## Watch

- Q3 2026 organic growth and margin bridge; confirm whether negative currency masks stable underlying demand.
- Net debt/EBITDA and interest cover after further acquisitions; flag leverage above 2.5×.
- ROIC recovery toward the pre-acquisition 12% level; persistent 10% or lower would weaken the allocation case.
- Read FY2025 remuneration and business-combinations notes to firm up incentive alignment and acquisition returns.
- Track electronic/access-control mix and price/volume disclosure as evidence of moat and runway.

# RENE.LS — REN - Redes Energéticas Nacionais, SGPS, S.A.

## Business Overview

REN owns and operates Portugal's core regulated energy-network infrastructure: very-high-voltage electricity transmission and system management, high-pressure gas transmission/storage/LNG infrastructure, regional gas distribution through REN Portgás, and a small telecoms fibre business. It also owns electricity-transmission assets in Chile. The Portuguese electricity concession runs for 50 years from 2007; the principal gas concessions run for 40 years from 2006/2008. Revenue and earnings are therefore mostly regulated-asset-base returns rather than merchant energy-price exposure.

The result is a stable utility cash-yield business, not a high-growth compounder. 2025 EBITDA was about €516m–€533m depending on definition, net income attributable to ordinary shareholders was €159.8m, and the dividend rate was about €0.16/share. Yahoo's 5 Jul 2026 quote was €3.795, implying ~13.1× trailing EPS and a 4.2% dividend yield.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.68 / 0.75

REN has shown clean profitability across Yahoo's available 2022–2025 annual data: net income attributable to ordinary shareholders rose from €111.8m in 2022 to €159.8m in 2025, with no loss years. EPS rose from €0.17 to €0.24. That fits a regulated network operator with predictable allowed returns.

The caveat is cash backing. The quantitative earnings-quality script shows cumulative FCF/net income of only 0.47× over 2022–2025, mainly because 2023 FCF was sharply negative (-€619.6m) and capex/investment is material. 2024 and 2025 FCF recovered to €321.6m and €159.2m respectively. This is not accounting-fraud style poor quality, but it means reported profit is not freely distributable every year.

To firm up confidence: use the full integrated-report cash-flow notes to separate regulated tariff timing from genuine capex burden and working-capital swings.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.55 / 0.70

REN is cash-generative, but not a pure low-capex cash cow. FCF margin was 15.6% in 2025 and 33.8% in 2024, but -65.4% in 2023; the four-year average was only 8.9%. 2025 capex was elevated, with external summaries citing capex around €474.9m, up 28.9% year on year, reflecting grid/gas infrastructure and Chilean expansion.

Interpretation: the regulated asset base creates dependable cash over time, but the business requires recurring network investment. The dividend is currently covered by FCF — Yahoo's income-floor read shows dividend FCF cover of 1.53× — but the cash stream can be lumpy.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.82 / 0.80

The core fundamentals look durable. Electricity transmission and gas infrastructure are monopoly-like regulated assets with long concessions, and the company is central to Portugal's energy transition. REN's investor materials frame the business around network reliability, renewables integration, gas-network decarbonisation, and disciplined financial policy.

Yahoo's fundamental-stability script shows revenue and EPS at their 2022–2025 highs in 2025, while operating margin has compressed from 29.2% in 2022 to 23.6% in 2025. The gas network has a long-term transition risk, but REN has begun positioning around hydrogen/renewable gases, and electricity-grid investment should be structurally supported by renewable integration.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.75 / 0.65

REN does not have ordinary commercial pricing power; it has something more utility-like: regulated monopoly concessions over essential infrastructure. The moat is structural — grid assets, concession rights, regulation, and system-critical reliability — but allowed returns are set by regulators, not by REN's unilateral pricing discretion.

Gross margins remain high but have drifted down from 75.3% in 2022 to 64.5% in 2025, so the score is strong rather than exceptional. The key risk is regulatory reset, not customer churn.

### Capital Allocation — 0.62 / 0.65

Capital allocation is mostly regulated-network reinvestment plus dividends. The dividend rate of ~€0.16/share equates to a 4.2% yield at €3.795 and a payout ratio around 53% of Yahoo trailing earnings, with FCF cover in 2025. REN also expanded in Chile, including the 2025 Tensa acquisition, while continuing Portugal network investment.

This is sensible for an infrastructure utility, but not obviously high-return allocation: debt is meaningful, buybacks are not part of the case, and capex has risen. The score reflects adequate stewardship rather than a clear per-share compounding machine.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.50 / 0.75

Returns are stable but modest. The returns-on-capital script shows ROIC around 4.6%–6.1% during 2022–2025, ROCE around 6.2%–6.8%, and ROE rising to 10.5% in 2025. For a regulated utility this can be acceptable if it sits above the allowed cost of capital, but it is not the high-return profile of a wonderful compounder.

The positive read is stability: ROIC has been flat-to-rising and goodwill is immaterial. The negative read is that leverage partly lifts ROE above operating returns.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.48 / 0.75

Debt is the main constraint. Net debt/EBITDA was 4.67× in 2025 and peaked near 4.98× in 2023; this is tolerable for a regulated utility but not conservative. EBIT interest coverage was 4.7× in 2025 and weakest at 3.6× in 2024. Current debt was close to €995m at 2025 year-end, while cash plus one year's FCF covered only about 0.2× of current debt in the script's simplified view, so refinancing access matters.

Because the business is regulated and infrastructure-like, this is stretched rather than alarming. It would become a thesis problem if rates/refinancing costs rose again or regulation cut allowed returns.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.85 / 0.70

The business is institutional and asset/concession-led. It should not depend heavily on any single executive; the grid, concessions, regulatory model, and operating teams matter more than one person's vision. Management quality still matters for regulation, financing, and capex execution, but the thesis would not break if the CEO changed.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.50 / 0.45

Evidence is incomplete. Yahoo reports high insider/strategic ownership (~47%) and institutional ownership (~27%), but governance flags are mixed, with shareholder-rights and board-risk scores elevated. I have not yet reviewed the remuneration report in enough detail to judge whether executive pay is tied to long-term per-share value, returns on capital, service quality, or easier accounting targets.

This is a mid score with low confidence until the remuneration policy, board independence, major holders, and related-party governance are inspected directly.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Wonderful and Fair:** loses on reinvestment/runway and returns. REN is durable, but ROIC around 5%–6% and regulated returns do not create a high-return compounder profile; the regulated capex runway is real but not obviously high-return.
- **Rule Breakers:** poor fit. Electricity-grid investment is a structural tailwind, but REN is a mature regulated utility, not an emerging dominant growth disruptor; durable-growth and right-place-and-time are too weak for this framework.
- **Freeroll:** incomplete setup. The dividend provides some support, but price/book is ~1.65×, tangible book is negative due concession intangibles, and there is no obvious ≤4-year catalyst forcing a re-rating.

## Overall

REN is a stable regulated utility cash-yield stock. The attraction is durability: essential network assets, long concessions, predictable earnings, and a covered dividend. The weakness is valuation and balance sheet: at €3.795 the stock is not obviously cheap, the dividend yield is moderate rather than exceptional, and leverage is stretched-but-normal for the asset class.

This is watchable as a defensive income utility, but not compelling at the current price. I would get more interested if the yield moved back toward the high-single-digit range, if regulated returns clearly improved, or if the share price fell close to a defensible floor without a corresponding deterioration in debt/refinancing risk.

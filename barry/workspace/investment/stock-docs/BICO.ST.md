# BICO.ST — BICO Group AB (publ)

## Business Overview

BICO is a Swedish life-science tools group focused on automating the life-science lab. Its portfolio now centres on two areas:

- **Life Science Solutions**: lab instrumentation, consumables and workflow applications for cell culture, imaging, sorting, sample preparation, diagnostics manufacturing, single-cell and NGS workflows. Brands include CELLINK, SCIENION, CYTENA/Cellenion and related consumables/platform assets.
- **Lab Automation**: Biosero-led lab automation software/integration, especially Green Button Go, plus automated workcells and workflow integration for pharma, biotech and diagnostics customers.

The strategy after BICO 2.0 is to simplify the portfolio toward lab automation and selected workflows for pharma/biotech. In 2025, BICO divested MatTek and Visikol to Sartorius for USD 80m, reduced debt, and rebuilt around commercial excellence, cost discipline, and software/AI-enabled lab automation.

Current financial picture is still turnaround-stage: FY2025 revenue was SEK 1,497m, down 7.9% organically; gross margin was 52.1%; adjusted EBITDA margin was about 1%; and operating cash flow was SEK 68.4m. Q1 2026 showed early demand improvement with 11.0% organic growth but still negative adjusted EBITDA of SEK -11.4m and a continuing-operations net loss of SEK -131.3m.

## Trait Assessment

### Downside Support / Valuation Floor — 0.55 score, 0.70 confidence

The floor is plausible but not hard. At SEK 14.48/share, BICO trades around SEK 1.02bn market cap and about 0.64x book value; Q1 2026 common equity was SEK 1.61bn, or roughly SEK 22.8/share. Liquidity is meaningful: Q1 cash and short-term investments were SEK 744m against SEK 691m total debt after the March 2026 convertible settlement and EUR 40m secured bond issue.

The caveat is asset quality. The tangible book floor is only about SEK 4.7/share at Q1 2026, and the business has a history of large impairments and disposals. Net cash is no longer the whole story after the new bond; the floor depends on book value, remaining portfolio value, and the strategic value of Biosero/CELLINK/SCIENION rather than an undeniable net-net cash backstop. I use SEK 12/share as a conservative downside case: below current book, above tangible book, and roughly where further impairment/cash-burn risk is still being recognized.

Confidence would firm up with the full Q2 2026 balance sheet and a cleaner segment-level cash-burn bridge after the bond/refinancing dust settles.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.48 score, 0.70 confidence

The business is not a melting ice cube in the obvious secular sense — lab automation, biological workflow standardization, and AI-ready lab data are attractive end markets. BICO cites a serviceable market around USD 2.7bn growing around 11% CAGR, with lab automation and life-science tools as the bulk of the opportunity.

But the operating record is still weak. Revenue fell from SEK 2.12bn in 2022 to SEK 1.50bn in 2025, with negative operating margins and recurring losses. FY2025 weakness was partly macro/capex-driven — muted academia, NIH funding pressure, biotech softness, project delays — and partly self-inflicted from the prior acquisition roll-up and Biosero project execution. Q1 2026 organic growth of 11% and positive operating cash flow are encouraging, but adjusted EBITDA was still negative.

Verdict: fundamentals may be stabilising after portfolio simplification, but this is not yet proven. The key proof would be several quarters of organic growth plus positive adjusted EBITDA without one-off working-capital help.

### Mispriced for an Identifiable Reason — 0.56 score, 0.70 confidence

The market appears to be pricing BICO as a broken roll-up: years of losses, impairments, project delays, convertible-refinancing risk, and distrust of management execution. That distrust gained fresh support on 6 July when the chair and two other directors announced simultaneous departures effective 31 July, with an extraordinary general meeting required to elect replacements. At SEK 14.48 the shares are only about 12% above the SEK 12 stressed floor, with Yahoo showing a SEK 12.94–40.88 52-week range, 0.64x book and 0.68x trailing sales.

The variant view is that the market may be over-extrapolating the worst part of the cleanup. BICO has already sold non-core assets, simplified the portfolio, settled the SEK 1,008m convertible, restored liquidity through the EUR 40m bond, and narrowed the strategy around lab automation/workflow software. If Q1's 11% organic growth is the start of a recovery rather than a one-quarter bounce, the equity could re-rate toward a normal small-cap life-science-tools multiple.

The edge is only moderate: this is not hidden information, the board disruption validates part of the discount, and the company still needs to earn back trust. The mispricing depends on execution improvement showing up in reported numbers.

### Identifiable, Time-Bound Catalyst — 0.44 score, 0.65 confidence

The catalyst is a 2026–2028 operating inflection: recovery in customer capex, execution of the BICO 2.0 simplification, closure of legacy Biosero projects, product launches such as GoSimple, and a move from negative/low adjusted EBITDA toward the stated target of EBITDA margin less capitalized development cost above 10%.

This is identifiable, but not forceful. There is no announced sale, buyback, dividend, takeout process, or hard near-term event that compels a re-rating. The market will likely wait for quarterly evidence: organic growth, positive adjusted EBITDA, lower project overrun noise, and stable cash. The 19 August Q2 report is the next concrete checkpoint; the pending board transition adds uncertainty rather than a clean catalyst.

Confirming signs: Q2–Q4 2026 positive organic growth, positive adjusted EBITDA, reduced restructuring/project re-estimation charges, and no renewed liquidity stress.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Rule-breakers**: BICO has a credible “right place/time” exposure to lab automation and AI-ready workflows, but it lacks the demonstrated durable hypergrowth, top-dog economics, and customer-devotion evidence needed for a high-confidence rule-breaker score.
- **Wonderful-and-fair**: returns on capital, earnings quality, and reinvestment economics are not yet demonstrated after impairments and losses.
- **Cash-cow**: not applicable; BICO is not producing dependable surplus free cash or returning capital.

## Overall

BICO is a speculative turnaround/freeroll candidate, not a core compounder. The good case is that the cleanup is mostly complete, the market is ignoring improving demand, and the remaining assets are worth materially more than the depressed equity price. The bad case is that project execution, capex cycles, weak profitability, and a poorly handled board transition keep eating the asset floor before the recovery becomes visible.

I would keep it watched, but the current score should stay restrained until the operating inflection is visible in reported EBITDA and cash flow.

This is not a quality-business expected return; the soft asset floor and governance disruption deserve a large discount to the headline asymmetry. My valuation-aware agent score is therefore 0.43.

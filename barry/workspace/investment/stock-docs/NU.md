# NU — Nu Holdings Ltd.

## Business Overview

Nu Holdings (NYSE: NU) is the holding company for Nubank, the largest digital bank in Latin America — a mobile-first bank with no branches serving **139 million customers** across Brazil (~118m), Mexico (~16m) and Colombia (~5.2m). Founded in 2013 by David Vélez, Edward Wible and Cristina Junqueira, it is headquartered in São Paulo, listed on the NYSE (Cayman-incorporated), and valued at ~$71b at the time of writing.

**How it makes money** (managerial P&L, Q2 2026): gross revenue of **$5.88b** split among Credit Income $3.60b (61%), Float income $1.45b (25%) and Fee income $0.82b (14%). It funds itself largely with deposits ($45.3b) priced at 88% of interbank, and earns a 22.9% NIM / 12.4% risk-adjusted NIM on a $39.4b credit portfolio (cards $26b, unsecured $10.3b, secured $3.1b). Cost-to-serve is ~$1/active customer/month, with an efficiency ratio of 19.5% — roughly a third to a half of incumbent Brazilian banks. Gross profit is contributed ~41% by credit, 34% by float and 25% by fees.

Q2 2026 (quarter ended 30 June 2026, reported 13 Aug 2026) was a milestone: **net income $1.06b (+49% YoY, +17% QoQ), ROE 33%**, gross revenue +39% FXN, NIM +180bps QoQ, cost of credit -9% QoQ. FY2025 (ended 31 Dec, 20-F AR): revenue $16.3b, net income $2.87b, assets $74.9b, equity $11.3b; 9,300+ employees. Fiscal year is calendar (Dec), confirmed by the most recent SEC filings.

## Trait Assessment

### Right Place and Time — 0.92 / 0.85. The industry setup is exceptional.

**Industry.** Digital banking in Latin America is still structurally under-penetrated: Brazil remains over-banked by branches with high fee loads, and Mexico/Colombia even earlier. Nu reaches only 16.5% of Mexico's adult population — comparable to Brazil in 2020 — with Mexico cohorts monetizing earlier (ARPAC $12.3 at the same stage vs $5.6 in Brazil). 90+ delinquency is improving for Nu across every income band since July 2025 while peer banks deteriorated, evidence the digital-credit wave still retains wins.

Growth drivers are durable (branchless economics, PIX/USP/SPEI modernization, regulation standardizing payment rails by year-end 2026 in Mexico, super-app credit culture) and the market is enormous relative to penetration. Main structural risks: Brazil macro/currency and Selic (14% after 4 consecutive 25bp cuts), competition in Mexico from Incumbents, and regulation.

### Top Dog — 0.85 / 0.80

**Position.** Nu is the largest digital bank by customers in Latin America, the country leader in **Primary Banking Relationships (PBR)** and the #1 provider of financial services to small businesses in Brazil. It serves 86% of Brazil's active adult population.

**Edge.** 118M customers in Brazil, the mass segment essentially owned; 16m in Mexico (now the largest digital bank there), 5m+ in Colombia. The moat is the data, the cost base and the brand — no incumbent has the same unit economics.

**Threats.** Brazil's incumbents (Bradesco, Itaú, Santander, Banco do Brasil) still control most credit; Mexico's telecom-backed wallets (CoDi/SPEI) and Mercado Pago compete. Lead is real but in the most lucrative segments the competitive intensity is rising — hence not 0.95.

### Sustainable Competitive Advantage — 0.85 / 0.80

**Moat evidence:**
- **Data flywheel / AI:** over a decade of transaction data on 100M+ customers, monetized through NuFormer (foundation model) now in production across credit-card underwriting in Brazil and Mexico, unsecured in Brazil, and AI agents that resolve 60%+ of Brazil customer support at or above human parity.
- **Cost structure:** ~$1 per active customer per month (~USD 0.6-0.9 real) vs hundreds for a branch model → structural pricing advantage on cards/fees (NIM 22.9%, cost of credit 12.4% risk-adjusted).
- **Switching costs / primary banking:** PBR customers have delinquency ~half the portfolio average; it deepens the relationship and the data.
- **Brand &amp; trust:** overwhelmingly negative-incumbent sentiment pre-IPO, now the most-trusted Brazilian bank by surveys; low churn, high activity (83.5% monthly activity).

**Pricing-power evidence** — NIM of 22.9% and fee line +49% YoY; but selector-power is secondary to scale. Confidence 0.80 because moats in banking are secured by data and brand, both present.

### Durable Growth — 0.82 / 0.80

**Stage & metric.** Growth-phase; revenue is the right metric. Q2 2026 annualized run rate ~$23.5b (gross revenue $5.88b), +39% FXN YoY; FY25 +$16.3b (vs FY24's ~$12.4b). Customers +4m in Q2 to 139m (+13% YoY); credit portfolio +37% YoY; deposits +18%; ARPAC $17 (+22% YoY). Net income grew ~2.6x YoY in Q2 (+49%).

**Second derivative.** Customer growth is decelerating (from 25-30% to ~13-20%/yr) but *monetization* is accelerating — ARPAC up 22% YoY and sequentially, fees +53% YoY, business value per user rising. Credit book +37% YoY is the compounding engine.

**FCF tracking.** Banks: OCF FY25 $3.5b vs NI $2.87b — fine; capex marginal (net PPE only ~$60m for the whole holding). Deliberate reinvestment (R$45b/2026 in Brazil, Mexico expansion, AI) currently *suppresses* near-term margin — its first phase of profitable growth is just beginning to show.

### Organic Growth — 0.95 / 0.90

All growth is organic — no M&A of any size (small dev-shop tuck-ins like Easynvest in 2020). Goodwill is ~$0.4b on $75b assets. Share count is essentially flat (~4.9bn diluted vs ~4.7bn at IPO), SBC modest. The engine is native customer acquisition, not bought growth.

### Management Backing — 0.88 / 0.75

**People.** Founder & CEO David Vélez (ex-Sequoia) is genuinely exceptional and long-term — the company publishes founder letters with Munger-style philosophy. Co-founder Junqueira/CFO institute strong operator culture; small exec team. They recently built a US approval pathway (conditional OCC license Jan 2026) but the core story is Brazil/Mexico/Colombia.

**Backers.** Berkshire (initial $1b co-invest at IPO), Sequoia, Kaszek, Tiger, Tencent, DST — the cleanest bucket in LatAm fintech. 82% institutional ownership.

**Confidence 0.75** — the leadership is founder-driven and the 2026 proxy has not been re-read line by line; the 20-F/remuneration details would firm it up.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.62 / 0.65

**Scoring basis.** SBC modest (~1.5%/yr dilution), share count essentially flat. Insider holding is low at ~2% (mostly founder Vélez ~15-20% of class-B-style stock but diluted overall); institutions 82%. Pay mixes long-term metrics (stock award to CFO July 2026) but the bulk is granted not bought — moderate alignment rather than strong bought-in skin in the game.

The **open question** flagged for confidence 0.65: the exact 2026 proxy details (SBC quantum, long-term plan vesting) have not been audited in this review. No self-dealing or reprice-option red flags observed.

### Customer Devotion — 0.88 / 0.75

**What the numbers say.** Activity 83.5% monthly (Brazil 86%), 4m net new customers/quarter, PBR leadership, and customers with Nu as primary bank use much more (delinquency ~half). No explicit NPS disclosed this quarter; social-driven organic growth. Branchless loyalty is preference-based (no contractual lock-in), a genuine NPS proxy: Brazil NPS has historically been above 90.

Confidence 0.75 because NPS is not reported formally in the last release — a named source would raise it.

### Why the other strategies lost

- **Wonderful &amp; Fair** (would be ~0.75): high ROC and durable moat, but the business is reinvestment- and growth-carried with margin deliberately suppressed by market expansion; not a fair-value harvest. A compounder is better captured by the growth lens here.
- **Cash Cow** (would be ~0.72): meets the quality/cash bar, but Nu has huge reinvestment runway (Mexico, Colombia, upmarket) — a cow frames away this growth.
- **Freeroll** (low ~0.4s): no identified downside floor near the current price and no time-bound re-rating catalyst; the multiple is mid, the upside is shipping results. Not a floor+catalyst trade.

## Overall

Nu is a high-momentum compounding fintech: top-dog brand with a real data moat, cash-engine unit economics ($0.7-1 cost to serve), yet still early in Mexico/Colombia. Q2 2026 crossed $1b quarterly net income — its profitability is real but not mature.

**Top detractors** from a higher score: (1) decelerating customer growth — revenue quality now a function of monetization, a 2-year clock; (2) Brazil rate cycle + macro (Selic 14%); (3) credit quality normalized after riskier cohorts (90+ NPL 6.9%, +35bps); (4) competition rising in Mexico from incumbent capital; (5) the share multiple is not distressed — 20x trailing, 13x forward.

Current rating (agent_score 0.68): best-of-breed LatAm fintech at a reasonable but not cheap price; ROI hinges on the growth path holding high-teens EPS gains for the decade.

# Trait: Scale Economies

**Production role:** selected direct-ridge feature for both `s1` and `r`.

**What we're looking for:**
Unit cost keeps falling as volume grows — high fixed costs spread over more output, purchasing
power, or learning-curve effects — so subscale players cannot match the leaders' cost and margin,
and are competed out.

**Expected direction:** stronger scale economies raise `s1` and lower `r`: persistent cost
advantages concentrate volume on the largest producer and squeeze out the tail. Where returns to
scale flatten early, smaller competitors can survive.

**Scoring guidance (0–1, how strong the returns to scale are):**

* **0.70–1.00** Steep, sustained unit-cost decline with volume; subscale is structurally unviable
* **0.40–0.69** Some advantage, but it flattens at moderate scale (many can reach it)
* **0.00–0.39** Roughly constant returns; small players compete on equal cost footing

**Documentation:** the source of the scaling (fixed-cost leverage, buying power, learning curve),
and evidence the cost gap persists rather than flattening at moderate size.

# ETN — Eaton Corporation plc

## Business Overview

Eaton is a power-management industrial: electrical equipment and systems, aerospace components, and a soon-to-be separated mobility business. The investment case is mostly Electrical + Aerospace, not legacy vehicle exposure.

2025 sales were **$27.4B**, up **10%**, with **8% organic growth**, adjusted EPS of **$12.07** (+12%), segment margin of **24.5%**, operating cash flow of **$4.47B**, and free cash flow of roughly **$3.6B**. Segment mix is attractive: Electrical Americas was **$13.3B** of sales at **29.9%** margin; Electrical Global **$6.8B** at **19.4%**; Aerospace **$4.25B** at **23.9%**. Vehicle/eMobility were weaker and lower-growth, and Eaton has now signed a Reverse Morris Trust agreement to separate Mobility and combine it with Dana, targeted to close in Q1 2027.

Q2 2026 strengthened the demand case. Sales rose **21% to $8.53B**, including **14% organic growth**; adjusted EPS reached **$3.15**, operating cash flow was **$1.1B**, and free cash flow was **$874M**. Electrical Americas organic sales grew **18%**, Electrical Global **18%**, and Aerospace **7%**. Rolling 12-month organic orders were up **41%**, **33%**, and **17%**, respectively; total backlog rose **33%** in Electrical Americas, **103%** in Electrical Global, and **28%** in Aerospace. Management raised 2026 organic growth guidance to **11–13%** and adjusted EPS guidance to **$13.40–$13.60**. The trade-off is acquisition financing: June gross debt was about **$20.6B**, largely funding Ultra PCS and the **$9.55B** Boyd Thermal acquisition.

## Trait Assessment

Best-fit strategy: **Wonderful and Fair**. Eaton is a proven, high-margin compounder with a major reinvestment runway in electrical infrastructure, data centers, grid investment, and aerospace. The issue is not quality; it is price and the balance-sheet step-up after acquisitions.

Universal disqualifiers checked: no evidence found of accounting-integrity failure, imminent solvency risk, hidden equity-threatening liabilities, abusive control, or a business-model kill-shot. The company is eligible.

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.80 / confidence 0.90

Eaton has demonstrated consistent earning power. 2023–2025 net income attributable to ordinary shareholders was **$3.22B → $3.79B → $4.09B**, diluted EPS **$8.02 → $9.50 → $10.45**, and adjusted EPS **$12.07** in 2025. The business remained profitable while absorbing commodity/wage inflation, restructuring charges, and acquisition/divestiture costs.

Cash backing remains good. Q2 operating cash flow rose **23% to $1.1B** and free cash flow rose **22% to $874M**; first-half operating cash flow improved by **$478M**, helped by working capital. The haircut is the widening gap between GAAP and adjusted profit: Q2 adjusted EPS of **$3.15** added back **$0.50** of intangible amortization, **$0.49** of acquisition/divestiture charges, and **$0.05** of restructuring, while GAAP EPS fell to **$2.11** from **$2.51**. Adjusted earnings are decision-useful, but acquisition-related exclusions are now too large to treat as trivial.

What would firm this up: a 7–10 year FCF/net-income bridge and working-capital trend, rather than the 3-year annual-report window used here.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.66 / confidence 0.78

For an industrial operating company, ROIC/ROCE is the right lens. A rough 2025 ROIC using EBIT less tax over average debt + equity less cash/securities is in the mid-teens, not a clean 20%+ standout. Segment margins, however, show strong underlying economics: Electrical Americas near **30%**, Electrical Global near **19%**, Aerospace near **24%**. Returns on tangible operating capital are likely stronger than headline ROIC because goodwill/intangibles from acquisitions are large.

The score is good, not elite, because the capital base has stepped up sharply. Eaton paid **$1.53B** for Ultra PCS and **$9.55B** for Boyd Thermal in the first half of 2026; goodwill and intangible amortization are now material, and the all-in return on those purchase prices is unproven. Strong orders and margins support the operating assets, but acquisition-adjusted incremental returns will determine whether the latest allocation creates value.

What would firm this up: company ROIC history including/excluding goodwill, and post-acquisition incremental-return disclosure for Fibrebond, Boyd Thermal, and Ultra PCS.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.76 / confidence 0.78

The moat is based on scale, installed base, engineering qualification, mission-critical products, channel relationships, and switching costs. Electrical equipment and aerospace components are not casual purchases; customers care about reliability, certification, uptime, and integration risk. Eaton's ability to hold very high Electrical Americas margins while backlog and orders grow supports a real moat.

Q2 provides better evidence, though still indirectly. Consolidated segment margin was **23.1%**, above guidance, while commodity and wage inflation reduced Electrical Americas and Electrical Global margins by roughly **470** and **460 basis points**, respectively. Higher sales, mix, and operating efficiencies offset much of that pressure; Electrical Americas margin improved **190 basis points sequentially to 27.5%**. This supports strong customer value and cost pass-through, but the filing still does not cleanly separate price from volume.

What would firm this up: management or segment disclosure separating price, volume, mix, and cost inflation; plus share data in core electrical categories.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.90 / confidence 0.90

This is Eaton's strongest trait. The company is exposed to several long-duration capital cycles: AI/data-center power demand, grid modernization, electrification, reindustrialization, infrastructure investment, and aerospace recovery/growth. Q2 broadened the evidence beyond a single data-center statistic: management raised 2026 organic growth guidance to **11–13%**, rolling orders rose **41%** in Electrical Americas, **33%** in Electrical Global, and **17%** in Aerospace, and Electrical/Aerospace book-to-bill was about **1.2**.

The reinvestment routes are concrete: capacity expansion, systems growth in Electrical Americas, global electrical infrastructure, aerospace content/aftermarket, and targeted acquisitions. The June 2026 Mobility/Dana RMT should also sharpen the portfolio toward higher-growth Electrical and Aerospace assets; Eaton says it will be immediately accretive to organic growth and operating margins on closing. The main caveat is that part of the runway is being accelerated with M&A, not purely organic growth, and elevated capex/working capital may absorb cash while the backlog converts.

### Capital Allocation — 0.68 / confidence 0.85

Allocation has been broadly rational: Eaton reinvests behind high-demand electrical/aerospace markets, pays a covered dividend, buys back shares over time, and is pruning Mobility. On June 11, 2026, Eaton announced a definitive RMT agreement to combine Mobility with Dana: Eaton values Mobility at about **$5.1B**, Eaton shareholders should own at least **50.1%** of the combined Dana/Mobility company, and Eaton expects an approximately **$1.1B** cash distribution for capital allocation including debt repayment. Shares outstanding declined from **401.1M diluted** in 2023 to **391.2M** in 2025, so repurchases have been real rather than merely cosmetic.

The caveat is acquisition and transaction execution. Eaton spent **$11.08B** on acquisitions in the first half, financed mainly with **$8.5B** of U.S. notes, **€1.2B** of euro notes, and commercial paper. No shares were repurchased in the half, and management does not intend to repurchase stock in 2026 because of Boyd Thermal. These deals may be strategically sound, especially around thermal/data-center and aerospace exposure, but the purchase prices, integration returns, and debt paydown now dominate the allocation assessment.

What would firm this up: purchase multiples, synergy targets, integration milestones, and later impairment/return evidence.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.45 / confidence 0.92

This is the main quality haircut. At June 30, Eaton had **$483M** of cash, **$212M** of short-term investments, **$2.09B** of short-term debt, **$11M** of current long-term debt, and **$18.51B** of long-term debt. Commercial paper of **$2.09B** was outstanding, and Q2 net interest expense rose to **$201M** from **$71M**. Liquidity is adequate — the **$4B** revolver was undrawn and Eaton was covenant-compliant — but the balance sheet is no longer conservative. The Mobility/Dana transaction should bring about **$1.1B** of cash in 2027, but that only partly offsets the acquisition financing and remains conditional on closing.

First-half operating cash flow improved, but acquisition cash outlay was **$11.08B**, buybacks are paused, and the company plans roughly **$1.15B** of 2026 capex. For a cyclical industrial, the balance sheet is stretched-but-manageable rather than conservative; integration and debt reduction must now go right.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.82 / confidence 0.74

Eaton does not look like a one-person thesis. The advantage sits in product breadth, installed base, manufacturing footprint, channel relationships, engineering capability, and customer qualification rather than a founder or star operator. CEO Paulo Ruiz matters, but the business is institutional and has a visible executive bench across Electrical, Industrial, Finance, HR, Strategy, and regional leadership.

What would firm this up: more explicit succession planning detail and deeper board/management tenure review.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.74 / confidence 0.80

The 2026 proxy indicates better-than-average alignment. Short-term incentives used **Adjusted EPS** and **Adjusted Operating Cash Flow**; 2025 targets were adjusted EPS **$12.00** and adjusted OCF **$4.8B**, with actuals of **$12.07** and **$4.47B**. Importantly, the compensation committee reduced the formulaic payout from **96%** to **70%** after excluding one-time/non-operational items, which is a useful governance signal. Long-term incentives are tied to relative TSR through PSUs. Eaton also has ownership guidelines, anti-hedging/pledging policies, and clawback provisions.

The soft spot is that EPS can be gameable via buybacks and adjusted metrics can normalize away real recurring costs. Overall, the plan points management broadly toward shareholder outcomes, but it is not a pure ROIC/per-share intrinsic-value system.

### Why the other strategies lost

**Rule Breakers:** Eaton benefits from major growth waves, but it is not an emerging disruptor with explosive optionality; it is a mature industrial compounder. Growth is strong for the category, but the thesis is quality + runway, not asymmetric venture-style upside.

**Freeroll:** Eaton is not cheap and does not have a clear valuation floor/catalyst setup. The Mobility/Dana RMT should help portfolio quality, but the stock is priced as a premium compounder, not as a mispriced special situation.

## Overall

Eaton is a high-quality compounder in the right markets. The business has real demand visibility, strong segment margins, improving mix toward Electrical/Aerospace, and a credible multi-year runway from data centers, electrification, grid investment, and aerospace. I would want it on the watchlist.

The stock is not screamingly attractive at the current price. At **$415.20**, it trades around **30.8x** the midpoint of raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance. That can work if Eaton compounds earnings at a low-double-digit rate, but the margin of safety is thin and the post-acquisition leverage step-up raises the bar for execution.

Bottom line: **wonderful business, fair-to-rich price**. Attractive enough to monitor closely; not obviously a bargain.

# PME.AX — Pro Medicus Limited

## Business Overview

Pro Medicus is an Australian healthcare imaging software company. Its core product is Visage 7, an enterprise imaging / PACS platform used by hospitals, imaging centres and health systems to store, view and interpret medical images. Revenue comes from long-term software contracts, implementation/professional services, support and related products across Australia, North America and Europe. The economic engine is unusually attractive: enterprise software sold into mission-critical clinical workflows, very high gross margins, low capital intensity, and long contract duration.

The business has been shifting deeper into large North American health systems. Recent contracts/renewals include a 5-year A$16m Ohio State University renewal, a 5-year A$28m Allegheny Health renewal, a 7-year A$16m TidalHealth contract, and an August 2026 7-year A$23m St. Luke's Health System contract spanning ten medical centres across three states. The St. Luke's deployment includes Visage 7 Viewer and Workflow in the cloud, uses a transaction-based model with potential upside, and targets a Q1 2027 go-live.[4] These contracts are not huge relative to the market cap, but they support the core thesis: large health systems keep choosing or renewing Visage, cloud adoption is broadening, and the installed base can compound over time.

Universal disqualifier screen: no obvious accounting-integrity, solvency, hidden-liability, value-leaking-control, or business-model kill-shot issue found in this pass. The company is net-cash/near debt-free and highly profitable. Source quality caveat: the company site and ASX website were partly hostile to scraping; financial statements here use Yahoo Finance/yfinance extraction, and ASX announcement facts use the ASX Markit announcements API.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.90 / confidence 0.85

Facts: FY2022–FY2025 net income rose from A$44.4m → A$60.6m → A$82.8m → A$115.2m, with diluted EPS A$0.425 → A$0.580 → A$0.791 → A$1.101. Free cash flow tracked reported profit well: A$52.6m, A$56.1m, A$75.3m, A$104.0m over the same period. Operating income rose from A$62.3m to A$157.6m, and operating margins are very high. Interpretation: this is not a turnaround or adjusted-EBITDA story; reported earnings are real and cash-backed.

The main caveat is history length: I have a clean four-year statement extraction rather than a full 7–10 year cycle in this pass. Confidence would firm up with the FY2025 annual report plus a 10-year financial table.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.90 / confidence 0.80

Facts: FY2025 net income of A$115.2m on average equity roughly A$222m implies ROE above 50%; Yahoo reports return on equity around 76.8% and return on assets around 26.9%. Invested capital is modest for the earnings base, and debt is trivial. Interpretation: the percentage ratios are partly “software business with little capital employed”, but that is the point — the business produces large profits without needing much incremental tangible capital.

Confidence would improve with a hand-built ROIC series from annual reports, including treatment of capitalised development/intangibles.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.90 / confidence 0.75

Facts: gross margin is effectively software-like — Yahoo reports gross margin around 99.9%, and FY2025 cost of revenue was only A$0.3m against A$212.9m gross profit. The moat appears to come from switching costs, workflow integration, clinical risk, speed/performance, and the difficulty of replacing a PACS platform inside a hospital network. Renewals with OSU and Allegheny support stickiness, while the St. Luke's win supports continued competitive success in cloud-based enterprise imaging.[4]

Interpretation: this is a structurally advantaged niche, not a commodity software seat. The missing evidence is direct pricing data: contract price uplifts, churn/renewal rate, or customer-level expansion metrics. Confidence would firm up with management commentary from FY2025/FY2026 results calls and renewal economics.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.82 / confidence 0.75

Facts: diagnostic imaging volumes and data intensity keep rising, large hospital systems continue modernising imaging infrastructure, and PME is still winning new and renewed North American health-system contracts. The August 2026 St. Luke's win adds a ten-medical-centre deployment across three states, with Viewer and Workflow delivered through PME's cloud platform and transactional pricing that allows upside.[4] The company has a long runway if Visage continues displacing legacy PACS/RIS infrastructure and expanding modules across existing sites.

Interpretation: the runway is real but not infinite; the addressable market is specialised, sales cycles are long, and growth depends on winning large enterprise contracts rather than cheap self-serve distribution. Confidence would improve with a sourced TAM/penetration estimate and backlog/contracted-revenue detail.

### Capital Allocation — 0.84 / confidence 0.75

Facts: the company has grown predominantly organically, pays covered dividends, has made modest buybacks, and has avoided meaningful leverage. FY2025 cash dividends paid were A$49.1m versus A$104.0m FCF; FY2025 repurchases were A$7.9m. It retained substantial cash and marketable liquidity.

Interpretation: allocation is conservative and owner-friendly. The weakness is not obvious value destruction; it is the usual question for a richly valued compounder — whether buybacks at high multiples create value. Confidence would improve with the annual report’s capital-management discussion and buyback price detail.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.98 / confidence 0.90

Facts: FY2025 total debt was about A$2.3m, essentially lease debt, against A$107.5m ending cash; Yahoo’s current info shows total cash around A$221.8m and debt around A$2.5m. Interest income exceeds interest expense. Interpretation: balance-sheet risk is negligible; a downturn would not force the company’s hand.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.58 / confidence 0.70

Facts: Pro Medicus still has strong founder/CEO association — Dr Sam Hupert remains central to the company’s public identity and strategy. The product and customer base are now institutional assets, but the market still appears to place meaningful weight on founder stewardship.

Interpretation: this is the biggest wonderful-and-fair quality blemish. Losing a founder-CEO would probably not break the product, but it could hurt strategy, culture, investor confidence and major-account execution. Confidence would firm up with board/succession disclosure in the latest annual report.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.78 / confidence 0.70

Facts: Yahoo reports insiders hold about 49% of shares, which is a strong alignment signal. Share count has been broadly stable around 104–105m, so dilution is not a major leak. Interpretation: ownership alignment is very strong; remuneration design was not fully reviewed in this pass.

Confidence would improve with the FY2025 remuneration report: STI/LTI metrics, vesting periods, hurdles, ownership requirements and related-party checks.

### Strategy fit / near-tie

The scorer flags a genuine near-tie: Wonderful-and-Fair wins on score × confidence (0.657 vs Rule Breakers 0.652), while Rule Breakers has the higher raw quality score (0.859 vs 0.842). I am framing the doc as Wonderful-and-Fair because the company is already a proven, profitable, cash-generative compounder, and the central investment question is whether the price is fair.

Rule Breakers remains a credible secondary lens. PME scores strongly on right-place-and-time (hospital imaging modernisation), top-dog (large health-system wins and renewals), sustainable advantage (workflow integration/switching costs), durable growth (FY2022–FY2025 profit and FCF compounding), organic growth, management, and customer evidence. The weak spots are evidence depth rather than business weakness: I do not yet have clean market-share, NRR/churn, or customer-satisfaction data, so top-dog and customer-devotion confidence stay below high-conviction levels.

Freeroll is a poor fit. There is no cheap downside-supported setup and no ≤4-year re-rating catalyst; the stock trades on a premium compounder multiple, not near a valuation floor. The freeroll score is intentionally low despite strong business stability.

## Overall

PME is a genuinely exceptional business: mission-critical software, almost absurd gross margins, clean cash-backed profits, net cash, founder-led stewardship, and a long runway in hospital imaging modernisation. The quality score is high.

The stock score is lower because valuation is doing a lot of damage. At A$176.67, the market is still paying roughly 98× the FY2026 forward EPS estimate I used as the current earnings anchor. Even assuming strong growth and a still-premium 55× exit P/E, the model only gives low-single-digit annualised returns. This is a “wonderful business, not obviously a wonderful buy” setup.

## Valuation

Method: exit-multiple on earnings per share.

Inputs used: price A$176.67; current earnings anchor A$1.803/share, based on Yahoo forward EPS as a “today” run-rate estimate rather than stale FY2025 EPS of A$1.101; five-year growth path of 22%, 18%, 15%, 12%, 10%; exit multiple 55× earnings; dilution -0.2% per year to reflect broadly stable share count / small buybacks; dividend yield 0.37%.

Dry-run result before stamping: expected annualised ROI about 3.5%, with business growth contributing ~15.3% p.a., tiny buyback accretion, dividend income under 1% p.a., and multiple compression from ~98× to 55× subtracting roughly 10.9% p.a. The return is therefore highly sensitive to the exit multiple: if PME keeps a 75–90× multiple the outcome improves sharply, but that is not a valuation assumption I want to underwrite as “fair”.

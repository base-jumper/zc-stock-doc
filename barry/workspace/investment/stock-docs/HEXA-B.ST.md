# HEXA-B.ST — Hexagon AB

## Business Overview

Hexagon supplies precision sensors, metrology hardware and software that turn physical-world data into digital models and operating decisions. Its products are embedded in manufacturing, surveying, construction, mining and other workflows where accuracy, certification, retraining and integration create switching costs. The model combines equipment sales with software, maintenance and service revenue. The planned separation of the Octave businesses materially changes the perimeter, so historical group figures are an imperfect guide to the remaining Hexagon.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality — 0.62 / 0.78 confidence

Hexagon remained profitable in each of 2022–2025, but net income moved from €1.01bn to €0.86bn, €1.02bn and €0.62bn; 2025 was 40% below 2024. Cumulative FCF conversion was nevertheless 0.95× and receivables fell from 24.9% to 20.5% of revenue. Earnings are real but not predictable enough for a high score, especially with restructuring, acquisition accounting and capitalised development. Confidence would rise with the FY2025 annual report's ten-year profit bridge and capitalised-development note.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.72 / 0.86 confidence

FCF was €0.74–0.96bn in 2022–2025 and €0.90bn TTM, averaging a strong 15.5% margin. Capex absorbed roughly 40–46% of OCF, so this is not exceptionally capital-light; much is capitalised software development. FCF broadly matched cumulative net income and SBC was only 4–8% of FCF. The result is durable cash generation with a meaningful reinvestment burden, not a pure royalty stream.

### Fundamental Stability — 0.57 / 0.72 confidence

Revenue was essentially flat near €5.4bn across 2023–2025 while gross margin held at 65–67%, supporting durability of the installed base. However, 2025 operating margin fell to 18.7% from 27.5% and EPS fell 40%; consensus also reflects the post-separation perimeter rather than clean organic decline. The weakness is mixed and partly structural/perimeter-related, not yet a proven melting ice cube. Confidence would rise after two comparable quarters of standalone Hexagon reporting following the Octave separation.

### Pricing Power — 0.70 / 0.68 confidence

High, stable gross margins and deeply integrated metrology/geospatial workflows indicate switching costs, proprietary technology and an installed-base moat. The evidence supports a stable moat, but public summary data do not isolate price versus volume or retention, and AI-native design tools plus capable industrial competitors are live threats. Confidence would rise with the FY2025 annual report's organic price/volume disclosure and recurring-revenue retention data.

### Capital Allocation — 0.43 / 0.77 confidence

Across 2022–2025 Hexagon spent €2.23bn on acquisitions, versus €1.34bn of dividends and €0.15bn of buybacks; the diluted share count was flat. Dividends were well covered (2.27× by 2025 FCF), but goodwill represented 64% of invested capital in 2025 and goodwill-inclusive ROIC fell. This is an acquisition-led record with only mixed evidence of per-share value creation.

### Returns on Capital — 0.28 / 0.88 confidence

Goodwill-inclusive ROIC was only 5.2–7.9% in 2022–2025 and declined to 5.2% in 2025, likely near or below the cost of capital. Goodwill was 64–71% of invested capital, showing the drag from acquired value; low leverage means ROE is not disguising the result. Tangible product economics may be better, but the shareholder-relevant acquisition-inclusive record is poor.

### Conservative Debt — 0.60 / 0.82 confidence

2025 net debt/EBITDA was 1.91× and EBIT covered net interest 7.5×, acceptable for a mixed recurring-software/industrial business. Cash plus one year's FCF covered only 0.9× current debt, so refinancing access matters, although liquidity is not distressed. The detailed maturity ladder and fixed/floating mix should be checked in the FY2025 debt note.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.72 / 0.65 confidence

Hexagon's advantage is institutional—products, installed base, engineering teams and distribution—rather than dependent on one inventor. Leadership transitions and the separation add execution risk, while concentrated voting control limits minority checks. Confidence would rise with the FY2025 governance report's succession and executive-responsibility disclosures.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.55 / 0.58 confidence

Substantial insider ownership aligns economic outcomes, but the dual-class structure weakens shareholder rights and the available summary evidence does not establish whether long-term pay is tied to ROIC and per-share FCF rather than revenue/adjusted earnings. Confidence would rise with the 2026 remuneration report's metric weights, vesting periods and holding requirements.

Rule Breakers loses on durable growth: revenue has stalled and the group is being simplified rather than compounding rapidly. Wonderful-and-Fair loses on acquisition-inclusive returns on capital. Freeroll lacks a sufficiently defined, time-bound catalyst/floor pairing beyond the separation itself.

## Overall

Hexagon owns valuable precision-data franchises with strong gross margins and dependable cash conversion. At SEK78.76 the valuation is far less demanding than a year ago, but stagnant organic growth, the post-separation reset, acquisition-heavy capital allocation and sub-cost-of-capital goodwill-inclusive ROIC keep this a middling cash-cow candidate rather than a high-quality compounder.

## Valuation

The exit-multiple model uses SEK78.76, five years, FCF/share of SEK3.75 (normalised from roughly €0.90bn TTM FCF and 2.68bn shares using an approximate contemporary EUR/SEK conversion), 3% aggregate growth, no net dilution, an 18× exit P/FCF and the current 1.93% dividend yield. The exit multiple is below the implied entry multiple, so the case does not rely on re-rating. The FCF anchor and currency conversion should be refreshed from the first clean post-separation accounts. Agent score: 0.52.

## Thesis Connections

Potential fit with industrial automation, digital twins and productivity software, tempered by the need to distinguish secular demand from acquisition-led breadth.

## Watch

- First two comparable post-Octave quarters: organic growth, recurring revenue and adjusted versus reported margin.
- FY2025/2026 debt note: maturity wall, facilities and fixed/floating mix.
- Capitalised development versus amortisation and cash R&D; verify that FCF is not flattered.
- Acquisition returns: goodwill impairments and ROIC recovery.
- 2026 remuneration report: ROIC/per-share metrics, vesting and ownership requirements.

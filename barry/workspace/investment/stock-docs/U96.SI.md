# U96.SI — Sembcorp Industries Ltd

## Business Overview

Sembcorp Industries is a Singapore-listed energy and urban-solutions group. On 11 June 2026 it completed the A$6.5b enterprise-value acquisition of Alinta Energy, adding a vertically integrated Australian electricity-and-gas retailer/generator; the historical figures below pre-date consolidation, so the 13 August interim result is the first useful test of the enlarged group. The key established profit pools are:

- **Gas and Related Services:** sale of energy molecules, natural gas, steam, electricity and related water products from integrated assets. This remains the largest earnings contributor: FY2025 underlying net profit was **S$701m**, down 4% year on year, with weakness from lower UK contribution and Singapore generation spreads partly offset by Senoko Energy. Management said about **80% of its contracted Singapore portfolio** was locked in for five years or more as of February 2026, excluding Senoko.
- **Renewables:** solar, wind, energy storage and grid-integration services. FY2025 net profit before exceptional items was **S$192m**, up 5%. Operational capacity rose to **15.0GW** from 13.1GW, and gross renewables capacity including secured/under-construction projects reached **20.4GW**.
- **Integrated Urban Solutions:** urban development, water, waste and waste-to-resource solutions. FY2025 net profit before exceptional items was **S$178m**, up 3%.

FY2025 group turnover was **S$5.8b**, reported net profit from continuing operations was **S$984m**, and total dividend was **S$0.25/share**. The business is profitable and strategically exposed to energy transition, but it is capital intensive and meaningfully levered.

## Trait Assessment

Primary fit: **wonderful-and-fair**, but only a moderate fit. The company has real assets, visible contracted earnings and a renewables reinvestment runway; the weak points are heavy capital intensity, stretched leverage, and returns on capital that look decent rather than exceptional.

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.72 / confidence 0.72

Sembcorp has remained profitable through the four Yahoo Finance annual periods available: net income rose from S$848m in FY2022 to S$984m in FY2025, with no loss year. FY2025 underlying group net profit was S$1.0b, comparable with FY2024, and reported continuing net profit was S$984m. Segment diversity helps: renewables and urban growth offset weaker gas earnings in FY2025.

The quality haircut is cash conversion: cumulative FCF conversion over FY2022-FY2025 was only about 45%, and FY2024 FCF was negative. That is partly growth capex, not necessarily poor earnings, but it makes accounting profit a less clean owner-earnings proxy. Confidence would improve with a maintenance-vs-growth capex split from the annual report and project-level cash yield disclosures.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.55 / confidence 0.70

The scorer data shows ROIC around **9%-12%** across FY2022-FY2025, with FY2025 at **9.3%** and a four-year average near **9.8%**. ROE is much higher at ~18%, but that is boosted by leverage. These are acceptable infrastructure-style returns, not the obvious high-return fingerprint of a truly wonderful business.

Interpretation: Sembcorp may still compound if it can redeploy into renewables and urban assets at satisfactory returns, but the current return base does not justify treating it as a high-ROIC compounder. Confidence is fair because Yahoo-derived statements provide the recent numbers, but long-cycle project economics would firm this up.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.58 / confidence 0.55

There is some moat from infrastructure assets, long-term contracts, grid relationships, land/urban-development platforms and execution capability across Asian power markets. The FY2025 release notes that roughly 80% of contracted Singapore portfolio load is locked in for at least five years, which improves earnings visibility.

The offset is that gas/power generation spreads and renewable project returns can be commodity-, policy- and auction-driven. The FY2025 decline in gas earnings due to lower spreads is a reminder that Sembcorp is not a pure pricing-power business. Confidence is only moderate without detailed contract terms, tariff structures and project return disclosures.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.72 / confidence 0.70

The runway is the strongest part of the case. Operational renewables capacity reached **15.0GW** at end-2025, gross renewables capacity including secured/under-construction projects reached **20.4GW**, and the group has expanded renewables capacity more than six-fold over five years. It also met 2025 sustainability-linked targets: 15GW gross installed renewable capacity versus a 10GW target, and GHG emissions intensity of 0.21 tCO2e/MWh versus a 0.40 target.

The constraint is that this runway needs capital. FY2025 capex was S$962m, and acquisitions added another S$352m. The opportunity is real, but shareholder value depends on project returns exceeding the higher debt burden.

### Capital Allocation — 0.58 / confidence 0.68

Management is balancing growth investment with shareholder returns. FY2025 dividends were S$0.25/share, up 9%, and buybacks were modest; diluted shares fell about 1.2% over four years. The dividend payout on earnings is reasonable at ~45%-47%.

The negative is that dividends are not covered by recent reported FCF: FY2025 FCF was S$208m versus dividends of S$462m, and FY2024 FCF was negative. This is tolerable if capex is growth capex earning attractive future returns, but it is not cash-cow discipline. Debt has also risen while acquisitions and renewables buildout continue.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.38 / confidence 0.75

Debt is the main blemish. Yahoo/scorer data shows FY2025 net debt around **S$7.8b**, net debt/EBITDA about **3.85x**, debt/equity about **1.75x**, and EBIT interest cover about **4.3x**. The balance sheet is not distressed, but it is not conservative either, especially for a capital-intensive company still funding growth.

Liquidity looks manageable near term, but the doc needs a future refresh on the maturity ladder, fixed/floating mix and project-finance covenants. If debt rises further without a visible uplift in renewables cash earnings, this trait should be cut.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.75 / confidence 0.45

The business appears institutionally run rather than dependent on a single founder or rainmaker. Operations are asset-heavy, regulated/contracted and geographically diversified. Confidence is low because this pass did not dig into management bench depth or succession planning.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.60 / confidence 0.45

Stock-based compensation is small relative to net income, and management messaging emphasises sustainable returns, dividends and energy-transition execution. However, this pass did not review remuneration scorecards or insider ownership in enough detail. Confidence is low; next update should inspect the annual-report remuneration section.

**Why the other strategies lost:**

- **Cash-cow:** fails the central free-cash-flow test. FCF is volatile and currently weak after heavy growth capex; dividends are covered by earnings, not recent FCF.
- **Freeroll:** there is a dividend yield and tangible asset base, but no clear time-bound catalyst or obvious mispricing edge.
- **Rule-breakers:** energy transition is an attractive theme, but Sembcorp is a mature infrastructure utility/developer, not a hypergrowth top-dog disruptor. Revenue has fallen since FY2022 despite renewables capacity growth.

## Overall

Sembcorp is a credible, moderately attractive infrastructure compounder with a genuine energy-transition runway. The quality is real but not pristine: returns on capital are only mid-high single digit to low double digit, free cash flow is suppressed by capex, and leverage is heavy enough to matter. At **S$5.29**, roughly 9.6x trailing earnings with a ~4.7% indicated dividend yield, the valuation is not demanding, so expected returns can be fair if earnings hold and renewables projects mature into cash generation. The price is also at its current 52-week low, but that alone is not evidence of mispricing: the first result incorporating Alinta Energy is still pending.

My judgement: **good watchlist candidate, not a slam-dunk buy**. The stock deserves follow-up because the market is not pricing heroic growth, but the debt/FCF profile keeps the rating capped.

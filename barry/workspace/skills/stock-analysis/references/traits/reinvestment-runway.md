# Trait: Reinvestment Runway

**What we're looking for:**
A long runway to **redeploy earnings back into the business at high returns**, for years — the growth
engine of a compounder. A high [return on capital](returns-on-capital.md) only compounds intrinsic
value if the company can keep *putting capital to work* at that rate: intrinsic value grows at roughly
**ROIC × the reinvestment rate** (the share of earnings reinvested at that return). A business earning
20% on capital that can reinvest most of its earnings at 20% for a decade compounds far harder than
one earning 40% with nowhere to put the next dollar. Runway is what turns a high-return *business*
into a high-return *compounder*.

The opposite is the cash cow: a wonderful business in a saturated market with no high-return place to
reinvest. It is still a fine holding — it returns the cash, which is the
[capital allocation](capital-allocation.md) question — but it compounds slowly, gated by its payout
rather than its returns. Runway therefore sets the **ceiling** on compounding; a short one caps the
prize without disqualifying the business.

A genuine runway is two things of its own, resting on a precondition measured next door. Its own two:
a large, growing, **under-penetrated** market the company is still small within, and **proven,
repeatable** economics it can replicate many times (new units, capacity, geographies, products). The
precondition: that the **incremental return** on the capital being deployed stays high — a runway only
compounds if the *next* dollar still earns well, and whether it does is the
[returns on capital](returns-on-capital.md) question (its *Average vs incremental* read), not this
trait's. This trait owns the *room and duration* for reinvestment; that one owns the *rate* earned on
it. Watch the failure modes: penetration quietly filling up as the market matures; growth that leans
on unproven adjacencies or serial M&A rather than the core; and the treadmill — reinvestment that
merely *maintains* position (maintenance capex) rather than *creating* value (growth capex).

**Scoring guidance:**

* **0.70–1.00** A long runway: a large, under-penetrated, growing market and proven, repeatable
  economics, with incremental returns still high (per [returns on capital](returns-on-capital.md)). The
  business can reinvest a large share of earnings at high returns for many years.
* **0.40–0.69** A moderate or maturing runway: real reinvestment opportunity, but penetration is
  rising, incremental returns are softening, or the runway leans on unproven adjacencies or
  acquisitions — compounding will slow.
* **0.00–0.39** Little runway: a saturated or shrinking market, low or falling incremental returns, or
  reinvestment that only sustains position. A cash cow that must return capital to create value —
  which caps rather than disqualifies the case (see [capital allocation](capital-allocation.md)).

**Documentation:**

* The size, growth, and the company's current penetration of the addressable opportunity, with source
* Confirmation that incremental returns on recently deployed capital remain high — drawn from
  [returns on capital](returns-on-capital.md), not re-derived here
* The runway's nature: the organic reinvestment routes (units, capacity, geographies, products) and
  how proven and repeatable the economics are; any reliance on M&A
* The growth-versus-maintenance capex split, where discernible
* The verdict: roughly how much of earnings can be reinvested at high returns and for how long, and
  the resulting ceiling on compounding

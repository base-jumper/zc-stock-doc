# IFT.NZ — Infratil Limited

## Business Overview

Infratil is a New Zealand-listed infrastructure investment company managed by Morrison. It owns a diversified portfolio across digital infrastructure, renewables, telecommunications, healthcare and airport assets. The largest exposures at 31 March 2026 were **CDC** data centres, **One NZ**, **Longroad Energy**, **Contact Energy**, **Kao Data**, **Wellington Airport**, and healthcare imaging assets including **RHCNZ** and **Qscan**.

The business is best read as an externally managed infrastructure holding company rather than a conventional operating company. Reported IFRS earnings are noisy because asset revaluations, associates and portfolio transactions move the income statement; the cleaner operating lens is portfolio **Proportionate Operational EBITDAF** and net asset value. FY26 Proportionate Operational EBITDAF was **NZ$989.4m**, up **10.5%** on FY25 and near the top of the **NZ$960–1,000m** guidance range. FY26 net asset value after management fees was **NZ$16.26/share** versus the latest Yahoo price used here of **NZ$15.12**.

## Trait Assessment

### Downside Support / Valuation Floor — 0.68 / confidence 0.85

The floor is real but not bomb-proof. Infratil disclosed **NZ$20.624b** of portfolio asset value and **NZ$16.251b** of net asset value after wholly owned group net debt and the present value of the management agreement, equal to **NZ$16.26/share** at 31 March 2026. The current price of **NZ$15.12** is below that stated NAV, so the first layer of support is the independently valued portfolio rather than a simple earnings multiple.

I use **NZ$13.50/share** as the valuation floor for the asymmetric-payoff model: roughly a 17% haircut to the disclosed NAV, allowing for valuation-model risk, CDC/data-centre execution risk, leverage, external-manager fees and the fact that private asset marks are not cash. The floor is not as hard as net cash or a take-private bid, but it is supported by a diversified set of infrastructure assets and demonstrated partial monetisations. Confidence is fairly high because the NAV bridge is directly disclosed in the FY26 results presentation; it would improve further with the full independent valuation assumptions by asset.

### Fundamental Stability / No Value Trap — 0.78 / confidence 0.85

The portfolio does not look structurally impaired. FY26 operating growth came from CDC and Longroad capacity additions, while One NZ and Wellington Airport still delivered positive EBITDAF growth despite weak New Zealand macro conditions. The main challenged areas are Galileo, RHCNZ and One NZ valuation pressure, but these are portfolio-level dents rather than evidence that the whole asset base is a melting ice cube.

The key stability concern is funding intensity: FY27 guidance calls for **NZ$3.8–4.4b** of proportionate capital expenditure, driven mainly by CDC, so growth consumes capital before it becomes distributable cash. Infratil had **NZ$1.1b** of available liquidity at FY26, an S&P **BBB+** issuer rating, a completed bank debt refinance, and about **NZ$495m** of post-balance-date Contact Energy selldown proceeds. That supports the growth plan, but the equity value remains sensitive to execution and debt-market access.

### Mispriced for an Identifiable Reason — 0.60 / confidence 0.75

The market’s hesitation is understandable: Infratil is not cheap on simple earnings multiples, uses private valuations, carries material debt and is funding a very large CDC/renewables capex programme. One NZ’s valuation was reduced **NZ$327m** in FY26 and some development platforms are taking longer than hoped. The external management agreement and incentive-fee structure also deserve a discount.

The variant view is that the market may still be undercrediting the contracted CDC step-up and Longroad’s renewable buildout. CDC has more than **1GW** of contracted capacity; management says fully deployed contracted capacity is expected to deliver about **A$2b** of annualised contracted EBITDAF, and FY27 CDC EBITDAF guidance is **A$680–720m** with expected >**A$1b** in FY28 subject to delivery and customer activation. This is a clear operating bridge from current NAV to higher future earnings, but the current price already reflects a fair amount of that promise, so the mispricing is only moderate.

### Identifiable, Time-Bound Catalyst — 0.66 / confidence 0.78

The catalysts are concrete, though not forced in the classic special-situation sense. The FY27 guidance range is **NZ$1.3–1.4b** Proportionate Operational EBITDAF, about **21%** higher on a like-for-like basis. CDC capacity conversion, Longroad projects moving from construction into operation, the completed/targeted asset recycling programme, and the Qscan strategic review provide 1–2 year events that should either validate or puncture the NAV-growth thesis. Infratil is also on track against its initial **NZ$1b** divestment target, with **NZ$600m** of assets sold by FY26 results and a post-balance-date **~NZ$495m** Contact Energy selldown.

The weakness is that none of these guarantees a market re-rating. If CDC build timing slips, customer activation lags, financing costs rise, or private-market data-centre valuations compress, the catalyst can become merely “more capex” rather than a value-unlocking event.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful-and-Fair loses because Infratil’s accounting earnings and free cash flow are too noisy to underwrite like a clean operating compounder; FY25 net income was negative and FY26 FCF was negative on Yahoo statement data because growth capex is heavy. Cash Cow loses for the same reason: the dividend is modest and the thesis is not surplus cash distribution. Rule Breakers has partial exposure through CDC and Longroad, but I do not want to score the holding company as an emerging top-dog product company; the right frame is an asset-backed re-rate/NAV-growth setup.

## Overall

Infratil is a decent **Freeroll / NAV-growth** candidate, not a screaming bargain. The portfolio is high quality, CDC and Longroad give real growth, and disclosed NAV provides useful support. But the upside from **NZ$15.12** to a reasonable **NZ$18.00** fair value is not huge, the dividend yield is low, and the next leg depends on capital-intensive execution. I’d keep it watched, but the setup is more “solid infrastructure compounder with moderate mispricing” than “fat-pitch asymmetric bet.”

## Thesis Connections

Relevant to FIT themes around **AI/data-centre infrastructure, renewable energy buildout, electrification and digital connectivity**. CDC is the cleanest connection: contracted data-centre capacity is the main growth engine. Longroad adds U.S. renewables exposure, including potential data-centre co-location opportunities.

## Watch

- CDC: conversion of contracted capacity into billable capacity; FY27 EBITDAF **A$680–720m** and FY28 >**A$1b** target/signpost.
- CDC capex: FY27 capex guidance **A$3.8–4.2b** excluding land; watch cost overruns, customer activation timing and funding mix.
- Longroad: FY27 EBITDAF **US$120–135m**, 2GW under construction and US$300m further Infratil support.
- FY27 group guidance: Proportionate Operational EBITDAF **NZ$1.3–1.4b** and development spend **NZ$95–110m**.
- Asset recycling: Qscan strategic review update expected in H1 FY27; proceeds and valuation versus carrying value.
- Balance sheet: liquidity, LTV, debt maturities, capital bond issuance and whether BBB+ funding savings materialise.
- One NZ: whether EBITDAF stays in the **NZ$600–640m** FY27 range and whether mobile/wholesale offsets weak fixed/enterprise markets.
- Firm up incentive alignment by reading the FY2026 annual report remuneration and management-fee sections in detail.

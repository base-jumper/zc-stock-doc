# Trait: Key-Person Risk

**What we're looking for:**
A business whose success **does not hinge on any single individual** — one that would keep compounding
if any one person walked out the door tomorrow. The ideal is Buffett's: *"a business so wonderful an
idiot can run it, because sooner or later one will."* A wonderful, durable business has its advantage
embedded in its brand, systems, scale, and culture — not in one irreplaceable head.

> **Scoring direction:** this trait is scored as **resilience**. A **high** score means **low**
> key-person risk (the business outlasts any individual); a **low** score means the thesis is really
> a concentrated bet on one person. Don't let the name invert the scale.

**Why this matters:**
Dependence on a single person is a hidden fragility that a clean income statement won't show. The
person can leave, retire, burn out, die, defect to a rival, or — if they hold outsized control —
make one catastrophic call with no check on them. For a strategy built on businesses we can
*underwrite for years*, "will the CEO still be here, and still be brilliant, in a decade?" is exactly
the kind of question we don't want the thesis to depend on.

**This is the *fragility* axis, not the *quality* axis — and they are orthogonal.**
A brilliant founder-CEO scores *well* on [management quality](management-backing.md) **and** *badly*
here at the same time, and that tension is the point: their brilliance is an asset, *and* their
indispensability is a risk. This trait does not re-judge whether the person is good — it asks how
badly the business breaks without them. Don't collapse the two; record both.

**Where key-person risk hides:**

| Locus | The dependence | What reduces it |
|---|---|---|
| **Founder / CEO** | The vision, decisions, or capital allocation run through one person | A deep bench, a real and tested succession plan, decisions institutionalised in process |
| **Star talent** | A specific fund manager, scientist, engineer, designer, or creative *is* the product | A team/platform that develops talent; output that survives any one departure |
| **Key relationships** | Major customer, supplier, or regulatory ties held *personally* by one individual | Relationships owned by the institution, contracts in the company's name, breadth of accounts |
| **Concentrated control** | Super-voting shares or entrenchment let one person act without checks | Sunset clauses, independent board, aligned ordinary holders, governance guardrails |
| **Thin bench** | No credible #2; the org chart has one indispensable node | Strong, visible second-line leadership with real authority |

**Key questions:**

* If the founder/CEO (or the single most important person) left tomorrow, would the business stumble
  briefly — or would the thesis break?
* Is there a credible, ideally *tested*, successor and a deep second line of leadership?
* Are the advantages embedded in the brand, systems, scale, and culture — or in one person's head,
  taste, or relationships?
* Does anyone hold entrenched or super-voting control that lets them act without a check?
* Is the value here a bet on a *business*, or a bet on a *person*?

**Scoring guidance:**

* **0.70–1.00** Low key-person risk: the business clearly outlasts any individual — deep bench, a real
  (ideally tested) succession path, advantage embedded in brand/system/scale rather than a person.
  Losing any one person would be a bump, not a break.
* **0.40–0.69** Moderate concentration: a strong founder/CEO or key figure who would be genuinely hard
  to replace, only partial succession, or meaningful control concentration. The business would survive
  their loss but with real disruption.
* **0.00–0.39** High key-person risk: critically dependent on one irreplaceable person — no credible
  successor, key relationships/IP/decisions concentrated in them, or entrenched control with no checks.
  The thesis is effectively a bet on that individual.

**Documentation:**

* The single most important person(s) and exactly what depends on them (vision, decisions, talent,
  relationships, control)
* Succession and bench depth: is there a credible, tested #2 and second line — with evidence
* Control structure: any super-voting / entrenchment, and what (if anything) checks it
* How much of the advantage is institutional (brand, systems, scale, culture) vs personal
* The reason for the score — and a note where this deliberately diverges from
  [management quality](management-backing.md) (a great-but-indispensable leader scores high there,
  low here)

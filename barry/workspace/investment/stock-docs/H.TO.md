# H.TO — Hydro One Limited

## Business Overview

Hydro One is Ontario's largest regulated electricity transmission and distribution utility, serving about 1.5 million customers and owning the province's backbone power network. It earns mainly through Ontario Energy Board-approved transmission and distribution rates on a large regulated asset base, not through merchant power-price exposure. In plain English: this is infrastructure compounding by putting capital into the grid, getting those assets approved into rate base, and collecting an allowed regulated return.

The business is boring in the useful way. Q1 2026 EPS rose to CAD 0.65 from CAD 0.60, revenue rose to CAD 2.65b, and the drivers were OEB-approved rates plus higher peak demand. Hydro One is also being handed a long queue of grid projects — Greenstone, Sudbury-to-Barrie, Red Lake, St. Clair, Bowmanville-to-GTA and other transmission lines — driven by electrification, reliability spending, and Ontario industrial load growth.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.75 / 0.75

Hydro One has a clean regulated-utility earnings profile: net income rose from CAD 1.05b in 2022 to CAD 1.34b in 2025, with no loss years in the Yahoo-served four-year window and low reported profit volatility. Q1 2026 continued that pattern, with EPS up 8% year on year to CAD 0.65. The weakness is cash conversion: free cash flow is negative because capex exceeds operating cash flow. For a growing regulated utility, that is not automatically poor earnings quality if the spend enters rate base, but it means accounting earnings are the better near-term valuation anchor than raw FCF. Confidence would firm up with the full 10-year filing history and rate-base reconciliation.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.50 / 0.75

Returns are stable but not high on a generic operating-company scale. Yahoo-derived ROIC has sat around 5.4–6.1% from 2022–2025, ROE around 9–11%, and incremental ROIC was estimated near 9%. That is acceptable for a regulated electric utility if allowed returns and cost recovery hold, but it is not the high-return, capital-light profile this trait ideally wants. The spread over Hydro One's allowed cost of capital and OEB rate decisions are the key evidence to keep checking.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.72 / 0.70

The moat is structural rather than brand-led: Hydro One is the regulated owner/operator of critical Ontario grid assets. Pricing power comes through OEB-approved rates and cost recovery rather than discretionary price hikes. Q1 2026 revenue growth was explicitly attributed to OEB-approved 2026 rates and higher peak demand. The risk is that regulated pricing can be politically constrained; the OEB's denial of CAD 69m of ice-storm Z-factor recovery is a live reminder that not every cost is automatically recoverable.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.72 / 0.75

This is the main reason Hydro One fits better as a modest regulated compounder than as a cash cow. The company is investing heavily in transmission and distribution reliability, new load connections, and Ontario grid expansion. Q1 2026 capital investments were CAD 715m and assets placed in service were CAD 484m. The company has been selected for multiple priority transmission projects expected in service around the early 2030s, and its 2025 annual report cites 14 transmission lines in development or construction. The runway is long, but returns are regulated and debt-funded, so the upside is steady rather than explosive.

### Capital Allocation — 0.60 / 0.65

Management is directing cash into regulated infrastructure where recovery should be available through rate base, and the dividend payout is reasonable against earnings: Yahoo shows a payout ratio around 58%, with the quarterly dividend increased to CAD 0.3531 in Q2 2026. The caveat is that free cash flow is negative after capex and dividends are not covered by post-growth FCF; debt issuance funds the expansion gap. That is normal for utilities during a growth cycle, but it narrows the margin for error if projects are delayed, costs are disallowed, or rates disappoint.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.48 / 0.75

Leverage is the biggest quality drag. Net debt/EBITDA was about 5.8x at 2025 year-end and 5.9x on Yahoo's TTM snapshot, with EBIT interest coverage around 3.4x. A regulated utility can carry more debt than a cyclical operating company because cash flows are stable and asset-backed, but this is still toward the stretched end even for the type. The business is not showing imminent solvency risk; the issue is that leverage and refinancing sensitivity can absorb a lot of the value created by rate-base growth.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.80 / 0.75

The thesis does not depend on one charismatic operator. Hydro One is an institutional regulated utility with embedded assets, OEB oversight, and deep operating processes. The 2026 CEO transition from David Lebeter to COO Megan Telford looks orderly and internal, which supports continuity. A fuller board/management bench review would raise confidence.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.60 / 0.55

Governance signals are mixed-to-okay. Yahoo's governance risk snapshot is low overall, and the regulated utility model naturally constrains extreme empire-building. However, the Government of Ontario remains an important stakeholder and appoints nominees, so political objectives can sit alongside minority-shareholder returns. Confidence is only moderate until the latest proxy is reviewed for pay metrics, ownership requirements, and long-term incentive design.

### Why the other strategies lost

Cash Cow loses because the company is not throwing off distributable free cash after capex; FCF was negative in 2023–2025 and TTM FCF is negative. Freeroll loses because the stock lacks a hard valuation floor or time-bound re-rating catalyst: at CAD 58.50 it trades around 25.7x trailing earnings and 2.7x book, with only a 2.4% dividend yield. Rule Breakers loses because this is a mature regulated utility, not a high-growth disruptor, despite a useful electrification/grid-investment runway.

## Overall

Hydro One is a durable, low-beta regulated infrastructure compounder with a credible multi-year capital-investment runway. The attractive part is predictability: demand is essential, the company is a dominant Ontario grid operator, and rate-base expansion should keep EPS moving upward at a modest pace.

The problem is price and leverage. At CAD 58.50, the market is already paying roughly 25x trailing earnings for a regulated utility whose ROIC sits around 6% and whose debt load is high. That does not make it a bad company; it makes it a low-margin-for-error stock. I would classify it as watchable quality, not an obvious buy at this price.

## Thesis Connections

Potential FIT connection: grid modernization/electrification infrastructure. Hydro One is not a high-upside electrification pick, but it is a direct beneficiary of transmission build-out, industrial load growth, EV/battery manufacturing demand, and Ontario reliability spending.

## Watch

- OEB decisions on cost recovery, allowed ROE, rate-base additions, and storm-cost treatment — especially after the denied CAD 69m ice-storm Z-factor recovery.
- Net debt/EBITDA and interest coverage as capex remains above operating cash flow.
- Execution and in-service timing for Greenstone, Sudbury-to-Barrie, Red Lake, St. Clair, Bowmanville-to-GTA and other priority transmission projects.
- Dividend payout versus earnings and funding mix; watch for dividend growth that outruns rate-base/earnings growth.
- Latest proxy statement for incentive metrics, ownership requirements, and Government of Ontario influence.

# CAR.AX — CAR Group Limited

## Business Overview

CAR Group operates digital vehicle marketplaces: carsales in Australia, Encar in South Korea, Trader Interactive in North America, webmotors in Brazil, and chileautos in Chile, plus RedBook vehicle data services. Dealers pay subscriptions, lead-generation and premium-listing fees; private sellers pay listing and enhancement fees; advertisers buy media; and newer products monetise finance, inspections, data and digital transactions.

The model is attractive because inventory attracts buyers, buyers generate leads, and lead volume makes the platform indispensable to dealers. That marketplace-and-data flywheel supports high margins and repeated price/yield increases. International operations now supply most group revenue, reducing reliance on Australian vehicle conditions while giving CAR several less-monetised marketplaces in which to deploy products proven at carsales.

## Trait Assessment

### Earnings Quality / Consistent, Predictable Profits — 0.88 / 0.94

CAR has produced recurring profits and strong cash conversion across different vehicle cycles. FY26 pro-forma revenue and EBITDA rose 12% in constant currency to A$1.253bn and A$700m; adjusted NPAT rose 11% to A$407m and reported NPAT rose 14% to A$314m. Reported EBITDA converted to operating cash flow at 100%. Owner FCF of about A$436m (A$577m operating cash flow less A$12m property/equipment and A$130m intangible investment) exceeded reported profit; acquired-intangible amortisation makes statutory earnings more conservative than cash economics.

Interpretation: earnings are recurring, geographically diversified, and well backed by cash. The main normalisation risk is management's use of adjusted/pro-forma measures, but the statutory cash-flow statement and reported growth corroborate the underlying result.

### Free Cash Flow Generation — 0.90 / 0.94

FY26 operating cash flow was A$577m and owner FCF was about A$436m, a 34.8% FCF margin and 1.39x reported-NPAT conversion. Property/equipment purchases were A$12m and capitalised intangible investment A$130m, together consuming about 25% of operating cash flow. Even after both, CAR funded A$298m of cash dividends internally.

Interpretation: this is genuine, recurring surplus cash rather than cash created by underinvestment. Capitalised software development needs watching because it can flatter operating expense and FCF definitions, but total cash conversion remains strong after intangible investment.

### Fundamental Stability / Secular Durability — 0.91 / 0.94

CAR's cash stream rests on vehicle turnover and dealer marketing rather than new-car unit growth alone. Used vehicles continue to change hands through economic cycles, while the marketplace can grow through yield, premium listing depth, data, finance and transaction products. FY26 delivered positive revenue and EBITDA growth across Australia, North America, Latin America and Asia, and FY27 guidance calls for double-digit constant-currency growth in every international region.

Interpretation: this is not a melting ice cube. The structural risks are marketplace disintermediation by social platforms or AI interfaces, dealer consolidation, and regulation of data/finance products; current audience, lead and margin trends do not show erosion.

### High, Durable Returns on Capital — 0.64 / 0.90

For this capital-light marketplace, returns including acquisition goodwill are the right shareholder test. Yahoo-derived ROIC was 10.4% in FY22, 7.8% in FY24 and 8.3% in FY25; FY23 is distorted by acquisition accounting and a large non-operating gain. A simple FY26 primary-source cross-check—A$482m EBIT, tax-adjusted, against roughly A$4.0bn of year-end equity plus borrowings less cash—puts ROIC around 9%. These returns are only modestly above a reasonable cost of capital and fall short of the usual 20% wonderful-business threshold. The underlying platforms are far better: FY26 group pro-forma EBITDA margin was 56%, Australia earned a 67% EBITDA margin, and group owner-FCF margin was about 35%.

Interpretation: the operating franchises have exceptional incremental economics, but CAR paid substantial prices to assemble them. Intangible assets were A$4.18bn at FY26, making consolidated ROIC the clearest soft spot. Management does not disclose acquisition-by-acquisition returns or post-deal return hurdles, so evidence of rising group ROIC remains the best test.

### Pricing Power / Durable Moat — 0.91 / 0.94

CAR has network effects, brand, proprietary data and workflow integration. Australia retained market leadership while FY26 dealer revenue rose 8% to A$270m through lead volume, yield and greater depth penetration; private, media, and data/services revenue rose 4%, 8%, and 5%. Australian EBITDA rose 8% and its margin increased to 67%, showing that yield and product depth are sticking despite continued investment.

The moat appears stable-to-widening. FY26 webmotors growth was supported by audience leadership and national expansion; Encar Guarantee inspections represented 57% of listings and Home delivery transactions rose 65%. CAR says its vehicle-ecosystem integrations create unique data that improves dealer tools and consumer trust. Live threats are dealer resistance to price increases, competing classifieds and social platforms, and AI search interfaces weakening destination-site traffic.

### Reinvestment Runway — 0.84 / 0.90

The runway is chiefly deeper monetisation rather than more car sales. FY26 constant-currency revenue grew 12% in North America, 19% in Latin America and 15% in Asia; the drivers were premium products, media and data, finance/dealer expansion, Guarantee 2.0, Home Services and Dealer Direct. Management guides to FY27 group revenue growth of 11–14% and double-digit growth in every international region.

The routes are repeatable: deploy Australian marketplace, data, finance, inspection and transaction products into less-developed platforms. Capital intensity is low, although software development is capitalised: FY26 intangible purchases were A$130m and property/equipment purchases A$12m. The constraint is that part of the historical geographic runway was bought through acquisitions; future returns must come increasingly from organic cross-sell and penetration.

### Capital Allocation — 0.66 / 0.90

FY26 operating cash flow was A$577m. CAR spent A$141m on tangible and intangible investment, A$203m acquiring associates and subsidiaries, and A$298m on cash dividends. The group has built valuable leadership positions through acquisitions, but the higher acquisition spend and A$119m increase in net debt reinforce that strategic fit has not automatically meant conservative deployment. Diluted shares remain broadly stable and the FY26 dividend increased 8% to A$0.86 per share.

The dividend remains FCF-covered: cash dividends were about 68% of owner FCF. The verdict is competent rather than exceptional allocation—strong acquired assets and good operating execution, offset by acquisition leverage, only moderate returns on total invested capital, and a larger post-year-end A$1.2bn revolving facility that creates capacity for further deployment.

### Conservative Debt / Financial Strength — 0.68 / 0.95

At FY26 CAR reported A$1.20bn net debt and 1.7x net debt/pro-forma EBITDA. Total borrowings were A$1.52bn against A$326m cash; owner FCF of about A$436m covered the debt load well. The bank covenants cap net leverage at 3.5x and require interest cover above 3.0x, and CAR complied throughout the year. At balance date it had A$246m of undrawn facilities; the August refinancing subsequently extended the A$1.2bn syndicated facility into staggered 2029–2031 maturities, alongside fixed-rate US private-placement notes maturing in 2030–2032.

Interpretation: debt should not threaten the business in a normal downturn, and the refinancing materially reduces near-term maturity risk. It is still not conservative for a capital-light marketplace: net debt rose 11%, much of the bank debt floats, and the larger facility creates both flexibility and acquisition-capacity risk.

### Key-Person Risk — 0.88 / 0.90

The company is institutionally managed rather than founder-dependent. William Elliott completed his first year as CEO after serving as CFO; FY26 then delivered double-digit constant-currency pro-forma revenue, EBITDA and adjusted NPAT growth. Geoff Trumbull joined as CFO in March 2026, and the marketplaces, data assets and local management teams would survive any one executive's departure.

Interpretation: the smooth succession and clean first full-year result are evidence of organisational depth. Elliott's longer-term capital-allocation record remains unproven, but key-person fragility is low.

### Incentive Alignment — 0.74 / 0.90

FY26 remuneration is performance-heavy: the STI is 70% tied equally to pro-forma revenue and EBITDA and 30% to strategic measures, with 25% deferred into equity for one year; the three-year LTI is 70% tied equally to adjusted EPS and relative TSR and 30% strategic. Elliott's holding rose from 34,994 to 58,477 shares, while 60% of his accounting remuneration was at risk. The design is broadly aligned and dilution remains modest, although adjusted/pro-forma measures and strategic scorecards are gameable and the STI outcome was a high 89.2% of maximum.

Interpretation: incentives and ownership are reasonably aligned, but CAR is not owner-operated and executives have no disclosed minimum holding requirement equivalent to that imposed on non-executive directors. Three-year vesting and relative TSR are positives; revenue/EBITDA incentives can still favour acquisition-led growth.

**Why the other strategies lost:** Wonderful and Fair is a genuine near-tie: CAR has the moat and international reinvestment runway, but acquisition-adjusted ROIC is only moderate and the scorer gives slightly more weight to its proven cash economics. Rule Breakers loses because online vehicle classifieds are established rather than an emerging industry, and group growth around 10–13% is compounder growth rather than hypergrowth. Freeroll loses because the share price has no hard asset or yield floor and there is no single time-bound catalyst; the thesis depends on ongoing compounding.

## Overall

CAR is a high-quality global marketplace cash generator with a strong network/data moat, excellent margins, dependable conversion and several organic monetisation levers. It is a genuine Cash Cow / Wonderful-and-Fair dual-fit: mature Australian cash economics fund dividends and international product penetration. The market's concern is understandable: growth has moderated from the acquisition step-ups, the founder-era CEO has changed, leverage is not trivial, and consolidated ROIC exposes the price paid for international expansion.

At A$28.98, the strong FY26 result has prompted a material re-rating from the July review price but the stock remains well below its A$42.06 52-week high. The business quality is confirmed; the prospective return is now only moderate. This is a **watch rather than chase** candidate, with a better margin of safety below roughly A$24–25 if the operating thesis remains intact.

## Valuation

I use FCF because acquired-intangible amortisation makes statutory EPS noisy and cash conversion is central to the thesis. The A$1.15 per-share fundamental is FY26 owner FCF of about A$436m divided by roughly 378m diluted shares. Inputs are: the A$28.98 intraday quote on 12 August 2026; five years; 8% aggregate FCF growth, below FY27 adjusted-NPAT guidance and without relying on further acquisitions; 0.2% annual dilution; 22x exit P/FCF; and a 2.97% dividend yield based on the A$0.86 FY26 distribution.

The model assumes multiple compression from the 25.2x P/FCF entry multiple to 22x. It produces an 8.27% annualised ROI and 1.49x total value over five years. Attribution is 8.00% business growth, a 0.20% dilution drag, a 2.68% multiple-compression drag and a 3.38% income contribution. The return is carried by business growth and dividends, but the entry price consumes much of it. On visible merits I assign an agent score of 0.61: a compelling business whose confirmed quality is now offset by modest valuation headroom, middling acquisition-adjusted ROIC and rising absolute debt.

## Thesis Connections

CAR fits a digital-marketplace compounding thesis: category-leading platforms use audience, inventory and proprietary data to deepen network effects and add high-margin monetisation layers. International product replication is the key connection—the Australian playbook can create value without requiring vehicle-market volumes to grow rapidly.

## Watch

- FY27 results: revenue growth of 11–14%, adjusted EBITDA growth of 10–13% and adjusted NPAT growth of 9–12% in constant currency.
- Australia: dealer lead volumes, yield and premium-depth penetration; watch for price resistance or margin erosion.
- Brazil: whether webmotors' market-share gains continue and translate into finance/media monetisation.
- Korea: Guarantee penetration beyond 60% of new listings and adoption of Guarantee++ and Dealer Direct.
- North America: whether premium products and media keep revenue growth above EBITDA growth without permanently lowering margins.
- Balance sheet: net leverage versus 1.7x, acquisition use of the expanded A$1.2bn syndicated facility, and interest cover against the 3.0x covenant floor.
- Incentives: whether Elliott builds a more meaningful personally funded stake and whether FY27 strategic targets and high STI outcomes remain rigorous.
- Acquisition returns: evidence that incremental ROIC rises as the acquired platforms scale.
- AI-disintermediation risk: organic traffic, direct audience and lead conversion as search behaviour changes.
